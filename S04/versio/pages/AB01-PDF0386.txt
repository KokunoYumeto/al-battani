Mumāzaǵah, and more rarely mizāǵ, are thus the applications of the planets to one another or to the Moon. The Greeks used σύγκρασις in this sense (Pseudo-Proclus on the Tetrabiblos, p. 76; Vettius Valens; a Laurentian codex in Boll, Sphaera, p. 10), more rarely μῖξις (Palchus); Firmicus Maternus says mixtura (ed. Kroll, I, 2 and I, 4), the Syriac mūzāgā (Bardesanes, The Book of the Laws of Countries, ed. Nau, Paris 1899, pp. 18-19 of the text, 44 of the translation). Among astrologers mizāǵ also means the temperament or nature of each heavenly body, as in the Arabic Tetrabiblos quoted in Part II, p. 290. A note adds that the Latin De magnis coniunctionibus of Albumasar (Augsburg 1489) is not this Kitāb al-qirānāt but the Kitāb fīhi ǵumal min dalālāt al-ashkhāṣ al-‘alawiyyah, as Steinschneider and Loth saw.
