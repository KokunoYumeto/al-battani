% Commentary on chapter 48 a) concluded: al-Battānī rightly preferred to compute in arcs of the equator, since arcs of the ecliptic would change in one climate through the year with the varying inclination of the ecliptic to the horizon (note 1, Schiaparelli: both kinds of arc differ with the latitude). Only on p. 118, l. 14-28, does he count the arc of visibility on the ecliptic, explaining his tables of the appearances and disappearances of the planets (t. II, pp. 142-43), made for the latitude 36° 0′ of ar-Raqqah after Ptolemy's tables, for those who avoid the direct (better) computation. Ḥabash (fol. 67 v.-70 v., tables fol. 73 r.-76 v.) and Ulugh Beg (p. 142 text, 192 transl.) always take arcs of the ecliptic; Barhebraeus (pp. 79-80 text) speaks after Ptolemy of the depression of the Sun, counted in its vertical circle. The Indians fixed the limits of visibility on the equator, like al-Battānī, with other numbers: Sūrya-Siddhānta IX, 4-9 (Burgess-Whitney pp. 365-67), al-Bīrūnī, India, chapter LVII. Chapter 49, a) p. 119, l. 3-41: the configurations of the fixed stars with the Sun. Tashrīq and taghrīb usually mean the heliacal rising and setting of stars, as the definition of the Mafātīḥ al-‘ulūm shows (note 2: ed. van Vloten, Leiden 1895, pp. 229-230; written between A.H. 363 and 381, A.D. 973 and 991); since this does not always fit al-Battānī's use in this chapter, Nallino says «ortus anterior» and «occasus anterior». The nine kinds (τρόποι) of configurations (σχηματισμοί) of the fixed stars with the Sun, after Ptolemy VIII, 4, with Halma's French: 1. morning east wind, in three ways (subsequent, true simultaneous, prior rising); 2. morning culmination (simultaneous not apparent, true, prior); 3. morning west wind, morning setting (subsequent, true simultaneous, prior); 4. southern east wind, diurnal and nocturnal; 5. southern culmination (begun).
