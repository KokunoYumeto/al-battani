% Commentary on chapter 54 d) II continued: the place of Albumasar (Abū Ma‘shar), as Steinschneider first noticed (Lettere, p. 15 note 3 and p. 20), is the chapter «On extracting the rays of the stars according to Ptolemy» in the Introductorium in astronomiam Albumasaris Abalachi octo continens libros partiales, Venice 1506, book VII, ch. 7, beginning: «In extracting the rays of the stars more diversity is found among the various treatises than elsewhere. So we have chosen Ptolemy, whose very words these are .....». The same precepts, with the Latin wording little changed, Nallino finds after the end of the version of ‘Alī ibn Riḍwān's commentary (Haly Heben Rodan) on the Tetrabiblos (note 1: Liber Quadripartiti Ptolemei; Centiloquium eiusdem, etc., Venice 1519, fol. 94 v.-95 r.), where some chapters, not noticed so far, are added, the first entitled «science of the projection of the rays». He has no doubt that this is the place which Ibn al-Qifṭī and Ibn Abī Uṣaybi‘ah say existed only in the copy of Rabban aṭ-Ṭabarī. The method of computation is nevertheless not wrongly ascribed to Ptolemy, as shown below. The rules, when the star is between the ascendant and the midheaven (note 2: all the cases are set out at length by John of Saxony in his commentary on Alcabitius, 1331 (p. 309, note 1), fol. ff7 r.-gg2 v. of the 1485 edition, 30 r.-32 v. of 1521; strangely he says at the end that he has not seen this method set out in any book; if the simple distance AR of the star's degree − AR of the midheaven is taken instead of the hours of distance, the divisor in the formula of the equated radiation is not 6 but the semidiurnal arc of the star's degree): (AR of the star's degree − AR of the midheaven) / temporal hours of the star's degree = hours of distance from the midheaven; the ecliptic arc corresponding to (AR of the star's degree + 60°) = first radiation; the ecliptic arc corresponding to (oblique ascension of the star's degree + 60°) = second radiation; radiation I ± (radiation I − radiation II)/6 × hours of distance = equated radiation, the longitude of the point of the ecliptic on which the left sextile falls; for the right one −60° for +60°; for the quadrature or trine 90° or 120°. Much easier when the planet is on the meridian: the radiation is given by the ecliptic degrees corresponding to (AR of the planet ± 60°); in the ascendant: the ecliptic degrees corresponding to (oblique ascension of the planet's degree ± 60°); in the west or descendant: those corresponding to (oblique ascension of the nadir degree ± 60°) + 180°. Clearly this «Ptolemaic» method is the same as that by which the astrologers determine the directions or profections, as Nallino will show in the notes on chapter LV; they take the amount of the radiation on either side of the star's longitude to be the arc of the ecliptic between the degree of the planet's longitude and the circle of position (note 3: circles of position are the great circles through the common intersections of the meridian and the horizon; their poles are on the prime vertical) drawn through the point of the equator which is 60° or 90° or 120° before or after the right ascension of the planet. But in this way (which Ḥabash also has, f. 114 r.-v., though he does not name Ptolemy) the star is taken to have no latitude and to lie on the ecliptic. If the latitude is not to be neglected, the computation must be changed a little, as in the direction of stars with latitude; that is, for the degree of the ecliptic that gives the longitude of the star the degree culminating with the given star is to be used, when (continued).
