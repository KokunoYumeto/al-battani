% Commentary on chapter 35, a) continued: the astrologers divide the heavens into twelve houses by six great circles meeting in the two ends of a diameter from south to north; Alcabitius and those before Campanus (after Nabod) put these ends at the poles of the equator: the meridian and the circle through the rising point of the ecliptic, the equator divided into four parts and each into three, with four circles of declination; the ecliptic divided into four equal parts only once a day. Others put the ends at the intersections of the horizon and the meridian, with circles of position: a) Campanus and Gazulus divide the prime vertical into twelve equal parts; b) Regiomontanus the equator; Schöner divides the ecliptic, with circles of latitude; Nabod on the astrologers; Ptolemy divides the equator into twelve parts of two equinoctial hours (begun). Notes: the Greek, Latin and Arabic names of the houses; Delambre on Alcabitius; Regiomontanus' Tabulae directionum; Gazulus; Schöner; al-Khayyāṭ, Abū Ma'shar, al-Ḥasan ibn al-Khaṣīb, Bonatti.
