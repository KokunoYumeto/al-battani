% Commentary on chapter 57 c) concluded: in this it differs from Ptolemy's parallactic rules, that in al-Battānī the degrees are marked on the rule HM, in Ptolemy on the rule FH, to whose division HM must afterwards be referred. According to R. Wolf, Handbuch der Astronomie, vol. II, p. 26, § 333, Regiomontanus was the first to mark the division of the degrees on the rule HM (note 1: I think in the work entitled Joh. de Monteregio and G. Purbach's Epitome, containing propositions and annotations by which the whole Almagest ... is explained and set out in clear and brief teaching, Basel 1543, book V, ch. III, p. 95 («to make the rules of Ptolemy»)); it is most likely that Regiomontanus learned it from al-Battānī's book. On appendix A. a) p. 145: general remarks on the appendices A-H. These appendices, which bear no number in the Arabic text, are wholly missing in Plato, in the printed editions as well as in the manuscripts. Yet there is no reason to doubt that they are genuine (D and probably F excepted). For: 1. As I indicated in their places in the translation, they always refer to genuine tables of al-Battānī. 2. The astronomical elements and the Arabic language do not differ from those of the other parts of the book. 3. Appendices A, B, C are the necessary complement of chapter XXXII, at whose end the author had promised them (note 2: p. 71, l. 4-5, let the translation be corrected thus: «... of the months we have set; how one must work with them we have explained near those tables, so that, God willing, ...». Likewise Plato: «for the tables by which the beginnings of the months are known, whose working is explained in them, so that the knowledge of which we have need» [so the editions, but rightly the Vatican codex: we have] «may always be easy, we have set down»); without them the use of the tables vol. II, pp. 7-8 cannot be understood. 4. Appendix E, needed to understand the tables vol. II, pp. 187-88, was already announced at the end of chapter LIII (p. 129) in these words: «..... we made tables, whose use we have explained in the last of the chapters after the end of the book, so that easily etc.». The words printed in italics Plato leaves out, so that the sense is not understood. 5. Likewise appendix H was foretold in chapter XLII (p. 94, l. 10-11) in these words: «..... after the seconds added to or taken from the motion of the Moon, which you have found under the difference between the Sun and the Moon in the way we taught in that chapter near those tables». Which Plato too translates: «the way which we taught in that very chapter in those very tables». From this last place and from the end of chapter XXXII just mentioned it is clear that al-Battānī placed some of these appendices near the very tables whose use they were to illustrate (note 3: cf. also the title of appendix C, p. 146: «Chapter on finding by these tables the eras of the Arabs etc.»); and that the copyists later moved them all to the end of the chapters. If so, it is easily understood why they bear no number, and why Plato, who had left out the tables, neglected them too. b) p. 145, l. 8 and following: I. When the «signs» are sought in the table of the collected years alone, al-Battānī orders one always to add a unit to the sign found there; not so when they are drawn from the table of the single years alone or from both together. In the interval of one common year the signs grow by 4 units; in the interval of 30 years (continued).
