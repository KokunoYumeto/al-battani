% Commentary on chapter 51 b) II concluded: al-Battānī knew that errors of some minutes easily creep into his observations of the fixed stars; rightly he would not emend Ptolemy's tables of latitude by very uncertain corrections. Between Ptolemy and al-Battānī the greatest change of latitude (for stars near 90° and 270° of longitude) from the diminished obliquity was only 6′, or a quarter of a degree if the obliquities found by Ptolemy and al-Battānī are taken. c) p. 125, l. 3-4: «besides adh-dhu’ābah, al-fard and al-mirzam»: aṣ-Ṣūfī, p. 38, sharply censures this place (note 1: the same error p. 126, l. 5-6): al-Battānī says that all the stars reported by Ptolemy in the Almagest (note 2: the Arabic names are written in this book's system, not Schjellerup's) are 1022, not counting adh-dhu’ābah, the Lock, al-fard, the Solitary, and al-mirzam. Al-fard is the bright star on the neck of the Hydra (ash-shujā‘), so called as isolated (munfarid) to the south; the Arabs call al-mirzam any star that precedes a bright one: the stars preceding Sirius and Procyon are the two mirzam of the two Shi‘rà (note 3: β Canis maioris and β Canis minoris); the star on the left shoulder of the Giant is also al-mirzam (note 4: γ Orionis). Adh-dhu’ābah is one of the three stars Ptolemy calls the Lock of hair (aḍ-ḍafīrah), omitted in his nomenclature (note 5: what al-Battānī says of adh-dhu’ābah is therefore true, not false: Ptolemy lists three stars of the Coma in his catalogue, but omits them at its end, where he counts the stars). So al-Battānī knew neither al-fard nor al-mirzam. d) p. 125, l. 10-22: the lunar stations or mansions of the Arabs: aṣ-Ṣūfī, pp. 37-38, censures al-Battānī's errors: wishing to show that he knew the mansions and stars after the method of the Arabs (madhhab al-‘arab), he gave for each of the twelve constellations the number of stars Ptolemy gives, and said of the Ram that ash-sharaṭān is in the horn and al-buṭayn in the tail (an error: al-buṭayn is three stars in a triangle); of the Bull that the Pleiades are on the back and ad-dabarān at the root of the ear (note 6: so the codex, «Pleiades in the horn and ad-dabarān on the back»; but Plato «on whose back is Achoria [ath-thurayyā], at the root of the horn [sic] Aldebaran», perhaps al-Battānī's true reading; see the catalogue, nr. 8 Tauri, Part II p. 156): an error, ad-dabarān being the bright red star of the five of the face, on the southern eye; al-haq‘ah...
