% Commentary on chapter 54 b) concluded: Porphyry's rays end: «... 9 after. Mars 16: 8 before and 8 after. Venus and Mercury 14: 7 before and as many after». The same numbers of Porphyry are found in Sahl ibn Bishr (note 1: Zahel, de interrogationibus, Venice 1519; he flourished in the first half of the third century H., ninth century; Suter p. 15-16, nr. 26; Steinschneider, ZDMG 54, 1900, p. 39-46), in ‘Alī ibn Abī 'r-Riǵāl (note 2: Albohazen Haly, de iudiciis astrorum, Basel 1571, book I, ch. 6, p. 20) and in the commentary of ‘Alī ibn Riḍwān (Haly Heben Rodan) on Tetrabiblos I, 24, who adds: «This is the opinion of all the astrologers. But Ptolemy holds otherwise, as appears from his words (note 3: Nallino does not know where these words of Ptolemy are; he finds them neither in the Tetrabiblos, apart from the place cited above, nor in the apocryphal Centiloquium or Καρπός): the whole body of the Sun before and behind is 17 degrees, the Moon 12 1/2, Saturn 10, Jupiter 12, Mars 7 1/2, Venus 8, Mercury 7». These numbers too, which ‘Alī ibn Riḍwān thinks Ptolemaic, do not agree with al-Battānī's except for Jupiter, Venus and Mercury. How such rays of light were computed is not clear; perhaps they are mere trifles of the astrologers. c) p. 131, l. 15-19 (Schiaparelli, in brackets): «the quadrature which for all the stars...»: let the planet P look at another in quadrature, 90 degrees of a great circle away; its two rays on the ecliptic are the arcs AP, PB of 90 degrees each; projecting P on the ecliptic at Q by the perpendicular arc PQ (its latitude), A and B are the poles of PQ, so AQ = QB = 90 degrees; the same holds for P' (figure). Hence «the quadrature is always the same and never differs from 90°, whether the latitude be small or great»; al-Battānī explains this further by comparing the great circle with poles A and B with the equator of the sphere, which always divides the circles through both poles into equal quadrants. Let the planet R be in sextile with another star: it casts on the ecliptic the ray RA = 60°; the perpendicular arc RS is its latitude; the projection of the sextile on the ecliptic is the arc AS, clearly less than 60°; in trine, the projection of the arc RB = 120° would be SB, always greater than 120°, since AS + SB must always be 180°. Ibn Yūnus in his introduction (Caussin p. 73) writes: «It is also false what al-Battānī says of the place where the rays of the stars fall when the stars have latitude; for by his principles a star of latitude 60 degrees has no sextile, which is intolerable (فظيع)». Indeed if the ray RA = the latitude RS = 60°, the ray touches the ecliptic only at S, the planet's place in longitude, and the projection of the rays is nil (figure); to avoid this the ray must be understood otherwise than al-Battānī does; what Ibn Yūnus (continued).
