% Commentary on chapter 44. a) pp. 104-106 (Schiaparelli): the computation of the apparent conjunction of the Sun and the Moon. In solar eclipses the relation between the apparent and the true conjunction gives two problems: from an observed apparent conjunction the true one follows at once from the parallax at the time of the true conjunction; but from the true conjunction the apparent one requires the parallax at the unknown time of the apparent one, and can only be found by approximations, which al-Battānī uses. The figure (redrawn): AB the ecliptic, L′ the true place of the Moon and S′ that of the Sun at the true conjunction, L′Z′ the vertical circle; the Moon appears at λ′, and S′σ′ is al-Battānī's first parallax [in longitude] at the time ϑ of the true conjunction; with the Moon west of the nonagesimal it falls behind the Sun; dividing S′σ′ by the relative hourly motion gives only an approximate time ε′, al-Battānī's hours of the first parallax, and the first approximation ϑ + ε′ (begun).
