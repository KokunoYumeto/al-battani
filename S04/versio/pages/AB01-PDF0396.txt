Note also that the astrologers use the radiation of the planets only, not of the fixed stars (p. 131, l. 4); so the latitudes are always much less than 60°. d) p. 131, l. 20-38: the computation of the projection of the rays. I. Al-Battānī's computation. The projection of the quadrature on the ecliptic is always 90°, as the author said before and as explained in note c; the projection of the trine = 180° − the projection of the sextile; so it suffices to know the projection of the sextile ray. Al-Battānī's formulas look extremely long and intricate at first sight; how they could be proved is not understood; developed analytically by trigonometric functions they become horrible monsters. No one will believe al-Battānī wrote them (note 1: if they came from al-Battānī, Ibn Yūnus would have blamed them in the place cited on p. 306); the hand of the most stupid interpolator is recognized again. What al-Battānī's solution was is shown by the words of l. 22-23. The problem is like the first of chapter XXVI: given a star of latitude β and a star on the ecliptic, to compute the distance of one from the other on the great circle through both (figure); now the distance (60°) is given, and the unknown x is the quantity L − L′ of chapter XXVI. With the names set there (p. 39): first side = x, second side = 2 arc (sin x/2 × cos β/R), third side = crd β; the rule of that chapter becomes crd I × crd II = square of crd 60° − square of the third side, that is (note 2: in the commentary, p. 202, cos β/2R is misprinted three times for cos β/R) crd x × crd x cos β/R = crd² 60° − crd² β, crd² x = R (crd² 60° − crd² β)/cos β, whence (α) crd x = √(R (crd² 60 − crd² β)/cos β), which Nallino thinks al-Battānī's formula. To restore this rule it suffices to omit a little of al-Battānī's text, add much less and keep the rest intact; marking the omissions by dots, Nallino has no doubt al-Battānī wrote: «in the tables of sines find the cosine of the latitude [and divide it by the radius; the quotient will now be] the whole chord of the second side, which we have mentioned only in the chapter on the diagonals of quadrilaterals and in this chapter. Keep this chord of the second side. Then find the chord of the latitude, which is twice the sine of half the latitude. ..... Subtract the square of the chord of the latitude from 3600p, that is from the square of the chord of the sextile aspect .....; divide the remainder by [the kept chord of] the second side [and take the square root of the quotient]; ..... the chord sought will result; convert it into an arc as whole chords are converted, etc.». To make sure this is al-Battānī's true rule, Nallino reduces it to the direct solution of the spherical triangle. Since (continued).
