% Commentary on chapter 50 a) continued: Ibn Rosteh gives the radius of the Earth as 3818 miles; finally, in the first half of the 12th century, the Hebrew-Spanish Abraham bar Ḥiyyā (note 1: Sphaera mundi, Basle 1546) gives the distances in radii (= 3250 miles) and in miles, and adds: «This calculation follows the opinion of al-Battānī, one of the sages who studied this subject in the time of the king al-Ma'mūn (sic!); he reduced the distances that the sages before him had measured; the ancients added much to these quantities». The distances in Earth radii after the three authors, in a table: al-Farghānī, Ibn Rosteh, Bar Ḥiyyā: least distance of the Moon 33 ½ 1/20, ....., 33 ½; greatest of the Moon and least of Mercury 64 ⅙, 64 ⅙, 64; greatest of Mercury and least of Venus 167, 166, 166; greatest of Venus and least of the Sun 1120, 1079, 1080; greatest of the Sun and least of Mars 1220, 1260, 1146; greatest of Mars and least of Jupiter 8876, 8820, 8000; greatest of Jupiter and least of Saturn 14405, 14187, 12400; greatest of Saturn 20110, 19880, 18000; fixed stars 20110, 20000, 18000. Some later Arabs ascribe the principle to Ptolemy himself: al-Qazwīnī, Cosmographia, says Ptolemy gave the thickness (spissitudo, note 10) of the spheres and the size of each planet, easy for one who knows Euclid's second book, and gives the miles of each sphere «after Ptolemy». Al-Qazwīnī's authority is small; but al-Bīrūnī, India, chapter LV, reports the doctrine collected in A.H. 161 (began 8 Oct. 777) by Ya‘qūb ibn Ṭāriq from Indian books: between the greatest distance of a lower planet and the least of the next there is a space equal to the diameter of the lower planet (note 11: the Sūrya-Siddhānta rests on another principle); Ya‘qūb's numbers in Earth radii (begun). Notes: (2) al-Farghānī's 33 ½ 1/20 is right in the Latin of Nuremberg 1537, the miles being 109037 (Golius' Arabic 109026, Golius' edition and version 33 ½ ⅕); (3) Ibn Rosteh's text has 64 ½ for 64 ⅙, the miles being 244988 ⅓; (4) al-Battānī, p. 121, has 1070 for the expected 1079; (5) the text has 33682760 miles for 33674760; (6), (8), (9) al-Battānī 8022, 12924, 18094; (7) the text has 4187.
