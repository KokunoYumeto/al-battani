In l. 21 and 26-27, instead of «and subtract it from the difference of the residues; what remains .....» there is to be restored: «and take that name from the difference of the residues; what shall be .....». The words «name» (sammi) and «name» (ism) have here a technical sense which never occurs either in the lexicons or in the other parts of this book, but is found among the Arab mathematicians of western Africa and Spain (note 1: I cannot affirm, however, that others do not use it, since so few Arabic writings on arithmetic have been printed. The word tasmiyah occurs also in the book al-ma'ūnah, which the Egyptian Aḥmad Ibn al-Hā'im finished in the month of sha'bān 791 (August 1389), as appears from the index of the book in Ahlwardt, vol. V, no. 5984, p. 339b), as al-Ḥaṣṣār (note 2: about the twelfth century A.D.; his book was summarized in German by H. Suter, Das Rechenbuch des Abû Zakarijâ el-Ḥaṣṣâr (Bibliotheca Mathematica, ed. G. Eneström, ser. III, vol. II, 1901, pp. 12-40); on tasmiyah pp. 17-20 and 35), Ibn al-Bannā' (note 3: died about 740 of the hijrah (beginning 8 July 1339); cf. Suter, Die Mathematiker, pp. 162-64, no. 399. His compendium (talkhīṣ) was translated into French by A. Marre, Le Talkhys d'Ibn Albannâ publié [sic!] et traduit (Atti dell'Accademia Pontificia de' Nuovi Lincei, vol. XVII, 1863-64, pp. 289-319); on tasmiyah («dénomination») pp. 302, 306, 310) and al-Qalaṣādī (note 4: died in the month of dhū 'l-ḥiǵǵah 891, that is at the end of the year 1486 A.D.; Suter, Die Mathematiker, pp. 180-82, no. 444. His kashf al-asrār was translated into French by F. Woepcke, Traduction du traité d'arithmétique d'Aboûl Haçan Ali ben Mohammed Alkalçâdi (Atti dell'Accademia Pontificia de' N. L., vol. XII, 1858-59, pp. 230-275, 399-438); he treats of tasmiyah in part I, ch. 6 and 8, part II, ch. 5 (in the text lithographed at Fez in 1315 H., pp. 17-18, 20-21, 26)). For these call appellation or denomination (tasmiyah) the division of a smaller number by another, larger one, in such a way that it is expressed by a sum of fractions whose denominators are the factors (prime or not) of the larger number (note 5: on this operation H. Hankel, Storia delle matematiche presso gli Arabi, Italian translation by F. Keller (in Bullettino di bibliografia e di storia delle scienze matematiche, vol. V, 1872, pp. 370-71); Cantor, Vorlesungen über Geschichte der Mathematik, vol. I, 2nd ed. (Leipzig 1894), pp. 764-65). Examples: 7 : 15 = 2/5 + 1/3 · 1/5, which is written (2 1 over 5 3); also 196 : 385 = 5/11 + 4/11 · 1/7 + 1/11 · 1/7 · 1/5, which is written (5 4 1 over 11 7 5), and is called «to denominate 196 from 385». Then the numerator is called «the denominated» (al-musammà), the denominator «that from which it is denominated» (al-musammà minhu). The same verb is sometimes used in a more general sense to indicate a fraction whose denominator is greater than the numerator; so al-Qalaṣādī, part I, ch. IV (ed. Fez 1315, p. 13 in the margin): «if [at the end of the division] a remainder smaller than the divisor is left to you, name it from it [the divisor]», which Woepcke rightly rendered «faites en une fraction ayant pour dénominateur le diviseur». The place in l. 21 therefore means: «divide the distance (in degrees of the equator) by the semidiurnal arc, and in this ratio take the proportional part of the difference of the residues», that is: difference of the residues × distance / semidiurnal arc, where clearly the factor distance / semidiurnal arc = temporal hours of the distance / 6. So the rule agrees with the precepts set out on p. 316. Instead of «name» and «name», other technical words are used in l. 31 and 36, «compare» (insib) and «ratio» (nisbah), less perhaps departing from the usage of the mathematicians of Mesopotamia (continued).
