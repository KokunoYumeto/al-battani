% Commentary on chapter 50 b) concluded (Schiaparelli): with δ the apparent angular diameter of a planet and r its mean distance, its linear diameter is rδ, so that diameter of the planet : diameter of the Sun = rδ : RΔ = r/R × δ/Δ; the diameter of the Sun being 5 1/2 Earth diameters, diameter of the planet : diameter of the Earth = 5 1/2 × r/R × δ/Δ = r × δ/Δ × 1/(R/5 1/2); with R = 1108, R/5 1/2 = 201 5/11, the rule of al-Battānī, or rather of those before him, who means the ratio of the apparent diameters, not of the true. II. The arcs which the apparent diameters of the epicycles subtend at the eye: the angle AXB of the two tangents from the eye X (figure). For Mercury and Venus it is the sum of the greatest and least elongations, as al-Battānī has. For Mars, Ptolemy X, 8 (Halma II, 236): the radius of the eccentric to that of the epicycle as 60p to 39p 30', 1 to 0,65833..., the sine of OXB, which subtends the radius OB; OXB = 41° 10' 1/2, AXB = 82° 21'. Jupiter (XI, 2, Halma II, 262): 60p to 11p 30', 1 to 0,19666 (printed); OXB = 11° 3', AXB = 22° 6'. Saturn (XI, 6, Halma II, 287): 60p to 6p 30', 1 to 0.10833; OXB = 6° 13', AXB = 12° 26'. Tycho Brahe, Progymnasmata I, 467-71, on the distances and sizes of al-Farghānī and al-Battānī and on al-Battānī's method of finding the sizes of the stars from their distance and visible diameter. c) p. 122, l. 33: «the diameter of the Sun subtends an arc of 39° 48'»: if the epicycle of the Sun is meant, its diameter is twice the greatest equation of the centre, 3° 58', which does not agree with the 39° 48' of the codex (here and p. 123, l. 21) or the 49° 48' or 9° 48' of Plato; if the apparent diameter of the Sun, al-Battānī (p. 58) puts its mean value at 32' 30": a foolish interpolation, like the one of the next note. d) p. 123, l. 18-20 and 20-30: the whole place, where nearly all the numbers of the codex differ from Plato's, cannot be healed; not al-Battānī but a most inept interpolator set out to add the greatest, mean and least apparent diameters of all the planets (begun).
