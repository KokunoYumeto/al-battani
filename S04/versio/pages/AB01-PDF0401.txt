% Commentary on chapter 54 d) II concluded: (the degree culminating with the star, when) right ascensions are computed, or the degree rising with the star when oblique ascensions are to be used. But the astrologers seem mostly to have neglected this in the radiations; the resulting error is often very small, since only the planets, not the fixed stars, are considered in this problem. Many Arab astronomers teach this «method of Ptolemy» together with others: Abū Naṣr al-Qummī (note 1: as appears from the list of chapters in Ahlwardt, Verzeichniss der arabischen Handschriften der k. Bibliothek zu Berlin, 1887-99, vol. V, p. 148, nr. 5661; al-Qummī lived in the fourth century H., tenth century; Suter pp. 74-75, nr. 174), al-Bīrūnī in the Canon Masudicus (note 2: Ahlwardt V, p. 154, nr. 5667), and Ulugh Beg, pp. 157-58 of the text, 209-210 of the version, who carries out the computation directly by circles of position and does not neglect the latitude of the star. The Arabs rightly call this computation of radiations «the method of Ptolemy»; for in the Tetrabiblos III, 10 (III, 15 of the Frankfurt version) Nallino finds this on the directions: «the significator being found, two ways are to be taken: one in the order of the signs, which is called the projection of rays (ἀκτινοβολία), when the significator is in the eastern places, from the midheaven to the ascendant; the other both in and against the order of the signs, by the horary method, when the significator is in the places declining from the midheaven». So Bouché-Leclercq, p. 248, note 1, was wrong to write «the theory of the ἀκτινοβολία is probably later than Ptolemy». Az-Zarqālī, in the place cited on p. 308, says: «the [old] Persians are said to make the projections of rays only in the right sphere, which serves only those who live under the equator; so we make no mention of it». These words probably mean that the old Persians added 60°, 90°, 120° to the right ascension of the star or subtracted them, and took the arc of the ecliptic corresponding to the right ascension so increased or decreased as the degree of longitude on which the left or right radiation falls. This method (note 3: John of Saxony, in his commentary on Alcabitius, says of it: «and in this way Ptolemy orders the aspects [i.e. the radiations] to be taken in the 60th proposition of the Centiloquium, where he speaks of the causes of the critical days»; the proposition of the pseudo-Ptolemaic Centiloquium is: «In the sick look at the critical days and the course (ἐποχήν) of the Moon in the angles of the figure of sixteen sides; for where you find those angles well disposed, it will be well with the sick; badly, if you find them afflicted»; Nallino does not find it easy to deduce a theory of radiations from this) is also mentioned, among others, by Regiomontanus in the 31st problem of the Tabulae directionum profectionumque (1475); al-Khuwārizmī (note 4: he died after 232 H. (beginning 27 August 846), as Nallino showed in his Al-Ḫuwârizmî e il suo rifacimento della Geografia di Tolomeo, Memorie della R. Accademia dei Lincei, 1894-95, p. 9; Nöldeke, ZDMG 55, 1901, p. 683, note 1, wrongly confuses him with Muḥammad ibn Mūsà ibn Shākir, and the same confusion is in the indices of the Annals of aṭ-Ṭabarī: III, 551, 937, 1085 concern al-Khuwārizmī, the others and the epithet al-munaǵǵim Muḥammad ibn Mūsà ibn Shākir) relied on it, if Nallino understands Ibn Hibintà f. 34 r.-v. rightly, in building the tables of radiations that Ibn Hibintà gives, f. 35 r.-37 v.; al-Khuwārizmī is known to have followed mainly Indian methods in his astronomical work. Clearly all these computations of the projection, which seem to come from the Greeks, the Indians and the Persians, rest on much less rational principles than al-Battānī's and are much less elegant (note 5: the European mathematical astrologers of the sixteenth century understood the projections in the same way as al-Battānī and the computation directly by the formula cos x = cos 60°/cos β, continued on p. 313).
