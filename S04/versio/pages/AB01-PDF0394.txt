% Commentary on chapter 53 a) concluded: the Arabs knew a book of the philosopher Hermes on the revolutions of nativities, translated from Greek, of which a Latin version from the Arabic was often printed; the Greek texts are unknown today, as Bouché-Leclercq says (L'astrologie grecque, 1899, p. 506, note 2): «I suppose these are the renewals and checks of the nativity that the annotator of Paul of Alexandria calls ἀντιγενέσεις. Apart from this problematic and late mention (note 1: the annotator Schaton belongs to the Middle Ages), I find no trace of the system in the Greek texts published so far». Chapter 54 a) p. 130, l. 5: «let it recede by half the space of both bodies»: somewhat differently in the Greek Tetrabiblos I, 24 (note 2: I, 21 in the Latin version from the Greek, Frankfurt 1622, by an unknown, most elegant translator): καὶ καθόλου δὲ συνάπτειν μὲν λέγονται τοῖς ἑπομένοις οἱ προηγούμενοι, ἀπεῤῥυηκέναι δὲ οἱ ἑπόμενοι τῶν προηγουμένων, ἔφ’ ὅσων ἂν μὴ μακρὸν ᾖ τὸ μεταξὺ αὐτῶν διάστημα, «in general the preceding are said to apply to the following, and the following to flow away from the preceding, provided the interval between them be not long»; Pseudo-Proclus and Porphyry also do not fix the size of the interval (note 3: their commentary and introduction, Basel 1559). But in the two Latin versions of the Tetrabiblos made from the Arabic and printed with the version of ‘Alī ibn Riḍwān's commentary (note 4: Venice 1519) the matter is as in al-Battānī: one has «when the planets go to approach others it is called their application, unless they are far from them beyond half the bodies of the two planets, whether the application be bodily or by one of the said figures»; the other (note 5: printed also at the end of Firmicus Maternus, Basel 1533 or 1551): «..... that the planets which precede and seek the conjunction of others are in their alictisal (note 6: the Arabic al-ittiṣāl, the applicatio of the Latin astrologers), unless the distance between them be great, greater than the two halves of the bodies of the two planets, whether the alictisal be by conjunction or by one of the said figures». b) p. 130, l. 11-13: the force of the light in applications: in Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos III, 10 (III, 14 of the Frankfurt version) Jupiter sends its light by a ray of 12° in applications, Venus by one of 8°, agreeing with al-Battānī; the rays of the other planets are not given. Porphyry, l. c., p. 204, at the end of his work: the rays of the Sun spread 30 parts, 15 before and 15 after; the Moon 24, 12 and 12; Saturn and Jupiter 18, 9 (continued).
