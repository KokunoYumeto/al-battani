% Commentary on chapter 49, a) concluded: Ptolemy's configurations of the fixed stars with the Sun, 6-9: 6. southern west wind (setting at noon); 7. evening east wind, evening rising, in three kinds (subsequent and visible, true simultaneous, prior and invisible); 8. evening culmination (subsequent and visible, true simultaneous, prior and invisible); 9. evening west wind, evening setting (subsequent and visible, true simultaneous, prior and invisible). b) p. 120, l. 1-7: the «aequatorial» arcs of visibility of the fixed stars, after the common tradition («dicunt»): counted on the equator, as shown on p. 283. Ptolemy, Almagest VIII, 6, treats the appearances and disappearances of the fixed stars and shows the problem very hard, but gives no arcs; Ideler (Handbuch der mathematischen und technischen Chronologie, I, 55) rightly says the arcs ascribed to Ptolemy in many books (12°, 13°, 14°, 15°, 16°, 18° for the six magnitudes) have no ground; these arcs are moreover the depression of the Sun, counted on the vertical circle. Ptolemy's lost book on the arcs of visibility in the vertical circle and the ecliptic; Ideler deduced from the Phases (φάσεις ἀπλανῶν ἀστέρων) depressions of 11° and 7° for stars of the first magnitude and 14° and 8½° for the second, the larger when star and Sun rise or set on the same side of the horizon. The 15° for the first magnitude perhaps come from Autolycus' On risings and settings, well known to the Arabs, translated by Qusṭā ibn Lūqā and corrected by Thābit ibn Qurrah; Autolycus counts the arc on the ecliptic, 15° for all fixed stars. Why the aequatorial arcs grow by 3° a magnitude to about 30° for the sixth is not clear (begun: Schiaparelli). Notes: (1) Ideler's paper, Berlin Academy 1816 and 1817, in French at the end of Halma's Table chronologique des règnes (Paris 1819); (2) Tannery, Autolycos de Pitane (Bordeaux 1885); Eudoxus' parapegma gives arcs from 20¼° or 21° (Pleiades, Hyades, Orion, the Dog) to 8¾° (Dolphin).
