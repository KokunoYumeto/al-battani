The aphetae are five: the Sun, the Moon, the ascendant, the planet with the greatest power (οἰκοδεσποτεία) over the degree of the Part of Fortune (note 1: ὁ κλῆρος τῆς τύχης, in Arabic sahm as-sa‘ādah, is the place of the ecliptic as far from the ascendant, in the order of the signs, as the Moon is from the Sun along the zodiac), and the planet with the greatest power over the degree of the last conjunction or opposition of the Sun and the Moon. An apheta by itself has no force unless it is found in an aphetic place. Which apheta is to be taken in the various cases, after Ptolemy's doctrine, may be read in V. Nabod, Enarratio elementorum astrologiae, Cologne 1560, pp. 383-84, 388-90, and Bouché-Leclercq, pp. 415-19. Finally the star whose body or hostile rays, meeting the significator in the profections, cut off life or at least foretell great dangers to it, was called by the Greeks ἀναιρέτης «slayer» or μοῖρα ἀναιρετική «destroying degree», by the Latins of the fifteenth to seventeenth centuries promissor. Direction or prorogation, in Greek ἄφεσις, in Arabic tasyīr (whence the medieval European alazir, alaçir, alhacir), is the arc of the equator that rises or sets together with the arc of the ecliptic between the significator and the promissor, with respect to the circle of position in which one of them lies. The astrologers distinguish two directions: the direct one, in the order of the signs (ἄφεσις εἰς τὰ ἑπόμενα τῶν ζωδίων, from west to east), in which the promissor (ἀναιρέτης) or «following place» (ἑπόμενος τόπος) is understood to be carried to the «preceding place» (προηγούμενος τόπος), i.e. to the circle of position of the significator or apheta; the converse one, against the order of the signs (ἄφεσις εἰς τὰ προηγούμενα τῶν ζωδίων), in which the significator is carried to the circle of the promissor. In both, for the nativities of men each degree of the equator means a year; for the revolutions of the years of the world and of nativities a degree means a day, etc. Profection is the uniform motion of the significator in the order of the signs. In the annual profection of a nativity each solar year receives one sign of the zodiac: if the nativity has Aries in the ascendant, the second year has Taurus, the third Gemini, etc. In the monthly profection the solar year is divided into 13 equal parts or profectional months, each with one sign, so the sign of the annual profection is that of the first month of the same year; in the daily profection the profectional month is subdivided into 13 equal parts, each with one sign. The doctrine of direction, as to its astrological use, is very obscure and intricate; besides, the astrologers rest on different principles in drawing their predictions, whence the greatest confusion arises, which not even Salmasius (De annis climactericis et antiqua astrologia diatribae, Leiden 1648, pp. 264-398, 407-439) could avoid. See Bouché-Leclercq, pp. 411-419 (note 2: p. 411, note 1: «it is not without hesitation that I venture into the darkness of the aphetic theory, thickened by the logomachy and contradictions of the old astrologers (above all Vettius Valens and Paul of Alexandria), of the Arabs and of the scholars of the Renaissance, who persist in mixing, combining and exchanging the two main systems, borrowing the length of life from one and the division of time from the other»), who however, as always, says nothing or little of value on the mathematical computations. b) p. 132, l. 10 - p. 134, l. 46: the computation of the ascensions of the signs outside the four cardines is the computation of the direction of the significator to the promissor and of the promissor to the significator.
