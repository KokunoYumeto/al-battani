% Commentary on chapter 40, a) on p. 84, l. 38-40: the part to be added to the parallaxes taken from Theon's tables, 1/18 in Plato, 1/38 in the codex; Theon's Handy Tables add 1/20 for the solar parallax; the solar parallax of 3′ is the eighteenth part of 54′, the lunar parallax at the first limit (53′ 34″), so Plato's 1/18 is good; the obliquity makes no sensible difference. b) on p. 85, l. 1-27: I. the parallax in altitude observed with the Moon at the nonagesimal, where the parallax in longitude vanishes; Tycho Brahe did the same; Ptolemy (Almagest V, 13) used the meridian instead, observing the Moon near the beginning of Capricorn on Athyr 13 of the year 20 of Hadrian. II. the distance of the Moon from the parallax and the zenith distance: two solutions of al-Battānī run together in the codex and in Plato (begun). Note: Tycho, Progymnasmata; Riccioli; Hipparchus at the nonagesimal (Almagest V, 3 and V, 5; Pappus).
