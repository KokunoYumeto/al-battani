% Commentary on chapter 52 a) II concluded: Thābit ibn Qurrah (Latin version, continued): Ptolemy found the fixed stars moving 1° in 100 years, since he counted their longitudes from the equator, and judged that they must not be fixed; he thought they move with a continuous motion in the order of the signs..... The observers found the motion of the fixed stars 1° in 66 years, and after Ptolemy and Hipparchus [the head of Aries] was in the southern part near its end, so that the motion was slow (note 1: a very obscure passage; the version of codex 4083: others after Ptolemy who considered this motion found it to be one degree in 66 years; Ptolemy and Hipparchus corrected...; they found that the motion slows). After Ptolemy the head of Aries came near the equator, cut it, and passed into the northern part, and the motion was fast. Yet the exception after Ptolemy did not happen as it should; therefore Albategnius doubted, and said: I see this exception not in the proportions of speed and slowness; so there was in the sphere a motion we do not know and do not comprehend; let him who comes after us consider and verify it, as we did. Then he returned to what was more worthy and fitting, along the path of estimation, so that his work might be good and he might achieve something [?]. We shall mark this motion and its different situations, with God's blessings. Finally the words of the Hispano-Jewish Abraham bar Ḥiyyā (note 2: chapter X, pp. 196-200 of the Hebrew text, the Latin version of this chapter lacking; on the book see p. 287) concerning this chapter of al-Battānī: the wise men of India, all the inhabitants of the land of the Romans, and the ancients among the wise men of the Chaldaeans held that the fixed stars do not traverse the whole sky but move only eight degrees of the zodiac forward and back, east and west; the cause is the pole of the ecliptic, which turns east and west on a small circle [of diameter] 8° of the zodiac, completing the circle in 1600 years; because of this revolution of the pole some learned men imagined the fixed stars to go round the whole sky, not knowing the revolution of the pole of the ecliptic, by which the fixed stars move from west to east for 800 years and are restored westward to their former place after 1600 years..... Ptolemy mentioned this motion [of trepidation] but would neither reject nor approve it. The wise men of Ismael [the Arabs] of the time of al-Ma’mūn gathered their strength to overturn the Indian arguments; among them al-Battānī, to refute the Indian opinion, said that the ancients before Ptolemy observed the places of these stars; 420 years later Ptolemy found a difference of 4° 1/5, whence 1° in 100 years; about 720 years after Ptolemy al-Battānī found a difference of about 11°, whence 1° 1/2 in 100 years; so in 1140 years the fixed stars advanced 15° 1/5, whereas by the Indian sayings they should have advanced only 11° 36′ from the beginning of the advance, or 3° 36′ from the regression or the middle of the direct motion; likewise between Ptolemy and al-Battānī only 7° 1/5 eastward should have been added to Ptolemy's places, beginning from the regression; and one may begin indifferently from the middle of the advance or of the regression. A great argument against them... (continued).
