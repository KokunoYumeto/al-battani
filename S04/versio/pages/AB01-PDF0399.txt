% Commentary on chapter 54 d) II continued: Valens (Libro de las armellas, book II, ch. XLIV) followed the method which the Arabs mostly ascribe to Ptolemy, to be discussed now. Ibn Abī Uṣaybi‘ah (note 1: ‘Uyūn al-anbā’ fī ṭabaqāt al-aṭibbā’, ed. A. Müller, 1884, vol. I, pp. 308-309; known before from manuscripts to Wüstenfeld, Geschichte der Arabischen Aerzte und Naturforscher, Göttingen 1840, pp. 5 and 20, from whom Steinschneider, ZDMG 24, 1870, p. 338, took it; but they wrongly understood «refraction» (Brechung der Lichtstrahlen); the same words, as far as «are found translated», are in Ibn al-Qifṭī, in Casiri, Bibliotheca Arabico-Hispana Escurialensis, Madrid 1760-70, vol. I, p. 436) says in the life of the physician and astrologer Rabban aṭ-Ṭabarī: «Abū Ma‘shar (note 2: died March 886; Suter pp. 28-30, nr. 53), asked about the projection of the rays, said much, and finally: in the copies of the Almagest (note 3: certainly an error for the Quadripartitum, the only one of these books that Ḥunayn translated; nor is it likely that the astrologer and physician Rabban issued a version of the Almagest, where a chapter on radiations is not at all to be expected, while it would have had a most fitting place in the Tetrabiblos; what is said below of the chapter added to the Latin version of the Tetrabiblos from the Arabic confirms the conjecture; so Suter's correction, p. 14, Isḥāq ibn Ḥunayn for Ḥunayn, becomes needless) derived from the Greek, the translators mention neither the rays nor their projection, which are found only in the copy translated by the physician Rabban aṭ-Ṭabarī (note 4: early ninth century; Suter pp. 14-15, nr. 25; Steinschneider thinks him the same man who is otherwise called Sahl ibn Bishr). The projection of rays of Ptolemy is not found in the old copies; neither Thābit nor Ḥunayn al-Qallūsī nor al-Kindī (note 5: all of the ninth century; Suter pp. 34-38, nr. 66; 21-23, nr. 44; 23-26, nr. 45) nor any of those great translators, nor any of the family of Nawbakht (note 6: eighth and early ninth century; Suter p. 3, nr. 2; 5, nr. 7; 16, nrs. 27 and 28) know it». The Libros del saber, vol. II, p. 69 (Libro de las armellas, book II, ch. LII), say, in Nallino's translation from the Spanish: «All that I have said here was the opinion of Ptolemy on the projection of the rays and on the [astrological] direction, know that I found it in none of Ptolemy's books, but that Abū Ma‘shar and Abū Isḥāq az-Zarqālī so reported it. It is not impossible that it was not Ptolemy's opinion but [falsely] ascribed to him; as errors often creep into versions and names are changed from one to another, especially over so long a time, more than a thousand years, as has passed since Ptolemy (note 7: the Spanish text). If you understand Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos rightly, you will find that his opinion inclines to the view of Ibn Mu‘ādh rather than to that of others» (note 8: the Spanish text; Muḥammad ibn Mu‘ādh al-Ǵayyānī lived in the tenth or eleventh century, not to be confused with the man named by Suter, nr. 213, as Suter himself recognized in his Nachträge und Berichtigungen, Abhandlungen zur Geschichte der mathematischen Wissenschaften 14). Finally, in the Liber tabularum Iahen cum regulis suis, made in Arabic at the end of the tenth or in the eleventh century and translated into Latin by Gerard of Cremona in the twelfth, many errors are said to be made in some methods of projecting the rays: «the first of them [the errors] is the work ascribed to Ptolemy. And Albumasar took it over from him, although Abunasar is not truthful in this science, that is, in the science of the form of the sphere» (note 9: in Steinschneider, Lettere a D. B. Boncompagni: II.
