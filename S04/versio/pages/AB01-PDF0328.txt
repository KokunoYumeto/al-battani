% Commentary on chapter 31, d) on p. 66, l. 1-8, 12-15. I. The mean motions in longitude of Saturn, Jupiter and Mars in 20 Julian years (20 Egyptian years and 5 days) after al-Battānī's tables (t. II, p. 103) and Ptolemy, their differences and those of the 740 years between the epochs (137 and 880); the Tabula Probata and Ḥabash, and the moderns. II. The apogees of the planets: al-Battānī's for 1191 Dhū 'l-qarnayn (p. 114) and Ptolemy's for 137; al-Bīrūnī's apogees for 420 of the Hijra after al-Battānī, against al-Battānī's with the precession of 149 years, 2° 15'; al-Bīrūnī's minutes of Saturn and Venus corrected in their Arabic letters; Mars excepted. Notes: the interval of 20 years and the thirds of al-Battānī's tables; the Tabula Probata in Ibn Yūnus.
