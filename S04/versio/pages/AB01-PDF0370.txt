% Commentary on chapter 44, Nallino quoting al-Khāzinī (concluded): the third method, after al-Battānī: the first parallax divided by the true praecessio gives the hours of the first parallax; with them and the first parallax taken from or added to the hours and the degree of the true conjunction, the hours of the second conjunction and the apparent place; the second parallax; if equal to the first, the hours of mid-eclipse; else their difference divided by the apparent praecessio of that hour, not the true one (al-Battānī's error, note 1), gives a fraction of an hour, added to or taken from the hours of the second parallax: the equated hours, taken from or added to the hours of the true conjunction: the hours of mid-eclipse; the equated hours times the motions of the Sun, the Moon, the node and the lunar anomaly give the exact places (al-murattabah). Ḥabash, fol. 133 r.–134 v., follows Ptolemy's method. b) p. 106, l. 22-26: the apparent latitudes within which the Moon must be for a solar eclipse: the limits of the codex wholly false, much better in Plato; the greatest = greatest semidiameter of the Sun + greatest of the Moon = ½ (33′ 40″ + 35′ 20″) = 34′ 30″, the least = ½ (31′ 20″ + 29′ 30″) = 30′ 25″; al-Battānī's diameters on p. 58 (Schiaparelli). The Alfonsine Tables (Venice 1518, fol. 19 r., prop. XXXII) give al-Battānī's rule more fully. c) p. 106, l. 31-33: the passage in italics, put by an unskilled man in place of a lost one (begun). Notes: (1) al-Battānī's error (p. 278); al-Khāzinī takes the simpler case in which the first approximation ϑ + ε′ suffices; (2) al-Battānī p. 105, l. 23 and 33; (3) al-Battānī does not state this rule for the node (p. 106, l. 7-11), but it is understood, the motion of the node being retrograde.
