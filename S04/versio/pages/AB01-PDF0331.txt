@obs notes_left L4: the addenda (Part I, p. LXXIV) on note 2: Nallino had explained the name after several scholars, above all because Clement of Alexandria complains that Alexander had himself portrayed in the image of a god, with horns. Its true origin is shown by Fr. Kampers, Alexander der Grosse und die Idee des Weltimperiums in Prophetie und Sage, Freiburg i. Br. 1901, p. 71–85 (Studien und Darstellungen aus dem Gebiete der Geschichte, ed. H. Grauert, fasc. II–III). In the legends of Alexander current in the East, especially among the Syrians, in the first centuries of the Christian era, Alexander appears as the restorer of a new age and saviour of the world, like the Messiah of the Hebrews; he is therefore given the two horns that Jewish writings call the sign of the Messiah. All this comes from very ancient Babylonian beliefs, set out briefly in E. Schrader, Die Keilinschriften und das Alte Testament, 3rd ed. by H. Zimmern and H. Winckler, Berlin 1902, p. 380–82; for the same reason Naram-Sin, king of Babylonia, was represented with two horns 25 centuries before Alexander.
