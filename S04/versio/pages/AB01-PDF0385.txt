% Commentary on chapter 51 d) continued (aṣ-Ṣūfī, in French): al-Battānī puts al-han‘ah and muqaddam adh-dhirā‘ayn in the Twins, an error, for al-haq‘ah belongs to the Giant, on the head between the shoulders, and he does not say where in the Twins al-han‘ah (two stars in their feet) and adh-dhirā‘ (two bright stars in their heads) are; he wrongly puts al-ghafr in the Scales: it is three stars, two on the hem (dhayl) of the robe of the Virgin and one on her left foot; he puts az-zubānayāni, the two zubānà, and al-iklīl, the Crown, in the Scorpion, a double error: az-zubānayāni belong to the Scales, on the two pans, though he took them for the claws of the Scorpion, its horns after the Arabs; al-iklīl is not three stars on the forehead of the Scorpion within the figure, but three stars in a transverse line above its forehead: the northernmost in the northern zubānà of the Scales, the eighth of Ptolemy's table, of the fourth magnitude; the second, in the middle, one of the stars about the Scales outside the figure, the sixth of these; the third, the southernmost, also one of the external stars of the Scales, the eighth of them; all three of the fourth magnitude, in a curved line like the three stars of the forehead. He puts an-na‘ā’im, the Ostriches, and al-baldah in the Archer, an error: al-baldah is a place in the sky behind al-qilādah (note 1: ξ, ο, π, d, ρ, υ Sagittarii), without stars, hence called al-baldah, the City; on several globes an-na‘ām al-wārid (note 2: σ, φ, τ, ζ Sagittarii) is marked an-na‘ā’im and an-na‘ām aṣ-ṣādir (note 3: γ, δ, ε, η Sagittarii) al-baldah; the Arabs made a mansion only in the middle between the two ostriches. Under the Goat he mentions sa‘d adh-dhābiḥ and sa‘d bula‘, an error, for sa‘d bula‘ is in the left hand of the Water-bearer, above the back of the Goat. He puts al-fargh al-awwal [= al-fargh al-muqaddam] and al-fargh ath-thānī [= al-fargh al-mu’akhkhar] in the Fishes, an error, for al-farghāni, the two fargh, belong to the Horse, toward the north: of the first fargh, the northern star is on the right shoulder where the leg (qāmah) begins, the southern on the back where the neck begins; of the second fargh, the northern is on the navel of the Horse and the head of the Chained Woman [= Andromeda], common to both, the southern on the loins (matn) of the Horse. None of these stars is in the zodiacal figures; so he knew neither the Fishes nor the Horse. After what was reported in note c, aṣ-Ṣūfī concludes: had al-Battānī kept to his own doctrine (madhhabuhu) and been content with what he expounded in his book on the celestial spheres, the seven stars [the planets] and their motion, the eclipses of the Sun and the Moon and other matters concerning the stars, he would not have fallen into such errors; he would be credited, for his fame and superiority in the art after the method of the astronomers, with knowledge after the method of the Arabs too.
