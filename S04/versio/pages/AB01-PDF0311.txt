@obs body L30-L37: Nallino's addenda et emendanda in Part II (pp. XXIV-XXV; AB01-PDF0441-body-L022 to AB01-PDF0442-body-L008) explain the fifth method: it is that of the astronomers of the caliph al-Ma’mūn in the Tabula Probata, as a passage of chapter V of the Ma‘āriǵ al-fikr al-wahīǵ of Muḥammad ibn Abī Bakr al-Fārisī (second half of the 7th century H., 13th century A.D.), quoted by Steinschneider (ZDMG XLVII, 1893, pp. 355-356), shows. Of the five methods of making tables of the equation of time the first is Ptolemy's and the fifth was found by Yaḥyà ibn Abī Manṣūr, who began from the beginning of Aries: having observed the apogee of the Sun in 82° 39′ in the year 199 of Yazdegerd (begun 28 April 830), he reduced it to the first day of the era of Yazdegerd, about 79° 38′; with this apogee and the greatest equation of the Sun 1° 59′ he found for the Sun's entry into Aries the mean longitude 358° 4′, the true 0°, its right ascension 360°; the difference of 1° 56′ he took from the right ascensions of the true Sun, and the difference between the diminished ascensions and the mean longitude of the Sun, divided by 15, was the equation of time. The example is for the year 634 of Yazdegerd (begun 9 January 1265), with the apogee 89° 13′ and the greatest equation 1° 59′: the mean longitude 59° 2′, the true at the beginning of Gemini.
