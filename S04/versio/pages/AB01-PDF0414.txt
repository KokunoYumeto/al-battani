% On appendix F. a) p. 149: the appendix seems spurious; it sets out more briefly what the author had already taught in chapter LV, leaving out the considerations about planets with latitude (pp. 133 and 317). I explained everything sufficiently in the notes on that chapter, pp. 313-17. In the title of the appendix it would have been better to translate: «and know that this direction is what Ptolemy explains in the direction of the hīlāj». In the text, vol. III, p. 224, l. 11, I accepted tasayyur (that is, walking, going about), which I translated by the word «profection»; but not rightly. For never, as far as I know, did the Arabs call profection so; when they distinguish it from direction (tasyīr), they designate it by the word intihā'. I was misled by the faulty spelling of the codex at that place, tasyr for tasyīr (that is, direction), and by the words of the book Mafātīḥ al-ʿulūm p. 230 (s. v. hīlāj): «that is, it tasayyur to the fortunes and misfortunes», in which tasayyur is without doubt an error of the editor for tusayyar, «are directed». In what follows, l. 14 of the text (twice), instead of tasīr it would have been better to read tusayyir; so in the translation (l. 15 and 16), for «to begin the direction» and «the direction to reach», I think only «to direct» is to be restored. The sense, however, does not change. The word ǵuwīrāst, which Ptolemy is now said to use in the Tetrabiblos when he speaks of the profection of the significator, certainly shows a Persian origin (note 1: rāst = straight, direct, directly; but the first part of the word clearly comes from the verb ǵustan (present stem ǵūy) «to seek, to inquire». The corruption of the codex, al-ḥwārāsb, arose because the archetype had al-ǵuwī rāst; the copyist, not knowing the word, thought the first part of the noun an Arabic word ending in alif maqṣūrah, and so, in the Maghrebi manner of writing, put alif for yā'); but I find it neither in the Greek text nor in the two Latin versions of the Arabic redaction of the Tetrabiblos (note 2: both are printed with the commentary of ʿAlī ibn Riḍwān (Haly Heben Rodan), Venice 1493 and 1519. The first, printed many times, was made from the Arabic by Plato of Tivoli and added to the other by the Venetian editors; the second, like ʿAlī's commentary, was made from the Spanish by Aegidius de Tebaldis by order of Alfonso X). In only one of the Arabic books printed so far have I met the word, namely in the Mafātīḥ al-ʿulūm, a small lexicon explaining many technical words of all the sciences, written in northern Persia between the years 363 and 381 of the hijrah (973-991 A.D.): «The ascensions of the right sphere are the quantities of the celestial equator that rise with arcs of the ecliptic on the equinoctial line; in Persian [they are called] ǵuwī rāst» (note 3: Liber Mafâtih al-ʿolûm auctore Abû ʿAbdallâh Mohammed ibn Ahmed al-Khowârezmî, ed. G. van Vloten, Leiden 1895, p. 219). The word is missing in Persian lexicons. In the index of chapters of the astronomical book entitled az-zīǵ al-ǵāmiʿ (note 4: the index is in Ahlwardt, Verzeichniss der arabischen Handschriften der k. Bibliothek zu Berlin, Berlin 1887-99, vol. V, no. 5751, p. 206a. On the author, Suter, pp. 83-84, no. 192), written in the second half of the fourth century of the hijrah (tenth A.D.) by Kūshiyār ibn Labbān in northern Persia, I had found not only tables of ascensions «on the line of the equator» (bi khaṭṭ al-istiwā'), that is of right ascensions, but also «of the ascensions of the signs by ǵuwīrāst», maṭāliʿ al-burūǵ bi-l-ǵuwīrāst. At my request the distinguished Ed. Sachau, with his great kindness, transcribed the tables for my use from the Berlin codex, and I saw at once that they give the right ascensions of all the degrees of the zodiac counted from the beginning of Capricorn (cf. pp. 163-64). So it seems false (continued).
