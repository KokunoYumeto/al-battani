% Commentary on chapter 44 c) concluded (Schiaparelli): the true reason for computing the apparent diameter of the Sun may be guessed from p. 58, l. 31 ff., where the apparent diameters of the Sun are proportional to the speed of its hourly motion (table t. II, p. 88): the least apparent diameter 31′ 20″ times the unequal hourly motion of the Sun for the given anomaly, divided by the least hourly motion 2′ 23″. Chapter 47, a) p. 115, l. 15 and 19: «afīǵiyūn» and «ferīǵiyūn», the columns of northern and southern latitude; Plato «effregion septentrionalis», «effregio meridiana»; in the table of the latitudes of the superior planets (t. II, p. 140, from the Almagest, ed. Halma t. II, pp. 412-13) the margin reads in Arabic «ἀπόγειον is the north, περίγειον the south». Ptolemy's columns are βόρειον πέρας and νότιον πέρας; Gerard of Cremona's Latin Almagest has «Efegion-Septentrio» and «Efregion-Meridies», so al-Battānī took the names from Arabic copies of the Almagest; Nallino explains them by the northern latitude of the superior planets when the centre of the epicycle is in the half of the eccentric with the apogee. Al-Khāzinī (cod. Vat., fol. 167, 178, 176) uses the same words, corrupted, in the opposite sense: «περίγειον of Saturn, ascending in the north», «ἀπόγειον of Saturn, descending in the south». From Plato's version the words passed into Johannes Schöner's Tabulae resolutae (Wittenberg 1587, p. 27). Notes: (1) the Almagest of Venice 1515 (Petrus Liechtenstein), fol. 149 r.; Gerard lived 1114-1187; (2) Schöner, born at Karlstadt near Würzburg in 1477, died at Nuremberg in 1547.
