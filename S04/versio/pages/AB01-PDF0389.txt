% Commentary on chapter 52 a) II continued: (Letronne and Martin both rightly) judged that Theon's words ὅπερ τῷ Πτολεμαίῳ οὐ δοκεῖ mean that Ptolemy did not even think the doctrine worth mentioning. The doctrine of trepidation, mentioned also by Proclus in the fifth century (note 1: Halma's Hypothèses de Ptolémée et Hypotyposes de Proclus, p. 88: «others held that the tropic points move, not round the whole circle but some degrees, and then back again»), both think arose from the wish to reconcile the Chaldaeo-Egyptian hypothesis of immobile equinoctial points with the theory of precession that Hipparchus had deduced from observations over less than two centuries (note 2: Letronne; Martin). The eight degrees would come from a change, wrongly interpreted, that Hipparchus made in the division of the ecliptic when, after the custom of the older astronomers, he restored the beginnings of Aries, Cancer, Libra and Capricorn to the equinoctial and solstitial points, which Meton, Eudoxus and others had put 8° west of the equinoxes and solstices. What Letronne and Martin say of Eudoxus (second half of the fourth century B.C.) and Meton (first half of the fifth) is not to be held true, for it comes from Columella (note 3: De re rustica IX, 14: the equinox at the eighth part of Aries, not at the first parts after Hipparchus; Columella follows the calendars of Eudoxus and Meton), of small authority in these matters; Hipparchus (note 4: his commentary on the Phaenomena of Aratus and Eudoxus, II, 3: Eudoxus divides the zodiac so that the tropic and equinoctial points are in the middle of the signs, of Cancer and Capricorn, of Aries and Libra; and the Parapegma of Geminus differs from Hipparchus: Callippus and Euctemon put the summer solstice at the beginning of Cancer, Eudoxus the winter solstice at 4° of Capricorn and the vernal equinox at 6° of Aries) expressly says that Eudoxus put the equinoctial and solstitial points in the centre, 15°, of Aries, Cancer, Libra and Capricorn; the modern historians of Greek astronomy do not doubt it (note 5: except Tannery, who says that Hipparchus gave a precise mathematical sense to texts of Eudoxus that indicated only approximate traces). Schiaparelli explains Columella's error: in Eudoxus' time the Greeks began the year with summer, whence the use of beginning the zodiac with Cancer (Hipparchus and the Parapegma of Geminus); the constellation Cancer occupies about half a sign, 15°; a grammarian, hearing that Eudoxus put the summer solstice in the middle of Cancer, confusing the dodecatemories with the constellations, thought Eudoxus put the solstice at 7° 1/2 of Cancer, in the eighth degree (continued).
