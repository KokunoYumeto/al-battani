II. The projection of the rays before al-Battānī. On the ἀκτινοβολία or projection of rays among the Greeks Salmasius (De annis climactericis et antiqua astrologia diatribae, Leiden 1648, pp. 398-409) and Bouché-Leclercq (L'astrologie grecque, Paris 1899, pp. 247-51) have written; but both considered only its philosophical principles and its use in predictions, and neglected to investigate by what mathematical method and in which celestial circle the Greeks computed the rays. Many, probably, neglecting the latitude of the planet, computed the radiations simply by adding or subtracting 60°, 90°, 120° to the longitude of the planet for the various aspects; so did al-Qabīṣī or Alcabitius (note 1: the Libellus isagogicus, Venice 1485, fol. aa4, in the chapter on the aspects of the planets, = the Praeclarum Alchabitii opus, Venice 1521, f. 4 r.; he lived in the fourth century H., tenth century; Suter pp. 60-61, nr. 132) and the Jew Abraham ibn Ezra (Avenezra). Other Greek methods can be learnt from Arabic books. Ibn Hibintà, fol. 33 v., says that Hermes, in his book entitled al-ʿarḍ (note 2: if read al-ʿaraḍ, as is probable, it means the book of the «accident», in the philosophical sense opposed to «substance»; if al-ʿarḍ, the book of «latitude»; Steinschneider, ZDMG 50, 1896, p. 189), takes the radiations by «equal degrees» (p. 28, note 3); while Dorotheus, according to the same Ibn Hibintà and Muḥammad ibn ‘Omar ibn al-Farrukhān (note 3: Omar, de nativitatibus, book I, p. 123, printed with other astrological books after the edition of Firmicus cited on p. 305, note 5; the author lived about 850; Suter p. 17, nr. 34), counted the radiations not on the ecliptic but on the equator, the sextile ray being the arc of the equator between the right ascension of the planet's degree of longitude and the right ascension corresponding to the longitude ± 60°. Hermes' method is also set out in the Libros del saber de astronomia (note 4: vol. II, p. 65, Libro de las armellas, book II, ch. XLV; vol. II, p. 308, Libros dell astrolábio llano, book II, ch. VII; vol. III, p. 211, Segunda parte del libro de la açafeha, ch. LXIV, each «according to the opinion of Hermes»), whence these rules: find the degree of the equator falling in the circle of position (cf. p. 311, note 3) drawn through the given planet; add or subtract 60° (or 90°, or 120°); the degree of the ecliptic falling in the circle of position drawn through that sum or remainder of the equatorial arc is the degree of the left and right sextile (or quadrature or trine). Finally Valens (note 5: of uncertain date; Salmasius and Bouché-Leclercq, p. XIII, think he lived under Constantine, E. Riess and W. Kroll assign him to the age of Hadrian and Antoninus), according to az-Zarqālī in the Libros del saber III, 204-5 (Segunda parte del libro de la açafeha, ch. LVII) and Rabiçag of Toledo, ibid. II, 62 (continued).
