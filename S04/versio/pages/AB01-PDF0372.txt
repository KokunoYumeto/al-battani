% Commentary on chapter 47, b) p. 116, l. 24: I. «ob eorum velocem circa Solem cursum»; likewise p. 117, l. 11. Plato calls the inferior planets ἰσοδρόμοι ἡλίῳ, Ptolemy (IX, 1) περὶ αὐτὸν ἀεὶ φερόμενοι; such phrases mean only the apparent motion of Venus and Mercury about the Sun, to and fro, as Schiaparelli showed (I precursori di Copernico nell'antichità, Milan 1873); cf. Hultzsch in Pauly-Wissowa, t. II, col. 1859. Al-Qazwīnī (13th century), Cosmographia, says Mercury has many retrogradations and progressions and «revolves about the Sun». II. Ibn Yūnus (Caussin p. 70) says al-Battānī erred «in the side of the latitude of Mercury»; Nallino finds no error: the precepts of pp. 115-16 and the tables agree with Ptolemy; perhaps Ibn Yūnus had a faulty copy, like the Escorial codex (not Plato) on p. 116. Chapter 48, a) p. 117, l. 28-29: the «aequatorial» arcs of visibility of the planets: with al-Battānī they are truly arcs of the equator, as the following precepts and the end of chapter 49 (p. 120) show; Ptolemy chose arcs of the ecliptic, the difference of longitude (Almagest XIII, 7: Saturn 14°, Jupiter 12½ + ¼°, Mars 14½°, Venus in the evening 5⅔°, Mercury in the evening 11½°); since he fixed them near the beginning of Cancer, al-Battānī could transfer them to the equator without sensible error. Notes: (1) Caussin «s'est trompé sur la latitude», missing the technical sense of جهة; (2) in Almagest XIII, 7 Ptolemy also computed the least depression of the Sun for the heliacal risings and settings: Saturn 11°, Jupiter 10°, Mars 11½°, Venus 5°, Mercury 10°; cf. R. Wolf, Handbuch der Astronomie, who wrongly says he did not find them in the Almagest.

The rule of the translation, p. 117, l. 31-38, compares the times of rising or setting, at the latitude of the place, of the degrees of the ecliptic that rise or set with the planet and with the Sun: arcs of the equator, as L21 says, but oblique ascensions, where L22 names right ascension. At the beginning of Cancer the arcs of right ascension exceed the corresponding arcs of the ecliptic in the ratio 1 : cos ε, 1.09 with al-Battānī's obliquity 23° 35′: Saturn's 14° of the ecliptic, taken equally on both sides of the solstice, are 15° 16′ of right ascension, Mercury's 11° 30′ are 12° 32′.
