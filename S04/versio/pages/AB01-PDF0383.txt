% Commentary on chapter 51 b) I concluded: al-Battānī found the longitudes of β Scorpii and α Leonis increased by 11° 50′ since Menelaus, after 782 years. His reasoning: Ptolemy made his catalogue 41 years (note 1: so al-Battānī and aṣ-Ṣūfī, but the true interval was 40; see note a, nr. I, p. 292; an error of no moment, the precession of one year being 54″ 33‴) after Menelaus; in that interval the precession is 37′ 21″; so Ptolemy's longitudes need 11° 50′ − 0° 37′ 21″ = 11° 12′ 39″, rounded 11° 13′. But Ptolemy uses only the fractions 1/2, 1/3, 1/6 (rarely 1/4) of a degree, so that his longitudes are in whole tens of minutes; adding 11° 13′ would make nearly all minutes end in 3, which would look strange and might lead someone to take uncertain numbers for exact ones; a difference of some minutes, even of a degree, matters little in the longitudes of the fixed stars; so 11° 10′ only. The reasoning would be excellent if Ptolemy had used al-Battānī's precession; but the Greek's was 36″ a year, and for 41 years Ptolemy had added 25′, not 37′ 21″, to Menelaus' longitudes; so al-Battānī should have added 11° 50′ − 0° 25′ = 11° 25′, not 11° 10′. Strangely he did not notice the discrepancy between the longitudes observed by himself and Menelaus and those of Ptolemy's catalogue (table: β Scorpii (note 2: translation, p. 124, note 6): al-Battānī 17° 45′ Scorpii, Ptolemy 6° 1/2 Scorpii, difference from Menelaus 11° 50′, from Ptolemy 11° 25′; α Leonis: 14° 0′ Leonis, 2° 1/2 Leonis, 11° 50′, 11° 30′). Aṣ-Ṣūfī avoided the error (pp. 42-43): his catalogue is for the year 1276 of Dhū 'l-qarnayn (beginning 1 October 964), 866 years after Menelaus, with precession 13° 7′ 16″; Ptolemy having already added 25′, he adds 13° 7′ − 0° 25′ = 12° 42′; hence aṣ-Ṣūfī's longitudes exceed al-Battānī's by 1° 32′ instead of 1° 17′, the precession in the 85 years between them. II. «We have found no sensible difference of latitude»: Tycho Brahe (Progymnasmata I, 246) sharply reproaches al-Battānī for keeping Ptolemy's latitudes of the fixed stars though he found the obliquity of the ecliptic about a quarter of a degree smaller than the ancients (23 . | . 36 [sic]), as too credulous of his predecessors, without observation of his own, or with unsuitable instruments, or negligently. Nallino: unjust; al-Battānī says not «no difference» but «no sensible difference»; in problems resolving the ecliptic coordinates of the fixed stars a discrepancy of 1/2 degree in latitude was then of no moment; and Ptolemy's latitudes contain no fraction of a degree but those answering to 10′, 15′, 20′, 30′, 40′, 45′ and 50′, so they are given only approximately in minutes and an exact comparison is impossible.
