% Commentary on chapter 44, a) concluded (Schiaparelli): al-Battānī (p. 105, l. 6–24) takes 1/6 hour, or 10 minutes of hour angle, as the longest interval over which the variation of the parallax may be held uniform; of the two parallaxes needed one is known, for ϑ + ε². Case 1: if ϑ + ε² differs from the nearest whole hour by more than 1/6 hour, a new parallax for ϑ + ε² + 1/6 or ϑ + ε² − 1/6 is computed directly; their difference times 6 is the hourly parallactic motion, which taken from the praecessio Lunae (the true relative hourly motion) gives al-Battānī's motus aequatus, the apparent relative hourly motion; Theon's tables cannot serve, their nearest entry lying more than 1/6 hour away. Case 2: if the fraction of the hour is under 1/6 or over 5/6, the interval to the nearest whole hour (1/8, 1/10 ...) is taken, whose parallax Theon's tables give. The parallax in longitude decreases with time between the ascendant and the nonagesimal and increases between the nonagesimal and the descendant; the precept to subtract or add 1/6 hour makes the auxiliary parallax always the greater, useful against errors of sign but not necessary. Δ divided by the motus aequatus gives the small time τ, added to or taken from ϑ + ε²: the time of the apparent conjunction. Summary of al-Battānī's precepts. — Schiaparelli. Nallino: al-Battānī's method differs from the older one; al-Khāzinī, az-Zīǵ as-Sanǵarī, fol. 80 r.–81 r. (treatise VIII, part II, chapter II), first method, after Ptolemy (begun).
