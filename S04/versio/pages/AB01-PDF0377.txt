% Commentary on chapter 50 a) continued: Ya‘qūb ibn Ṭāriq's numbers in Earth radii, in a table: least and greatest distance and diameter: Moon 35 15/21, 56 4/21, 4 16/21; Mercury 60 20/21, 251 9/21, 4 16/21; Venus 256 4/21, 1095 5/21, 19 1/21; Sun 1114 6/21, 2104 16/21, 19 1/21; Mars 2123 17/21, 8000, 19 1/21; Jupiter 8019 1/21, 13714 6/21, 19 1/21; Saturn 13733 7/21, 17161 19/21, 19 1/21; the zodiac 19047 13/21 (note 1: between the greatest distance of Saturn and the zodiac there is thus an empty space of 1885 15/21 Earth radii). Al-Bīrūnī adds that this opinion differs from the one on which Ptolemy, in the book al-manshūrāt, founded the computation of the distances, followed by the ancients and the moderns: the greatest distance of each planet is the least of the next, with no idle place between the spheres; the Indians put an empty place between the spheres, with something holding like the axle of a wheel, as if they believed there was weight in the aether. The book al-manshūrāt, otherwise unknown, is named also in al-Bīrūnī's booklet On the elements of astronomy, fol. 33 r.: the diameter of the Moon is known in Earth radii from the parallax (Ptolemy), that of the Sun from the eclipses (Almagest V); the ratios of the diameters of the planets and the fixed stars to that of the Sun are known, in the opinion of others, from observation, as told in the book al-manshūrāt; al-Bīrūnī's table follows Ptolemy (note 2: the Arabic, and his table of the diameters relative to the Sun, with faulty numbers). Finally Ibn Hibintā, Astrology, fol. 210 v. (to be compared with al-Battānī p. 123, l. 4-8): Ptolemy makes the stars of the first magnitude 96 times the Earth, each following order 16 times the Earth less, down to as-Suhà (note 3: near ζ Ursae maioris, missing from Ptolemy's catalogue), 16 times the Earth; al-Battānī, observing at ar-Raqqah in A.H. 295, makes the first magnitude 108 times the Earth, decreasing to as-Suhà, 18 times (continued).

@obs body L1-L9: Ya‘qūb ibn Ṭāriq's table is set with each piece placed as printed: the head in the small type of the notes (x-height 4.4 pt, against 4.9 pt in the rows), «dist minima» without a full stop and «dist. maxima» with it; the whole numbers on their right edges, the fractions from their left edges.
