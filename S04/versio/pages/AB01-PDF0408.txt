Such pictures can be seen in Yāqūt, Ibn al-Wardī and Lelewel, Géographie du moyen âge, Brussels 1852, atlas. b) p. 137, l. 20 sqq.: the picture of the sundial, very badly drawn in the codex and in Plato, was drawn again by Schiaparelli according to the author's precepts, for the latitude 36° and a gnomon of 12 millimetres. He marked the azimuth of the qiblah taking al-Battānī's geographical coordinates (cf. vol. II, pp. 37-38 and 41): Mekkah longitude 77° 53′, latitude N 21° 40′; ar-Raqqah longitude 73° 15′, latitude N 36° 0′. The error in the longitudes is not small, for by the modern ones the difference is about 50′. But the longitudes come from different sources that disagree among themselves, as I shall show in vol. II, in the commentary on the geographical tables. The construction of al-Battānī's sundial was already well known to the Greeks; cf. Fr. Woepcke, Disquisitiones archaeologico-mathematicae circa solaria veterum, Berlin 1842. Before al-Battānī's time several wrote books on making sundials: Muḥammad ibn ‘Omar ibn al-Farrukhān, Muḥammad ibn Mūsà al-Khuwārizmī, al-Farghānī, Ḥabash, al-Ḥusayn ibn Muḥammad al-Adamī, Thābit ibn Qurrah. Chapter 57 a) p. 141, l. 37: the pole (Arabic al-quṭb, whence among us alchiloth) or axis (Arabic al-miḥwar, whence among us almehuar) or key is a pin with a large head that passes through the middle of the alidade, the plates, the rete and the mother of the astrolabe. Near the point that comes out of the back of the astrolabe it has a hole, into which a small pin of copper or bronze is put to hold and fix the parts of the instrument pierced by the pole; from its shape it is called equulus «little horse» (al-faras «horse», whence among us alphorat or alferath). Some distinguish the pole from the axis, calling pole the hole made in the middle of the alidade, the plates, the rete and the mother, and axis the pin that passes through these parts of the instrument (note 1: Libros del saber de astronomía, vol. II, p. 264 (Libro dell astrolabio llano, book II, ch. V): «the hole in the middle of the alidade and of the mother and of the other plates and of the rete they call pole, and they name almehuar the pin that goes into these holes and passes through them. And they call cauallet the piece that goes into the almehuar to fix the said parts of the astrolabe»); but that al-Battānī makes no such distinction is clear from this very place and from p. 144, l. 1. b) pp. 139-141: description of the armillary sphere, with five rings (note 2: the picture of the instrument placed here Schiaparelli restored from al-Battānī's description).
