@obs body L15-L25, L30-L31: the statement of al-Battānī that Schiaparelli rejects holds. ε² is the second parallax divided by the relative hourly motion (L10-L11; translation, p. 104, l. 29–30), so that in the time ε² the Moon gains on the Sun exactly the second parallax, and at ϑ + ε² the true longitudes of the Moon and the Sun differ by the second parallax; Schiaparelli's own reason in L15-L17 rests on this. The difference Δ of the apparent longitudes at ϑ + ε² is therefore the difference of the second and the third parallax, whether it is null or not, and al-Battānī's rule, which divides the excess of the third parallax over the second, or its defect, by the motus aequatus (p. 105, l. 23–37), divides Δ itself. Schiaparelli's remark in L23-L25 is right about the inference it names, but al-Battānī's equality does not rest on that inference. Both assume, as the whole method does, a uniform relative motion over the interval.
