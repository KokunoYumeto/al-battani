% Commentary on chapter 50 concluded: the same African or Spanish glossator added at the end of the work (f. 241 v.-244 r.) tables taken from the book of Maslamah al-Maǵrīṭī, who lived at Cordova in the later half of the tenth century and made a compendium of al-Battānī's tables. Chapter 51 a) p. 124, l. 19-33: I. The epoch of Ptolemy's catalogue of the fixed stars: Ptolemy VII, 4 (Halma II, 30) gives the longitudes for the first year of Antoninus, year 885 of Nabonassar, beginning 20 July 137; why al-Battānī, l. 20, says 886 is unknown; it is not a copyist's error, for aṣ-Ṣūfī, p. 42, also writes 886 and counts 41 years between Menelaus' observation of the year 845 and the epoch of the catalogue. II. The motion of the fixed stars in longitude, the precession: after Schiaparelli and Th. M. Martin, Hipparchus (about 130 B.C.) first knew the precession (note 1: whether the Babylonians preceded him is unknown; Kugler); he put its least annual amount at 36″ and later deduced 42″ to 46″,8 from other observations; Ptolemy wrongly chose the least value, 1° in 100 Egyptian years of 365 days, 36″ a year (note 2: Delambre; Tannery). The Arabs rarely use Ptolemy's amount: al-Farghānī, ch. XIX, and al-Qazwīnī. Al-Battānī, comparing his observations with those of Menelaus, found the longitudes increased by 11° 50′ in 782 years, 54″,4757 = 54″ 28‴ a year, 1° in 66 318/3268 years; rightly judging the fractions of seconds and even the seconds uncertain, he put 1° in 66 years, 54″ 33‴ in a solar year (note 3: Bessel's annual precession, 50″,21129 − 0″,0002442966·t for the year t before 1750, was 50″,0 in 880, the epoch of al-Battānī's catalogue). His amount agrees with that of the astronomers of al-Ma’mūn in the Tabula Probata (aṣ-Ṣūfī, pp. 33 and 42), and with that found about 850 by the Banū Mūsà ibn Shākir (note 4: Ibn Yūnus, ch. V); most Arabic astronomers chose it: aṣ-Ṣūfī, al-Bīrūnī (note 5), Ḥabash (note 6: 1° in 70 years after Ibn Yūnus, an error of his), al-Kharaqī, al-Khāzinī, al-Ǵaghmīnī, Barhebraeus, the Persian Tables of the Greek Chrysococces (note 7: in Bullialdus' Astronomia philolaica, 1° in 68 years, but lunar years, continued).
