% Commentary on chapter 55 b) continued: (for α and β are taken the arcs of the ecliptic) corresponding. The same computation of the direction, a little later, is set out by Alcabitius, diff. IV, ch. VII, f. dd3 v.-dd5 v. of the 1485 edition, or 17 r.-18 r. of 1521. II. Al-Battānī gives another rule, p. 134, l. 21 sqq.; if A and B are in the eastern hemisphere: (β) direction = (right ascension of B − ascension of the midheaven) − hours of distance of A × horary times of B. This can be proved thus: from the first figure one deduces approximately (cf. p. 315, note 3) E′C : F′A = E′E : F′F or Dc : Da = Dβ : Dα, that is Dc : (right ascension of A − right ascension of the midheaven) = horary times of B : horary times of A, whence Dc = (right ascension of A − right ascension of the midheaven)/horary times of A × horary times of B, Dc = temporal hours of distance of A from the midheaven × horary times of B. But also Dc = bc + Db = direction + (right ascension of B − ascension of the midheaven); so, as al-Battānī has: direction = hours of distance of A × horary times of B − (right ascension of B − ascension of the midheaven). The precepts for stars in the western hemisphere or in different hemispheres are proved in the same way. At the end of the chapter (note 1: the last words, «in this way the hilegia etc.», were probably interpolated, as said in the preface) he says this is Ptolemy's computation. Rightly, for in the Tetrabiblos III, 10 (III, 15 of the Frankfurt version) al-Battānī's second method is set out and illustrated by a numerical example. Ptolemy explains why one must proceed so: «But if the preceding place is not on these cardines but falls in the spaces between, the times corresponding to the rising, setting or culminating arcs will not carry the following place to the preceding, but different ones. For a place is similar and the same which has a similar position and in the same degrees both to the horizon and to the meridian. This happens approximately (ἔγγιστα) to those that fall on one and the same of the semicircles drawn through the common intersections of the horizon and the meridian; [of these semicircles, standing at] approximately equal intervals [from one another], each makes one [temporal] hour. The equator is always inclined in the same way to each of these semicircles, and to the horizon and the meridian, as if it turned about those intersections; but the degrees of the ecliptic change their relation to each semicircle and pass both in unequal times. And in the same way, in whatever direction from the cardines [i.e. the paxilli] the significator stands, it passes those semicircles in unequal time. We will nevertheless set out one way by which ..... the number of equinoctial times that carries the preceding place to the following can be found by proportion». But Ptolemy (like Alcabitius and others) always considers points of the ecliptic, as if the planets to be directed never had latitude. Al-Battānī, on the contrary, rightly orders, when the planet departs from the zodiac, the degree culminating with the planet to be taken, instead of the degree of the ecliptic that gives its longitude, in the computation of right ascensions, and the degree rising with the planet in the computation of oblique ascensions. Like al-Battānī, John of Saxony in his commentary on chapter VII of the fourth difference of Alcabitius (note 2: fol. ll7 v.-mm2 r. of the 1485 edition, or 57 r.-59 r. of 1521); he says of his computation for planets with latitude: «and I have not seen [this] way set out in any book» (fol. mm1 v. = 58 v.), as he had already said of the radiations computed in the manner of the direction (cf. p. 311, note 2). Clearly he did not know this chapter of al-Battānī or had forgotten it.
