% Commentary on chapter 54 d) II concluded: special books on radiations were written before al-Battānī by Messahala or Mā shā’ Allāh, one of the oldest Arab astrologers, by al-Kindī (whose book was also translated into Latin), and by ‘Abd Allāh ibn Masrūr an-Naṣrānī, a pupil of Abū Ma‘shar. Chapter 55 a) p. 131, l. 45 - p. 132, l. 9: the Arabs call cardines (as said at length on p. 248) the four points of the ecliptic determined by the intersection of the horizon and the meridian: the ascendant, the midheaven, the descendant or setting point, and the lower midheaven or cardine of the Earth. The arc of the equator, counted from the beginning of Aries, that crosses the meridian together with a given star is its right ascension; the arc of the equator that rises or sets with a given star on the horizon is its oblique ascension or descension, or ascension or descension in the clime. The author has treated these arcs in the preceding chapters. It remains to know the arcs of the equator rising together with a given star at any other place of the sphere, that is, the arc of the equator or hour angle that a planet or a given degree of the ecliptic must traverse to pass the circle of position (note 1: cf. p. 311, note 3) of another planet or degree (note 2: Libros del saber, vol. II, pp. 65-66, Libro de las armellas, book II, ch. XLVII: «on knowing the risings of the signs over the circles that are like the circle of the horizon when the sign is between the angles»; ibid. III, 207-208, Segunda parte del libro de la açafeha, ch. LX: «on knowing the risings of the signs between the angles. These risings are the times of passage of the signs and their degrees through the great circles that pass through the two poles of the circle of east and west, and are like the circles of the horizons»). This problem, useless in astronomy, al-Battānī (p. 134, last line) calls necessary for the astrologers to compute the directions (note 3: some consider the projections of rays in the same way, as shown on pp. 311-12); for to direct point A to B is to find after how much time of the equator A reaches the circle of position drawn through B. To make the reason for this computation clearer, some definitions: comparing the life of men and nations to a horse let out from the starting gate towards the goal of the circus, the old astrologers said that life is let go (ἀφίεσθαι) more or less strongly from some point of the zodiac, called τόπος ἀφετικός, «the releasing place», locus apheticus or prorogans or hilegialis; the aphetic places are the midheaven, the ascendant, the beginning of the eleventh house (ἀγαθοδαίμων), the beginning of the seventh or descendant, and the beginning of the ninth (θεός). Life let go from the aphetic place perishes, or at least is in danger, when it has come along the ecliptic to some obstacle, called τόπος ἀναιρετικός, «the destroying place»; the degrees of the equator corresponding to the interval between the aphetic and the anaeretic place were to be converted by various methods into years, giving the years of life from birth to the unlucky event or death. The star which holds the aphetic place at the time of birth is to be taken as the ruler and moderator of life, called by the Greeks ἀφέτης «releaser» or κύριος τῆς ζωῆς «lord of life»; by the Arabs dalīl (plural dalā’il, adillā’, adillah) «guide, indicator» or, with a Persian word, haylāǵ (plural hayāliǵ); by the Latins of the Middle Ages and later apheta, significator (continued).
