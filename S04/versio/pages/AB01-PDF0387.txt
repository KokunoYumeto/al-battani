% Commentary on chapter 51 e) concluded (aṣ-Ṣūfī): al-Battānī had suppressed all the stars on which the copies differ in the least, many of the third and fourth magnitude, while giving many of the fifth and sixth. Nallino: aṣ-Ṣūfī's words are not free of malice, imputing as a fault and a fraud what al-Battānī himself professes to have done. Chapter 52 a) p. 126, l. 35 sqq.: the doctrine of some astrologers on the trepidation of the fixed stars. I. «Ptolemy says in his book...»: not Ptolemy but Theon of Alexandria (fourth century) in the Handy Tables, ed. Halma I, 53, chapter on the solstice (περὶ τροπῆς): according to certain opinions (κατά τινας δόξας) the old astrologers (οἱ παλαιοὶ τῶν ἀποτελεσματικῶν) hold that the tropic points, after a certain epoch, move 8° forward [eastward] and then as many back, which Ptolemy does not admit, because without such a hypothesis the equinoxes computed from the tables agree with those found by instruments (note 1: the Greek); so we too advise (ὅθεν καὶ ἡμεῖς παραινοῦμεν) that the opinion be rejected, but set out their reckoning: taking as epoch 128 years before the reign of Augustus, when the greatest digression of 8° eastward was reached, adding the 313 years from Augustus to Diocletian and the years since Diocletian [to the given time], they find the place [of the tropic points], with a motion of 1° in 80 years; the quotient of this division taken from 8°, the remainder (the degree reached by the tropic points at that time) is added to the places of the Sun, the Moon and the five planets computed in this way. Theon's «which Ptolemy does not admit» is somewhat ambiguous; it probably means that Ptolemy silently rejected the doctrine, never mentioning it; al-Battānī took it to mean that Ptolemy mentioned and refuted those astrologers (note 2: Delambre is uncertain in interpreting Theon's passage; see Letronne and Martin, below). The other Arabic writers cite Theon, not Ptolemy, for the doctrine of trepidation (note 3: the Hispano-Jewish Abraham bar Ḥiyyā, who says that Ptolemy knew the trepidation, took his knowledge from al-Battānī's work itself). Thābit ibn Qurrah (d. 288 H., 901), in his epistle to Isḥāq ibn Ḥunayn (note 4: d. in Rabī‘ II 298 H., the end of 910; Suter), preserved by Ibn Yūnus, ch. IV (Caussin, p. 116), rightly says: some, whom Theon and others mention and call astrologers (aṣḥāb al-aḥkām), held that the zodiac has a motion by which it advances 8° and then returns as many, completing one degree in every 80 years.
