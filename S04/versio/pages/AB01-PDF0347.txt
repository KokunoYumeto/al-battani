@obs body L20-L29: (α) is the law of sines (p. 181, note 1) in the triangle GBH, right-angled at B, where the meridian meets the horizon: sin GHB = R sin GB : sin GH. GH is the distance of the Moon at the ascendant from the culminating degree, GB the meridian altitude of G, 90° − φ ± δ, whose sine is cos (φ ∓ δ); GHB, the angle of the ecliptic with the horizon, is the complement of ZHG, the vertical HZ being perpendicular to the horizon. It is al-Battānī's rule of p. 81, L23-L29. The label «(α)» stands at the left edge of the lines beside the figure and the formula where it is printed. The passage in italics is p. 81, L31-L33, for a latitude smaller than the northern declination of the culminating degree: sin ang. lat. = R sin (δ − φ) : sin dist. @obs body L35-L37: on the meridian the vertical circle through the Moon is the meridian itself, and the angle of latitude is the angle η of the ecliptic with the meridian. In the right spherical triangle of the ecliptic, the equator and the meridian, with the hypotenuse λ, the distance from Aries or Libra, and the side δ, the declination, cos η = tan δ : tan λ = sin δ cos λ : (cos δ sin λ), which is (α). It is al-Battānī's rule of p. 81, L40 - p. 82, L3. The label «(α)» stands at the margin and the formula where it is printed.
