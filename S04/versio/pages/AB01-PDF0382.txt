% Commentary on chapter 51 a) concluded: about 1260 Muḥyī ad-dīn al-Maghrabī and others at the observatory of Marāghah found the precession 1° in 66 solar years from Aldebaran, α Scorpii and other stars (note 1: Abū 'l-Faẓl i ‘Allāmī, Āyīn-i-akbarī); the same amount in the Jewish authors Abraham bar Ḥiyyā' and Leo de Balneolis. 1° in 70 Egyptian or Persian years, 51″ 3/7 = 51″ 25‴ 42ⁱᵛ 51ᵛ 3/7 a year, was found by Ibn al-A‘lam (note 2: he died at the beginning of 375 H., which began 23 May 985), Naṣīr ad-dīn aṭ-Ṭūsī (note 3), Quṭb ad-dīn Maḥmūd ash-Shīrāzī and Oloug Beg (note 4: the French version has «soixante» for «soixante-dix»); Joseph ben Eliezer, about 1358, wrongly ascribes it to al-Battānī (note 5). Ibn Yūnus put 1° in 70 1/4 Egyptian years, 51″ 14‴ 43ⁱᵛ 59ᵛ a year (note 6: Sédillot's conjecture that az-Zarqālī computed 49″ 1/2 or 50″). The Indian Sūrya-Siddhānta, written before the fifth century, mentions with the doctrine of trepidation an annual precession of 54″, near al-Battānī's, but the passage is held a late interpolation (note 7: Whitney; Thibaut; Bhāskara's reading of only 9″); Varāha-Mihira (about 505) ignores the precession, Brahmagupta (628) denies it, though Āryabhaṭa had accepted it with trepidation in 499; Colebrooke. b) p. 124, l. 34-37: I. The addition of 11° 10′ to Ptolemy's longitudes of the fixed stars: Ptolemy VII, 3 (Halma II, 25 and 27) made much of Menelaus' observations at Rome: in the night of 15/16 Mechir of the first year of Trajan, 845 of Nabonassar (9-10 February 98 as printed), Spica at 176° 1/4, and in the night of 18/19 Mechir (12-13 February) β Scorpii at 215° 55′ (continued). The left column of the notes ends note 7 of p. 292: lunar years are meant, 68 of which make 66 solar years; Rudloff and Hochheim on al-Ǵaghmīnī are to be corrected; on the date of Chrysococces' tables see p. 159, note 8.
