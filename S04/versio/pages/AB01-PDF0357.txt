% Commentary on chapter 41, c) continued (Schiaparelli): the corrections to the mean arc of visibility added together; al-Battānī's rules: I. the interval A between the settings of the Sun and of the Moon (with parallax), compared with 11° 45′; II. the arc reduced in the inverse ratio of the apparent elongation to 12° 11′, giving B; III. the distance of the Moon from the third column of the table of correction (vol. II, p. 89): with n the number of that column, the crescent changes in the ratio 1 : 1 ∓ (1/12)(30 − n)/30, and B in the inverse ratio, giving C; the Moon is visible when A exceeds C. The theory ingenious and elegant, its proportions perhaps not so simple. Al-Farghānī: the new moon seen 12° of the equator from the Sun, a day after the conjunction; al-Ǵaghmīnī likewise; Ḥabash begins with the mean motions (begun). Notes: the elongation as the hypotenuse of a right triangle; «secundam»; the Indians already knew the rule: Sūrya-Siddhānta X, 1 and Pañcasiddhāntikā V, 1-3, 2 nāḍikā = 48 minutes = 12°.
