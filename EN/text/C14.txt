Chapter 14. On finding the latitudes of localities by observation
@p AB01-PDF1109-L16..AB01-PDF1108-L03
He said: If you wish to find the latitude of any locality you like, which is the altitude of the north pole there and also its distance from the equator, find the altitude of the sun at noon, in whichever way you like, when the sun passes the meridian line: with the quadrant or by finding the shadow. When you know the altitude, in either of the two ways, find the declination of the degree of the sun at that time. If it is northern, subtract it from the altitude; if it is southern, add it to it. The altitude after the addition or subtraction is the altitude of the beginning of Aries or of the beginning of Libra in that locality; subtract it from 90, and the remainder is the latitude of that locality. If you take the latitude of the locality from the table of the latitudes of the cities, it is approximate, and not as accurate as one found by observation.
