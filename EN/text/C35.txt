Chapter 35. On setting up the ascendant and the twelve houses from the hours, and on knowing the hours from the ascendant
@p AB01-PDF1043-L16..AB01-PDF1042-L08
He said: If you wish to know the ascendant and the rest of the twelve houses from the hours that have passed of the daytime or of the night — although this has been mentioned before in this book, briefly, in the chapter on knowing the hours from the altitude — look: if the time is daytime, take the hours from sunrise to the given hour, and if the time is night, take them from sunset. If they are equal hours, multiply them by 15, whichever of the two times they belong to, I mean the night or the daytime. If they are seasonal hours and are hours of the daytime, multiply them by the times of the hours of the daytime, and if they are hours of the night, multiply them by the times of the hours of the night. Add the times that result from the multiplication of the hours of the daytime to the times of the ascensions set down under the degree of the sun in the given clime, and add what results from the multiplication of the hours of the night to the times of the ascensions set down under the degree opposite the degree of the sun in the clime. If either of them comes to more than a circle, cast off a circle from it. With what results after that, or before it if it was less than a circle, find the ascendant and the middle of the sky in the way set out at the beginning of the book: carry the number that you have to the tables of the ascensions of the clime and take what stands opposite it of the degrees of the signs, by the common [interpolation] in that way; the result is the rising degree of the sign in which you found the number. Likewise enter with that same number into the ascensions of the right sphere and take what stands opposite it of the degrees of the signs: the result is the degree of the middle of the sky. When you know the ascendant, the setting point is its counterpart, and the degree of the fourth is the counterpart of the cardine of the middle of the sky.
@n The chapter on knowing the hours from the altitude is chapter 16; the ascensions and the hours of the clime are those of chapter 13 (Nallino). Al-Battānī counts the right ascensions from the beginning of Capricorn (chapter 5), so that the ascension of the ascendant in the clime, counted from Aries, is the right ascension of the middle of the sky (Nallino). The cardines are the four principal points of the ecliptic: the ascendant, the setting point, the middle of the sky and the fourth, or lowest, point.
@p AB01-PDF1042-L08..AB01-PDF1042-L13
If you wish to know the ascendant from the hours taken from the time of noon, take their count from the time of noon to the given hour. If they are equal hours, multiply them by 15; if they are seasonal hours, multiply those of them that are hours of the daytime by the times of the hours of the daytime, and those that are hours of the night by the times of the hours of the night. To what results, whichever of the two times it is, add the ascension of the degree of the sun itself in the right sphere; with the result you find the ascendant and the middle of the sky in that way, if God wills.
@p AB01-PDF1042-L13..AB01-PDF1041-L08
If you wish to set up the rest of the twelve houses, take the times of the hours of the degree of the ascendant in that clime, double them, and add them to the ascensions by which you found the ascendant and the middle of the sky, which are the times of the ascensions of the degree of the ascendant in the clime. Look up the result in the ascensions of the right sphere and take what stands opposite it of the degrees of the signs: the result is the beginning of the eleventh house, in the sign in which the number falls. Then add the doubled times of the ascensions of the degree of the ascendant again to these times by which you found the beginning of the eleventh house, and take what stands opposite the result of the degrees of the signs in the ascensions of the right sphere: the result is the beginning of the twelfth house, in the sign in which the number falls. Then add those same doubled times again to the number by which you found the beginning of the twelfth house, and take what stands opposite it in the ascensions of the right sphere: it agrees with the degree of the ascendant exactly. Then subtract the doubled times from 60: the remainder is the rest of the doubled times, so keep it. Add it to the number by which you found the degree of the ascendant in the right sphere, which is the one that I told you agrees with the degree of the ascendant, and take what stands opposite the result of the degrees of the signs, again in the ascensions of the right sphere: the result is the beginning of the second house. Then add the rest of the doubled times again to the number by which you found the beginning of the second house, and take what stands opposite the sum of the degrees of the signs in the ascensions of the right sphere: the result is the beginning of the third house. Likewise, if you added the rest of the times to this number by which you found the beginning of the third house, it would agree with the degree of the fourth. When you know the beginnings of these houses, the beginning of the fifth is the counterpart of the beginning of the eleventh, the beginning of the sixth the counterpart of the beginning of the twelfth, the beginning of the eighth the counterpart of the beginning of the second house, and the beginning of the ninth the counterpart of the beginning of the third. The meaning of «counterpart» is the degree opposite it on a diameter of the sphere, which stands 180 degrees from it. When you have done this, you have computed the beginnings of the twelve houses in degrees of the signs.
@n The houses are divided as al-Qabīṣī (Alcabitius) and Ibn Ezra teach: the half arc of the day of the degree of the ascendant, which is six of its seasonal hours of the daytime, and its half arc of the night are each divided into three equal parts, and circles through the poles of the equator mark the beginnings of the houses (Nallino). The «doubled times» are the times of two seasonal hours of the daytime of the ascendant; what they fall short of 60 is the times of two seasonal hours of its night, since the hours of the day and of the night of a degree together make 30 times. Nallino would add, among the counterparts, «the beginning of the seventh is the counterpart of the ascendant».
@p AB01-PDF1041-L08..AB01-PDF1041-L16
As for knowing the hours from the ascendant: look at the given ascendant. If it is between the degree of the sun and its counterpart, in the order of the signs, the time is daytime; if it is between the degree of the counterpart of the sun and the degree of the sun, the time is night. If the time is daytime, subtract the ascension of the degree of the sun from the ascension of the degree of the ascendant in the clime: the remainder is what has turned of the sphere from sunrise to the rising of that degree. If the time is night, subtract the times of the ascension of the counterpart of the degree of the sun from the times of the ascension of the degree of the ascendant: the remainder is what has turned of the sphere from the setting of the sun to the rising of that degree. If the time is daytime, divide what has turned of the sphere by the times of the hours of the daytime: what comes out is hours, and what remains is a fraction of an hour. If the time is night, divide what has turned of the sphere by the times of the hours of the night: the result is what has passed of the night, in seasonal hours. If you divide it by 15, what comes out is equal hours.
