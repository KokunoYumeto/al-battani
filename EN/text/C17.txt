Chapter 17. On finding the altitude from the hours
@p AB01-PDF1106-L03..AB01-PDF1106-L11
He said: If you wish to find the altitude from the hours of the day that have passed, take the hours from sunrise to the given hour. If they are equinoctial hours, multiply them by 15; if they are seasonal, multiply them by the times of the hours of that day. Look at what you obtain in either way: if it is less than half the arc of the day, subtract it from half the arc of the day; if it is more, take half the arc of the day from it. The result is the distance of the sun from the middle of the sky. Find its versed sine and subtract it from the versed sine of half the arc of the day. Multiply the remainder by the sine of the altitude of the sun at noon on that day, divide the product by the versed sine of half the arc of the day, and find the arc of the quotient, as I described to you for taking the arc of the sines. The arc you obtain is the amount of the altitude above the horizon at the given hour. [If the hour is] before noon, the altitude is on the east side; if it is after noon, it is on the west side.
