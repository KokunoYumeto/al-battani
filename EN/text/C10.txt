Chapter 10. On finding the altitude and the shadow, each from the other, by computation or from the table
@p AB01-PDF1121-L17..AB01-PDF1120-L05
He said: If you wish to find the shadow from the altitude, find the sine of the altitude and the sine of what the altitude falls short of 90. Then divide the gnomon into as many parts as you like, multiply the sine of the complement of the altitude by the number of parts of the gnomon, and divide the product by the sine of the altitude. The quotient is the amount by which the shadow extends and stretches over the level ground, in the measure in which the gnomon has that number of parts. What the calculators have worked with, and what we have worked with in this book for the amounts of the shadow, is that the gnomon has 12 parts. It may be divided into fewer or more, as the person computing wishes, because the parts of the shadow are related only to the parts of the gnomon: one says that the shadow is so many parts long, in the measure in which the gnomon was made so many.
@n In modern terms the horizontal shadow is g · cot h, where g is the gnomon and h the altitude, and the vertical shadow, described below, is g · tan h.
@p AB01-PDF1120-L05..AB01-PDF1120-L13
If you wish to find the altitude from the horizontal shadow that we have described, multiply the whole [shadow] by itself, and add to the product the parts of the gnomon multiplied by themselves, which, on the basis we have laid down for the computation, is 144, the gnomon having 12 parts. Take the root of the sum: it is the hypotenuse of the shadow triangle. Keep it. Then multiply the parts of the gnomon by the radius; on the basis we have laid down, this always comes to 720. Divide these 720 by the hypotenuse of the shadow triangle that you kept, and find the arc of the quotient; the arc you obtain is the amount of the altitude. If you wish to compute it another way, multiply the shadow by the radius, divide the product by the hypotenuse of the shadow triangle, and find the arc of the quotient. The arc you obtain is the distance of the degree of the sun, or of another degree, from the zenith on the circle of altitude; subtract it from 90, and the remainder is the altitude.
@n «The whole» (*al-kull*) is the whole length of the shadow; Nallino translates «the square of the shadow». The hypotenuse of the shadow triangle is literally «the diameter of the triangle of the shadow» (*quṭr muthallathat al-ẓill*): the line from the tip of the gnomon to the tip of the shadow.
@p AB01-PDF1120-L13..AB01-PDF1120-L17
As for the erect shadow, I mean the vertical shadow, it is the reverse of the horizontal one: it is longest at noon and shortest at sunrise. If you wish to find this shadow from the altitude, multiply the sine of the altitude by the number of parts of the gnomon, and divide the product by the sine of what the altitude falls short of 90; the quotient is the amount of the shadow in parts of the gnomon.
@p AB01-PDF1120-L17..AB01-PDF1120-L22
If you wish to find the altitude from this vertical shadow, multiply the shadow by itself, add to it the product of the parts of the gnomon by themselves, and take the root of the sum: this is the hypotenuse of the shadow triangle. Then, if you wish, multiply the parts of the gnomon by the radius, divide the product by the hypotenuse of the shadow triangle, and find the arc of the quotient. The result is the amount between the degree of the sun and the zenith on the circle of altitude; subtract it from 90, and the remainder is the altitude. Or, if you wish, multiply the shadow by the radius, divide it by the hypotenuse of the shadow triangle, and find the arc of the quotient: the arc you obtain is the altitude.
@p AB01-PDF1120-L22..AB01-PDF1119-L03
If you wish to find either of the two shadows from the altitude with the table, and you want the horizontal shadow, look in the table of the altitude and the shadow, in the column of the altitude, for the altitude you have, and take what stands opposite it in the column of the shadow: that is the amount of the shadow of that altitude. If you wish to find the altitude from this shadow, look for the shadow you want in the column of the digits of the shadow; where you find it, take the degrees of altitude entered opposite it in the column of the altitude: that is the amount of the altitude for that shadow.
@n The digit (*iṣbaʿ*, literally «finger») is a twelfth of the gnomon.
@p AB01-PDF1119-L03..AB01-PDF1119-L11
If there are minutes with the degrees of the altitude, or minutes with the digits of the shadow, find the value by interpolation, as I described to you in the chapter on the declination. That is: if there are minutes with the altitude, see what part of 60 they are, and take the same part of the difference between [the shadows of] the whole degrees and of the degree one greater. Always subtract it from the digits of the shadow of the whole degrees, since every greater altitude has a smaller shadow than a lesser one; the remainder is the shadow of that altitude. If there are minutes with the shadow whose altitude you wish to find, look at the shadow that you find in the table and subtract it from the shadow you have. Find what part the remainder is of the difference between that shadow and the one that belongs to the degree of altitude one less, and take the same part of the 60 minutes by which the column of the altitude increases. Subtract the minutes you obtain from the altitude that you found opposite the shadow you took in the table, the one nearest to your shadow among those smaller than it; the remainder is the altitude.
@p AB01-PDF1119-L11..AB01-PDF1119-L16
If you wish to find the erect shadow, which is the vertical shadow, from the altitude with the table, subtract the altitude from 90 and take the shadow opposite the remainder, interpolating in the same way; the result is the vertical shadow. If you wish to find the altitude from this shadow, look for the shadow you want in the table, take what stands opposite it in the column of the altitude, interpolating, and subtract the result from 90; the remainder is the altitude. The shadow has been entered in this table on the basis that the gnomon is 12 parts, so whatever you work out with it of the shadow is on the basis that the gnomon is 12 digits.
@n The vertical shadow of an altitude is the horizontal shadow of its complement: g · tan h = g · cot(90° − h).
