Chapter 54. On establishing the amounts of the applications as they depend on the latitudes of the stars
@p AB01-PDF0958-L03..AB01-PDF0958-L12
He said: Since the circle of the signs is divided into twelve signs, and nothing measures twelve, among the numbers of which it has an exact part, I mean a fixed ratio, except six twice, four three times, three four times and two six times, these amounts are used and no others. They are four configurations besides the conjunction. Among them is the opposition, which is of six signs and contains two right angles and 180 degrees; the reason for its force is evident in itself. After it comes the configuration of the quadrature, which is half the opposition and contains three signs, one right angle and 90 parts; then the trine, which contains four signs, a right angle and a third of an angle, and 120 degrees; then the sextile, which is half the trine and contains two signs, two thirds of a right angle and 60 degrees. The conjunction, however, is not a configuration. These parts of the sphere of the signs are the ones that share configurations with one another, and no others; signs in this relation to one another are called the connected signs, and the rest, which are not connected with one another and share no configuration, are those that are one sign, five signs or seven signs apart.
@n These are Ptolemy's aspects (*Tetrabiblos* I 14; Nallino). Plato of Tivoli adds signs eleven apart, which are as unconnected as those one sign apart.
@p AB01-PDF0958-L12..AB01-PDF0958-L19
Since the rays of the stars, when they share in the four configurations, meet only at the centre of the earth and come together there, attention to their latitudes in that case is superfluous; but their latitudes need attention mostly at the conjunction alone. For when the meeting of two stars is a bodily meeting and their latitudes are not together on one side and of one amount, their conjunction is not a true conjunction, and the lower of them does not hide the higher; for their conjunction, when it is in the state we have mentioned, takes place in longitude and in latitude, and when they are not on one side and of one amount, their conjunction is in longitude without latitude, and that conjunction is not called a true bodily union, above all when they are on different sides, for then no meeting is counted for them at all. When they are united bodily in longitude, latitude and side, they remain in conjunction until one of them moves away from the other by half of their two bodies.
@n That is, until their centres are apart by half the sum of their diameters. Ptolemy (*Tetrabiblos* I 24) leaves the interval unstated; the two Latin versions of the *Tetrabiblos* made from the Arabic give half the two bodies, as here (Nallino's commentary).
@p AB01-PDF0957-L01..AB01-PDF0957-L03
It is also said of the applications that when stars are going towards the sextile, the quadrature or the opposition of other stars, they are applying to them; when they equal them in the number [of degrees], the application is complete; and when the light ones have passed the heavy ones, they are separating from the heavy ones until they apply to other stars; if they do not apply to others, they are called separating.
@n The bracketed words are supplied by Nallino. The light stars are the faster ones, the heavy the slower.
@p AB01-PDF0957-L03..AB01-PDF0957-L09
Likewise of the lights of the stars and their force in the applications it is said that the force of the sun in the applications reaches 15 degrees before it and as many behind it; the force of the moon reaches 12 degrees before and behind it; likewise the force of Jupiter reaches 12 degrees before and behind it as well; the force of Venus 8 parts before and behind it; the force of Mars 7 parts before and behind it; likewise the force of Mercury 7 parts before and behind it; and likewise the force of Saturn 7 parts before and behind it too. As for the force of the strong amounts, they are the amounts that we mentioned in the chapter on the size of the bodies, with the arcs of the circle of the sphere that their diameters subtend, above all of the upper ones; we have explained this fully before.
@n Ptolemy gives Jupiter 12 degrees and Venus 8 (*Tetrabiblos* III 10); Porphyry gives the sun 15 degrees on each side, the moon 12, Saturn and Jupiter 9, Mars 8, Venus and Mercury 7 (Nallino's commentary). The chapter on the size of the bodies is chapter 50 (Nallino).
@p AB01-PDF0957-L09..AB01-PDF0957-L17
It has also been said that the parts of the signs whose distances from the two solstitial points, the head of Cancer and the head of Capricorn, are equal, on the preceding side and on the following side, look at one another and are equal in force, because the day of each of the two parts is equal to the day of the other. For example, 10 parts of Cancer equal 20 parts of Gemini in force, because the distances of these two parts from the beginning of Cancer are equal and the day of one equals the day of the other; those of these parts that look at one another are called equal in force for this reason. Likewise the parts equally distant from the head of Capricorn are also equal in force: for example, a star at 5 parts of Sagittarius and another star at 25 parts of Capricorn are, at these two distances, equal in force.
@n The parts that look at one another and are equal in force are Ptolemy's (*Tetrabiblos* I 16; Nallino).
@p AB01-PDF0957-L17..AB01-PDF0956-L02
Likewise also the parts whose distances from one of the two equinoctial points are equal, on the preceding side of the signs and on the following side, are called the commanding and the obeying; they are also said to be the high and the low. Those that hear and obey [the others] are the low ones, and those that are obeyed are the high ones. The parts in the southern half of the sphere, from the beginning of Libra to the end of Pisces, are the low ones, and the parts in the northern half of the sphere, from the beginning of Aries to the end of Virgo, are high above those parts; for the amount by which the day increases at these northern parts is the amount by which it decreases at those southern ones, when they are equally distant from one of these two points. For example, 20 degrees of Pisces hear and obey 10 parts of Aries, because the increase of the day at 10 of Aries is equal to the decrease at 20 of Pisces.
@n The commanding and obeying parts are Ptolemy's (*Tetrabiblos* I 15; Nallino). The bracketed words are added for the sense.
@p AB01-PDF0956-L02..AB01-PDF0956-L09
These amounts that we have mentioned in these two kinds may coincide and fall in one of the configurations. So it happens and comes about that the beginning of Sagittarius is connected with the beginning of Aquarius and shares with it the configuration of the sextile, while their distances from the beginning of Capricorn are equal, so that it combines both; likewise the beginning of Pisces shares a configuration with the beginning of Taurus, and the beginning of Pisces hears the beginning of Taurus, so that it combines both as well. This may also happen with the trine, the quadrature and the opposition: the middle of Taurus may be in quadrature with the middle of Leo, the middle of Aquarius in quadrature with the middle of Taurus, the beginning of Taurus in trine with the beginning of Virgo, the beginning of Capricorn in trine with the beginning of Taurus, and the head of Cancer in opposition to the head of Capricorn, while the distances of these parts from the solstitial point and the equinoctial point are equal; and likewise the beginning of Aries is opposite the beginning of Libra.
@n The beginnings of Capricorn and of Taurus are in trine, but they are not equally distant from a solstice or an equinox; the pairs in trine that are equally distant are the beginnings of Taurus and Virgo, of Leo and Sagittarius, of Scorpio and Pisces, and of Aquarius and Gemini. Nallino's translation has the same example. The other examples hold.
@p AB01-PDF0956-L10..AB01-PDF0956-L17
The wandering stars may also apply to the fixed stars, when there is between them the distance of a sextile, a trine, a quadrature or an opposition; likewise the wandering and the fixed stars cast their rays on the circle of the sphere of the signs at different amounts, which increase and decrease with the difference of the latitudes, so that when the amount between the two stars is known, it is known whether they are in one of the configurations of the applications. As for the fixed stars, because of the slowness of their motion no account is taken in these configurations of the application of the wandering stars to them, nor of the casting of their rays on the circle of the signs, their distance from the circle of the signs being always the same; but one looks to the configurations that they have with them at the cardines, and to the bodily union, above all with the sun. As for the wandering stars, one needs to know their distances from one another and the amounts at which they cast their rays on the circle of the signs according to their latitudes, at nativities and in the directions from one to another.
@n «Their distance from the circle of the signs being always the same» renders «when their distance from the circle of the signs is one distance»; Nallino's translation has «when they are distant from it by some aspect». The directions are the astrologers' way of timing events by the arc through which a point of the horoscope is carried to the place or the ray of another star.
@p AB01-PDF0956-L17..AB01-PDF0955-L01
As for the opposition, it is evident that it is complete only if the two stars are both on the circle of the signs, or the latitude of each of the two stars is equal to that of the other and they are on different sides. If one of the two stars is on the circle of the signs and the other is inclined from it in latitude, the distance between them is then less than the distance of the opposition by the latitude of the star; and if the latitudes of the two stars are one latitude on one side, the distance between them is less than the opposition by the two latitudes together. As for the quadrature that falls from any of the stars on the circle of the signs, it is always in one state, neither more nor less than 90, whether the latitude is great or small; this is evident on the sphere on whose poles the circles fall. As for the sextile, if the star has a latitude, its ray is cast on the circle of the signs at less than 60 parts from the part in which it is, and its ray of the trine is cast at more than 120 by as much as the sextile falls short.
@n The quadrature stays 90° because the two points of the ecliptic 90° from the star are the poles of the great circle drawn through the star at right angles to the ecliptic, and so are 90° from its foot on the ecliptic as well; the rays of 60° and 120° lie on one great circle and meet the ecliptic at two opposite points, so that the sextile and the trine on the ecliptic together make 180° (Schiaparelli, in Nallino's commentary). Ibn Yūnus objects that by al-Battānī's principles a star with a latitude of 60° has no sextile at all; the astrologers, however, use the rays of the planets only, whose latitudes are far smaller (Nallino's commentary).
@p AB01-PDF0955-L01..AB01-PDF0955-L21
If you wish to know at how many parts a star that has a latitude casts its ray of the sextile and of the trine on the circle of the signs, subtract the latitude of the star from 90 and find the chord of the remainder in the tables of halved chords: it is always the full chord of the second side, which we have mentioned in the chapter on the diagonals of quadrilaterals earlier in this book, and in this chapter in which we are, at this place only. Keep it: it is the chord of the second side. Then find the full chord of the latitude of the star, by taking half its latitude, finding its halved chord and doubling it: the result is the full chord of the latitude of the star. Multiply it by itself and keep the product under its name. Then take the full chord of the second side that you kept and multiply it by 60; to the product add this product of the chord by itself that you kept under its name, and take the root of the sum. Of what results take what exceeds 60, multiply it by itself, and divide the product by the full chord of the second side that you kept; subtract what results from the division from 60: the remainder is the corrected chord. Keep it. Then take the excess of the root over 60 once more, a second time, multiply it by the full chord of the second side that was kept, and divide the product by the corrected chord: the result is the share of the correction. Keep it. Then take the full chord of the latitude of the star multiplied by itself and subtract it from 3600, which is the full chord of the sextile multiplied by itself; take the root of the remainder, and from the root that results subtract the share of the correction that you kept: the remainder is the corrected second side. Note it. Then subtract the full chord of the latitude multiplied by itself from 3600 once more, and divide the remainder by the corrected second side: the result is the chord that you want. Turn it into an arc as you turn full chords into arcs, by taking half of it and finding its arc in the table; double the arc that comes out: the arc that results is the amount of the star's sextile, on whichever side its latitude is. Subtract it from 180: the remainder is the amount of the star's trine. Subtract each of these two amounts from the part of the star, and add each of them to the part of the star as well, and note where the part of the star comes after the addition or the subtraction: the diminished position is the position of its first sextile and trine, and the increased position is the position of the second trine and sextile, from which they fall on the circle of the signs, if God wills.
@n The translation reads «التي» for «الني» (p. 197, line 13), a misprint that the corrigenda do not list. The chapter on the diagonals of quadrilaterals is chapter 26 (Nallino). The exact amount x of the sextile for a latitude β follows from the right spherical triangle whose hypotenuse is the ray of 60° and whose legs are the latitude and x: cos x = cos 60° / cos β. In the terms of chapter 26, with chords in a circle of radius 1, crd² x = (crd² 60° − crd² β) / cos β. Nallino takes the long rule of the text for the work of an interpolator and restores this one as al-Battānī's; the Alfonsine *Libro de las taulas* gives it as al-Battānī's method (Nallino's commentary). The rule of the text begins with the chord of the second side that an arc of 60° would give, 60 cos β, and ends with the theorem of chapter 26, dividing 3600 − crd² β by a corrected second side. Read as the Arabic has it, the rule is not exact but close: it falls short of the exact amount by less than 1″ at a latitude of 5°, by 13″ at 10° and by 3′ at 20°. Nallino's translation squares the excess of the root over 60 a second time in finding the share of the correction, where the Arabic takes the excess itself; with the square the rule falls short by 7′ at 5° and by 17′ at 10°.
