Chapter 24. On finding the distance of a star from the equator, and the part that culminates with it, from the parts of the signs that rise or set [with it] and from the amplitude of its rising and setting on the horizon circle
@p AB01-PDF1098-L06..AB01-PDF1098-L10
Al-Battānī said: Suppose you wish to find which degree of the signs culminates with a star, and the distance of the star from the equator, from the azimuth of its rising or setting and the part of the ecliptic that rises or sets with it, when these are known from some measurements. Multiply the sine of the altitude of the beginning of Aries in that locality by the sine of the azimuth of the rising or setting of the star, divide the product by the radius, and find the arc of the quotient. The arc you obtain is the distance of the star from the equator, on the side of the azimuth.
@n In modern terms sin δ = cos φ · sin A, where A is the amplitude; this is the rule of chapter 7 solved for the declination.
@p AB01-PDF1098-L10..AB01-PDF1098-L15
With it find half the arc of its day, in the way described for finding half the arc of the day of a star from its distance from the equator. Then look: if the star is on the eastern horizon, add half the arc of its day to the times of ascension, in the clime, of the degree that rises with it; if it is on the western horizon, subtract half the arc of its day from the ascensions of the part with which it itself sets. Enter what you obtain, in either case, in the ascensions of the right sphere and take the degree of the signs opposite it: that is the part that culminates with the star.
@n The rule for the eastern horizon is exact, with the ascensions in the right sphere counted from the beginning of Capricorn (Nallino). The rule for the western horizon is wrong as it stands: the ascension to be taken is that of the counterpart of the part that sets with the star, as in chapter 20, since that counterpart rises when the star sets. With the setting part itself the result is wrong by 180° plus twice the ascensional difference of that part. Nallino's commentary states the rule as printed, without remark.
