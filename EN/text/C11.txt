Chapter 11. On finding the azimuth of the altitude and of the shadow on the horizon circle, in any locality you want, at all times
@p AB01-PDF1119-L19..AB01-PDF1118-L07
Al-Battānī said: If you wish to find the azimuth of the altitude and of the shadow at any degree of all the signs, in any locality you want, take the declination of that degree and find its sine, and the direction of the declination. Then subtract this declination from 90 and find the sine of the remainder: this is the sine of the complement of the declination of the degree. Then find the sine of the latitude of the locality and the sine of what the latitude of the locality falls short of 90. Then take the altitude at whatever time of the day you like, and find its sine and the sine of what the altitude falls short of 90. Then multiply the sine of the declination of the degree by the radius and divide the product by the sine of the complement of the latitude of the locality: the result is the sine of the amplitude of the rising. Keep it, and note its direction, which is the direction of the declination. Then multiply the sine of the altitude by the sine of the latitude of the locality and divide the product by the sine of the complement of the latitude of the locality: the result is the sine of the difference of the horizon, which is always southern. If the sine of the amplitude of the rising and the sine of the difference of the horizon are in the same direction, add them; if they are in different directions, subtract the smaller from the greater and note the direction of the remainder. Multiply what you obtain from the addition or the subtraction by the radius, divide it by the sine of the complement of the altitude, and find the arc of the quotient: the result is the azimuth of the altitude.
@n In modern terms, sin Z = (sin δ / cos φ − sin h · tan φ) / cos h, where Z is the azimuth counted from the east or west point, positive towards the north, δ the declination, φ the latitude and h the altitude. sin δ / cos φ is the sine of the amplitude of the rising, and sin h · tan φ, always southern, is what al-Battānī calls the sine of the difference of the horizon.
@p AB01-PDF1118-L07..AB01-PDF1118-L09
The shadow is then at the degree for which you computed. If at that time the degree is between the ascendant and the middle of the sky, this azimuth is counted on the horizon circle from the rising point of the beginning of Aries and of Libra towards the direction that you obtained.
@n The codex gives only the case of the eastern half of the sky. Plato of Tivoli's Latin shows that words are missing after «direction»; Nallino restores them in Arabic in the apparatus, and the Latin translation includes them: «[the direction] in which you found the two sines, or the greater of them if they differ; and if the degree is between the middle of the sky and the setting point, this azimuth is counted from the setting point of the beginning of Aries and of Libra on the horizon circle, towards that direction».
