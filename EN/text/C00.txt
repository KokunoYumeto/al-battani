In the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate. God bless the Prophet Muḥammad, God's noble Messenger, and the Prophet's family and companions, and grant them peace.
@p AB01-PDF1151-L03..AB01-PDF1151-L04
This is the whole of what Muḥammad ibn Jābir ibn Sinān al-Ḥarrānī, known as al-Battānī — may God pardon them — set down on the computation of the stars and on the places of their courses as they have been tested. The book contains fifty-seven chapters in all, and this is an account of them.
@n The heading gives the author's name as «Muḥammad ibn Jābir ibn Sinān»; the title page that Nallino set above it has «ibn Sinān ibn Jābir». «Tested» (*al-mumtaḥan*) is the word al-Battānī uses for what has been verified by observation.

The opening of the book.

On dividing the circle of the sphere, and on multiplying degrees by one another, taking their roots, and dividing them by one another.

On finding the lengths of the chords of the parts of the circle, on entering in tables the halves of the chords of double the arcs, and on the procedures with them that follow from this.
@n The halves of the chords of double the arcs are the sines; chapter 3 introduces them under this name.

On the amount of the inclination of the ecliptic to the equator; on the division of this inclination, its directions, and its stages as it rises and descends. It is the declination of the sun from the right sphere.
@n The print has «ونجزية»; Nallino's corrigenda read «وتجزية», «the division», which is translated.
@li AB01-PDF1151-L11..AB01-PDF1151-L13 | 5
On finding the amounts of the equator that rise together with given arcs of the ecliptic [in places] under the equator, which is called the line of equality. By these same amounts, too, the signs pass over the meridian circle in every place on the earth; this is why they are called the ascensions of the signs in the right sphere.
@n Nallino suspects that the words «in the localities that are» have fallen out before «under the equator»; the bracket supplies their sense. «The line of equality» (*khaṭṭ al-istiwāʾ*) is the terrestrial equator.

On finding the amplitudes of the winter and summer risings and settings on the horizon circles of the localities. These are the arcs of the horizon between the equator and the places of the ecliptic, and they are called the azimuths of the risings and settings on the horizon circle.

@li AB01-PDF1150-L10..AB01-PDF1150-L12 | 11
On finding the azimuth of the altitude and of the shadow on the horizon circle, in every locality, at every time of the day, for all degrees of the ecliptic. This is the part of the horizon circle, counted from the limit of rising and setting, that is cut off by the arc passing through the zenith and the sun.
@li AB01-PDF1150-L13..AB01-PDF1150-L14 | 12
