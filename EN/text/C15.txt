Chapter 15. On finding the altitude of the sun at noon on any day you want
@p AB01-PDF1108-L06..AB01-PDF1108-L11
Al-Battānī said: If you wish to find the altitude of the sun at noon on any day you want, find the declination of the degree in which the sun is. If it is northern, subtract it from the latitude of the locality; if it is southern, add it to it. Subtract the latitude of the locality, after the addition or the subtraction, from 90; the remainder is the altitude of the sun at noon. If the declination is greater than the latitude of the locality, you know that the sun is to the north of the zenith: add 90 to the latitude of the locality and subtract the declination of the degree; the remainder is equal to the altitude above the northern horizon.
@p AB01-PDF1108-L11..AB01-PDF1108-L14
If you wish to find the altitude of the sun at noon in another way, subtract the latitude of the locality from 90; the remainder is the altitude of the beginning of Aries. If the declination is northern, add it to the altitude [of the beginning of Aries]; if it is southern, subtract it from it. The altitude of the beginning of Aries after the addition or the subtraction is the altitude of the sun at noon. If it exceeds 90, subtract it from 180; the remainder is the altitude above the northern horizon.
@n The words in brackets are supplied by Nallino; the codex lacks them.
