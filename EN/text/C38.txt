Chapter 38. On knowing the latitude of the moon from the belt of the signs, and its directions
@p AB01-PDF1039-L09..AB01-PDF1038-L02
Al-Battānī said: If you wish to know the latitude of the moon, which is its distance from the belt of the signs, subtract the computed position of the head from the computed true position of the moon: the remainder is the argument of the latitude. Or, if you wish, add to the true position of the moon the mean position of the head; if the result is more than a circle, cast off a circle from it, and what results, after that or before it, is the argument of the latitude; the meaning is the same in the two ways. When you know the argument of the latitude, whichever of the two ways it was found, enter with it in the two columns of numbers of the tables of the equation of the moon and take what stands opposite it in the sixth column, headed «latitude of the moon»: the result is its latitude at that time. If you wish to know it by computation, take the sine of the argument of the latitude, multiply it by 5 parts 13 minutes, which are the sine of the whole latitude, and divide the product by the radius; find the arc of the quotient, and the arc that results is the latitude of the moon. When you know the latitude of the moon, whichever of the two ways it was found, look: if the argument of the latitude is from 0 to 180, the latitude is on the northern side of the ecliptic, and if it is from 180 to 360, the latitude is on the southern side.
@n The mean position of the head is the motion taken from the tables, before it is subtracted from 360 (chapter 37). The columns are counted without the columns of numbers (Nallino). 5;13 parts are the sine of 5 degrees, the greatest latitude of the moon (5;13,46 in al-Battānī's table of sines; Nallino); Plato of Tivoli has 5;30. In modern terms the rule is sin β = sin 5° · sin u, with u the argument of the latitude.

If you wish to know whether it is ascending or descending on its side, look: if the argument of the latitude is from 0 to 90, the moon is increasing in latitude and ascending in the north; if it is from 90 to 180, it is decreasing in latitude and descending from the north; from 180 to 270 it is increasing in latitude and descending in the south; and from 270 to 360 it is decreasing in latitude and ascending from the south. In short, when the moon leaves the head it is northern until it reaches the tail, and when it passes the tail it is southern until it reaches the head, because from the node of the head its passage is towards the north and from the node of the tail its passage is towards the south, if God wills.
