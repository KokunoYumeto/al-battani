Chapter 46. On knowing the retrogradation of the wandering stars
@p AB01-PDF0979-L09..AB01-PDF0979-L19
Al-Battānī said: If you wish to know the retrogradation of one of the wandering stars and its direct motion, enter with the centre of the star corrected by the third column into the two columns of numbers, which go in steps of six parts, in the tables of the retrogradation of the stars, and take what stands opposite it in the column of the star that you want: write down what is in the first column as the first station, and what is in the second column as the second station. When you know the first and the second station, look: if the corrected anomaly of the star is less than the second station and more than the first station, the star is retrograde; if it is more than the second station or less than the first, it is direct in its course. If the star is retrograde and you wish to know how long ago it turned back, subtract the first station from the corrected anomaly and divide the remainder by the course of the anomaly of the star in a day, which is: Saturn 0;57, Jupiter 0;54, Mars 0;28, Venus 0;37, Mercury 3;6. What results is the days since it turned back. If you wish to know in how many days it will be direct, subtract the corrected anomaly from the second station and follow this procedure with the remainder. Likewise, if you wish to know when it will turn back while it is direct, subtract its corrected anomaly from the first station and proceed with the remainder in the same way.
@n The daily motions in anomaly are Ptolemy's, rounded to the minute (*Almagest* IX 3; Nallino); the codex has 0;36 for Venus.
