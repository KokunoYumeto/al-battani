Chapter 51. On knowing by observation the motion of the fixed stars, which move in their sphere or whose sphere moves with them, and on knowing the positions of those of them that are needed from the tables
@p AB01-PDF0965-L04..AB01-PDF0965-L11
Al-Battānī said: The states of the fixed stars in their rising, their setting, their culmination and their stay above the earth and below it, and the state of those of them that are near the pole or far from it in every locality, we have mentioned before in this book. The fixed stars revolve about the two poles of the ecliptic and have not departed from it since a motion of theirs became known; likewise their latitude is one latitude, from which they do not change, and they are in this state as long as no change in their motion has been known. Likewise the distances between them have stayed in one state since they were observed, and for that reason they are called the fixed stars: what is meant is that their distances are fixed. For their motion is all together one motion, as if they were in one sphere: either they move together in it, or it moves them by its motion and carries them from the azimuth of one place of the sphere to the azimuth of another place of the sphere, from the west to the east, like the other motions of the running stars, which are the seven planets.
@p AB01-PDF0965-L11..AB01-PDF0965-L17
We found their positions in longitude and latitude in the book of Ptolemy for the first year of the reign of Antoninus, which is the year 886 of the reign of Nabonassar. One of the observations of them on which Ptolemy worked was the observation that Menelaus reported from the year 845 of the reign of that Nabonassar. Menelaus reported that the northern one of the stars between the two eyes of the Scorpion was in that year, when examined with the moon with the armillary sphere, at 5 parts and 55 minutes of Scorpio; and by what Ptolemy set down in the book, the Heart of the Lion must have been in that year at 2 parts and a sixth of a part of Leo, and the Yemeni Shiʿrā at 17 degrees of Gemini.
@n Ptolemy (*Almagest* VII 4) gives the year 885 of Nabonassar for the first year of Antoninus, 137-138; aṣ-Ṣūfī also has 886 (Nallino). Menelaus observed the star, β Scorpii, at Rome in 98 (*Almagest* VII 3; Nallino's commentary). The Heart of the Lion is Regulus, the Yemeni Shiʿrā Sirius.
@p AB01-PDF0965-L17..AB01-PDF0964-L14
We observed these stars that we have mentioned, and other fixed stars, many times in successive years. One of the observations on which we rely was in the year 1191 of the years of Dhū l-Qarnayn: we observed with the moon and with the passage of the stars through the middle of the sky, and so found their distances from the equator and the parts of the signs that culminate with them; then from that we found at which parts of the signs their positions fall in longitude, and their latitudes from the circle of the signs on the side on which we found them. We found the northern one of the stars between the eyes of the Scorpion at 17;45 of Scorpio, the Yemeni Shiʿrā at 28;50 of Gemini, and the Heart of the Lion at 14 of Leo. The year in which we made this observation was the year 1627 of the reign of Nabonassar mentioned. When we divide these 11 parts and 50 minutes between their first positions and the positions they had reached at the time of our observation by the 782 years between the two observations, we find their motion to be one degree in every 66 solar years; so we have set it down in the table of the motions of the fixed stars, which are found from it by the collected and the single years and the months. Likewise we have added these 11 parts and half a third to the positions that we found set down in the book of Ptolemy, and set down their positions in the year 1191 of the years of Dhū l-Qarnayn. In what we observed of many stars we found no evident variation in latitude, except what is of no account and can be passed over. So we have made tables for them, in which we have set down their positions in longitude and latitude, the side and the magnitude, so that the positions that they reach after this year are known from their motions taken from the tables, when these are added to their positions in the year 1191; and likewise their positions before this year are known by what we have said.
@n The year 1191 began on 1 September 879 (Nallino). 17;45 − 5;55, 28;50 − 17 and 14 − 2;10 are each 11;50. 11;50 in 782 years is 54.48″ a year, and one degree in 66 years 54″ 33‴ (Nallino's commentary). «Half a third», 11;10, is Nallino's reading; the codex and Plato of Tivoli have «a half and a third», 11;50. Al-Battānī took 37′ from the 11;50 for the 41 years between Menelaus and Ptolemy's catalogue, at al-Battānī's own rate; but Ptolemy, at a rate of 36″ a year, had added only 25′ to the longitudes of Menelaus, so that 11;25, not 11;10, should have been added to Ptolemy's longitudes; aṣ-Ṣūfī avoided the error (Nallino's commentary).
@p AB01-PDF0964-L14..AB01-PDF0964-L20
The stars that Ptolemy mentions in the book of the *Almagest* are one thousand and twenty-two in number, besides the stars of the Lock, the Solitary One and al-Mirzam. Ptolemy arranged their size in six degrees, the greatest of which is the first magnitude and the smallest the sixth, and stated that these stars make up forty-five figures: of them fifteen constellations are on the southern side, and six constellations of the southern signs, which are Libra, Scorpio, Sagittarius, Capricorn, Aquarius and Pisces; on the northern side eighteen constellations, and the six remaining constellations of the signs. Their sides may change over a long time, so that the northern ones become southern and the southern northern.
@n Ptolemy has forty-eight constellations, twenty-one of them north of the zodiac; Nallino takes the forty-five and the eighteen of the text for errors. The Lock is Coma Berenices, which Ptolemy leaves out of the count of 1022; the Solitary One is α Hydrae; al-Mirzam is a name the Arabs give to several stars that precede a bright one (Nallino, after aṣ-Ṣūfī).
@p AB01-PDF0964-L20..AB01-PDF0963-L03
Of these northern stars in the constellations of the signs and others are: the stars of Aries, which are 13, among them the two Sharaṭ, on its horns, and al-Buṭayn, on its rump; then the stars of Taurus, 33, the Pleiades on its horn and al-Dabarān on its back; then Gemini, whose stars are 18, among them the front of the two arms, al-Haqʿa and al-Hanʿa; then Cancer, whose stars are 9, among them al-Nathra; then Leo, whose stars are 27, among them the Lock, al-Ṭarf, and al-Jabha, which is the Heart of the Lion, al-Zubra and al-Ṣarfa; then Virgo, whose stars are 26, among them al-ʿAwwāʾ and the unarmed Simāk. This is what is in the northern half of the constellations of the signs.
@n The named stars, apart from the Lock, are lunar mansions of the Arabs, the twenty-eight stations of the moon that divide the zodiac (Nallino's commentary). Aṣ-Ṣūfī finds several of the placements wrong: al-Buṭayn is three stars in a triangle on the belly of the Ram, and al-Dabarān is the bright red star in the southern eye of the Bull (Nallino's commentary).
@p AB01-PDF0963-L03..AB01-PDF0963-L15
In the southern side of the constellations of the signs are Libra, whose stars are 8, among them al-Ghafr; then Scorpio, which has 21 stars, among them the two Zubānā, al-Iklīl, the Heart and al-Shawla; then Sagittarius, which has 31 stars, among them al-Naʿāʾim and al-Balda; then Capricorn, which has 28 stars, among them Saʿd al-Dhābiḥ and Saʿd Bulaʿ; then Aquarius, which has 42 stars, among them Saʿd al-Suʿūd and Saʿd al-Akhbiya; then Pisces, which has 34 stars, among them the front Spout, the rear Spout [and al-Rishāʾ]. All the stars in the constellations of the signs are three hundred and forty-six; all the stars in the eighteen northern constellations named in the table are three hundred and sixty; all the stars in these fifteen southern constellations are three hundred and sixteen. All these stars counted are one thousand and twenty-two: of them fifteen are of the first magnitude, 45 of the second, 208 of the third, 474 of the fourth, 217 of the fifth and 49 of the sixth; the cloudy ones, like clouds, are five, and the dark ones 9; and the stars of the Lock, the Solitary One and al-Mirzam. Of these stars we have set down those for which we found a known temperament in the book of Ptolemy, of the constellations outside the circle of the signs, above all the great ones; for those in the constellations of the signs we have mentioned their temperament and their force, resembling the force of the two luminaries and the wandering stars.
@n The bracketed words follow Nallino's emendanda (Part II, p. XXXI), which add «والرِّشَاء», «and al-Rishāʾ», after «the rear Spout» (p. 189, line 7). With them the stars named in the twelve signs, apart from the Lock, are the twenty-eight lunar mansions of the Arabs; al-Rishāʾ, the Rope, is the last. The stars counted in the twelve signs come to 290, not 346, since the stars outside the figures are not counted among them (Nallino). 346 + 360 + 316 = 1022, and 15 + 45 + 208 + 474 + 217 + 49 + 5 + 9 = 1022. Nallino takes «temperament» here for the data that fix a star's place, so that al-Battānī listed the stars whose places were not in doubt in the Arabic copies of the *Almagest* (Nallino's commentary).
@p AB01-PDF0963-L15..AB01-PDF0962-L07
After that we have set down, for those of the first and the second magnitude and some of the third, separate tables, in which we give their distances from the equator, half their stay above the earth, their altitude in the middle of the sky, and the parts of the signs with which they rise, culminate and set, where the altitude of the northern pole above the horizon is 36 parts, which is the latitude of the city of al-Raqqa; we have given these states of theirs described for the year 1211 of the years of Dhū l-Qarnayn. In the tables before these we have set down their positions in longitude from the beginning of Aries. If you wish to know the position of any of the fixed stars set down in the tables that you wish, take their motion in the years that come after the year 1191 of the years of Dhū l-Qarnayn, add it to their positions set down in the tables, and cast that off from the beginning of Aries: where the count ends for you, there is the position of the star in the sign to which it comes. Its latitude is that set down in the table, on the side set down, and so too its magnitude among the six. Likewise you know the parts with which they rise, set and culminate from the later tables, by taking what is in each of the three columns and casting it off from the beginning of Aries; after this year for which we have set down these states in these tables, you add to what is in the tables as much as is due, since there is much change, whose computation must be carried out anew at every time. We have set it down for our time only so that it may be known in it approximately; the method by which these things are known is explained earlier in this book. From these tables the nine configurations mentioned that the stars have with the sun are known in our time, above all for the great ones, which are those set down in the tables.
@n The year 1211 began on 1 September 899; the codex has 1251 (Nallino).
