⟨11⟩ On knowing the events that occur in the countries from eclipses
@n *Tetrabiblos* II.4–8.
@p AB03-F026v-L13..AB03-F027r-L09
We begin by examining the place of the eclipse in the sphere of the signs: to which of the triplicities it belongs, and to which of the regions that triplicity is attributed. Then we turn to the countries and cities that are akin to [the sign of] the eclipse in particular, in that triplicity, as we have set out, and see whether the eclipse is seen in all those places or in some of them; for in those of them where the eclipse [does not] appear nothing happens. We set up the ascendant of the middle of the eclipse and its cardines for the places among those countries where we know that it is seen, reckoning the hours and the altitude of the pole there. After that we infer the time of the event and the length of its duration by seeing how many equal hours there are from the beginning of the darkening of the eclipse to the end of the clearing in each of those places. If the eclipse is solar, the event lasts as many years as there are hours; if it is lunar, as many months. If the place of the eclipse is on the eastern side of the horizon, we know that the event begins in the first third of that period and that its greatest intensity falls in that first third; if it is on the side of the middle of the sky, we infer that it is in the second third of the period; and if on the western side of the horizon, in its last third. We determine the place of the eclipse relative to the horizon as I shall describe to you. If the place of the eclipse is less than 4 seasonal hours from the ascendant towards the middle of the sky, it is on the eastern side; if it is past the 4 hours, up to the cardine of the sky and 2 seasonal hours beyond it, it is on the side of the middle of the sky; and if it is past that, it is on the western side. In this you proceed on the basis that if it is in the very degree of the ascendant, [the event] is at the beginning of the period; if it is in the degree of the middle of the sky, in the middle of the period; and if it is in the very degree of the setting point, the event is at the end of the period.
@n «[Does not]» supplies a negation that the sense requires: Ptolemy considers the countries where the eclipse is visible (*Tetrabiblos* II.5–6). The division of the sky by seasonal hours is al-Battānī's addition.
@p AB03-F027r-L09..AB03-F027r-L18
The particular increases and decreases in the events are known from the conjunctions and oppositions that occur in the meantime, if they happen in the places that are the cause of the events or in places that share in a configuration with them, I mean the places of the eclipse and of the cardines and the places that share in a configuration with them; and from the positions of the very wandering stars that are in charge of the governing, when they appear eastern or western, or stationary, or rising at the beginning of the night, and share in a configuration with the signs of the eclipse or the signs of the cardines, which are the cause of the events. For if they are eastern or stationary, an increase of the events comes from them; and if they are western, or under the rays, and rising at the beginning of the night [and] retrograde, a decrease of the events comes from them. Look also at the decrease of the upper planets and their staying in each of the times, I mean the stations of Saturn, Jupiter and Mars, when they are stationary and are the lords of the indication through their dominion over the places of the eclipses and of the cardines, in the way that we shall mention.
@p AB03-F027r-L18..AB03-F027v-L13
The class in which the event occurs is known from the conditions and the forms proper to the signs in which the eclipses occur, and in which are the wandering and the non-wandering stars that govern the sign of the eclipse; and from the governing star, and the sign of the cardine that precedes the eclipse. This cardine, which the eclipsed luminary passes before the time of the eclipse, is either the ascending cardine or the cardine of the middle of the sky, whichever it happens to pass before the eclipse. The governor of these two places among the wandering stars is known as I shall describe to you. Look for the planet that has many shares in these two signs that we have mentioned: either it is conjoined with the degree of the eclipse or the degree of the cardine, or is coming to it, or has just left it (?); or it shares in a configuration with it by one of the connections, I mean the trine, the sextile, the quadrature, the opposition and the like; [and look] at the dignities it has in the signs, by being the lord of the house, of the exaltation, of the triplicity and of the terms. If all this comes together in one planet, it alone is the lord of the governing. If no single planet is found that is the lord both of the eclipse and of the cardine, one should take together the two planets that have the most familiarities with each of the two places, as said before, choosing first the sign of the eclipse and putting it first. If the planets that have shares in these two places are more than two, and what each of them has in the two places is equal to what the other has, we choose for the governing, and put first, the one of them that has the better claim to be said to be in the cardine, or is stronger in action, or is stronger in that domain.
@p AB03-F027v-L13..AB03-F028r-L01
We know the governors among the fixed stars as well. We begin with the star that was with the place of the eclipse in the cardine that came before the eclipse: we mean, among the bright stars, the star that rose with the degree of the eclipse or culminated with the degree of the eclipse, in whichever of these two cardines one of the luminaries passed before its eclipse — we mean the great stars — […] on people and on kings. Likewise, if at the time of the eclipse they are in the places that Ptolemy described, in the east in particular, they signify that the event will be in the fruits, in the age of youth, in foundations and in whoever is young in age; if they are in the middle of the sky above the earth, they signify that the event will be in the temples, in the kings and in those of middle age; and if they are in the west, they signify that the event will be in the matter of the customs and their changes, and in those of old age, and the dead (?).
@n Something seems to be lost before «on people and on kings»: Ptolemy's account of the classes signified by the forms of the signs ends with the bicorporeal signs, which concern people and kings (*Tetrabiblos* II.7). «Customs» reads السنن for السنين, «the years».
@p AB03-F028r-L02..AB03-F028r-L12
The size of the part of the class concerned in which the event occurs is known from the amount of the darkening of the eclipse and from the relation of the planets in charge of the governing to the place of the eclipses. When they are in configurations attributed to the evenings at eclipses of the sun, or in configurations attributed to the mornings at eclipses of the moon, the events are in the smaller part of that class; if they are in opposition, on the diameter, the event concerns half of that class; and if they are in configurations attributed to the mornings at eclipses of the sun, or attributed to the evenings at eclipses of the moon, it is in the greater part of that class. Whether what happens is good or the opposite is known from the nature of the governing, chief planets that bring about the event, from their mixture with one another and from the places in which they are. For the sun and the moon are two princes and chiefs of the other stars, in that they are the cause of all the action, the cause of the governing of the stars, and the cause of the strength and weakness of the governing stars.
@p AB03-F028r-L12..AB03-F028v-L07
The consideration of the mixture of the planets in charge of the governing with one another is what shows the condition of the events that occur. Saturn, when it alone is the lord of the governing, is in general the cause of corruption that comes from cold. As for its particular action proper to it: if the event is in people, it produces long illnesses, consumption, wasting, harm from humours and the flowing of superfluities, quartan fevers, flight, perplexity, imprisonment, grief, fear and death, especially for those advanced in age. If the event is in the beasts that people use much, the harm to them is their scarcity: corruption of their bodies and diseases befall those of them that exist, so that, because of that, something like these illnesses befalls the people who eat these beasts. If the event is in the air, there comes severe cold with frost and fog, heat (?) that brings pestilence, badness of the air, thickness of the clouds, gloom and destructive snowstorms, and from this many vermin are generated that are harmful to the nature of people. If the indication is in the rivers and the seas, there comes, in what they have in common, wintry weather, the sinking of ships, difficulty in travelling on the water, and scarcity and corruption of fish; in the seas in particular ebb and flood, and in the rivers in particular excessive rising and badness of the waters. If the events are in the fruits that grow from the earth, they suffer want, scarcity and corruption, especially those that people need of necessity, and the corruption that befalls them comes from caterpillars or from locusts, or from floods, or from heavy rains, or from hail and the like, so that at times drought comes of it, and people perish because of it.
@p AB03-F028v-L07..AB03-F028v-L17
Jupiter, when it alone is the lord of the governing, in general makes things grow. Its particular actions in what happens are, in people, elevation of rank, good condition, plenty, a state of safety and security, the growth of necessary things, the good condition of bodies and souls, rewards and gifts from kings, the increase of the affairs of kings, their greatness and their magnanimity; in general it is the cause of good fortunes. In beasts, if the event is in them, those that people use much multiply and increase greatly, and those of the opposite kind decay and perish. As for its indication in the air, [the air] is temperate, healthful, windy and moist, and it makes the fruits that grow from the earth increase; the passage of ships at sea is safe, the risings of rivers are moderate, and all things like these. Whenever its indication of the event is in one of the kinds named, it sets it right, and what comes of it is good, moderate, agreeable, praiseworthy and conducive to well-being.
@p AB03-F028v-L17..AB03-F029r-L10
Mars, when it alone is the lord of the governing, is in general the cause of the corruption that comes from dryness. Its particular actions: if the event is in people, wars arise among them, and seditions between a people and those of their own kind, captivity and slavery, the uprising of the mass of the people and the wrath of the leaders; from these things sudden death befalls some people, and illnesses with tertian fevers and the spitting of blood, and swift and violent death, especially for those in the age of the prime. Likewise some suffer robbery, contempt, aggression, lawlessness, arson, the killing of men, plunder and highway robbery. If the event is in the air, there come severe heat, hot, pestilential and harmful winds, the falling of thunderbolts and of the thunderbolt that is called *prēstēr*, and scarcity of rain. If it is at sea, there comes the sudden sinking of ships through changeable and turbulent winds, through thunderbolts and the like, thunder, burning and what resembles them. In the rivers there come a decrease of the water, the drying up of springs and the corruption of drinking water. In the animals and plants that are fit for the use of people there comes scarcity, and the corruption of the fruits, which is produced by burning «احرًّا ىاها», or by the pestilence of locusts, or by the scattering that the winds bring upon them, or by their burning in the stores in which they are kept.
@n *Prēstēr* renders فريطس, the Greek πρηστήρ, a fiery whirlwind; Robbins translates «hurricanes».
@p AB03-F029r-L10..AB03-F029r-L20
Venus in general produces something like what Jupiter produces, and in what comes from it there is, besides, a stirring of matters of intercourse, of amusement and of the affairs of women. Its particular action, in people, is elevation of rank, joy, good condition, the soundness of marriage, many children, the success of all matters of union, the increase of possessions, clean and pious conduct, goodness of conduct in matters of religion, health of the bodies, connection with leaders, and marriage alliances with governors. In the condition of the air temperate, moist and very nourishing winds come, and the air is good and clear; the fertilizing waters and the rains it makes abundant; ships come through safely at sea; and from it come successes in trade, profits, and the rising and fullness of the rivers. The animals that people use, and the fruits of the earth in particular, increase, flourish and are of use; and whenever the event falls in any kind of these kinds, its condition is praiseworthy and useful to people.
@p AB03-F029r-L20..AB03-F029v-L12
Mercury, when it alone is the lord of the governing: in general, when it mixes with any of the other planets, it is akin to their natures (?). Its proper action is to set things in motion, more than the other planets. Its action in the events concerning people is sharp, strong and subtle in contriving the thing it aims at; it is a maker of highway robbery, thefts, brigandage and raids, and it also causes difficulty in sea voyages when it shares in a configuration with the maleficent planets. It is the cause of dry illnesses, of fevers that come every day, of coughing, spitting and consumption; and [the cause] of the matters of divination, of the worship of God, the blessed and exalted, of what is levied on the revenues of the temples, and of the change of laws and customs at some times, in proportion to its mixing with each of the planets. Since its mixture is dry and quick in motion, because of its nearness to the sun and the speed of its return in its revolutions, it produces in the air in particular turbulent, swift and easily changing winds, thunder, the thunderbolt that is called *prēstēr*, the sinking of the ground, earthquakes and lightning; and sometimes, because of these things I have mentioned, corruption comes to the animals and plants that people use much. At the times of its setting it causes a decrease in the waters and the rivers, and at the times of its rising it increases them.
@p AB03-F029v-L12..AB03-F030r-L01
Each of these planets, when it is in the natural condition proper to it, produces things such as these. When they are mixed with one another, according to their sharings in the configurations, the diversity of the signs, their appearances relative to the sun, and their separations and meetings, then, from the mixing that there is in their actions too, their proper action becomes of different kinds accordingly, and mixed from the natures that have shared in its nature. Since the description of the proper action that comes from each kind of mixture is without end and impossible, and, in general, the mention of all that, in whatever configurations it happens to be, may be understood in many ways, we must leave this kind to the one who considers it, so that they may examine the particulars in it. One must look at the familiarity of the countries and cities with the planets from which the events that befall them come.
@p AB03-F030r-L01..AB03-F030r-L07
For if those planets are beneficial and akin to the places in which the events occur, and the maleficent planets contrary to them do not overpower them, their action proper to their natures is more beneficial; just as, if they are not akin to those countries and the maleficent planets overpower them, their benefit is less. If their mixture is maleficent and harmful and they are the agents of the event, then if they are akin to the countries in which the events occur and the beneficent planets overpower them, their harm is slight; and if they are not akin to the countries and the beneficent planets do not overpower them, the effect of their corrupting mixture on them is greater.
@p AB03-F030r-L07..AB03-F030r-L21
Ptolemy's words «the planets overpower them» admit many meanings and senses. One of them: a planet is in conjunction with a planet; then the one of the two with the greater latitude in the north overpowers the other; if both are in the south, the one with the smaller latitude overpowers; and if one is in the north and the other in the south, the northern overpowers the one in the south. Another: a planet is akin to a planet by the configuration of quadrature; then the one in the preceding signs overpowers, because it is in the tenth sign from the sign in which the other is, which is the middle of the sky of that sign. Another: the overpowering is in the way that we mentioned at the beginning of the book, so that one of them is in the high signs and the other in the low signs, and their distances from the two equinoctial points are one and the same. Another: one of them is in a cardine and the other declining from the cardine, or one of them is above the earth and the other below the earth. In general, when the beneficent planets look at the maleficent, from whatever configuration it may be, they diminish their evil; and when the maleficent look at the beneficent, they diminish their benefit, especially if it is from quadrature and opposition. What Ptolemy mostly means by overpowering is that a planet follows a planet and rises to its place: that is, when it rises after it, so that the first declines and the other remains in its place and overpowers it.
@n The high and the low signs at equal distances from the equinoctial points are the commanding and the obeying signs of chapter 6 (*Tetrabiblos* I.14).
@p AB03-F030r-L21..AB03-F030v-L07
For the most part the general accidents befall people when [those places] are the necessary places of their nativities, I mean the places in which the luminaries are, or the places of the cardines, in the nativities; for these are the places of the things that are the causes of the general accidents, whose causes are the eclipses occurring in those places or in the places opposite them. It is most severe and hardest to guard against when the degrees of the place of the eclipses occurring in those places, or in the places opposite them, are the degrees of the places of the luminaries in the nativity, and next the degrees opposite them. The most severe of them is an eclipse of the sun, because it can only occur at the revolution of the year, while the moon may be eclipsed in its own place in the root [nativity] at the time of the revolution.
