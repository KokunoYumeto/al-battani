⟨14⟩ On the indications taken from the phenomena of the upper air
@n *Tetrabiblos* II.13.
@p AB03-F033v-L09..AB03-F034r-L03
In foreknowing the particular indications it is useful to examine the signs that appear around the sun, the moon and the stars at their times. One should examine what appears of this in the sun: when it rises, in seeking the conditions of the day; when it sets, in seeking to know the conditions of the night; and, in knowing the conditions of longer duration, at the times when the sun shares in a configuration with the moon, since each configuration for the most part signifies the condition from that time to the time when the moon reaches the configuration that follows it. When the sun rises or sets clear, without anything covering it, pure and without cloud, it signifies fair weather. If its disc is of varied colours, or inclines to fiery red, or the rays that go out from it and that are around it are red, and the clouds that are called «suns» are on one side of it, of the colour of cloud inclining to red, and the rays of the sun are stretched out long, it signifies strong winds, and these winds blow towards the angles in which the signs mentioned are. Instead of the words «the clouds that are called suns» one may also say «what is beside the sun». If it rises black or inclining to green, or sets with clouds, or has around it one halo or two, or the clouds called suns are on both its sides, and its rays incline to green or are black, it signifies wintry weather and rain.
@n The transcription adds after «with the moon» the words «not when it rises or sets clear, without anything covering it», which recur below. The «suns» are Ptolemy's mock suns, the parhelia. «Halo» follows the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (هالة) for the transcription's حالة here and below.
@p AB03-F034r-L03..AB03-F034r-L15
As for the moon, its position should be examined on the day three days before the conjunction, three days before the opposition and three days before the half in light, [and] three days after these times. If it appears thin and clear then, with nothing around it, it signifies fair weather. If it is thin and red, and all of its circle that has no light seems to be in motion, it signifies the blowing of winds from the regions towards which it inclines. If it is black or green and thick, it signifies wintry weather and rains. One should also examine the halo around it. If there is one, clear and fading little by little, it signifies clear weather; if there are two or three, wintry weather. If they incline to bright red and seem broken, they signify wintry weather caused by strong winds; if they are turbid and thick, wintry weather caused by snowstorms; if they incline to green, black and broken, wintry weather of both kinds; and the more haloes there are, the more the things we have mentioned increase.
@n «Thin» follows the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (دقيقًا), and «snowstorms» likewise (الدمق); the transcription has فيها and الزمن.
@p AB03-F034r-L15..AB03-F034v-L04
Again, the halo around the wandering stars and around the bright stars that are not wandering signifies things [akin] to the colours of those haloes and to the natures of what they surround. One should also examine the stars that are not wandering and look at the groups of them in which there are many stars close to one another, and examine their colours and sizes; for when they appear brighter and larger than is usual with them, it signifies the blowing of winds from the region in which the stars that appear are, whatever region of the sphere it may be. Likewise, when the stars that resemble smudges, especially like the star called the Manger, I mean the one in the figure of Cancer, and the like, are hidden in fair weather and do not appear; or the things that occur at some times among the phenomena of the upper air: for the smudge-like stars with tails always signify dryness of the air [and] winds, the strength of that depending on the number of the things and on the length of their stay.
@n «The Manger» follows the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (المعلف); the transcription has القلب, «the Heart». The words «likewise» stand twice. The sentence on the clusters lacks its conclusion; Ptolemy says that when they are dim or invisible in a clear sky they signify heavy rains.
@p AB03-F034v-L04..AB03-F034v-L12
As for the falling of stars and their running: if it is from one angle, it signifies the blowing of a wind from that region; if they fall from different angles, they signify winds that blow without order or arrangement; and if it is from the four angles, they signify wintry weather of many changes, going as far as the occurrence of thunder, lightning and the like. So too the clouds: when they resemble tufts of wool, they sometimes signify wintry weather. And the rainbow that appears at some times: when it appears in fair weather it signifies wintry weather, and when it appears in wintry weather it signifies fair weather. I say, in general, that for the most part the particular colours that appear in the air signify conditions that suit the accidents that the colours mentioned before signified.
@n «Falling» follows the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (انقضاض) for the transcription's انفضاض, «dispersing»; the copyist repeated «and the rainbow that appears» and struck the repetition out.
