⟨37⟩ On the division of the native's times
@n *Tetrabiblos* IV.10, the last chapter of the work.
@p AB03-F075r-L01..AB03-F075r-L18
Now that we have come to this point, in outline, in each of these kinds, we must add in the same manner all the things that must be considered in the division of the times of life, by the natural method and as befits the methods we have mentioned for particular matters. A complete judgement and decree, taken from the places mentioned in the account of nativities, precedes the particular matters; but greater than it is the decree taken from the countries themselves, as we explained in the book, and the condition of the air (?) and the general causes, which are judgements that include the particular things considered in nativities in general, such as the form and appearance of bodies, the properties of the conditions of souls and the variety of customs and laws. So it is incumbent on anyone who considers such things by the natural method to hold first to the first cause, which comes first and has a better claim than any other, lest anyone be misled by the likeness of nativities, and say, for example, that one born in the land of the Abyssinians is white and straight-haired, and that one born in the land of Germania or of Galatia is dark and curly-haired, and that these are gentle in character, or fond of reasoning and of the consideration of things, and that those in the land of Hellas (?) are savage of soul and uncultured. Or one may be misled in it through the laws and customs that belong to certain matters, and say in the matter of marriage, for example, that a man of the people of Italy marries his sister, when one ought to add that this is so when the native is Egyptian; or say of the Egyptian that he marries his mother, when one ought to add: when the native is Persian. In general, then, we should first take the general conditions, in the decree and the judgement they give, and then come to the particular conditions by which we judge, so that from them the increases and decreases that occur in it may become clear.
@n «Precedes» follows the Berlin manuscript (تقدّمه). «Laws» reads السنن for السنين, «years», here and below; «a better claim» reads وأحقّ for ولحقّ, and «lest» لئلّا for ليلًا, «by night». Galatia is the land of the Gauls; «gentle» follows the Berlin manuscript (أنس) for إنث. «Hellas» guesses at الآفاس (the Berlin manuscript الاس); Ptolemy has the Greeks.
@p AB03-F075v-L01..AB03-F075v-L13
Likewise, in the division of the native's times and seasons, we must of necessity know first the differences of the ages of life and what suits each of the conditions that occur, and examine them, lest we be misled at some time in the common and simple accidents considered in nativities, and say that an infant undertakes works, or marries, or does one of the things that only one older than he does, or that a decrepit old man has a child born to him, or does one of the things that only the young do. In general, we should attach the things that appear to us from considering the times to what suits them and can happen in them, among the ages suited to that. For there is here a single way and method for all the differences of time, one and the same approach, because what belongs to the general nature common to all people is like and parallel to the order of the seven wandering stars. This way and approach begins with the first age and with the sphere nearest to us, I mean the sphere of the moon, and ends with the last of the ages and with the last of the wandering stars, the one called the sphere of Saturn; and in truth there happens in each of the ages what suits the nature of the wandering star that has become the counterpart of that age. That is something we should examine, to know from it the matter of each of these times as a whole.
@n «Of necessity» reads ضرورة for صورة, «form»; «we must know» follows the Berlin manuscript (نعلم). «Has a child born» reads يولد له for ولد كذا. «Wandering» follows the Berlin manuscript (المتحيّرة).
@p AB03-F075v-L13..AB03-F076r-L08
Their particular differences are known from the properties found [in the nativities]. Since the moon governs the age of infancy, which is for the most part the first four years — the number called the four-year period — the child's body in them is moist, not firm, and quick to grow; its food is for the most part watery; its disposition changes easily; and its soul is neither firm nor strong, as befits the accidents that come from the active part of {the soul of} the child (?). Since the ten years after these, which belong to childhood, the second age, are governed by the second planet, Mercury, which has half the number of the twenty years, the native begins in these years to grow strong in the understanding and speech of the soul; it is as though the seedlings of learning and the roots of character were borne and planted in them, and there appear, among the properties of the works he does, learning and culture from the first kinds of exercise. Since the governor of the third age, the age of youth, in the eight years that come after the years we have mentioned, is Venus, by a number equal to what is called its proper period, the movement of the seminal passages begins for that reason as they fill, and the native is moved towards the matters of intercourse. This is the time when there comes into souls in particular something like frenzy, lack of restraint, passion, love of whatever chance of intercourse offers, excitement, lust, being deceived, and blindness in what presents itself.
@n Ptolemy has «suitably to the moon's own active qualities» where the Arabic has «the active part of the soul of the child». «Age» follows the Berlin manuscript, corrected, and the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (السنّ) for اليقين, «certainty»; «frenzy» reads الكَلَب for الكلب, «the dog»; «lack of restraint» follows the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (قلّة) for «strength», and «presents itself» reads سنح for نسخ.
@p AB03-F076r-L08..AB03-F076v-L09
Since the governor of the fourth age, the middle one in order, the age of young adults, is the lord of the middle sphere, I mean the sun, for years equal in number to the nineteen years that are its proper period in the conjunctions, it makes the soul master of its works and able in them, intent on managing a livelihood, on gain and on uprightness, and moving from the conditions of jest, play and much error to self-respect, gravity and the love of honour. After the sun, Mars takes the fifth age, the age of adulthood, fifteen years, by the number of the years of its proper period. It produces hardship of livelihood, calamities and injuries, and brings cares to the soul and the body; it is as though it gives a sense and an understanding of the body's decline, and calls a man to do some work of worth that counts for something, from which toil comes to him, and to strive to do it before he comes near death. Then the sixth of the planets, Jupiter, has the age of the elderly, twelve years, by the number of the years of its proper period. Then a man turns away from doing works himself, from drudgery, turmoil and running risks, and keeps in their place to good conduct and forethought in affairs; he keeps himself clear of what is foul, reflects on affairs, admonishes and consoles; and at this time in particular he is concerned with acquiring honour and independence, with modesty and dignity. At the end, Saturn has the last age, the age of old age, to the end of life: the age in which there come cold, and difficulty in the movements of body and soul, in rising, in desires and enjoyments, and the quick decline that comes by nature; the condition in which a man's life comes to its end, and dejection, weakness, injuries and impatience with all things befall him, in keeping with the slackening of all his movements.
@n The sun's nineteen years are the cycle after which the conjunctions return to the same days of the year. «Adulthood» reads الرجلة for الرحلة, «the journey»; «counts for something» follows the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (يعتدّ به) for يعتز به. «Is concerned» reads معنيًّا for معيّنًا; «dejection» follows the Berlin manuscript (الكآبة) for الكتابة, «writing».
@p AB03-F076v-L09..AB03-F076v-L16
This is the description of the properties of the conditions of the times as they are seen by the general and common course of nature. He begins here with the moon and gives it the least of the parts, the twentieth (?), of its lesser period and of the lesser period of the sun, which is known for the two of them from their meeting in their returns. Then he passes on to the greatest of the parts, the half, and takes half of Mercury's period; then he comes to the complete parts, and uses the period of each planet as it is. So all these ages come to ninety-eight years. If the native passes beyond them, he lives into the age of the moon; if he passes beyond that, he returns to the age of Mercury; and if he passes beyond that, to the age of Venus, which completes the hundred and twenty years that are the sun's greater years. That is why its years were made of this amount.
@n The ages are 4, 10, 8, 19, 15 and 12 years, and Saturn's thirty years make the sum 98; with the moon's four, Mercury's ten and Venus's eight years again the sum is 120. The part given to the moon is not clear in the transcription. «Begins» reads ويبدأ for ويدي; «were made» follows the Berlin manuscript (جعلت) for {جعلتم}.
@p AB03-F076v-L16..AB03-F077r-L17
As for the particular properties of the times: since they must be reckoned by the properties of the nativities, we take what is general in them, in the way we have mentioned, from the chief haylājs; but we take it from all of them, not from one of them, as we did for the length of life. The haylāj taken from the ascendant we use for the accidents that befall the body and for life abroad; that taken from the lot of fortune, for matters of wealth and acquisition; that taken from the moon, for the conditions of the soul and marriage; that taken from the sun, for matters of favour and honour; and that taken from the middle of the sky, for the other particular conditions of the conduct of life, such as works, friendship and the matter of children. When we do so, the lord of the matter is not one planet, either beneficent or maleficent; and this is right, since contrary accidents often happen at one and the same time: as when a man loses one of his kin and inherits wealth from him, or falls ill and in that very condition of his illness obtains favour and gain, or honour, or is without work and has children born to him, and the like among the accidents that often happen. For the condition in body, in soul, in wealth, in favour and in good and bad accidents is not one condition, such that a man must of necessity meet with success or failure in all these things. Perhaps something like that may happen to him at the times of utmost happiness or misery, when it happens that all the beneficent planets, or all the maleficent, meet all these haylājs or most of them; but that seldom happens, because human nature falls short of reaching either of these two extremes, or because of its quick inclining and departure from balance owing to the change of good and bad things. In this way we distinguish and separate the places of the haylājs.
@n «Not from one» reads لا من واحد for لأمر واحد, and «the length of life» العمر for القمر, «the moon». «Favour» reads حظوة for خطوة, «step». «Seldom» follows the Berlin manuscript (وقلّ ما) for وكلّما, «whenever», and «falls short» reads تقصير for تعصير. Ptolemy has human nature inclined towards the balance of good and evil that comes from their alternation.
@p AB03-F077r-L17..AB03-F078r-L01
As for the places that meet the haylājs, we should not use only the places that signify killing, as we do for the length of life, but all of them together; nor should we limit ourselves to what meets it bodily, or by opposition, or by quadrature only, but we use also what meets it by sextile and by trine, by the regard of equal power, and from the high, commanding signs and the low ones. We give the times first, in each of the haylājs, to the planet that is in the degree of the haylāj itself or shares a configuration with it. If no planet is in that degree or shares a configuration with it, we should give them instead to the nearest of the planets that precede that degree, or of the rays that one of the planets casts from one of the configurations, until it reaches the planet that sees the degree following that degree in the order of the signs; then we give the time after it to the planet that it reaches, or to its ray. So we do in the other haylājs; and we take some of the planets we use and put them in the place of the governor with the planet that has the division of the times, [giving] times to the planets that are lords of the terms of the degrees, until it reaches them in years. We should give the degrees of the distances as degrees of years: in the haylāj taken from the ascendant, a number of years equal to the number of the times of the ascensions of the clime that fall between the degree of the ascendant and the planet that follows the degree of the ascendant, or its ray — I mean the clime and the town in which the native was born; in the haylāj taken from the middle of the sky, years equal in number to the times of the ascensions of the signs in the right sphere; and in the remaining haylājs, taken from the remaining places, the matter goes by this analogy, according to their nearness to the cardines, at the risings, at the settings or in their passage through the middle of the sky, as we explained and set out in knowing the ascensions of the signs between the quarters of the sphere, at the beginning of this book. By these things we have mentioned the planets in charge of the general time are known. Ptolemy made the lord of the matter the lord of the meeting, and put the lord of the term in the place of the governor, below the lord of the meeting. This is the most correct, since the rays of the planets are stronger than the terms and besides last longer than the times of a term. So it may happen, for example, that the years fall, in one of the haylājs, in the ray of a beneficent planet over many terms, until another ray meets it, so that the native's condition in that period is either praiseworthy in that kind throughout, or blameworthy on the whole.
@n «Meets» reads يلقاه for نلقّيه; «the low» reads المنخفضة for المنحفظة. «We give» follows the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (نعطي) for the doubtful {يقتر}, and «or to its ray» the Berlin manuscript (أو إلى). For the ascensions between the quarters see chapter 24, and chapter 55 of the *Zīj*; the comment on Ptolemy's lord of the meeting is al-Battānī's own.
@p AB03-F078r-L01..AB03-F078r-L12
The planets in charge of the years we find by taking the number of the complete years that have passed since the time of birth and casting it from each of the places of the haylājs among the signs, in the order of the signs, a solar year to each sign, and taking the governor of the sign at which the count ends for the year in which the native's revolution falls. We do the very same for the months: we take the number of the months from the month of birth, cast it from the places to which the governing of the year belongs, from that sign, and take the governor of the sign at which it ends. We do likewise for the days: we take the number of the days from the day of birth and of the revolution, of the month and the hour of the day, and cast it from the place of the months, two days and a sixth, approximately, to each sign. We do that so that the year is thirteen months, and the thirteenth month returns to the sign that had the governing of the year; and likewise each of these months becomes thirteen signs, so that the end of the month comes to the sign in charge of governing the month, as befits and agrees with this method. We shall set out a separate table of the number of the days of the months of the revolution, of their particular days, and of the share of each of the signs, when we have finished this account.
@n The words from «cast it» to «take the governor of the sign» follow the Berlin manuscript, which the transcription lacks. «The thirteenth» reads الثالث عشر for الثالث, «the third». A thirteenth of a thirteenth of the year of 365¼ days is 2.16 days, about two days and a sixth; Ptolemy gives twenty-eight days to a sign for the months and two and a third days for the days. The tables follow the commentary, on ff. 80r–80v.
@p AB03-F078r-L12..AB03-F078v-L06
We should also look at the ingresses of the planets into the places of the times, since that helps not a little in knowing the events that happen at those times. We look at the ingresses of Jupiter and Saturn into the signs that govern the years, at the ingresses of Mars, Venus, the sun and Mercury into the signs that govern the months, and at the ingresses of the moon into the places of the days among the signs. We use the lords of the general times among the planets for the coming about of the event and its completion — they are the lords of the ages and of the meeting — and we use the lords of the particular times that follow them for help towards it, or for hindrance, according to the familiarity of the natures of the things we find in the places. They become causes of strong and weak accidents, that is, causes of the strength and weakness of the accidents, or of their remoteness from familiarity. For what signifies the particular quality of the general condition and the length of the time is the place of the haylāj and the lord of the general times together with the lords of the terms, because each of these planets has been familiar, in the places themselves, with the places of the planet to which the matter of the luminaries (?) belonged from the beginning. Whether the accident is good or the opposite is known from the particular natural condition of the lords of the time and their mixture in doing good or evil, and from their familiarity with the thing from the beginning, or their opposition to it.
@n Ptolemy assigns Saturn's ingresses to the general places of the times and Jupiter's to those of the years. «The places» reads المواضع for الموانع, «the obstacles».
@p AB03-F078v-L06..AB03-F079r-L07
At what times in particular the event becomes clear and appears is known from the condition of the signs in charge of the year and the month, compared with the places that are the causes of the event, and from the condition at the ingresses of the planets and the sun into the signs in charge of the years and the months. For the planets that agree with the places mentioned from the time of birth, and share a configuration with them at the ingress with an agreeing sharing, do good in the matter in question, just as they do evil when they disagree with them; the agreeing sharing is by trine or sextile. Those that share with them a disagreeing sharing, by being opposite them on the diameter or in quadrature to them, and that are against the good, are a cause of evil; in the other conditions they produce nothing. If the very same planets dominate the times and the ingresses, the nature of the event becomes excessive and intense, whichever way it inclines, to good or to evil; all the more so when their dominion over the cause is not only as lords of the times, but because they were also the governors in the matter of the native. The native may be successful or disappointed in all things at once: when all the kinds of the haylājs, or most of them, are in one and the same place; or when they are scattered, but at one and the same time all the things that meet them happen to be beneficent, or all of them maleficent, or most of them so. This is the method by which the matters of the times are examined in the way that befits the natural course. As for the particular methods by which the conditions of the events of the times are examined: since the enumeration of what happens of them is of many kinds and hard to describe, especially in this place, their mention had to be left out, because of the aim we set ourselves in this book, of explaining the action of the general nature of the planets and the fitting of particular things to that method.
@n «Becomes clear» reads يتبيّن for ثلثين; «evil» reads الشرّ for السرّ, «the secret». «Being opposite» follows the Berlin manuscript (بأن); «beneficent» reads سعودًا for سعده. «Conditions» follows the Berlin manuscript (حال), and «aim» reads الغرض for التعرّض. The last sentences are Ptolemy's own conclusion, as one branch of the Greek tradition has it.
