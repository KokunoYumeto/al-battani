⟨28⟩ On the afflictions of the soul
@n *Tetrabiblos* III.14, the last chapter of Book III.
@p AB03-F061r-L13..AB03-F061v-L08
In seeking to know the afflictions of the soul, we should examine in general the condition of Mercury and the moon relative to each other, to the cardines, and to the planets whose nature is to do harm. For if Mercury and the moon are not connected with each other, and on the eastern horizon some planet in a harmful and unsuitable condition overpowers them, surrounds them or faces them in longitude, afflictions of many kinds (?) befall the proper condition of the soul; and their distinction and detail come from the particular conditions that we have mentioned of the planets familiar to the places. We have also, to some extent, distinguished many of the weak afflictions of the soul in what we said before on its properties, and their strength or their increase can be known from the excess of the actions of the maleficent planets: for one would perhaps be right to call afflictions the characters that are not held in check, whether they fall short of the middle condition or go beyond it. Those whose excess is severe, which are like one of the diseases, whose occurrence departs from nature altogether, and which are in the rational part of the soul and in the part that receives impressions, are recognized in the way that I describe.
@n «Overpowers» follows the Berlin manuscript and the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (كان يعلو) for كانوا يعلموا; Ptolemy has Mercury and the moon unrelated to each other or to the eastern horizon. «Of many kinds» reads فنون for the doubtful {فتون}; «detail» follows the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (تفصيله) for تحصيله.
@p AB03-F061v-L08..AB03-F062r-L06
For the most part, epileptics are those in whose nativities the moon and Mercury, as we have said, are not connected with each other or with the ascendant, while Saturn by day and Mars by night is on the cardine and is the lord of this condition. The mad are those in whom, besides that, the matter is the reverse of what we said, the lord of the condition being [Saturn] by night and Mars by day, especially if it is in Cancer, Virgo or Pisces. Those called the stricken by the jinn, and those called *hydrokephaloi* (?) — that is, those whose heads are overcome by moisture — are so when the maleficent planets are in the condition we have described and dominate the moon as it comes out of the rays of the sun, the one dominating it being Saturn at the time of the conjunction and Mars at the time of the opposition, especially in Sagittarius and Pisces. When the two maleficent planets alone prevail in that condition, in the manner we have mentioned before, they make the diseases that we said befall the rational part of the soul incurable, but neither repugnant nor notorious. If the two beneficent planets, I mean Jupiter and Venus, are familiar with them, and the two maleficent planets are on the western cardine and the beneficent on the eastern cardine, they make the illnesses curable, but evident (?) and repugnant. If the one familiar with them is Jupiter, the cure is by medical treatment, either by regimen or by drugs; if the one familiar with them is Venus, the illness is cured by revelation and help from God, mighty and glorious.
@n «Mars» is written in the margin, here and in the Berlin manuscript, in place of «the moon». «The stricken by the jinn» reads المضروبين من الجنّ for المضرّ وبين من الجزء. The Greek ὑδροκέφαλοι, «water-headed», is written أوعر وقًا قالس (the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* …مافاليسا). «Out of the rays» follows the Berlin manuscript (الشعاع); for «Sagittarius» (الرامي) the transcription has الرأس, «the Head», and the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* الراعي. «Rational» follows the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (العقليّ) for الفعليّ, and «notorious» (مشهورة) for مستهونة, «despised»; Ptolemy has latent and obscure. «Evident» reads بيّنة for ميتة, «dead»; Ptolemy has noticeable.
@p AB03-F062r-L06..AB03-F062r-L14
If the maleficent planets are on the eastern cardines and the beneficent ones setting, they make the diseases inescapable, repugnant and very notorious. Of these, the illnesses of epilepsy they make continuous and unremitting, with shouting and danger, from which death comes. The illnesses of madness and of loss of reason make the one who has them unable to control themselves, disobedient to their kin, babbling (?), foul in speech, and the like. Those of the diseases that are called the stroke of the jinn and the prevalence of harmful moisture in the head they make into possession (*enthousiasmos*), and notorious, with assaults on people, ransoming (?) and beatings, and the like among disgraceful things.
@n «The beneficent» follows the Berlin manuscript and the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (السعود) for النحوس, and «disobedient» (يعصي) for يعطي, «gives». «Possession» renders the Greek ἐνθουσιασμός, which the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* writes أنثوسيماسموس and the transcription أسوسامس. Ptolemy has possession, confession and torments.
@p AB03-F062r-L14..AB03-F062r-L22
Some of the places in which that condition is found give a particular help to it: the places of the sun and Mars help especially towards madness; the places of Jupiter and Mercury towards the illness of epilepsy; the places of Venus towards revelation and the telling of secrets; and the places of Saturn and the moon towards harm from moistures and the stroke of the jinn. Most kinds of the diseases that arise in the active kind of the soul are of these kinds that we have mentioned, and they arise from such conditions according to the whole of their natures.
@n «Kind» reads جنس for جنّ, «jinn», here and in the next sentence.
@p AB03-F062r-L22..AB03-F062v-L18
The disturbance that arises in the kind that is subject to governing appears especially in excesses and deficiencies of the natural matters that make male and female, and the inquiry into it proceeds in a way like the former, after we put the sun with the moon here in the place of Mercury with the moon there, and use the familiarity of Mars and Venus with them. We say, after that, that if both luminaries or one of them are in masculine signs, the men go to excess in what is natural to them, and the women go to excess in what departs from what is natural to them, passing beyond nature to manliness and strength of soul. If Mars and Venus as well, either of them or both together, are masculine, the men are absorbed in intercourse that follows the natural course, mocking and ever quick to foul acts contrary to the law in matters of intercourse; and the women are lustful for intercourse in the way that departs from nature, leering, like those called *sāḥiqāt*, for they do with women the acts of men. If Venus alone is masculine, what they do of that is secret, not open; if Mars too is masculine, it is open, so that at times they declare the women who are with them to be their wives.
@n One doubtful word, {خبرة}, is left untranslated after «the natural course»; «quick» reads سراعًا for the editor's queried عراعًا. For «lustful» the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* has «mocking» (مستهزآت من). «Leering» reads طمّاحات for طماخات (the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* طقاحات). Ptolemy calls these women tribades.
@p AB03-F062v-L18..AB03-F063r-L17
If the matter in the planets is the reverse of that, I mean that the luminaries in the condition we have mentioned are alone in feminine signs, the women go to excess in the natural matter ⟨…⟩ to the ugliness and effeminacy of the soul. If Venus too is feminine, the women also are absorbed in the matter of intercourse, lustful (?), inclined to what is done to them in it in the natural way, at any time and with anyone (?), so that they refuse nothing in the matters of intercourse, whether base or contrary to the law; and the men are effeminate and slack, inclined to intercourse that departs from nature, not refusing to have foul acts done to them, but in secret and concealment (?). If Mars too is feminine, each of these two kinds does these things — I mean fornication, impurity, committing blameworthy acts and every foul deed — openly and without shame, so that their lust carries them to the point of being made notorious (?) and reproached. The eastern conditions of Mars and Venus, attributed to the mornings, help towards manliness and notoriety; their western conditions, attributed to the evenings, towards effeminacy and obscurity. Likewise, when Saturn is with them, it helps towards filth, impurity and great disgrace, for its nature helps towards each of these things; if Jupiter is with them, it helps towards decency, restraint and modesty; and if Mercury is with them, it helps towards the notoriety of the matter, the quickness of the events, their many kinds and their growth.
@n Ptolemy has the men exceeding in the unnatural, so that their souls become soft and effeminate; the Arabic lacks the men here. The copyist repeated two short words and struck them out. «Being made notorious» reads يُشهَروا for يشتهوا, «desire»; «western» follows the Berlin manuscript and the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (الغربيّة) for القريبة, «near», and «growth» the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (نموها) for سوها.
