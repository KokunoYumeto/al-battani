So too is the condition of the four quarters of the horizon and of the angles from which the winds blow to all sides. Dryness prevails in the quarter next to the east, and the wind that blows from it, the east wind, which is called Apeliotes, is without moisture and drying. The winds that blow from the side of the south, which are called southern winds, are hot and burning. The quarter of the west is moist, and the winds that blow from it, which are called western, soften and moisten. The quarter of the north is very cold, and the winds that blow from this quarter, which are called northern, are cold and condensing.

The knowledge of these matters is also useful and helps to distinguish the mixtures. For it is clear that the actions of the power in the stars change with the conditions of the seasons of the year, of the ages or of the angles; that when the conditions are akin to them, their qualities are pure and unmixed, with nothing mingled in them, and their action is stronger, as the stars that heat by nature are stronger in hot conditions and the moist stars stronger in moist conditions; and that in the opposites of these conditions their power is not pure and unmixed but, being mixed with something else, weak, as the power of the heating stars is weaker in cold conditions and that of the moist stars weaker in dry conditions. The natures of the fixed stars that mix with those stars, or share with them in one of the nine configurations that they have, especially with the sun, which were mentioned earlier in this book, increase this, decrease it and change it not a little. In the same way the change in all the other conditions follows the mixing of the qualities.
