⟨17⟩ On the division of the doctrine of nativities
@n *Tetrabiblos* III.3.
@p AB03-F035v-L10..AB03-F036r-L06
Having first set these things down, we say: anyone who wishes to divide the whole consideration of nativities in an orderly and systematic way finds that some of the things that can be grasped by the natural course are the accidents that occur before the birth, like the account of the parents; and some are the accidents that occur before the birth and after it, like the account of the brothers. Those at the birth itself are not one simple matter, like what we have described. The last of the accidents that can be grasped are those that occur after the birth, and this kind has many branches and parts. What one wishes to know of what is at the time of the birth itself is the account of male and female, the account of those born as twins and as more than twins, the account of the malformed, and the account of those who are not nourished. What one wishes to know of what comes after the birth is the account of the length of life, since it is not connected with the account of those who are not nourished; and of that is the account of the form of the body and of the illnesses and injuries that befall the body. After that comes the account of the conditions of the soul and of the accidents of the soul; after that, the account of the native's fortune in the matter of possessions and in the matter of rank and elevation; after that, the account of the native's condition in the works they engage in; after that, the account of their marriage and married life, of the children born to them, of their mixing with people and their partnerships with them, and of their friends; after that, the account of travel abroad; and at the end, the account of the condition of their death, which in its power is akin to the account of the length of life, and whose place must be after all this. We shall describe the matter of each of these things in outline.
@n «The account of the malformed» is written in the margin. «We shall describe» follows the Berlin manuscript (سنصف); the transcription has ويتّصف.
@p AB03-F036r-L06..AB03-F036r-L12
Since we have explained the degree of the ascendant and its correction in [this] way, we add [an account] of everything that occurs in the conditions of nativities, and mention it together with our mention of the pure active powers, as we said just now. We discard what many people have described in which there is no proof or convincing argument suited to the causes that come from the first nature, and examine what of it can be grasped, not by lots and numbers that have no cause that necessitates them, but by considering the configurations of the stars and their fitness to their places. So that there is no need to repeat and reiterate the account in each of the chapters that we have mentioned, we should give a general account for all of them.
@n «Lots» reads القرع for الفرع: Ptolemy rejects the method of lots and numbers (*Tetrabiblos* III.3).
@p AB03-F036r-L12..AB03-F036v-L12
We look first at the place in the sphere of the signs that suits the kind of the chapter sought among the chapters of the consideration of the nativity: as the place of the middle of the sky, for example, suits the matter of the works one engages in, I mean the crafts, or as the place of the sun suits the matter of the father. Then after that we look at the planets that deserve the governing of that place by the five kinds that we mentioned in what we said before. If the lord of the place is one planet in all respects, we give it the governing of that matter; if there are two planets or three, we give the governing to the one of them that has the greater number of shares. After that we direct our consideration of the condition of the event, and our examination of it, to the natures of the very planets that have deserved the governing, and to the natures of the signs in which the planets are, and of the places akin to them. Then we examine the event and its size from their strength: we look whether they are strong in their condition in the world, or in their condition in the nativity, or the matter is the opposite. They are strongest in their condition in the world when they are in places of their own or akin to them, and when they are eastern and increasing in motion; strongest in their condition in the nativity when they are in the cardines, or in the places that rise to the cardines, and especially the ascendant and the middle of the sky; weakest in their condition in the world when they are in foreign places, or are western, or retrograde, or decreasing in motion; and weakest in their condition in the nativities when they decline from the cardines. Direct your consideration of the length of the time of the event by examining whether they are attributed to the mornings or to the evenings, relative to the sun or to the ascendant: for the two quarters that precede each of these, and the two opposite them on the diameter, are attributed to the mornings, and the remaining quarters that follow them are attributed to the evenings. And look whether they are in the cardines [or] rising to the cardines: if they are attributed to the mornings and are on the cardines, they are quicker in action; and if they are attributed to the evenings and rising to the cardines, they are slower in action.
@n «Matter» follows the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (لأمر) twice for the transcription's لأمن, «safety»; «their strength» and «their condition» read قوّتها and حالها for قوّتنا and حالنا, «our strength», «our condition».
@p AB03-F036v-L13..AB03-F036v-L20
One should also keep in mind, in all the matters that arise from nativities, that a planet that brings about something in the place sought must necessarily have been akin to it from the beginning. For if it is not akin to it, it cannot bring about anything in it, since it did not share in the first beginning: that is, if the sign in which the planet is is not joined to the sign sought into which the planet falls, it cannot bring about anything of its nature in that sign, since it was not joined to it in the nativity. The time when the event comes about does not follow the first governing, but follows the condition of the planet that has dominion over the governing, relative to the sun and to the angles of the world.
@n «If it is not» supplies the negation (لم) that the Berlin manuscript has.
