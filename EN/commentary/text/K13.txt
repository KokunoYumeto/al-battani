⟨13⟩ On knowing the conditions of the year
@n *Tetrabiblos* II.10–12.
@p AB03-F031r-L05..AB03-F031r-L12
In determining the year we should use the entry of the sun into the beginnings of the solstitial and equinoctial signs, I mean the points of the two equinoxes and the two solstices, which are the beginnings of the seasons of the year; and examine the conjunctions and oppositions of the sun and the moon that occur near these beginnings, before these times, and especially those of them at which an eclipse occurs. So from the beginning [taken] at the sun's entry into Aries it may be known what the spring will be like; from the beginning found at its entry into Libra, what the autumn will be like; from the beginning found at its entry into Cancer, what the summer will be like; and from its entry into Capricorn, what the winter will be like; since these general times and their conditions come only from the sun.
@n «Examine» follows the Berlin manuscript (تتفقّد); the transcription has تنعقد.
@p AB03-F031r-L12..AB03-F031v-L04
In this we should use the properties of the signs, and infer from them the winds that blow from the quarters and their natures in general. The changes towards increase and decrease at some times are also inferred, in general, from the conjunctions and oppositions that occur after the sun's entry into the beginnings of these signs, that is, the conjunctions and oppositions that follow these points, through the wandering stars' sharing in configurations with them. The particular matters that occur in one of the quarters [of the year] are inferred from the conjunctions that occur in each of the signs and from the positions of the planets; and this may be called the examination of the months. In this subject, besides what we have said, one needs to know the particular natural properties of each of the signs as they bear on the conditions of the year. The mixtures of the wandering stars, and of the fixed stars whose mixtures are like those of the wandering ones, and their familiarity with the air and the winds, we have mentioned before, and likewise the familiarity of all the signs with the winds; it remains only to report their particular natures and their familiarity with the seasons, I mean the familiarity of each sign.
@p AB03-F031v-L04..AB03-F032r-L05
Aries is on the circle of the equator, and the nature of the sign of Aries as a whole, because of the equinox that is in it, generates thunder and lightning; its northern part is hot and corrupting, and its southern part generates frost and is cold. Taurus is nearer to the summer circle; the sign of Taurus as a whole signifies both mixtures, inclining to heat; its northern part is of a temperate mixture, and its southern part generates disturbance and earthquakes. Gemini is nearer to the summer circle; its nature as a whole produces a temperate mixture; its northern part generates winds and produces earthquakes, and its southern part is fiery and burning. Cancer is on the summer circle; as a whole it produces fair weather and is hot, and its northern and southern parts are fiery and burning. Leo is nearer to the summer circle; as a whole it is burning, with hot, stagnant air and pestilence; its northern part is stirring and fiery — that is, it stirs up hot winds — and its southern part moistens. Virgo is nearer to the circle of the equinox; as a whole it moistens and produces thunder; its northern part produces winds, and its southern part is of a temperate mixture. Libra is on the circle of the equator; as a whole it is changeable and turning; its northern part generates sand (?), and its southern part moistens and produces pestilence. Scorpio is nearer to the winter circle; its nature as a whole produces thunder and is fiery; its northern part is fiery and burning, and its southern part moistens. Sagittarius is nearer to the winter circle; as a whole it produces winds; its northern part produces winds, and its southern part is very moist and changeable. Capricorn is on the winter circle; as a whole it is very moist, and its northern and southern parts are moist and very corrupting. Aquarius is nearer to the winter circle; as a whole it is cold and watery; its northern part is burning, and its southern part produces burning. Pisces is nearer to the circle of the equator; as a whole it is cold and generates winds; its northern part generates winds, and its southern part is watery.

The nature of the parts of the signs is known from the mixture of the fixed stars in each sign, among the stars in the figures of the sphere of the signs and those outside them, according to their sizes in magnitude and the likeness of their natures to the natures of the wandering stars. Among the nebulous stars in the sphere of the signs, those like the Pleiades, whose mixture is first of the moon and second of Mars, produce earthquakes, winds and fog; the nebulous one in the figure of Cancer, which is called the Manger, whose mixture is first that of Mars and second the moon, produces hot, stagnant air, earthquakes and gloom; the nebulous one that follows the Shawla of the Scorpion, whose mixture is that of the sun and then of Mars, produces earthquakes, and so does the nebulous one on the eye of the Archer. What is of the mixture of Mars, [like] al-Dabarān, which is of the greatest magnitude, is fiery and generates lightning and thunderbolts. [Stars] like the Heart of the Lion, whose mixture is first that of Mars and then of Jupiter, produce stagnant hot air and pestilence. Those of them of the mixture of Venus alone signify much moisture and rain; those of the mixture of Jupiter, moderate heat and moisture; those of the mixture of Saturn alone, cold; and those of the mixture of Mercury alone now dry and now moisten, and generate winds, according to what we have mentioned of the powers of the wandering stars and of its familiarity with the winds. After that their natures are mixed with the natures of the signs in which they are and with the natures of the seasons of the year. The moist stars in the western quarter [are moister], and likewise the hot stars in summer increase in heat through their familiarity with the season, and when they are in the southern quarter their heat is greatest. When the signs in which they are suit their natures, that increases their action more; likewise, when they incline in latitude to the north or to the south, that increases or decreases their action, according to how far their natures suit that direction. When the mixture of the stars is attributed to two of the wandering stars, the first of the two is stronger in action, and the second mixes with it afterwards, according to the marks, for some of them resemble the second star only a little in their mixture; [and it depends] on how the wandering stars connect with one another when they take over the governing, in the familiarity of their natures with one another and in their contrariety; so that from the whole of what comes together from these mixtures you arrive at the condition that will be, and give the preponderance in power to what is most manifest in action and strongest in position.

Since we have first set out these things in this way, the methods of the particular indications are as I shall describe. One of these methods is the consideration of the matter as a whole, which is known from considering the condition of each of the quarters [of the year]. In it one needs to consider what we have said: to examine the conjunction of the sun and the moon and their opposition that come nearest before the points of the solstices and the equinoxes, and to set up the cardines, at the time of the conjunction or the opposition, for each of the climes and the cities that one intends to examine. Then after that take the planets that govern the places in which the conjunction or the opposition takes place, and those that govern the cardine that follows these places, that is, the cardine through which one of the luminaries passes shortly before the time of the conjunction or the full moon, as we have explained and set out in the chapter on eclipses; so that the governor among the wandering and the non-wandering stars is known in that way, and we know the matter as a whole from the properties of the quarters, and know the increase and decrease in the severity or mildness of that whole from the nature of the governing planets, by looking at their quality, which of the qualities it is, and what conditions are set in motion by them.
@p AB03-F032v-L21..AB03-F033r-L10
The second method is the consideration of the affairs of the months. In it one needs to look at the conjunctions and oppositions that occur in each of the signs in the very way mentioned, keeping in mind what I shall mention: if the conjunction falls near the point of the solstice or of the equinox that has been passed, we use the conjunctions until [the sun] reaches the quarter that follows that quarter. Likewise one should look at the cardines and at the planets that govern both the places that we have mentioned, and examine in particular the nearest appearances of the planets, the applications and separations of the wandering stars, their properties and conditions, and which winds these planets and the parts of the signs in which they happen to be stir up. Also, as we said, we look towards the quarter of which wind the latitude of the moon inclines when it inclines from the belt of the signs; so that, proceeding [in this way], one knows from all these things what for the most part prevails in the conditions of the months and their winds.
@p AB03-F033r-L10..AB03-F033v-L08
The third method is to examine the finest indications, which show the slackening and the severity of the matter. This is known from the sharing of the sun and the moon in particular configurations, not only in the configurations of conjunction and opposition but also in those at the half of the moon in light; and the beginning of the change indicated is for the most part three days before the moon's progress [matches that of] the sun, and sometimes three days after, at equal distances. It is also known from their sharing in configurations with the wandering stars in each of these conditions and in others, such as the condition of the trine and the sextile; for these things follow the natural course. The condition of the change we come to know by following the natural familiarities that the stars that are present, and the signs, have with the surrounding air and with the winds. The strength of these particular conditions that occur from day to day is [greatest] especially when the fixed stars that are greatest in size and strongest in action make appearances in the mornings or in the evenings, in the east or in the west, relative to the sun; for, for the most part, they change and turn the particular conditions to their own natures. This also happens, a little less than what we have said, when the luminaries pass one of the cardines; for the slackening and severity that the conditions have from time to time occur when the luminaries come to these places, just as the ebb and the flood occur when the moon comes to them; and the change of the winds in particular occurs when the luminaries go to the cardines, the change being towards the wind to which the latitude of the moon inclines. In every case one should proceed on the basis that the first, universal cause takes precedence, that what follows it is the cause of the particular accidents, and that the action is most certain and strongest when the stars that govern the universal natures are found sharing in a configuration with the particular natures.
