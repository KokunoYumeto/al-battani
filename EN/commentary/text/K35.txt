⟨35⟩ On life abroad [and] journeys
@n *Tetrabiblos* IV.8.
@p AB03-F072v-L04..AB03-F072v-L14
Life abroad is known from the condition of both luminaries relative to the cardines, and especially from the condition of the moon. For when the moon is setting, or declining from the cardines, it signifies life abroad and moving from place to place. Mars sometimes does the like when it is in the setting point, or when it declines from the place over our heads, that is, to the ninth, and its distance from the luminaries is that of opposition or that of quadrature. If the lot of good fortune too falls in a sign that produces travel, the person's whole life, movements and works are abroad. If the beneficent planets look at the places we have mentioned, or are moving to them and following them, the works undertaken abroad are noble and profitable, and the return from abroad is quick, with nothing to hinder it; if the maleficent planets look at them, those works are toilsome, bringing harm and full of danger, and the return from abroad is slow. The mixture of these is always taken according to the condition of the planets that share a configuration with these places, as we explained before.
@n «Or when» reads أو for وأمّا; «profitable» reads مربحة for مرتجة; «hinder» follows the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (يعوقه) for يفوقه, «surpass», and «taken» the Berlin manuscript and the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (يؤخذ) for يوجد, «found».
@p AB03-F072v-L14..AB03-F073r-L03
For the most part, when the luminaries fall in the region of the two eastern quarters, the travel is mostly to the dwellings in the region of the east and the south; and if they fall in the two western quarters, or in the setting point itself, the travel is towards the north and the west. If the signs that produce life abroad are of one form, either themselves or the signs of the planets in charge of governing them, the travel is from time to time and on some occasions; if they are bicorporeal or of two forms, the travel is continuous, extending over a long time. If the lords of the places that produce travel and of the places of the luminaries are Venus and Jupiter, there is no danger in the travel, and it is pleasant besides: for it comes about by the governors of the lands sending them on account of their friends' needs, and the good temper of the air and the abundance of the things suited to them help them in it. If Mercury is with them, they often obtain, through the cause we have mentioned, benefits, success in their efforts, gifts and honours.
@n «In charge» reads المتولّية for المتولّدة, «generated»; «extending» follows the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* (تمتدّ) for the doubtful {عند}. Ptolemy has the resources of their friends.
@p AB03-F073r-L03..AB03-F073r-L15
If Saturn and Mars enclose the luminaries, especially when they are opposite in longitude — here, and wherever this phrase «opposite in longitude» occurs, it means in one place in longitude (?) — what they obtain is of no use, and they fall into severe hardship. If the two are in moist signs, the hardship comes through sea voyages and drowning, or through travelling on roads hard to pass and in places neither peopled nor inhabited; if they are in fixed signs, through falls from cliffs and the blowing of stormy winds; if in the signs of the equinoxes and the solstices, through want and shortage of the things needed, and an unhealthy condition of the air; if in signs whose figures resemble human figures, through highway robbery, ambush and plunder; and if in signs whose figures are those of land animals, through the attacks of wild beasts, or earthquakes. If Mercury is there, it comes through the temper [of the air], through dangerous slander, and through the stings of beasts and venomous vermin. We should take our view of the particular qualities of the accidents, whether beneficial or harmful, from the chief places that are the causes of works, of wealth, of the matter of the body or of favour, according to the condition mentioned before.
@n «Land» reads البرّيّ for البريء, as in chapter 26. Ptolemy adds that the times of these events are judged from the ingresses of the five planets.
