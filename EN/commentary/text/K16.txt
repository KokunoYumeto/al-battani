⟨16⟩ On knowing the degree of the ascendant of the native
@n *Tetrabiblos* III.2.
@p AB03-F034v-L16..AB03-F035r-L08
Since doubt often arises about the first matter on which one relies, the degree of the ascendant at the time of the coming out of the child — for for the most part only those who observe it with the astrolabe, alone among all the instruments for the hours, know the hours at the time of birth accurately, while the other instruments for the hours, with which many who are concerned with this measure, may often be false and depart from the truth: those of them by which the hours are measured by the sun, because of the inclination that may befall them in their place and the crookedness of their gnomons, and the water clocks, because of the stopping of the water and the inequality of its flow, which come from various causes and from the least cause that befalls it — it is necessary first to report the way by which one can know the degree of the sphere of the signs that, by the natural, necessary reasoning, must be the one rising, after we have found approximately, from the hour that we have been given, the degree of the ascendant from the ascensions. We say that one should take, of the conjunction and the opposition that came before the birth, the one nearest to it, whichever was nearer, and find its degree accurately. If what we have taken is a conjunction, we take the degree of the luminaries; if what was taken is an opposition, we take the degree of whichever of the luminaries happens to be above the earth.
@n «Doubt» reads الشكّ for الشدّ. «The astrolabe» is ذات الصفائح, as in the *Zīj*; the Arabic *Tetrabiblos* has الأسطرلاب. The copyist wrote «the lord of the sphere» before «from the ascensions» and struck it out.
@p AB03-F035r-L09..AB03-F035v-L01
Then we look which of the planets deserves to have the governing of that degree at the time of the birth. In general, the way in which a planet deserves the governing is completed by five things, I mean the triplicity, the house, the exaltation, the term, and the appearance near the rays or the sharing in a configuration with the sun; so the governing of the place belongs to the planet that has more than one of these qualifications in that place, or all of them. If we find all or most of these things in one planet, we look at its degree, taken accurately, in the sign in which it is at the time of the birth, and judge that the degree of equal number in the sign that we had taken approximately as the ascendant is the degree of one of the cardines of the ascendant. If we find that two planets or more share in the governing, we look at the number [of degrees] that each of them has travelled at the time of the birth; the one whose number is nearest to the number of the degree of the ascendant that came out for us from the ascendant [found approximately] is the one that rises at the time of the birth. If we find the number of the degrees of two planets or more near the number of the degree of the ascendant that came out for us, we follow in that the planet that has more shares in the cardines and in whose domain [it is]. If the distance of the degree of the governing planet from the degrees of the ascendant taken roughly is greater than its distance from the degrees of the middle of the sky, [we take it for the middle of the sky] and set up the other cardines from it.

One should look at the latitude of the planet, since it does not stay on the belt of the signs, so that the degree of the sphere of the signs with which the planet rises, and the degree with which it culminates, are known, because of the latitude, as we have explained at the beginning of the book. Then we find the degree of the ascendant and the degrees of the middle of the sky that we found approximately from the ascensions. If the degrees of the planet are nearer in number to the degrees of the middle of the sky than to the degrees of the ascendant, we make the number of the degrees of the middle of the sky equal to the degrees with which the planet culminates. Disagreement may arise in what is taken with the astrolabe in various ways, and for that reason this chapter has become a standard for all.
