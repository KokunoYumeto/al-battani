\begin{center}\Large AL-BATTĀNĪ\par\vspace{4mm}\LARGE Commentary on Ptolemy's \emph{Tetrabiblos}\par\vspace{2mm}\large \emph{Sharḥ Kitāb al-Arbaʿ maqālāt fī aḥkām ʿilm al-nujūm}\par\vspace{5mm}\normalsize translated from the Arabic of the Escorial manuscript, árabe 969, ff. 20r–80v\end{center}\vspace{8mm}
\noindent This is an English translation of al-Battānī's commentary on the \emph{Tetrabiblos}, the earliest that survives. It is made from Nadine Löhr's transcription of the Escorial manuscript in \emph{Ptolemaeus Arabus et Latinus} (Bavarian Academy of Sciences and Humanities, 2021, CC BY 4.0). The commentary follows Ptolemy's work chapter by chapter, restating his doctrine and adding al-Battānī's own explanations, examples and procedures; the notes give the chapter of the \emph{Tetrabiblos} in F.~E. Robbins's numbering, which was used as a check on the meaning, and explain terms. Every sentence of the Arabic is translated, in the order of the text; numbers are given in figures. Words added for the sense stand in square brackets; words supplied by the editor of the transcription in angle brackets ⟨ ⟩. Words that the editor marks as additions of the copyist, such as invocations, stand in double brackets ⟦ ⟧, and words struck out by the copyist are left out. A doubtful reading of the transcription is marked (?). The folios of the manuscript are given in the margin.

\begin{center}\Fol{20r}The Book of the Four Treatises on the Judgements of the Stars, by the lord of the sages, Ptolemy:\end{center}
\begin{center}the commentary of Abū ʿAbd Allāh Muḥammad ibn Jābir Abū Sinān al-Ḥarrānī, known as al-Battānī\footnote{So the manuscript, «Abū Sinān», for «ibn Sinān» (PAL: sic).}\end{center}

\ChapterHead{{\mdseries ⟨}1{\mdseries ⟩} On knowing the power of the sun, the moon, the wandering stars and the fixed stars}{01}{}{\footnote{Ptolemy, \emph{Tetrabiblos} I.4 and I.9. The chapters of the \emph{Tetrabiblos} are cited in F. E. Robbins's numbering.}}
\par Abū ʿAbd Allāh Muḥammad ibn Jābir al-Ḥarrānī, known as al-Battānī, said: The action of the substance of the sun is to dry and to heat, the drying being slight. As for the moon, most of the action of its power is moistening, and it changes bodies visibly, because it ripens and rots most of what is in them; it also shares a little in heat with the sun, because it receives light from it. Saturn acts mostly by cooling, and it dries a little, because of its cold and dryness. Jupiter is of a temperate mixture, for it heats and moistens together, and it produces fertilizing winds. Mars dries above all, and most of the action of its power is burning, because its nature is burning, its colour being like the colour of fire. Venus is of a temperate mixture, except that it moistens much and heats a little. As for Mercury, its moistening and its drying are equal: at some times it dries and absorbs moisture, at others it moistens, and it passes quickly from one state to the other; and it generates winds.\footnote{«Fire» reads \RL{\ArNote النار} with the Arabic \emph{Tetrabiblos} (PAL's T); the manuscript has \RL{\ArNote العار}, «shame».}\par
\par As for the powers of the fixed stars, from their mixtures, compared with the powers and natures of the wandering stars as they were found for them when they were observed: we have set them down in the tables of the positions of the fixed stars, in a book other than this, and drawn beside each star a mark by which the wandering stars that its nature resembles are known. Some resemble in their mixture the mixture of one wandering star; some resemble the mixture of two stars equally; and some resemble one of two stars much and the other a little. Their natures resemble [the wandering stars] as the marks found there show, and for that reason we can dispense with mentioning their powers here.\footnote{«Beside» reads \RL{\ArNote إزاء} for the transcription's \RL{\ArNote إذا}. Ptolemy gives the powers of the fixed stars in \emph{Tetrabiblos} I.9. The star catalogue of al-Battānī's \emph{Zīj}, as Nallino edits it from the Escorial codex, has no such marks.}\par
\ChapterHead{{\mdseries ⟨}2{\mdseries ⟩} On the powers of the planets according to the configurations they have relative to the sun}{02}{}{\footnote{\emph{Tetrabiblos} I.8.}}
\par \Fol{21r}The moon and the three upper planets receive increase and decrease in their particular powers according to the configurations they have with the sun. From the time when the moon appears as a crescent to the time of its first half in light, it produces moisture above all; from its first half to its full, when it is in opposition to the sun, heat; from its full to its second half in light, dryness; and from its second half to its disappearance, cold.\par
\par Saturn, Jupiter and Mars, from the time of their rising in the mornings and their appearing from under the rays of the sun to the time of their first station, moisten more; from their first station to their rising at the beginning of the night, which is when they are in opposition to the sun, they heat more; from their rising at the beginning of the night to their second station, they dry more; and from their second station to their setting and disappearance, they cool more. When they mix with one another, they produce in the air very many kinds of very different conditions, and these conditions are called the causes of the stars.\par
\ChapterHead{{\mdseries ⟨}3{\mdseries ⟩} On the powers of the four seasons of the year and of the ages of animals}{03}{}{\footnote{\emph{Tetrabiblos} I.10.}}
\par In spring moisture prevails, in summer heat, in autumn dryness and in winter cold. In the same way, in the first age of animals the moist substance prevails; in the second age, up to the time of the prime, heat; in the third age, when it nears the decline and begins to decrease, dryness; and in the last of the ages, when it nears dissolution, cold: like spring, summer, autumn and winter.\par
\ChapterHead{{\mdseries ⟨}4{\mdseries ⟩} On the powers of the four angles and the four quarters of the horizon}{04}{}{\footnote{\emph{Tetrabiblos} I.10.}}
\par So too is the condition of the four quarters of the horizon and of the angles from which the winds blow to all sides. Dryness prevails in the quarter next to the east, and the wind that blows from it, the east wind, which is called Apeliotes, is without moisture and drying. The winds that blow from the side of the south, which are called southern winds, are hot and burning. The quarter of the west is moist, and the winds that blow from it, which are called western, soften and moisten. The quarter of the north is very cold, and the winds that blow from this quarter, which are called northern, are cold and condensing.\par
\par \Fol{21v}The knowledge of these matters is also useful and helps to distinguish the mixtures. For it is clear that the actions of the power in the stars change with the conditions of the seasons of the year, of the ages or of the angles; that when the conditions are akin to them, their qualities are pure and unmixed, with nothing mingled in them, and their action is stronger, as the stars that heat by nature are stronger in hot conditions and the moist stars stronger in moist conditions; and that in the opposites of these conditions their power is not pure and unmixed but, being mixed with something else, weak, as the power of the heating stars is weaker in cold conditions and that of the moist stars weaker in dry conditions. The natures of the fixed stars that mix with those stars, or share with them in one of the nine configurations that they have, especially with the sun, which were mentioned earlier in this book, increase this, decrease it and change it not a little. In the same way the change in all the other conditions follows the mixing of the qualities.\par
\ChapterHead{{\mdseries ⟨}5{\mdseries ⟩} On the fixed, the solstitial and the bicorporeal signs}{05}{}{\footnote{\emph{Tetrabiblos} I.11.}}
\par Of the signs, two are solstitial and two are equinoctial. The solstitial ones are Cancer and Capricorn, and the equinoctial ones Aries and Libra. These signs are given these names because when the sun comes to the point of the summer solstice it brings about summer; when it comes to the point of the winter solstice, winter; when it comes to the point of the spring equinox, spring; and when it comes to the point of the autumn equinox, autumn; and at these two points night and day are equal over the whole earth.\par
\par Of the eight remaining signs, which lie between the points of the equinoxes and of the solstices, four are fixed. They are called fixed because the season stays fixed in them in one state; they are the ones that follow the two signs of the equinoxes and the two signs of the solstices, and they are called Taurus, Scorpio, Leo and Aquarius. The bicorporeal signs are those that follow the fixed signs. They are given this name because they lie between the fixed signs and the signs of the equinox and the solstice, and share, as it were, with their beginnings in the fixed signs and with their ends in the changing signs; they are Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius and Pisces.\par
\ChapterHead{{\mdseries ⟨}6{\mdseries ⟩} On the sharing of the signs in the configurations}{06}{}{\footnote{\emph{Tetrabiblos} I.13–17.}}
\par \Fol{22r}As for the matters of the sharings in the configurations, which are the conjunction, the opposition, the quadrature, the sextile and the trine; of the signs that look at one another and are equal in power; of the commanding and the obeying signs; and of the signs that are not joined to one another and do not share in the configurations: we have explained them earlier in this book, since [there was given] the cause that shows why these distances alone are used and none other of the geometrical things. So too the others: the contrary and associated signs are those opposite one another; the hostile signs are those in quadrature to one another; and the friendly signs are those in trine to one another, and also those in sextile, since these two configurations fall between signs of one kind, either masculine or feminine, while the first two configurations are otherwise. Moreover, the houses of the two maleficent planets are in opposition and in quadrature to the houses of the luminaries, and the houses of the two beneficent planets in trine and in sextile to the houses of the luminaries.\footnote{Ptolemy explains why only these distances are used in \emph{Tetrabiblos} I.13, al-Battānī in chapter 54 of his \emph{Zīj}. «The two maleficent planets» reads \RL{\ArNote النحسين} for the transcription's \RL{\ArNote الجنسين}: the houses of Saturn, Capricorn and Aquarius, are opposite those of the moon and the sun, Cancer and Leo, and the houses of Mars, Aries and Scorpio, in quadrature to them (\emph{Tetrabiblos} I.17).}\par
\ChapterHead{{\mdseries ⟨}7{\mdseries ⟩} On the masculine and feminine signs, their other conditions and the names given to them}{07}{}{\footnote{\emph{Tetrabiblos} I.12. Ptolemy declines to list the epithets that al-Battānī gives below.}}
\par The signs feminine and masculine by nature we have explained in mentioning the triplicities. Some people have used another way of ordering the masculine and feminine signs, from the sign of the ascendant: they counted one masculine and one feminine, and made the beginning from the masculine; just as some people take the solstitial signs beginning from the sign of the moon, because the moon, through the speed of its motion, turns more than the other stars. Some of them divided the signs into four parts and held that the signs attributed to the mornings, the masculine ones, are those from the ascendant to the middle of the sky and those opposite them, from the setting point to the cardine of the earth; and they held that the two remaining quarters are attributed to the evenings and are feminine, so they made the signs that fall in these two quarters feminine and attributed them to the evenings.\par
\par \Fol{22v}They have given the signs many epithets, taken from the figures to which they are attributed: they called some of them four-footed, some wild, some governing, domestic, of much sowing, of little sowing and the like. They said that the domestic signs are Aries and Taurus; the wild, Leo and Sagittarius; the speaking signs, which resemble human beings, Gemini, Aquarius, Libra, Virgo and the first half of Sagittarius; the winged, Virgo, Sagittarius, Gemini and Pisces; the \emph{maʿqūma}, Aries, Taurus, Scorpio, Capricorn and Cancer; those of few offspring and little sowing, Aries, Taurus, Libra, Sagittarius, Capricorn and Aquarius; those of many offspring, Cancer, Scorpio and Pisces; the barren, Gemini, Leo and Virgo; those with mutilated limbs, Aries, Taurus, Leo and Scorpio; those of much anger, Aries and Leo; those that signify beauty and comeliness, Gemini, Libra, Virgo, Scorpio, Sagittarius and Pisces; those that gather and fill, Aries and its triplicity; those that give great wealth, Capricorn and its triplicity; those that pour out and empty, Libra and its triplicity; and those that grasp and take, Cancer and its triplicity. The signs of a strong voice are Gemini, Virgo and Libra; the moderate, which have half a voice, Aries, Taurus, Leo and Sagittarius; those of a weak voice, Capricorn and Aquarius; and those with no voice, Cancer, Pisces and Scorpio. Those that signify great care are Leo, Scorpio and Capricorn; those of little care, Virgo and Libra, for their darkness (?); and those of cunning and deceit, Leo, Sagittarius, Capricorn and Pisces. And they said that Aries, Taurus and Gemini, when their lords are found in them, {\mdseries ⟨}are{\mdseries ⟩} moving; Cancer, Leo and Virgo, when their lords are found [in them], are at rest; Libra, Scorpio and Sagittarius likewise, when their lords are found in them, are moving; and Capricorn, Aquarius and Pisces likewise, when their lords are found in them, are at rest.\footnote{The meaning of \RL{\ArNote المعقومة}, \emph{maʿqūma}, from the root of barrenness, is not clear; the barren signs follow under their usual name, \RL{\ArNote العقيمة}.}\par
\ChapterHead{{\mdseries ⟨}8{\mdseries ⟩} On the planets' becoming masculine and feminine relative to the sun, and also relative to the horizon}{08}{}{\footnote{\emph{Tetrabiblos} I.6.}}
\par \Fol{23r}They hold that the planets become masculine and feminine according to the configurations they have relative to the sun: when they are eastern, preceding the sun, they are masculine, and when they are western, following it, they are feminine. This also happens relative to the horizon: when they are in the positions from the east to the middle of the sky, or from the west to the cardine of the earth, they are masculine, because these two quarters are masculine; the first of them is called «the swift» and the second «the middle of speed». When they are between the middle of the sky and the west, or between the cardine of the earth and the ascendant, they are feminine, because these two quarters are feminine; the first of them is called «the middle of slowness» (?), and the second, between the ascendant and the cardine of the earth, «the slow and heavy».\footnote{The words from «they are masculine, because» to «or between the cardine of the earth» are written in the margin. «The swift» reads \RL{\ArNote السريع} for \RL{\ArNote الشربع}, and «the slow» \RL{\ArNote البطيء} for \RL{\ArNote البطن}, «the belly».}\par
\ChapterHead{{\mdseries ⟨}9{\mdseries ⟩} On facing, illumination and the like}{09}{}{\footnote{\emph{Tetrabiblos} I.23, where Robbins translates Ptolemy's terms «proper face» and «chariots».}}
\par It is said that the planets have a proper facing when each of them shares in a configuration with the sun and the moon in this way: that there is between it and the sun or the moon the same distance as between its house and the house of the sun or of the moon, except that it is western of the sun, I mean in the following signs, and eastern of the moon, in the preceding signs. For example, Venus in sextile to one of the luminaries, but in the third sign from the sign of the sun in the order of the signs, or in the eleventh sign from the sign in which the moon is; or Mercury in the second sign from the sign of the sun, or in the twelfth from the sign of the moon. For the other planets reckon their houses in the same way. When the planets are in this condition with respect to the luminaries, they are facing them.\footnote{The manuscript first wrote «or in the twelfth from the sign of the moon, and the other planets», struck it out, and wrote the correct passage in the margin, from «in the order of the signs» to «or in the twelfth».}\par
\par As for illumination, it is said [to be] when the planets are in their own domain and in places like it that agree with them; and when one of them has two or more of its dignities, as when it is in its house, its exaltation, its triplicity and its term and the like, [it is said] to be in the place of its joy and honour, and then its power increases in its actions that resemble the signs that belong to it and are akin to them in nature. It is also said that they rejoice when they are in the dignities of planets of their own domain; this is weaker than the first, because it is below it in power, but they share in it in this way all the same: just as, when they are found in foreign places or in a domain contrary to their own, their proper power weakens, and the contrariety of their mixture to the mixture of the signs in which they are becomes a cause for an action compounded of the two different natures to arise from them.\footnote{«Domain», \RL{\ArNote حيّز}, \emph{ḥayyiz}; Ptolemy speaks here of the «sect» of the planets.}\par
\ChapterHead{{\mdseries ⟨}10{\mdseries ⟩} On the familiarity of the triplicities with the countries and the planets}{10}{}{\footnote{\emph{Tetrabiblos} II.3. In the lists of this chapter, names that the Arabic forms allow to be identified are given in English, and «(?)» marks a doubtful identification; names too corrupt to identify are given in Arabic letters as the transcription has them.}}
\par \Fol{23v}Ptolemy divided the whole inhabited earth that lies to the north of the equator by two lines, which cut it in length and in breadth into quarters, and made each quarter akin to the triplicity, among the triplicities of those four [sets of] signs, to which it is attributed. He made the parts of each quarter that lie next to the middle of the whole inhabited earth receive familiarity from the triplicity that governs the angle opposite to that angle, and he made Mercury a partner with the governing planets, because it is of a common domain. He made the familiarity of the countries to be with the planets that are the lords of the houses of those triplicities, together with the signs of the triplicities too. He divided each of these quarters into three parts, by which he marked off the countries in it, so that each of these three parts became akin to one sign of the triplicity, together with the planet that is the lord of that sign. He explained the natures of the nations that live in each of these particular countries, their characters and their conditions, and treated each of the quarters as a whole according to what belongs to it, the triplicities akin to it and the planets that govern those triplicities, and according to the natures of the signs and of the planets that are their lords; he needed to do that, and summed it up in what we can dispense with mentioning here. Then he described the familiarity of each of the countries in each quarter, and of what in it lies next to the middle of the inhabited earth, and classified it separately by the signs and the planets that are the lords of those signs, so that it might be easy to consult, following what he had said before. We have confined ourselves to that and set it out in this way: we began with the countries akin to each sign and its lord, attributed to that third, and followed it with the countries that lie next to the middle of the inhabited world, and we set out in detail what makes each of them known. Then we recorded the familiarity of the cities with the signs and the planets, according to what is said to have been rising at the time of their building, or the positions of the sun and the moon, or the middle of the sky of the kings who lived at the time when they were built.\footnote{«Inhabited» reads \RL{\ArNote المعمورة} for \RL{\ArNote المحمودة}, «praised», and «the cities» \RL{\ArNote المدن} for \RL{\ArNote الدرج}, «the degrees».}\par
\par \Fol{24r}So of the countries and districts, those akin to Aries are the lands of Britain, Bastarnia (?), Germania and Galatia, the land of the Jews and Deep Syria; by sharing with Aries these countries belong to Mars. Those akin to Taurus are the lands of Parthia, Media, Persia, the Cyclades, the coasts of Asia Minor and Cyprus; by their familiarity with Taurus they belong to Venus. Those akin to Gemini are the lands of Jurjān, Ṭabaristān, Armenia, al-Qayrawān, Marmarica, which is below the land of Egypt, and «\RL{\ArNote فورينا}»; by their familiarity with Gemini they belong to Mercury. Those akin to Cancer are Numidia, Carthage (?), Ifrīqiya, «\RL{\ArNote سوسا}» and Phrygia; by their familiarity with Cancer they belong to the moon. Those akin to Leo are Italy, «\RL{\ArNote وعاليلواىولىا}», Sicily, Phoenicia (?) and Chaldaea (?); by their familiarity with Leo they belong to the sun. Those akin to Virgo are Babylon, the Jazīra, the land of Āthūr, «\RL{\ArNote الأمن}», Achaia (?) and Crete; and they are akin to Mercury too. Those akin to Libra are «\RL{\ArNote ينظر بالي}», Casperia (?), Serica, Thebais (?), the Oasis (?) and Troglodytica (?); and they are akin to Venus too, with Libra. Those akin to Scorpio are Metagonitis (?), Mauritania (?), Gaetulia (?), Syria, Commagene (?), «\RL{\ArNote فافا حرٯما}» and the Ḥijāz; and they are akin to Mars too. Those akin to Sagittarius are Tyrrhenia (?), Celtica (?), Spain (?) and also the cultivated land of the Arabs; their familiarity is with Jupiter. Those akin to Capricorn are the land of India, Ariana, «\RL{\ArNote ادىا}», China, Gedrosia (?), Thrace (?), Macedonia and Illyria (?); and they are akin to Saturn. Those akin to Aquarius are Sarmatica (?), «\RL{\ArNote طلساي}», Sogdiana (?), «\RL{\ArNote ارانياىا}» and the Middle «\RL{\ArNote ىىوىا}», meaning the middle land of the Abyssinians; and they are akin to Saturn too. Those akin to Pisces are Phazania (?), «\RL{\ArNote ٯعلاعرىىا}», Nasamonitis (?), Garamantica (?), Lydia (?), Cilicia (?) and Pamphylia (?); and they are akin to Jupiter, with Pisces. All of these are «\RL{\ArNote ما يميّزه}» and ninety regions, of those in the four [quarters] mentioned and of those next to the middle of the inhabited world.\footnote{Ptolemy's list (\emph{Tetrabiblos} II.3, at the end): Aries, Britain, Gaul, Germania, Bastarnia, and in the centre Coele Syria, Palestine, Idumaea, Judaea; Taurus, Parthia, Media, Persia, and the Cyclades, Cyprus, the coast of Asia Minor; Gemini, Hyrcania, Armenia, Matiana, and Cyrenaica, Marmarica, Lower Egypt; Cancer, Numidia, Carthage, Africa, and Bithynia, Phrygia, Colchica; Leo, Italy, Cisalpine Gaul, Sicily, Apulia, and Phoenicia, Chaldaea, Orchenia; Virgo, Mesopotamia, Babylonia, Assyria, and Hellas, Achaia, Crete; Libra, Bactriana, Casperia, Serica, and Thebais, Oasis, Troglodytica; Scorpio, Metagonitis, Mauritania, Gaetulia, and Syria, Commagene, Cappadocia; Sagittarius, Tyrrhenia, Celtica, Spain, and Arabia Felix; Capricorn, India, Ariana, Gedrosia, and Thrace, Macedonia, Illyria; Aquarius, Sauromatica, Oxiana, Sogdiana, and Arabia, Azania, Middle Ethiopia; Pisces, Phazania, Nasamonitis, Garamantica, and Lydia, Cilicia, Pamphylia. The Arabic gives Hyrcania as Jurjān, Cyrenaica as al-Qayrawān, Mesopotamia as the Jazīra and Coele Syria as «Deep Syria». The number at the end is corrupt; the Berlin manuscript has «two hundred».}\par
\par One must add to this as well that each of the fixed stars is akin to the countries to which the parts of those signs are akin, and they all act on them: these are the parts that the stars face and that correspond to them on one of the circles that pass through the poles of those signs; this is evident from the latitudes of the stars when their passage is above the zenith. This is the account of what we have described concerning the cities.\par
\par \Fol{24v}The cities of the Ḥijāz: Scorpio. Mecca: Libra and Saturn. The City of the Messenger, peace be upon him: Scorpio and Venus. Al-Rabadha: Taurus and the moon. Al-Thaʿlabiyya (?): Sagittarius and Mercury. The cities of Yemen: Sagittarius. Ṣanʿāʾ: Gemini and Mercury. Al-Janad (?): Pisces and Mars. ʿUmān: Taurus and Venus.\footnote{Ptolemy ends \emph{Tetrabiblos} II.3 by relating each city to the signs that the sun, the moon and the ascendant occupied at its foundation, or to the middle of the sky of its rulers; al-Battānī's list of cities is not in Ptolemy. In it «the Head» (\RL{\ArNote الرأس}) stands several times in the place of a sign; whether the ascending node or a sign written in a corrupt form is meant is not clear.}\par
\par The cities of Iraq: Leo. Basra: Pisces and Mercury. Kufa: Virgo and Jupiter. Baghdad: Taurus and Mercury. Wāsiṭ: Pisces and Jupiter. Daskarat al-Malik: Leo and Jupiter. Al-Madāʾin: Leo and Jupiter. Al-Sūs: the Head and the moon.\par
\par The cities of Māh: Taurus. Ḥulwān: Taurus and the moon. Al-Dīnawar: the Head and the sun. Nahāwand: Scorpio and Mars. Hamadhān: Taurus and Venus. Al-Rayy (?): Taurus and Saturn. Nahāwand: the Head and the moon. Al-Daylam: Scorpio and the moon. Ṭabaristān: Capricorn and the moon. Sāriya of Ṭabaristān: Aries and Venus. The city of Jurjān: the Head and Jupiter. Qūmis: Capricorn and the moon. Iṣfahān: the Head and the sun. Nīsābūr: Aries and Jupiter. Nasā: the Head and the sun. Abīward: Libra and Mercury. Ṭūs: Aries and Mercury. Sarakhs: Virgo and the moon. Marw al-Rūdh: Gemini and Saturn. Al-Ṭālaqān: Libra and Mars. Marw: Pisces and Saturn. Balkh: Libra and Mars. «\RL{\ArNote أحل}» of Khurāsān: Taurus and the moon. Khwārazm: Gemini and Saturn. Samarqand: Aries and Mercury. «\RL{\ArNote الدسقوته}»: Libra and Mars. Al-Shāsh: Virgo and the sun. Farghāna: Taurus and Jupiter. \ChapterHead{{\mdseries ⟨}11{\mdseries ⟩} On knowing the events that occur in the countries from eclipses}{11}{}{\footnote{\emph{Tetrabiblos} II.4–8.}}
\par \Fol{27r}We begin by examining the place of the eclipse in the sphere of the signs: to which of the triplicities it belongs, and to which of the regions that triplicity is attributed. Then we turn to the countries and cities that are akin to [the sign of] the eclipse in particular, in that triplicity, as we have set out, and see whether the eclipse is seen in all those places or in some of them; for in those of them where the eclipse [does not] appear nothing happens. We set up the ascendant of the middle of the eclipse and its cardines for the places among those countries where we know that it is seen, reckoning the hours and the altitude of the pole there. After that we infer the time of the event and the length of its duration by seeing how many equal hours there are from the beginning of the darkening of the eclipse to the end of the clearing in each of those places. If the eclipse is solar, the event lasts as many years as there are hours; if it is lunar, as many months. If the place of the eclipse is on the eastern side of the horizon, we know that the event begins in the first third of that period and that its greatest intensity falls in that first third; if it is on the side of the middle of the sky, we infer that it is in the second third of the period; and if on the western side of the horizon, in its last third. We determine the place of the eclipse relative to the horizon as I shall describe to you. If the place of the eclipse is less than 4 seasonal hours from the ascendant towards the middle of the sky, it is on the eastern side; if it is past the 4 hours, up to the cardine of the sky and 2 seasonal hours beyond it, it is on the side of the middle of the sky; and if it is past that, it is on the western side. In this you proceed on the basis that if it is in the very degree of the ascendant, [the event] is at the beginning of the period; if it is in the degree of the middle of the sky, in the middle of the period; and if it is in the very degree of the setting point, the event is at the end of the period.\footnote{«[Does not]» supplies a negation that the sense requires: Ptolemy considers the countries where the eclipse is visible (\emph{Tetrabiblos} II.5–6). The division of the sky by seasonal hours is al-Battānī's addition.}\par
\par The particular increases and decreases in the events are known from the conjunctions and oppositions that occur in the meantime, if they happen in the places that are the cause of the events or in places that share in a configuration with them, I mean the places of the eclipse and of the cardines and the places that share in a configuration with them; and from the positions of the very wandering stars that are in charge of the governing, when they appear eastern or western, or stationary, or rising at the beginning of the night, and share in a configuration with the signs of the eclipse or the signs of the cardines, which are the cause of the events. For if they are eastern or stationary, an increase of the events comes from them; and if they are western, or under the rays, and rising at the beginning of the night [and] retrograde, a decrease of the events comes from them. Look also at the decrease of the upper planets and their staying in each of the times, I mean the stations of Saturn, Jupiter and Mars, when they are stationary and are the lords of the indication through their dominion over the places of the eclipses and of the cardines, in the way that we shall mention.\par
\par \Fol{27v}The class in which the event occurs is known from the conditions and the forms proper to the signs in which the eclipses occur, and in which are the wandering and the non-wandering stars that govern the sign of the eclipse; and from the governing star, and the sign of the cardine that precedes the eclipse. This cardine, which the eclipsed luminary passes before the time of the eclipse, is either the ascending cardine or the cardine of the middle of the sky, whichever it happens to pass before the eclipse. The governor of these two places among the wandering stars is known as I shall describe to you. Look for the planet that has many shares in these two signs that we have mentioned: either it is conjoined with the degree of the eclipse or the degree of the cardine, or is coming to it, or has just left it (?); or it shares in a configuration with it by one of the connections, I mean the trine, the sextile, the quadrature, the opposition and the like; [and look] at the dignities it has in the signs, by being the lord of the house, of the exaltation, of the triplicity and of the terms. If all this comes together in one planet, it alone is the lord of the governing. If no single planet is found that is the lord both of the eclipse and of the cardine, one should take together the two planets that have the most familiarities with each of the two places, as said before, choosing first the sign of the eclipse and putting it first. If the planets that have shares in these two places are more than two, and what each of them has in the two places is equal to what the other has, we choose for the governing, and put first, the one of them that has the better claim to be said to be in the cardine, or is stronger in action, or is stronger in that domain.\par
\par \Fol{28r}We know the governors among the fixed stars as well. We begin with the star that was with the place of the eclipse in the cardine that came before the eclipse: we mean, among the bright stars, the star that rose with the degree of the eclipse or culminated with the degree of the eclipse, in whichever of these two cardines one of the luminaries passed before its eclipse — we mean the great stars — […] on men and on kings. Likewise, if at the time of the eclipse they are in the places that he described, in the east in particular, they signify that the event will be in the fruits, in the age of youth, in foundations and in whoever is young in age; if they are in the middle of the sky above the earth, they signify that the event will be in the temples, in the kings and in those of middle age; and if they are in the west, they signify that the event will be in the matter of the customs and their changes, and in those of the age of old men, and the dead (?).\footnote{Something seems to be lost before «on men and on kings»: Ptolemy's account of the classes signified by the forms of the signs ends with the bicorporeal signs, which concern men and kings (\emph{Tetrabiblos} II.7). «Customs» reads \RL{\ArNote السنن} for \RL{\ArNote السنين}, «the years».}\par
\par The size of the part of the class concerned in which the event occurs is known from the amount of the darkening of the eclipse and from the relation of the planets in charge of the governing to the place of the eclipses. When they are in configurations attributed to the evenings at eclipses of the sun, or in configurations attributed to the mornings at eclipses of the moon, the events are in the smaller part of that class; if they are in opposition, on the diameter, the event concerns half of that class; and if they are in configurations attributed to the mornings at eclipses of the sun, or attributed to the evenings at eclipses of the moon, it is in the greater part of that class. Whether what happens is good or the opposite is known from the nature of the governing, chief planets that bring about the event, from their mixture with one another and from the places in which they are. For the sun and the moon are two princes and chiefs of the other stars, in that they are the cause of all the action, the cause of the governing of the stars, and the cause of the strength and weakness of the governing stars.\par
\par \Fol{28v}The consideration of the mixture of the planets in charge of the governing with one another is what shows the condition of the events that occur. Saturn, when it alone is the lord of the governing, is in general the cause of corruption that comes from cold. As for its particular action proper to it: if the event is in men, it produces long illnesses, consumption, wasting, harm from humours and the flowing of superfluities, quartan fevers, flight, perplexity, imprisonment, grief, fear and death, especially for those advanced in age. If the event is in the beasts that men use much, the harm to them is their scarcity: corruption of their bodies and diseases befall those of them that exist, so that, because of that, something like these illnesses befalls the men who eat these beasts. If the event is in the air, there comes severe cold with frost and fog, heat (?) that brings pestilence, badness of the air, thickness of the clouds, gloom and destructive snowstorms, and from this many vermin are generated that are harmful to the nature of men. If the indication is in the rivers and the seas, there comes, in what they have in common, wintry weather, the sinking of ships, difficulty in travelling on the water, and scarcity and corruption of fish; in the seas in particular ebb and flood, and in the rivers in particular excessive rising and badness of the waters. If the events are in the fruits that grow from the earth, they suffer want, scarcity and corruption, especially those that men need of necessity, and the corruption that befalls them comes from caterpillars or from locusts, or from floods, or from heavy rains, or from hail and the like, so that at times drought comes of it, and men perish because of it.\par
\par Jupiter, when it alone is the lord of the governing, in general makes things grow. Its particular actions in what happens are, in men, elevation of rank, good condition, plenty, a state of safety and security, the growth of necessary things, the good condition of bodies and souls, rewards and gifts from kings, the increase of the affairs of kings, their greatness and their magnanimity; in general it is the cause of good fortunes. In beasts, if the event is in them, those that men use much multiply and increase greatly, and those of the opposite kind decay and perish. As for its indication in the air, [the air] is temperate, healthful, windy and moist, and it makes the fruits that grow from the earth increase; the passage of ships at sea is safe, the risings of rivers are moderate, and all things like these. Whenever its indication of the event is in one of the kinds named, it sets it right, and what comes of it is good, moderate, agreeable, praiseworthy and conducive to well-being.\par
\par \Fol{29r}Mars, when it alone is the lord of the governing, is in general the cause of the corruption that comes from dryness. Its particular actions: if the event is in men, wars arise among them, and seditions between a people and those of their own kind, captivity and slavery, the uprising of the mass of the people and the wrath of the leaders; from these things sudden death befalls some people, and illnesses with tertian fevers and the spitting of blood, and swift and violent death, especially for those in the age of the prime. Likewise some suffer robbery, contempt, aggression, lawlessness, arson, the killing of men, plunder and highway robbery. If the event is in the air, there come severe heat, hot, pestilential and harmful winds, the falling of thunderbolts and of the thunderbolt that is called \emph{prēstēr}, and scarcity of rain. If it is at sea, there comes the sudden sinking of ships through changeable and turbulent winds, through thunderbolts and the like, thunder, burning and what resembles them. In the rivers there come a decrease of the water, the drying up of springs and the corruption of drinking water. In the animals and plants that are fit for the use of men there comes scarcity, and the corruption of the fruits, which is produced by burning «\RL{\ArNote احرًّا ىاها}», or by the pestilence of locusts, or by the scattering that the winds bring upon them, or by their burning in the stores in which they are kept.\footnote{\emph{Prēstēr} renders \RL{\ArNote فريطس}, the Greek πρηστήρ, a fiery whirlwind; Robbins translates «hurricanes».}\par
\par Venus in general produces something like what Jupiter produces, and in what comes from her there is, besides, a stirring of matters of intercourse, of amusement and of the affairs of women. Her particular action, in men, is elevation of rank, joy, good condition, the soundness of marriage, many children, the success of all matters of union, the increase of possessions, clean and pious conduct, goodness of conduct in matters of religion, health of the bodies, connection with leaders, and marriage alliances with governors. In the condition of the air temperate, moist and very nourishing winds come, and the air is good and clear; the fertilizing waters and the rains she makes abundant; ships come through safely at sea; and from her come successes in trade, profits, and the rising and fullness of the rivers. The animals that men use, and the fruits of the earth in particular, increase, flourish and are of use; and whenever the event falls in any kind of these kinds, its condition is praiseworthy and useful to men.\par
\par \Fol{29v}Mercury, when it alone is the lord of the governing: in general, when it mixes with any of the other planets, it is akin to their natures (?). Its proper action is to set things in motion, more than the other planets. Its action in the events concerning men is sharp, strong and subtle in contriving the thing it aims at; it is a maker of highway robbery, thefts, brigandage and raids, and it also causes difficulty in sea voyages when it shares in a configuration with the maleficent planets. It is the cause of dry illnesses, of fevers that come every day, of coughing, spitting and consumption; and [the cause] of the matters of divination, of the worship of God, blessed and exalted be He, of what is levied on the revenues of the temples, and of the change of laws and customs at some times, in proportion to its mixing with each of the planets. Since its mixture is dry and quick in motion, because of its nearness to the sun and the speed of its return in its revolutions, it produces in the air in particular turbulent, swift and easily changing winds, thunder, the thunderbolt that is called \emph{prēstēr}, the sinking of the ground, earthquakes and lightning; and sometimes, because of these things I have mentioned, corruption comes to the animals and plants that men use much. At the times of its setting it causes a decrease in the waters and the rivers, and at the times of its rising it increases them.\par
\par \Fol{30r}Each of these planets, when it is in the natural condition proper to it, produces things such as these. When they are mixed with one another, according to their sharings in the configurations, the diversity of the signs, their appearances relative to the sun, and their separations and meetings, then, from the mixing that there is in their actions too, their proper action becomes of different kinds accordingly, and mixed from the natures that have shared in its nature. Since the description of the proper action that comes from each kind of mixture is without end and impossible, and, in general, the mention of all that, in whatever configurations it happens to be, may be understood in many ways, we must leave this kind to the one who considers it, so that he may examine the particulars in it. One must look at the familiarity of the countries and cities with the planets from which the events that befall them come.\par
\par For if those planets are beneficial and akin to the places in which the events occur, and the maleficent planets contrary to them do not overpower them, their action proper to their natures is more beneficial; just as, if they are not akin to those countries and the maleficent planets overpower them, their benefit is less. If their mixture is maleficent and harmful and they are the agents of the event, then if they are akin to the countries in which the events occur and the beneficent planets overpower them, their harm is slight; and if they are not akin to the countries and the beneficent planets do not overpower them, the effect of their corrupting mixture on them is greater.\par
\par His words «the planets overpower them» admit many meanings and senses. One of them: a planet is in conjunction with a planet; then the one of the two with the greater latitude in the north overpowers the other; if both are in the south, the one with the smaller latitude overpowers; and if one is in the north and the other in the south, the northern overpowers the one in the south. Another: a planet is akin to a planet by the configuration of quadrature; then the one in the preceding signs overpowers, because it is in the tenth sign from the sign in which the other is, which is the middle of the sky of that sign. Another: the overpowering is in the way that we mentioned at the beginning of the book, so that one of them is in the high signs and the other in the low signs, and their distances from the two equinoctial points are one and the same. Another: one of them is in a cardine and the other declining from the cardine, or one of them is above the earth and the other below the earth. In general, when the beneficent planets look at the maleficent, from whatever configuration it may be, they diminish their evil; and when the maleficent look at the beneficent, they diminish their benefit, especially if it is from quadrature and opposition. What he mostly means by overpowering is that a planet follows a planet and rises to its place: that is, when it rises after it, so that the first declines and the other remains in its place and overpowers it.\footnote{The high and the low signs at equal distances from the equinoctial points are the commanding and the obeying signs of chapter 6 (\emph{Tetrabiblos} I.14).}\par
\par \Fol{30v}For the most part the general accidents befall people when [those places] are the necessary places of their nativities, I mean the places in which the luminaries are, or the places of the cardines, in the nativities; for these are the places of the things that are the causes of the general accidents, whose causes are the eclipses occurring in those places or in the places opposite them. It is most severe and hardest to guard against when the degrees of the place of the eclipses occurring in those places, or in the places opposite them, are the degrees of the places of the luminaries in the nativity, and next the degrees opposite them. The most severe of them is an eclipse of the sun, because it can only occur at the revolution of the year, while the moon may be eclipsed in its own place in the root [nativity] at the time of the revolution.\par
\ChapterHead{{\mdseries ⟨}12{\mdseries ⟩} On the colours of eclipses and the stars with tails}{12}{}{\footnote{\emph{Tetrabiblos} II.9.}}
\par One should also consider the colours that appear at the times of eclipses, I mean the colours of the luminaries themselves and the colours of the things around them, when such arise around them, like those called rods, staffs, haloes and the like. If they are black, dense and inclining to green, they signify the things that we have said are of the nature of Saturn; if white, the things of the nature of Jupiter; if inclining to red, the things of the nature of Mars; if tawny and golden, the things of the nature of Venus; and if of varied colours, the things of the nature of Mercury. If the colour is over the whole body of the luminaries, or in all the places around them, the event will be in most parts of the countries; if it is in one part of the luminaries or of what is around them, whatever part it may be, the event befalls only the region that faces the part in which the colour is.\par
\par \Fol{31r}You should also look at the stars with tails that appear at the times of eclipses or at other times, and at the other general phenomena that arise, like those called staffs, trumpets, jars and the like; and take their natures, in what they produce, to be the same as what we said the nature of Mars and the nature of Mercury produce: wars, burning, severe heat, earthquakes and the accidents that come from them. The places in which the events occur are inferred from their positions in the sphere of the signs, or from the places that the direction of the tail of the stars faces. From the form of the phenomenon that appears one infers the kind of event that occurs; the class in which the event occurs, from the positions in the sphere of the signs in which that thing appears; the duration of the events, from the time for which the phenomenon that appears lasts; and the time of the beginning of the event, from its relation to the sun. \ChapterHead{{\mdseries ⟨}13{\mdseries ⟩} On knowing the conditions of the year}{13}{}{\footnote{\emph{Tetrabiblos} II.10–12.}}
\par In determining the year we should use the entry of the sun into the beginnings of the solstitial and equinoctial signs, I mean the points of the two equinoxes and the two solstices, which are the beginnings of the seasons of the year; and examine the conjunctions and oppositions of the sun and the moon that occur near these beginnings, before these times, and especially those of them at which an eclipse occurs. So from the beginning [taken] at the sun's entry into Aries it may be known what the spring will be like; from the beginning found at its entry into Libra, what the autumn will be like; from the beginning found at its entry into Cancer, what the summer will be like; and from its entry into Capricorn, what the winter will be like; since these general times and their conditions come only from the sun.\footnote{«Examine» follows the Berlin manuscript (\RL{\ArNote تتفقّد}); the transcription has \RL{\ArNote تنعقد}.}\par
\par \Fol{31v}In this we should use the properties of the signs, and infer from them the winds that blow from the quarters and their natures in general. The changes towards increase and decrease at some times are also inferred, in general, from the conjunctions and oppositions that occur after the sun's entry into the beginnings of these signs, that is, the conjunctions and oppositions that follow these points, through the wandering stars' sharing in configurations with them. The particular matters that occur in one of the quarters [of the year] are inferred from the conjunctions that occur in each of the signs and from the positions of the planets; and this may be called the examination of the months. In this subject, besides what we have said, one needs to know the particular natural properties of each of the signs as they bear on the conditions of the year. The mixtures of the wandering stars, and of the fixed stars whose mixtures are like those of the wandering ones, and their familiarity with the air and the winds, we have mentioned before, and likewise the familiarity of all the signs with the winds; it remains only to report their particular natures and their familiarity with the seasons, I mean the familiarity of each sign.\par
\par \Fol{32r}Aries is on the circle of the equator, and the nature of the sign of Aries as a whole, because of the equinox that is in it, generates thunder and lightning; its northern part is hot and corrupting, and its southern part generates frost and is cold. Taurus is nearer to the summer circle; the sign of Taurus as a whole signifies both mixtures, inclining to heat; its northern part is of a temperate mixture, and its southern part generates disturbance and earthquakes. Gemini is nearer to the summer circle; its nature as a whole produces a temperate mixture; its northern part generates winds and produces earthquakes, and its southern part is fiery and burning. Cancer is on the summer circle; as a whole it produces fair weather and is hot, and its northern and southern parts are fiery and burning. Leo is nearer to the summer circle; as a whole it is burning, with hot, stagnant air and pestilence; its northern part is stirring and fiery — he means that it stirs up hot winds — and its southern part moistens. Virgo is nearer to the circle of the equinox; as a whole it moistens and produces thunder; its northern part produces winds, and its southern part is of a temperate mixture. Libra is on the circle of the equator; as a whole it is changeable and turning; its northern part generates sand (?), and its southern part moistens and produces pestilence. Scorpio is nearer to the winter circle; its nature as a whole produces thunder and is fiery; its northern part is fiery and burning, and its southern part moistens. Sagittarius is nearer to the winter circle; as a whole it produces winds; its northern part produces winds, and its southern part is very moist and changeable. Capricorn is on the winter circle; as a whole it is very moist, and its northern and southern parts are moist and very corrupting. Aquarius is nearer to the winter circle; as a whole it is cold and watery; its northern part is burning, and its southern part produces burning. Pisces is nearer to the circle of the equator; as a whole it is cold and generates winds; its northern part generates winds, and its southern part is watery.\footnote{Ptolemy (\emph{Tetrabiblos} II.11) also divides each sign into a leading, a middle and a following part, which al-Battānī leaves out. For the northern part of Libra Ptolemy has «windy», and for the southern part of Aquarius «cloudy»; for Cancer the transcription has \RL{\ArNote للصحر}, read \RL{\ArNote للصحو}, «fair weather».}\par
\par \Fol{32v}The nature of the parts of the signs is known from the mixture of the fixed stars in each sign, among the stars in the figures of the sphere of the signs and those outside them, according to their sizes in magnitude and the likeness of their natures to the natures of the wandering stars. Among the nebulous stars in the sphere of the signs, those like the Pleiades, whose mixture is first of the moon and second of Mars, produce earthquakes, winds and fog; the nebulous one in the figure of Cancer, which is called the Manger, whose mixture is first that of Mars and second the moon, produces hot, stagnant air, earthquakes and gloom; the nebulous one that follows the Shawla of the Scorpion, whose mixture is that of the sun and then of Mars, produces earthquakes, and so does the nebulous one on the eye of the Archer. What is of the mixture of Mars, [like] al-Dabarān, which is of the greatest magnitude, is fiery and generates lightning and thunderbolts. [Stars] like the Heart of the Lion, whose mixture is first that of Mars and then of Jupiter, produce stagnant hot air and pestilence. Those of them of the mixture of Venus alone signify much moisture and rain; those of the mixture of Jupiter, moderate heat and moisture; those of the mixture of Saturn alone, cold; and those of the mixture of Mercury alone now dry and now moisten, and generate winds, according to what we have mentioned of the powers of the wandering stars and of its familiarity with the winds. After that their natures are mixed with the natures of the signs in which they are and with the natures of the seasons of the year. The moist stars in the western quarter [are moister], and likewise the hot stars in summer increase in heat through their familiarity with the season, and when they are in the southern quarter their heat is greatest. When the signs in which they are suit their natures, that increases their action more; likewise, when they incline in latitude to the north or to the south, that increases or decreases their action, according to how far their natures suit that direction. When the mixture of the stars is attributed to two of the wandering stars, the first of the two is stronger in action, and the second mixes with it afterwards, according to the marks, for some of them resemble the second star only a little in their mixture; [and it depends] on how the wandering stars connect with one another when they take over the governing, in the familiarity of their natures with one another and in their contrariety; so that from the whole of what comes together from these mixtures you arrive at the condition that will be, and give the preponderance in power to what is most manifest in action and strongest in position.\footnote{«Nebulous» reads \RL{\ArNote السحابيّة} for \RL{\ArNote السماويّة}, «heavenly», and «the Manger» \RL{\ArNote المعلف} for \RL{\ArNote العلف}; the words «of the fixed stars in each sign» stand twice, and the text has «Mars» before «the nebulous one on the eye of the Archer» and «the Head» (\RL{\ArNote الرأس}) for «the Archer» (\RL{\ArNote الرامي}). Ptolemy (\emph{Tetrabiblos} I.9) gives the cluster after the sting of the Scorpion the mixture of Mars and the moon, and the cluster in the face of the Archer that of the sun and Mars.}\par
\par Since we have first set out these things in this way, the methods of the particular indications are as I shall describe. One of these methods is the consideration of the matter as a whole, which is known from considering the condition of each of the quarters [of the year]. In it one needs to consider what we have said: to examine the conjunction of the sun and the moon and their opposition that come nearest before the points of the solstices and the equinoxes, and to set up the cardines, at the time of the conjunction or the opposition, for each of the climes and the cities that one intends to examine. Then after that take the planets that govern the places in which the conjunction or the opposition takes place, and those that govern the cardine that follows these places, that is, the cardine through which one of the luminaries passes shortly before the time of the conjunction or the full moon, as we have explained and set out in the chapter on eclipses; so that the governor among the wandering and the non-wandering stars is known in that way, and we know the matter as a whole from the properties of the quarters, and know the increase and decrease in the severity or mildness of that whole from the nature of the governing planets, by looking at their quality, which of the qualities it is, and what conditions are set in motion by them.\par
\par \Fol{33r}The second method is the consideration of the affairs of the months. In it one needs to look at the conjunctions and oppositions that occur in each of the signs in the very way mentioned, keeping in mind what I shall mention: if the conjunction falls near the point of the solstice or of the equinox that has been passed, we use the conjunctions until [the sun] reaches the quarter that follows that quarter. Likewise one should look at the cardines and at the planets that govern both the places that we have mentioned, and examine in particular the nearest appearances of the planets, the applications and separations of the wandering stars, their properties and conditions, and which winds these planets and the parts of the signs in which they happen to be stir up. Also, as we said, we look towards the quarter of which wind the latitude of the moon inclines when it inclines from the belt of the signs; so that, proceeding [in this way], one knows from all these things what for the most part prevails in the conditions of the months and their winds.\par
\par \Fol{33v}The third method is to examine the finest indications, which show the slackening and the severity of the matter. This is known from the sharing of the sun and the moon in particular configurations, not only in the configurations of conjunction and opposition but also in those at the half of the moon in light; and the beginning of the change indicated is for the most part three days before the moon's progress [matches that of] the sun, and sometimes three days after, at equal distances. It is also known from their sharing in configurations with the wandering stars in each of these conditions and in others, such as the condition of the trine and the sextile; for these things follow the natural course. The condition of the change we come to know by following the natural familiarities that the stars that are present, and the signs, have with the surrounding air and with the winds. The strength of these particular conditions that occur from day to day is [greatest] especially when the fixed stars that are greatest in size and strongest in action make appearances in the mornings or in the evenings, in the east or in the west, relative to the sun; for for the most part they change and turn the particular conditions to their own natures. This also happens, a little less than what we have said, when the luminaries pass one of the cardines; for the slackening and severity that the conditions have from time to time occur when the luminaries come to these places, just as the ebb and the flood occur when the moon comes to them; and the change of the winds in particular occurs when the luminaries go to the cardines, the change being towards the wind to which the latitude of the moon inclines. In every case one should proceed on the basis that the first, universal cause takes precedence, that what follows it is the cause of the particular accidents, and that the action is most certain and strongest when the stars that govern the universal natures are found sharing in a configuration with the particular natures.\footnote{«The change» reads \RL{\ArNote التغيير} for \RL{\ArNote التفسير}, «the interpretation», and «the half [of the moon]» \RL{\ArNote انتصاف} for \RL{\ArNote اتصاف}.}\par

\ChapterHead{{\mdseries ⟨}16{\mdseries ⟩} On knowing the degree of the ascendant of the native}{16}{}{\footnote{\emph{Tetrabiblos} III.2.}}
\par \Fol{35r}Since doubt often arises about the first matter on which one relies, the degree of the ascendant at the time of the coming out of the child — for for the most part only those who observe it with the astrolabe, alone among all the instruments for the hours, know the hours at the time of birth accurately, while the other instruments for the hours, with which many who are concerned with this measure, may often be false and depart from the truth: those of them by which the hours are measured by the sun, because of the inclination that may befall them in their place and the crookedness of their gnomons, and the water clocks, because of the stopping of the water and the inequality of its flow, which come from various causes and from the least cause that befalls it — it is necessary first to report the way by which a man can know the degree of the sphere of the signs that, by the natural, necessary reasoning, must be the one rising, after we have found approximately, from the hour that we have been given, the degree of the ascendant from the ascensions. We say that one should take, of the conjunction and the opposition that came before the birth, the one nearest to it, whichever was nearer, and find its degree accurately. If what we have taken is a conjunction, we take the degree of the luminaries; if what was taken is an opposition, we take the degree of whichever of the luminaries happens to be above the earth.\footnote{«Doubt» reads \RL{\ArNote الشكّ} for \RL{\ArNote الشدّ}. «The astrolabe» is \RL{\ArNote ذات الصفائح}, as in the \emph{Zīj}; the Arabic \emph{Tetrabiblos} has \RL{\ArNote الأسطرلاب}. \par \Fol{35v}Then we look which of the planets deserves to have the governing of that degree at the time of the birth. In general, the way in which a planet deserves the governing is completed by five things, I mean the triplicity, the house, the exaltation, the term, and the appearance near the rays or the sharing in a configuration with the sun; so the governing of the place belongs to the planet that has more than one of these qualifications in that place, or all of them. If we find all or most of these things in one planet, we look at its degree, taken accurately, in the sign in which it is at the time of the birth, and judge that the degree of equal number in the sign that we had taken approximately as the ascendant is the degree of one of the cardines of the ascendant. If the distance of the degree of the governing planet from the degrees of the ascendant taken roughly is greater than its distance from the degrees of the middle of the sky, [we take it for the middle of the sky] and set up the other cardines from it.\footnote{«Term» reads \RL{\ArNote الحدّ} for \RL{\ArNote الجدّ}.}\par

\ChapterHead{{\mdseries ⟨}17{\mdseries ⟩} On the division of the doctrine of nativities}{17}{}{\footnote{\emph{Tetrabiblos} III.3.}}
\par \Fol{36r}Having first set these things down, we say: if one wishes to divide the whole consideration of nativities in an orderly and systematic way, he finds that some of the things that can be grasped by the natural course are the accidents that occur before the birth, like the account of the parents; and some are the accidents that occur before the birth and after it, like the account of the brothers. Those at the birth itself are not one simple matter, like what we have described. The last of the accidents that can be grasped are those that occur after the birth, and this kind has many branches and parts. What one wishes to know of what is at the time of the birth itself is the account of male and female, the account of those born as twins and as more than twins, the account of the malformed, and the account of those who are not nourished. What one wishes to know of what comes after the birth is the account of the length of life, since it is not connected with the account of those who are not nourished; and of that is the account of the form of the body and of the illnesses and injuries that befall the body. After that comes the account of the conditions of the soul and of the accidents of the soul; after that, the account of the native's fortune in the matter of possessions and in the matter of rank and elevation; after that, the account of his condition in the works he engages in; after that, the account of his marriage and his life with his wife, of the children born to him, of his mixing with people and his partnerships with them, and of his friends; after that, the account of travel abroad; and at the end, the account of the condition of his death, which in its power is akin to the account of the length of life, and whose place must be after all this. We shall describe the matter of each of these things in outline.\footnote{«The account of the malformed» is written in the margin. «We shall describe» follows the Berlin manuscript (\RL{\ArNote سنصف}); the transcription has \RL{\ArNote ويتّصف}.}\par
\par Since we have explained the degree of the ascendant and its correction in [this] way, we add [an account] of everything that occurs in the conditions of nativities, and mention it together with our mention of the pure active powers, as we said just now. We discard what many people have described in which there is no proof or convincing argument suited to the causes that come from the first nature, and examine what of it can be grasped, not by lots and numbers that have no cause that necessitates them, but by considering the configurations of the stars and their fitness to their places. So that there is no need to repeat and reiterate the account in each of the chapters that we have mentioned, we should give a general account for all of them.\footnote{«Lots» reads \RL{\ArNote القرع} for \RL{\ArNote الفرع}: Ptolemy rejects the method of lots and numbers (\emph{Tetrabiblos} III.3).}\par
\par \Fol{36v}We look first at the place in the sphere of the signs that suits the kind of the chapter sought among the chapters of the consideration of the nativity: as the place of the middle of the sky, for example, suits the matter of the works one engages in, I mean the crafts, or as the place of the sun suits the matter of the father. Then after that we look at the planets that deserve the governing of that place by the five kinds that we mentioned in what we said before. If the lord of the place is one planet in all respects, we give it the governing of that matter; if there are two planets or three, we give the governing to the one of them that has the greater number of shares. After that we direct our consideration of the condition of the event, and our examination of it, to the natures of the very planets that have deserved the governing, and to the natures of the signs in which the planets are, and of the places akin to them. Then we examine the event and its size from their strength: we look whether they are strong in their condition in the world, or in their condition in the nativity, or the matter is the opposite. They are strongest in their condition in the world when they are in places of their own or akin to them, and when they are eastern and increasing in motion; strongest in their condition in the nativity when they are in the cardines, or in the places that rise to the cardines, and especially the ascendant and the middle of the sky; weakest in their condition in the world when they are in foreign places, or are western, or retrograde, or decreasing in motion; and weakest in their condition in the nativities when they decline from the cardines. Direct your consideration of the length of the time of the event by examining whether they are attributed to the mornings or to the evenings, relative to the sun or to the ascendant: for the two quarters that precede each of these, and the two opposite them on the diameter, are attributed to the mornings, and the remaining quarters that follow them are attributed to the evenings. And look whether they are in the cardines [or] rising to the cardines: if they are attributed to the mornings and are on the cardines, they are quicker in action; and if they are attributed to the evenings and rising to the cardines, they are slower in action.\footnote{«Matter» follows the Arabic \emph{Tetrabiblos} (\RL{\ArNote لأمر}) twice for the transcription's \RL{\ArNote لأمن}, «safety»; «their strength» and «their condition» read \RL{\ArNote قوّتها} and \RL{\ArNote حالها} for \RL{\ArNote قوّتنا} and \RL{\ArNote حالنا}, «our strength», «our condition».}\par
\par One should also keep in mind, in all the matters that arise from nativities, that a planet that brings about something in the place sought must necessarily have been akin to it from the beginning. For if it is not akin to it, it cannot bring about anything in it, since it did not share in the first beginning: he means that if the sign in which the planet is is not joined to the sign sought into which the planet falls, it cannot bring about anything of its nature in that sign, since it was not joined to it in the nativity. The time when the event comes about does not follow the first governing, but follows the condition of the planet that has dominion over the governing, relative to the sun and to the angles of the world.\footnote{«If it is not» supplies the negation (\RL{\ArNote لم}) that the Berlin manuscript has.}\par
\ChapterHead{{\mdseries ⟨}18{\mdseries ⟩} On the parents}{18}{}{\footnote{\emph{Tetrabiblos} III.4.}}
\par \Fol{37r}The sun and Saturn are by nature akin to the fathers, and the moon and Venus akin [to the mothers]; and as the condition of these planets is found to be with respect to one another and to the others, so we must suppose the condition of the parents will be. For one should consider their fortune (?) and their possessions from the attendance of the planets on the luminaries. When the beneficent planets of their own domain surround them, those planets being either in the signs of the luminaries themselves or in the signs that follow them, they signify that the affairs of the parents will be splendid and noble; especially when the planets attending the sun are attributed to the mornings and those attending the moon to the evenings, that is, when they rise before the sun and after the moon, so that their position is praiseworthy for that reason. If, besides, Saturn and Venus are eastern, or in the facing proper to them, or on the cardines, we should suppose from that a manifest good fortune of the parents, according to what suits each of them. If the matter is otherwise, and the luminaries proceed alone with no planet attending them, they signify the low station and meanness of the parents, especially when the position of Venus and Saturn is not praiseworthy. If they are attended by maleficent planets not of their domain, as happens when Mars rises after the sun and Saturn after the moon, and the position of the beneficent planets relative to the luminaries is not praiseworthy and they are not of their domain, one should infer from that the meanness of the parents' living and the unsettledness of their condition. If the lot that signifies fortune, which is the lot of good fortune that we shall mention, agrees in the nativity, in a praiseworthy agreement, with the planets attending the sun or the moon, the possessions of the parents remain safe. If the lot is not as we have said and the matter is otherwise, and then none of the beneficent planets attends the luminaries, or those that attend them are maleficent, there is no benefit from the possessions of the parents, and they become harmful to them.\footnote{«Will be» is written between the lines. «Suits» follows the Arabic \emph{Tetrabiblos} (\RL{\ArNote يلائم}).}\par
\par \Fol{37v}The length and the shortness of the parents' life are considered from the other sharings of the planets in configurations. As for the father's life: if Jupiter and Venus share in a configuration with the sun or with Saturn, in whatever way, or if Saturn too shares with the sun, his sharing with the sun being one of agreement — either being with it, or in its trine, or in its sextile — and both are strong, it signifies long life for the father. If they are not strong, it is not so; but they do not necessarily signify short life. If the matter is not so, and Mars is above the sun or above Saturn, or follows them — he means that it rises after them — and Saturn is not in agreement with the sun but in its quadrature or in opposition to it on the diameter, or both are declining from the cardines, weakness of the fathers comes from them. If they are on the cardines, or where they rise to the cardines, they make the fathers short-lived or bring an injury upon them: shortness of life they bring about if they are on the ascendant or on the middle of the sky, and injury if they are on the two remaining cardines, I mean the setting point and the cardine of the earth, or where they rise. For when Mars looks at the sun in the way that I mentioned at the beginning of the book — that is, the parts that we said look at one another and are equal in power — the father dies suddenly, or an injury to his eyes befalls him. If Mars's look is towards Saturn, there befalls the father either death, or fever with shivering, or an injury from cutting or burning. Saturn too, when its configuration with the sun is bad, brings about the death of the fathers by diseases and by illnesses that befall them from harmful humours.\footnote{Robbins takes Ptolemy's «in the way described» as quadrature, opposition or overcoming; al-Battānī refers it to the signs that look at one another and are of equal power (\emph{Tetrabiblos} I.15; chapter 6 above).}\par
\par \Fol{38r}As for the mother's life: if Jupiter shares in a configuration with the moon or Venus, in whatever way, or Venus is in agreement with the moon, either being with it, or in its sextile or its trine, and both are strong, they signify long life for the mother. As for Mars, if it looks at the moon, or follows Venus, or is in quadrature or in opposition to her, or Saturn is in this condition with the moon: if they are decreasing in motion, retrograde, or declining from the cardines, they bring upon the mothers only a falling into illnesses and sicknesses; and if they are increasing in motion or on the cardines, they shorten the mothers' lives and bring [injury] upon them. They shorten life when they are on the two eastern cardines or where they rise to them, and bring about injury when they are on the two western cardines or where they come to them. For when Mars looks at the moon in the way we have mentioned, and the moon is eastern, sudden death of the mothers comes of it, or an injury that befalls them in the eyes; if the moon is western, their death comes of it from miscarriage or the like, or an injury from cutting or burning. Instead of his word «western» one may also say «declining». If Mars looks at Venus, their death by fevers comes of it, and illnesses that befall them from darkness of sight and from matter that flows down suddenly. When Saturn looks at the moon, death and illnesses befall them: if the moon is eastern, their death is from fevers with shivering; if it is western, it is from spreading tumours and ulcers in the womb.\footnote{For «eastern» and «western» of the moon Ptolemy has «waxing» and «waning». «Shivering» reads \RL{\ArNote نافض} for \RL{\ArNote ناقص}.}\par
\par \Fol{38v}In knowing the kinds of the particular injuries and illnesses, or the death that befalls the parents, we should use, besides what we have mentioned, the conditions of the signs in which the planets that bring about the particular condition are found; we shall mention the conditions of the signs and explain them when we speak of the native. And we should examine by day in particular the sun and Venus, and by night in particular Saturn and the moon. What befits us, and is required of us, in examining the remaining particular matters of the parents is to set up the place of the father or of the mother of the [proper] domain in the place of the ascendant, and to consider all their other matters as is done in the nativities of the parents themselves, according to the methods, accidental and active, that are found in the general kinds, which we shall explain later. The ways in which the causes combine with one another we must remember here and in all the places that come later in the matters of nativities: if the planets that are the lords of the places sought are not of one kind but of different kinds, producing opposites, we look which of the planets that are strongest in power, dominion and prevalence we find to have the most shares, so that it has the best claim to what happens; and in examining them we always keep to the natures of these. If the shares are of equal weight, then if the planets are [together] (?), we arrive, with what we have described, at knowledge of what comes together from the mixture of these different natures with ease; and if they are apart, we give each of them the accidents that suit it according to the particular times, giving first to those of them attributed to the evenings.\footnote{«Of the domain» reads \RL{\ArNote للحيّز} for \RL{\ArNote للخير}, «of the good»: Ptolemy has «the paternal or maternal place of the sect» (\emph{Tetrabiblos} III.4). «Causes», «lords» and «dominion» follow the Arabic \emph{Tetrabiblos} (\RL{\ArNote الأسباب، أرباب، استيلاء}) for \RL{\ArNote الأشياء، أرباع، استعلاء}; «we arrive» likewise (\RL{\ArNote وصلنا}). «Together» is a guess for the transcription's «the high» (\RL{\ArNote العالية}): Ptolemy has «when the rulers are together», and gives the events first to the more eastern planets, not to those attributed to the evenings.}\par
\par \Fol{39r}Others than Ptolemy have said the like about the indications of the parents, except that they added to it, by night and by day, the lot of the father and of the mother and the sign of the parents, and considered the strength and weakness of all the significators, their positions relative to the cardines, and the lords of these places. If they found them strong, they inferred the excellence of the parents and their good condition; if they found them fallen and weak, they inferred their meanness. In this they also used the lords of the triplicities of the significators, and said: if, with the corruption of the significators, these are afflicted and corrupted with them, they signify chronic illness of the parents. If a beneficent planet is found in the house of the parents, and the lords of the houses and of the terms, and the terms of the luminaries, look at it, they judged the parents to be noble; if the maleficent planets look at the house of the parents from quadrature or opposition, or are in it, and the beneficent do not look at it, they judged that they are slaves or like slaves. If the sign of the parents is the house of the sun, the indication is for the father; if it is in the house of the moon, the indication is for the mother. If you find the lord of the twelfth house with the moon in the thirteenth (?) from the cardine, his mother is a slave-woman; if this is the condition of the sun, his father is a slave. If the lords of the triplicities of someone's significator are in their own dignities, in praiseworthy places of the sphere, free from the maleficent planets, with the beneficent looking at them, they signify gain and rank in what is to come; especially when the lot of good fortune and its lord are fortunate together with the lot of the parents, for that signifies the continuance of the matter and its inheritance by the sons. If the matter is the opposite, the parents have no benefit from their possessions, and [the significators] signify the fall of their rank.\footnote{«Significators» reads \RL{\ArNote الأدلّاء} for \RL{\ArNote الأولاد}, «the children», and «fortunate» \RL{\ArNote مسعودين} for \RL{\ArNote منحوس سعودين}.}\par
\par They considered the agreement of the parents with the native and their love for him. One looks at the planet that prevails over the ascendant and the one that prevails over the matter of the father by the number of shares: if they agree and suit each other, they love one another; if the matter is the opposite, they hate one another. Likewise, if the sun is in the term of Mars by day, or of Saturn by night, especially when the maleficent planets look at the sun and the beneficent do not, it signifies mutual hatred, or the native is deprived (?) of rank and of wealth. If the moon is as I have described of the condition of the sun, the mother meets with affliction from her child. [The parent] whose lot is found in the twelfth [place] from it, the native will be an enemy to him. When the sun is in a cardine and Mars looks at it from opposition, the father hates his child; likewise, when the moon is in this condition, the mother hates the child. If Jupiter and Saturn are in opposition and overlook one another, it signifies mutual hatred, and if they are in quadrature likewise; from the sextile and the trine it signifies love. If the lord of the house of the parents does not look at the parents, nor the lord of the sun at it, nor the lord of the lot of the parents at the lot, the native [will have] sorrow (?) and hardship. If the sun is afflicted by day from opposition and quadrature and is in a bad place, the father hates his child and drives him away from him (?).\footnote{«Sorrow» guesses at the transcription's \{\RL{\ArNote سعود}\}; the Berlin manuscript seems to have \RL{\ArNote لغمر}.}\par
\par \Fol{39v}They said about the life of the parents: if the sun looks at the ascendant by day, it is directed for the life of the father; if Saturn looks at the ascendant, the direction is from it, if the sun is fallen; if Saturn is fallen, the degree of the middle of the sky by day, and by night the degree of the cardine of the earth; and, by night and by day, the lot of the parents. Compare what comes out of the direction with the years of the planet that has the most shares in the place that you direct for the father: if it agrees with them, or is near their number, it signifies the death of the father. Use the like for the significators of the mother. Some of them looked by day and began with the sun for the father: if it is joined to the ascendant, with one of the lords of the shares that follow its place looking at it, they used it for the life of the father; if one did not find it so, one looked at Saturn; if one did not find it in this condition, one looked at the middle of the cardine of the sky, provided that the lot of the father does not have this condition. By night you begin with Saturn, then the sun, then the lot of the father, then the cardine of the earth; and you make the planet that has a share and a look a helper of the significator of the moon (?), according to its years. Use the like for the significators of the mothers.\footnote{«The death» reads \RL{\ArNote هلاك} for \RL{\ArNote هلال}, «the new moon».}\par
