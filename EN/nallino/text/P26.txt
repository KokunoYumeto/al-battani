Al-Khāzinī, *al-zīj al-sanjarī*, «the Sanjarī astronomical work», in the codex of the Vatican Library marked cod. Vat. Arab. 761. — From fol. 121v it is gathered that the book was composed in the reign of the caliph al-Mustarshid bi-llāh (16 Rabīʿ II 512 – the month of Dhū l-Qaʿda 529, that is, 6 Aug. 1118 – Aug. 1135); the longitudes and latitudes of 43 fixed stars are given for the year 509 H (beginning 26 May 1115) on f. 191v–192r. The tables of the oblique ascensions (f. 160v–161r) and of the equation of time (f. 161v) are computed for the latitude of the city of Merw, 37° 40′.

Leo de Balneolis, *Astronomia*, contained on f. 1r–108v of the codex Vatic. Lat. 3098, which also contains the version of al-Battānī (cf. p. LII). It was written originally in Hebrew and forms the fifth treatise of the work *Milḥamōt hash-shēm*, «the wars of God»; being a single and great book that had to be presented on its own, it is left out in many codices and in the editions of the whole philosophical-theological work. The treatise is divided into three parts, comprising 136, 9 and 14 chapters; their list in Hebrew and Latin is in Neubauer, in the great work *Histoire littéraire de la France*, vol. XXXI, 1893, p. 624 ff. The author reports several astronomical observations of his own. Writing in Hebrew he called himself Lēwī ben Gerson; he died at Avignon in 1344.

Thābit ibn Qurra, *De motu octavae sphaerae*. The little book survives in two different Latin versions. One, in the Vatican Library (cod. Vatic. Lat. 4275, f. 83vb–85ra), is entitled *Liber thebit filii chore de motu spere octaue* and begins: «Ymaginabor speram equatoris diei et tres circulos in eo signatos, qui sunt circuli orizontis et circulus linee medii celi.....» (I shall imagine the sphere of the equator of the day and three circles marked on it, which are the circles of the horizon and the circle of the line of the middle of the heaven.....). The other (cod. Vatic. Lat. 4083, f. 56v–57r) opens thus: «Incipit *liber tebit de motu 8. spere seu accessionis et recessionis*. Tractatus in quo continetur doctrina motus accessionis et designationis figure ipsius sicut est in celo. Speram equinoctialem tum et tres circulos in ea signatos. orizontis videlicet circulum. et meridiei circulum..... sola mentis intuitione concipe» (here begins *the book of Thebit on the motion of the eighth sphere, or of access and recess*. A treatise that contains the doctrine of the motion of access and of the drawing of its figure as it is in the heaven. Conceive by the mind's eye alone the equinoctial sphere, and then three circles marked on it, namely the circle of the horizon and the circle of the meridian.....). After all the notes had already been printed, I was able to examine three other codices of the first version[^77.2], which I use in the *Addenda*: of the Marcian Library (Venice), cod. Lat. class. VIII, cod. LXIX, f. 22r–26r, entitled *Thebit de motu octavae sphaerae*; of the same library cod. Lat. class. XIV, cod. CLXV, f. 46rb–49va («*Incipit liber tebith ben chorath de motu 8$^{\text{e}}$ spere*»); lastly, of the Laurentian Library (Florence), S. Marco 194. I shall denote them by the letters *A*, *B*, *F*. Of the error of those who think that the little book has been printed I have spoken on p. XXXVI, n. 1. — The author died in the month of Ṣafar 288 H (Febr. 901).
