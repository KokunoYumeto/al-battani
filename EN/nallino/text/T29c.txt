@p 0715b001..0715b009
It is clear enough that all these discrepancies have arisen not from different computations but from *Arabic* copies of one and the same archetype. In the printed Alfonsine Tables themselves only these slips come from European copyists or compositors: the minutes 77′ (for 27′) in the appearance of Venus at the morning setting for the beginning of Sagittarius, and the minutes 32′ (for 23′) and 88′ (for 8′) in the disappearance of Mercury at the morning rising for the beginnings of Taurus and Libra[^n]. The minutes 31′ for 0′ in the appearance of Venus at the evening rising at the beginning of Aries probably arose because some Arabic codex had marked the zero (0) at that place by the Arabic negation لا «not» (as, very rarely, the Escorial codex also does), which the Latin translator took at the numerical value of its letters.
@n In the table above the 88′ stands at Leo.
@p 0715b010..0715b015
Very strangely, as I noticed later, the *Latin* version of the *Almagest*, made from the Arabic by Gerard of Cremona and printed at Venice in 1515 (edited by P. Liechtenstein), gives on f. 152r not the table of the Greek text but *another*, agreeing with that of al-Battānī, of Ḥabash and of the Alfonsine Tables. Who put it in place of the true Ptolemaic one, some Greek scribe, the Arabic translator (al-Ḥajjāj), Gerard or the editor, I do not know; it is very useful for finding the true readings, as will appear below.
@p 0715b016..0715b030
I had already sent the foregoing to the press when I read again the *Canons* of Theon, from which no help in this matter was to be expected; and I was not a little astonished to find al-Battānī's amounts in him. For in vol. III, pp. 30–31, he gives the same table that Ptolemy gives in the *Greek Almagest*, headed φάσεων ἀποστάσεις πρὸς τὸν ἀκριβῆ ἥλιον, «elongations of the phases from the true sun», of which there is no mention in the rules on the use of the canons, vol. I, pp. 61–66; but in the same vol. III, pp. 16–29, he gives for each of the seven climates, whose longest days vary from 13ʰ to 16ʰ by intervals of half an hour, the appearances and disappearances of the planets[^715.1] with numbers *that can in no way be reconciled with Ptolemy's!* Theon mentions these tables fitted to the single climates in his rules, vol. I, p. 63, from which it is quite certain that they contain «the distances of the phases (i.e. of the appearances and disappearances) from the sun measured on the ecliptic» (αἱ πρὸς τὸν ἥλιον ἐπὶ τοῦ διὰ μέσων τῶν ζωδίων τῶν φάσεων ἀποστάσεις), the planet always being placed at the beginnings of the signs. All the columns of the fourth climate, whose longest day is 14½ʰ and latitude 36°, agree with *al-Battānī's* etc.; so, although the only edition, Halma's, teems with frequent errors, with the help of the Greek readings compared with the others we can almost everywhere correct the errors of the Escorial codex with full certainty.
@f 715.1 0715nl001..0715nl007
In the *superior* planets they are headed φάσεις ἑῴας ἀνατολῆς, «appearances of the morning rising», and φάσεις ἑσπερίας δύσεως, «appearances of the evening setting»; in the *inferior* planets φάσεις ἑσπερίας ἀνατολῆς, φ. ἑῴας κρύψεως, φ. ἑῴας ἀνατολῆς, φ. ἑσπερίας κρύψεως. The headings of pp. 23–25 are wrongly placed by Halma; for they belong to pp. 25, 23, 24 (instead of 23, 24, 25).
@p 0715b031..0715b036
Of the method and the elements with which these tables of the climates were constructed Theon says nothing; so the matter is still very obscure for us. The different obliquity of the ecliptic adopted by Theon (23° 51′) brings only a very small difference into the angles BED of the ecliptic and the horizons[^715.2], which is of no importance in the construction of the tables. But the longitudes of the apogees taken by Theon[^715.3] do not at all suit the tables themselves, which on the contrary fit al-Battānī's apogees, as we saw on p. 259; so we are in the greatest perplexity about the origin of the tables.
@f 715.2 0715nl008..0715nr002
Computing with Theon's tables of the oblique and right ascensions, for φ = 36° the angles come out 30° 9′ at the beginning of Aries, 31° 49′ of Taurus, 50° 1′ of Cancer, 63° 36′ of Leo and 74° 4′ of Virgo (Alb. 30° 25′, 32° 26′, 50° 5′, 63° 31′, 73° 50′), etc.
@f 715.3 0715nr003..0715nr010
They occur in the headings of the tables of the equations of the planets, where they are called ἐπιλήψεις, «prehensions, retentions»: Saturn 110° 30′, Jupiter 38° 30′, Mars 353° 0′, Venus 292° 30′, Mercury 67° 30′. That is 113° 40′ (for Jupiter 113° 39′) *less* than Ptolemy's longitudes of the apogees! Theon does not say where he took them from. The apogee (ἐπίληψις) of the sun is 65° 30′, as in the Almagest.
