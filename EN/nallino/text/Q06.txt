XXV and XLIX: The question of the author and the year of the version of Ptolemy's *Planisphaerium* from the Arabic, which was uncertain when I was writing, has now at last been settled by A. A. XXXIII, ll. 13–14: As Suter too has pointed out to me by letter, the hitherto unknown author of the book *tasṭīḥ al-ṣuwar* is al-Bīrūnī, who lists this little work of his in the catalogue of the books he wrote up to the year 427 H (in Sachau's preface to the text of the *Chronol.*, p. XXXVI, n. 1: Following indications supplied to me by Phil. Angelitti, I have found that the little book *de motu octave spere*, printed at Bologna in 1480, agrees entirely with the first part of the last chapter («de motu octavae sphaerae», on the motion of the eighth sphere) of the *Theoricae novae planetarum* of Georg Purbach[^433.2], from which it must therefore be judged to have been excerpted. After the last words of the Bologna booklet («cum autem talis motus accessus et recessus fuerit semicirculo minor equatio erit semper addenda sed cum maior fuerit minuenda», but when such a motion of access and recess is less than a semicircle, the equation must always be added, and when it is greater, subtracted), Purbach goes on thus in the *Theoricae*: «Thebith vero duplicem tantum octavae sphaerae motum inesse dixit ...» (but Thebith said that the eighth sphere has only a double motion ...), and he sets out Thebith's method and his own observations on it. In that very chapter Purbach says that he is writing in 1460: «Nunc nostro tempore, scilicet anno Domini MCCCCLX, factum est caput Arietis septentrionale fere 66 gradibus a sectione parvi circuli et aequatoris distans ...» (now in our time, namely in the year of the Lord 1460, the northern head of Aries has come to be about 66 degrees distant from the intersection of the small circle and the equator ...). — He had plainly taken his information about Albatenius from Gerard's *Theorica*.

LXXII and 163–164: Having examined Theon's tables (I, 148–155), I found that in them too the right ascensions of the signs are plainly counted from the beginning of Capricorn, and hence the heading of those tables is understood: ὀρθὴ σφαῖρα μεσουράνησις πανταχῇ[^n], «[the ascensions of the signs in the] right sphere, [which are also the] culmination [of the signs] everywhere [on the earth]». Al-Battānī therefore had a Greek model before his eyes. — Tables of the right ascensions of the degrees of the ecliptic, counted from the beginning of Capricorn, survive in several books, as appears from Ahlwardt, *Verzeichniss der Arab. Hss.*, vol. V, nos. 5710, 5758, 5771, 5772, 5798, 5808; it is gathered from nos. 5710, 5758 and 5772 that some later writers call them *maṭāliʿ al-zawāl*, «the ascensions of the culmination», or (from no. 5808) *maṭāliʿ al-qubba*, «the ascensions of the Dome [of the Earth]». — Cf. also Barhebraeus, *Astron.*, p. 170 of the text, 150 of the translation.
