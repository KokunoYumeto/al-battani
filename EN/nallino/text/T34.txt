@h 0757b001
On pp. 299–307: general considerations
@p 0757b002..0757b003
I must say briefly why I judge spurious these tables, all of which but one are missing in the *Spanish version*[^757.1].
@p 0757b004..0757b009
The *astrological rose*, so to speak, of p. 299 occupies fol. 197r of the codex; that is, it stands between the figure of the azimuths (p. 92) and the tables of the parallaxes of altitude (p. 93). Not only is it never mentioned by al-Battānī, but it is altogether useless to his work, since it deals with matters that belong to judicial astrology alone, not to the mathematical and astronomical computations of the astrologers. Moreover, at the end of the title one reads the words وليست من الام «and it is not from the archetype», which can only mean that the rose was taken from other books[^757.2].
@p 0757b010..0757b014
The other tables are placed in the codex after the tables of pp. 187–188, which, according to the title of appendix *E* (vol. I, p. 148: «on finding by means of the tables *that we have placed at the end of the book* .....»), concluded the book, and in the Spanish version do conclude it. Nor can those tables be thought to have been moved wrongly by the copyists to the end of the work from another place in the book, since, as will appear from what follows, they quite certainly do not come from al-Battānī.
@p 0757b015..0757b019
**Pp. 300–301** are said in their very title to be taken from the astronomical work of the Spanish Arab Maslama [al-Majrīṭī][^757.3]. The table of the signs of the Arabs was not only useless in this place, since the same matter had already been treated in small tables on p. 7, but it is constructed on a principle altogether different from the one that al-Battānī sets out in vol. I, p. 145. — The table of the signs of the Persians is useless to al-Battānī, who does not use the Persian chronology and never mentions this table.
@p 0757b020..0757b024
**P. 302** is altogether useless, since the signs of the years and months of the era of Dhū l-Qarnayn are already given on p. 8. In the titles, the names of the months given only in their *Latin* form (cf. pp. 201–03) are very suspect, and so is the word *al-Suryān*, unknown to al-Battānī, for the Syrians. Lastly, an inspection of the table shows that these signs refer to the Julian or Roman years of the era of Dhū l-Qarnayn counted not from the beginning of *September*, as is al-Battānī's custom, but from *October*.
@p 0757b025..0757b027
**P. 303** is never mentioned by al-Battānī; moreover it rests on *fixed* years[^757.4], whereas in his tables al-Battānī treats the Coptic years as *vague* years counted from the era of Dhū l-Qarnayn, as I noted on p. 205.
@p 0757b028..0757b030
**Pp. 304–305** are mentioned nowhere in the chapters, and they refer to Roman and Coptic eras different from al-Battānī's, as I shall show in the particular notes. On p. 305, moreover, the suspect word *al-Suryān* occurs.
@p 0757b031..0757b035
**Pp. 306–307** are mentioned nowhere; they are likewise useless to al-Battānī, who in the chapter on computing the syzygies always uses the Roman years of Dhū l-Qarnayn or the Coptic years. And the reckoning by *signs*, degrees, minutes, etc. (that is, not from the beginning of Aries but from the beginnings of the single signs) is altogether foreign to al-Battānī's usage; it does not occur even in the catalogue of the stars, in which Ptolemy, however, had followed this way of reckoning.
@p 0757b036 + 0758b001..0758b002
It is not improbable that the tables of pp. 302–307 too were copied from the work of Maslama, as is stated of pp. 300–301; Maslama could not leave out tables of the syzygies for the years of the hijra, since we know that he adapted the canons of others as well to the Arab years[^758.1].
@f 757.1 0757nl001..0757nl003
I have dealt on pp. 218–20 with the two small spurious tables that give the longitudes and latitudes of the cities of North Africa and Spain.
@f 757.2 0757nl004..0757nr002
In the circle of the *houses* Mars is once denoted by the Persian word *bahrām*, which al-Battānī never uses; but since the Arabic word *mirrīkh* is used in all the other places of the rose, and they are very many, it is plain that this *bahrām* comes not from the author of the rose but from one of the copyists.
