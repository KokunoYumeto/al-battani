{ar:هَيْلَاجٌ}, pl. {ar:هَيْلَاجَاتٌ}, ἀφέτης, *the significator*, *the apheta*, *alhyleg*, *alhylech*, *hilegium*, in spurious passages 203$_{3}$ and 224$_{11}$; cf. {ar:دليل}. The version of the *Tetrabiblos* made by Ḥunayn ibn Isḥāq and revised by Thābit always uses {ar:هيلاج}. From the Persian {ar:هَيْلِجْ} «lord of the house, master of the house», as if the apheta were the lord of the aphetic place. Sachau does not render the astrologers' {ar:هيلاج} correctly as «mistress of the house»; for the passage of al-Bīrūnī, *Chron.*, p. 79, l. 1 of the text (= 90, last line, of the translation), {ar:ان يكون الشمس فيها هيلاجًا وكَدْخُداهًا اعني في بيتها او شرفها}, must be translated thus: «when the Sun is at the same time apheta and οἰκοδεσπότης, that is, when in its aphetic place of the nativity it also has its astrological house or its exaltation». For the οἰκοδεσπότης τῆς γενέσεως or *kadkhudāh* (from which our writers made *alchochoden*) is the planet that has the most dignities (house, term, exaltation, decan, lordship of the triplicity) in the aphetic place of the nativity; sometimes, therefore, the apheta itself is the οἰκοδεσπότης.
