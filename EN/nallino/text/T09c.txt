III, pp. 241–242), under the title «*Longitudes and latitudes of the known and tested cities and towns of al-Andalus* [i.e. of Spain] *and al-Maghrib* [i.e. of Mauretania]», two series of cities are given, left out in the Spanish version, whose names by themselves show clearly enough that the table was *interpolated*[^667.1] by the *western Arab* scribe of the codex, who seems to have lived in the second half of the eleventh century. For (as also on ff. 184r and 185v; see the note on pp. 68–71 of the tables) he mentions the city of *Bijāya*, founded in the year 457 H (which began on 12 Dec. 1064) by al-Nāṣir ibn ʿAlnās on a site where there had been a village of shepherds before, and made the seat of the Ḥammādid dynasty; likewise *Qalʿat Banī Ḥammād*[^667.2][^n], founded in the last twenty years of the fourth century H (the tenth AD) by Ḥammād ibn Yūsuf Bulukkīn, and the capital of the kingdom until Bijāya was built; but he leaves out *Marrākush*, or Morocco, built in the year 454 H (which began on 14 Jan. 1062) or a little later by Yūsuf ibn Tāshufīn, and afterwards the chief city of the Almoravids, and *Tilimsān* (Tlemcen), a most flourishing town already in the twelfth century.
