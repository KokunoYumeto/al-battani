**§ 2.** — As I noted in the preface of vol. I, Plato's version does not contain the tables. The *Spanish version* became known to me after the tables had already been printed; but it could be of some use, and has been, only for the third kind of tables, as I have set out in the commentaries. For it comes from the same corrupt western Arabic archetype as the Escorial codex and Plato's codex; in the tables it shows *more frequent* errors than the Escorial codex, arising for the most part from the confusion of similar Arabic letters. On the authority of the Spanish version I was able to fill a gap in the catalogue of the fixed stars (vol. II, pp. 274–277).
@p 0424b008..0424b027
After what I had had copied from the Spanish version in 1903, I received at the beginning of 1904, from the most distinguished Graffin and Nau, photographic sheets of some passages of the text and of the tables I had wished for; I give them the greatest thanks publicly. With the help of these sheets I can now also correct in part what I had set out in vol. I, p. LX; for from a few passages of chapter VI it is gathered with complete certainty that *the Spanish translators had in their hands the Arabic text, not Plato's version*. The passage of vol. I, p. 18, ll. 11–12, «those six inhabited islands are found, which are called the islands of the Blessed (*al-suʿadāʾ*)», is in Plato (p. 12r of the Nuremberg ed.): «6 insulae ... ex inhabitatis insulis, quae vocantur insulae *fortunatorum*» (6 islands ... of the inhabited islands, which are called the islands of the fortunate); in the Spanish version (fol. 8r, col. II): «las .VI. yslas ... son yslas pobladas et dizenle las yslas de *Soade*» (the six islands ... are inhabited islands, and they call them the islands of Soade). — Vol. I, p. 18, l. 12, the city of Gadira is called *Gadir* by Plato, *Adira* by the Spanish translator, who read عديرة for غديرة[^424.1]. — Vol. I, p. 18, the island of *Cyrnus* is in Arabic قرنس *Qurnos*; in Plato, who read القريس, it is *Alcaris* (so the Vatican codex; the editions wrongly *Alcayir*); in the Spanish version *Farandiz*, that is, فرندس. — The city of *Lādhiqa* became, rightly, *Alledhica* in Plato, but *Ladaquia* in the Spanish translator, who read لادقية. — Vol. I, p. 19, Europe is in Arabic *Ūrūfī* or *Ewrūfī* اوروفي (cod. Escur. اورنقى), rightly *Europa* in Plato, but *Oriqui* in the Spanish version, after the false reading اوريقى. — The city of *al-ʿArīsh* occurs twice in vol. I, p. 19. Plato had the true reading before his eyes and rendered it *Vitis*, vine (p. 12v of the Nuremberg ed.), after the common meaning of the word among the western Arabs[^424.2]; but the Spanish translator wrongly read الغرس *al-Furs*, «the Persians», for العريش, and translated it «tierra de *Perssia*» (land of Persia).

We have seen in vol. II, p. 271, and elsewhere that the Spanish version too derives from a copy written in western Arabic letters; it lacks the list of the kings and the caliphs and the table of the altitudes of the hours (vol. II, p. 188). It gives the geographical tables much abridged, and the concordance of the years of the hijra with the Roman years incomplete; it lacks the small interpolations that I mentioned in vol. II, pp. 198 and 222, and the tables that the Escorial codex itself indicates as taken from elsewhere, except one (cf. vol. II, p. 317). The spurious appendix D (see vol. I, p. 147), «capitulo de saber quando es la planeta endereçada. et quando es estacionaria. et quando es retrograda» (chapter on knowing when the planet is direct, and when it is stationary, and when it is retrograde), also occurs, on f. 63v–64r, between the tables of the lunar parallaxes in the climates and the tables of the mean motions of the planets. The genuine appendix G (see vol. I, p. 150), up to the words «the arc will be the argument of the difference of the day that belongs to the collected decades»[^424.3], is on f. 43v, near the small table of the right ascensions for the decades of degrees of the zodiac; and the necessary appendix H (vol. I, p. 150[^n]) is found on f. 57r, near the small table of the unequal motions of the Sun and the Moon in the syzygies (vol. II, p. 88), whose use it explains. — The order of the tables agrees with the Escorial codex; but the sines are given on f. 28r–v, before all the other tables, and the precession, or motion of the fixed stars, is placed after the appearances and disappearances of the planets. More correctly than in the Escorial codex, the ecliptic limits are indicated on the pages of the months for computing the syzygies (cf. vol. II, p. 207).

In vol. I, pp. LVII–LVIII, I had discussed the codex of the Spanish version that once belonged to Juan Cortés, which seemed to be lost in our time. But its description in Nicolás Antonio agrees so well with the codex of the *Bibliothèque de l'Arsenal in Paris*[^425.1] that the Paris copy seems to have to be regarded as the very copy once owned by J. Cortés. Both contain the same writings, arranged in the same order; both are of parchment, in folio, written most sumptuously and in inks of various colours, as though they were meant for the Royal Library. My opinion is confirmed by the librarian of the Arsenal, Henry Martin[^425.2].

**§ 3.** — In the *Arabic* tables a few things are found that have been interpolated or corrected by an unskilled hand: p. 6, the caliphs after al-Muʿtaḍid (cf. 198); p. 7, the small table on the right-hand side (cf. 198 and 200); p. 8, the Latin names of the months (cf. 201 ff.); pp. 31 and 87, the first and the last small tables (cf. 206); p. 188, the directions of the hours (cf. 296); lastly the additions in the titles of the tables, on which see p. 222. The almost incurable corruptions in the list of some stars for the year 1211 of the Seleucids, pp. 178–186, are set out on pp. 293–294. — As I noted on pp. 218–220 and 308–309, the tables that are properly spurious are mostly indicated by the codex itself.
@p 0425b020..0425b029
That al-Battānī had a mediocre version of the *Almagest* before his eyes I noted already in vol. I, p. XLI; and it is clear also from some descriptions of stars, as vol. II, p. 270, 274 no. 8, and 184 no. 61, where ἀσπιδίσκη, «little shield», is badly rendered *tadwīr*, «roundness»[^425.3]. I conjectured, II, 234, that it was made from the *Syriac*; it seems not to differ from the one that al-Ṣūfī, in the St Petersburg codex, calls «the old translation» (*al-naql al-qadīm*); for its error in the side of the latitude (al-Ṣūfī, p. 102, n. 2) also occurs in al-Battānī, II, 151, n. 7[^425.4]. — The tables that the author copied from the *Almagest* or from *Theon of Alexandria* he almost always indicates in the text or in the titles of the tables themselves. He tacitly followed Theon's example in counting the right ascensions from the beginning of Capricorn; cf. vol. II, the addenda to I, p. LXXII. I have indicated the *Syro-Christian* sources on pp. 192–195 and 210–211.
