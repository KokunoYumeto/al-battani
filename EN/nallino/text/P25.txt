Al-Bīrūnī, *Elementa astronomiae*. A small work without a title, no. 2497 of the Arabic codices of the Bibliothèque Nationale in Paris, according to the printed catalogue. From f. 28v it appears that the author himself translated it from the Persian in the year 420 H (beginning 19 Jan. 1029). It sets out briefly, by questions and answers, the rudiments of astronomy and of mathematical astrology, and also the chief feast days of several nations. In the codex there are some gaps, and the spaces of some small tables are empty. — The author seems to have died in the month of Rajab 440 (December 1048).

Al-Farghānī. — Besides the edition with Golius's translation (Amsterdam 1669) and the version of John of Seville (Nuremberg 1537), I have used the Arabic codex no. 2504, III, of the Bibliothèque Nationale in Paris. — The author, commonly called Alfraganus, wrote about the middle of the third century of the hijra, the ninth AD.
@p 0074b033..0074b039 + 0075b001..0075b002
Al-Jaghmīnī. — *Die Astronomie des Maḥmūd ibn Muḥammad ibn ʿOmar* al-Ǵâgminî, [*übersetzt*] *von G. Rudloff und Ad. Hochheim*, in Zeitschrift der deutschen morgenländischen Gesellschaft, vol. XLVII, 1893, pp. 213–275. — I have collated everything with the Arabic text kept in the National Library at Naples, no. 100, II, in the catalogue made by L. Buonazia (*Catalogo dei codici orientali di alcune biblioteche d'Italia*, fasc. II, p. 240); it is furnished with the commentary of an unknown author, which is dedicated to the sultan Mubārak-shāh son of Ibrāhīm and begins: اما بعد حمد الله المتفرد في ارضه وسمائه والصلاة على محمد خاتم انبيائه[^n]. The *naskhī* script is fine and correct enough; but the numbers are often faulty. — Al-Jaghmīnī, as Suter has shown, died in the year 745 H (beginning 14 May 1344).
@n The Arabic reads: «Now, after the praise of God, who is alone in His earth and His heaven, and the blessing upon Muḥammad, the seal of His prophets».
@p 0075b003..0075b007
Ḥabash, the astronomical work (*zīj*) contained in the Arabic codex of the Royal Library of Berlin described by Ahlwardt, *Verzeichniss*, vol. V, pp. 200–03, no. 5750. It was composed by the author in the city of Baghdād in the years immediately following 300 H (beginning 17 Aug. 912), and so a very little later than al-Battānī's work. The information in Suter, pp. 12–13, no. 22, and in Brockelmann, *Gesch. der arab. Litteratur*, vol. I, p. 221, must be corrected accordingly.
@p 0075b008..0075b011
Ibn Hibintā, *al-mughnī fī l-nujūm*, «the sufficient [book] on [the knowledge of] the stars», an Arabic codex of the Royal Library of Munich (in Aumer, no. 852). From the work itself it is clear that the book was written after the year 329 H (beginning 5 Oct. 940); the year 214 H, which is commonly given on the authority of a *false* inscription on the first leaf of the codex, must be rejected.

J. de Goeje, Leiden 1889 (Bibliotheca Geographorum Arabicorum, vol. VI). — Written a very little before 272 H (beginning 17 June 885).

@p 0075b022..0075b031
Ibn Yūnus, *al-zīj al-ḥākimī*, «the Ḥākimite astronomical work». When «in Caussin» is added, the work meant is *Le livre de la Grande Table Hakémite observée par..... Aboulhassan Ali..... ebn Iounis, [publié et] traduit par Caussin*, in Notices et extraits des manuscrits de la Bibliothèque Nationale, vol. VII, 1804, pp. 16–240. Caussin's book was also published separately, without the Arabic title, which took up p. 49 of the Notices; in that edition, therefore, the page numbers are always 15 less than mine for pp. 16–48 of the Notices (= 1–33 of the offprint), 16 less for the rest[^n]; moreover, the text is given on the odd pages, the translation on the even ones. — What is not among Caussin's excerpts I have taken from the Leiden codex no. 1057 (Catal., vol. III, p. 88), which contains the first 22 chapters (a most careful copy of it, keeping the same numbering of the pages, is Paris no. 2495), and from the Paris codex no. 2496, which contains other chapters. — Ibn Yūnus died in the month of Shawwāl 399 (June 1009).
@n The print has «Caussin's book was also published separately; in it the numbers of the single pages are always 15 less than mine»; the wording here is that of Nallino's Addenda et emendanda (Part II, p. XVII).
@p 0075b032..0075b044 + 0076b001..0076b007
Al-Kharaqī, *muntahā l-idrāk fī taqāsīm al-aflāk*, «the utmost that can be attained in the divisions of the celestial spheres». I use two codices: Paris no. 2499, and that of the Laurentian Library (Florence), cod. orient.-palat. no. 110 (it bears no. 290 in Assemani's *Catalogue*, where it is falsely said to be the *Nihāyat al-idrāk* written by Quṭb al-Dīn al-Shīrāzī). In the Laurentian codex the last leaf is missing, which held the end of the eleventh *bāb* of the third treatise. — About the author's other names there is the greatest disagreement. Some codices have: Abū Muḥammad ʿAbd al-Jabbār ibn ʿAbd al-Jabbār ibn Muḥammad al-Thābitī al-Kharaqī; and these are confirmed by the old authority of al-Būnī (d. 622 H), *Shams al-maʿārif al-kubrā*, [Cairo] 1291 H, vol. I, p. 27, who says that the stars of the Little Bear «are mentioned, among the fixed stars, by Abū Muḥammad ʿAbd al-Jabbār, known [by the name of al-Kharaqī], in the book entitled *al-Tabṣira*»[^75.1] {ar:ذكرها ابو محمد عبد الجبار المعروف في كتابه المسمى بالتبصرة (sic) في الكواكب الثابتة}. Other codices, however, have: Bihāʾ al-Dīn Abū Bakr Muḥammad ibn Aḥmad ibn Abī Bishr al-Kharaqī; and with these the much later Ḥājjī Khalīfa agrees (vol. II, p. 180, no. 2379, and vol. VI, p. 170, no. 13124), writing Muḥammad ibn Aḥmad al-Ḥusaynī al-Khiraqī. I think the former should be followed; the others have confused the astronomer with the more famous Abū Bakr Muḥammad ibn Aḥmad ibn Abī Bishr al-Kharaqī, a great expert in law and dogmatic theology, who died in 533 H (beginning 7 September 1138). — The book was composed about AD 1132, for in the first treatise the longitudes and latitudes of 83 fixed stars (cod. Laurent. f. 36r–37v) and the places of the apogees of the planets (f. 45v) are given for the year 1444 of Dhū l-Qarnayn (beginning 1 October 1132).
