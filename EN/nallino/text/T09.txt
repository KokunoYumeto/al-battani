The longitudes and latitudes of the chief cities are given in all the astronomical works of the Arabs, and in the Handy Tables of Theon of Alexandria. They are missing in the *Almagest*, because Ptolemy, as he himself says in II, 12 (ed. Halma, vol. I, p. 148), intended to publish the Geography separately.

From the very headings of the tables (vol. II, pp. 33 and 37), and from what he says in vol. I, pp. 19–20, it is plain that al-Battānī took the positions of the cities from the *Book of the Figure of the Earth* (*Kitāb ṣūrat al-arḍ*), in which the longitudes and latitudes had been derived either from astronomical observations or from itineraries «in a way nearest to the truth» (p. 20, ll. 4 and 9). I, p. 30) he warns: «The latitude of a place found from the table of the latitudes of the cities is only near the truth, and not so certain as a latitude known by observations». @p 0659b022..0659b035 + 0660b001..0660b010
Now we must investigate what that «*Book of the Figure of the Earth*, which is called *Geography*», was. Comparing the positions of al-Battānī's tables with those that Abū l-Fidāʾ[^659.3] gives here and there after al-Khwārizmī, Lelewel thought that it was the book *of the Figure of the Earth* written by Muḥammad ibn Mūsā al-Khwārizmī by order of the caliph al-Maʾmūn[^659.4]. But when the matter is weighed better, and the whole book of al-Khwārizmī is read, which now exists in a codex of the Library of Strassburg, it is at once clear that that opinion must be rejected. The Greek names are often written in one way by al-Khwārizmī and in another by al-Battānī; compare the former's *Ghermāniyā, Muwesiya, Janjis, Nighīrā, Qadabathmūs* with the latter's *Jehrmāniyā, Muwehsiyā, Ghanjis, Nijīrā, Qāṭābathmūs*. The Greek letter γ is always rendered غ *gh* by al-Khwārizmī[^659.5], by al-Battānī now غ *gh*, now ج *j*: *Ghāliyā, Belghīqī, Yāzūghūs, Lūghdunīsiyā, Eghifṭos, Ghālāṭiyā, Faflāghūniyā, Ghanjis* (no. 89), *Jehrmāniyā, Ṭijiṭāniyā, Frūjiyā, Nijīrā, Frūjis* (no. 116). Al-Khwārizmī determines the middle places of 56 provinces in longitude and latitude, al-Battānī those of 93 provinces (which are the ἐπαρχίαι or χῶραι of Ptolemy), differing from al-Khwārizmī in the geographical coordinates too. Moreover, leaving aside eight names (nos. 102, 112, 203, 216, 230, 231, 233, 249) whose reading is too uncertain, 58 cities occur in al-Battānī's tables that are missing in al-Khwārizmī. Of the positions of the 172 cities whose names are certain in al-Battānī's codex, 68 come from Ptolemy's *Geography*[^660.1], 57 from al-Khwārizmī (with 10 degrees added in the longitudes[^660.2]), and finally 47 from *another Arabic source*, but fitted more or less successfully to Ptolemy's maps[^660.3]. Of the cities not mentioned by al-Khwārizmī, these are the ones whose positions agree with the Ptolemaic[^660.4]: *94, 97, 98, 99, 100, 101, 105, 106, 108, 110,* 111, 114, 134, 139, 140, 171, 173, 179, 191, 200, 204, *205, 212,* 213, *215, 217, 218,* 219, 220, 223, 224, 225, *232, 272*; and these the ones whose longitudes and latitudes come from another source[^660.5]: 137, ∗141, ∗155, ∗157, ∗159, ∗160, 163, ∗165, ∗166, ∗180, 201, 202, 207, 208, ∗209, 211, 221, *222*, ∗226, ∗228, 229, ∗249, 265, ∗266.[^n]

@p 0660b011..0660b024
So the author of the *Book of the Figure of the Earth* had at hand Ptolemy's Geography and al-Khwārizmī's work, to which he added not a few things. He knew Ptolemy translated into *Syriac*[^660.6], or into *Arabic* from the *Syriac*; for a Syriac origin is betrayed by *eh* for ε in *Brehṭanīqī, Jehrmāniyā, Mehṭānīsā, Muwehsiyā, Qehsarensiyā, Lehfṭis*, by the biblical *Kūsh* for Αἰθιοπία (nos. 45 and 46), and perhaps by *Asdūd* (no. 213) for Azdūd. Two villages are mentioned, *Ḥuwwārīn* (no. 208) and *Ūrim* (no. 204), almost unknown to the Arab geographers but, as seats of bishops, not seldom celebrated by *Syro-Christian* writers. Nor should it be passed over that the geography of chapter VI (see the note in vol. I, pp. 166–77) and the chronology of vol. II, pp. 1–3, also flowed from a *Syriac* archetype, and perhaps the rose of the winds of vol. II, p. 92 as well. Perhaps the «Book of the Figure of the Earth, which is called Geography» is an Arabic recension of Ptolemy's work composed by the very famous Ḥarrānian Thābit ibn Qurra (211–288 H, 826–901), most skilled in Greek and Syriac. For it is likely that in the ninth century Thābit translated Ptolemy's Geography, not without changes, so that it might answer better to the new times, and that al-Battānī chose from it the cities, above all of *Syria* and *Mesopotamia*, that seemed to him the chief ones.
@f 660.6 0660nr007..0660nr011
A Syriac version was still extant in the tenth century, as the *Kitāb al-Fihrist*, p. 268, attests. — The first Arabic version seems to be the one that al-Kindī had had made for his own use in the first half of the ninth century (*Fihrist*, loc. cit.).
@p 0660b025..0660b034
In the passage of chapter VI quoted before and in the heading of the tables (vol. II, p. 33) the provinces whose middle points are given are said to be 94; as many are enumerated in the eighth book of Ptolemy's Geography. But afterwards we find only 93 in the tables; this may have arisen from the negligence of the copyists, or also from some confusion of the author, the cause of which is Ptolemy himself. For in book VIII, § 3 of the general survey, he states that Europe comprises 34 or 35 regions, Libya 12 and Asia 49, whose sum is 95 or 96; but in § 4 he says that they are 87! In the same eighth book, ch. 29, he gives Europe 34 provinces, Libya 12 and Asia 49 (so the sum is 95); but afterwards he names only 46 in Asia, so that the sum becomes 92. Yet in book VIII, ch. 29, § 29, Ptolemy himself makes the sum and finds 94! Nor are other small discrepancies lacking, if all this is compared with the first seven books.
@p 0660b035 + 0661b001..0661b017
In the tables that al-Battānī excerpted from the *Book of the Figure of the Earth*, the provinces of *Europe* answer exactly to Ptolemy's, apart from a small difference pointed out in the notes to nos. 15 and 16; of *Africa*, Μερόη is missing (which, however, in the general survey of Ptolemy's fourth book is not held to be an ἐπαρχία distinct from the others), but Numidia, as in the survey of the fourth book, is distinguished from the province of Africa, so that the number of the regions is still 12. Discrepancies and errors also occur in the *Asiatic* provinces. Phrygia and Paphlagonia, which in Ptolemy belong the one to Asia Proper and the other to Galatia, become two separate provinces in al-Battānī's tables, and their addition compensates for Arabia Deserta and Mesopotamia, which are strangely left out. There are two errors of little importance in nos. 51 and 66; a far greater one in no. 92, because the region of the Ḥimyarites is already contained in no. 75, and in this way the Asiatic provinces become 47 instead of 46. — Other things too must be noted. The provinces of *Europe* and *Africa*, and of *Asia* down to no. 68, are designated by the Greek names unchanged, or at most translated into Arabic; but from no. 69 on, the names of the provinces used by the Arabs are put in place of the Greek ones. Not always rightly, for Ṭabaristān is placed in the far East to represent Σηρική (no. 83), and the latitude of the region of Farghāna is lessened by seven degrees so that it answers to the Paropanisadae. — In the latitudes and longitudes of the provinces from 21 to 93 the codex leaves out the minutes; for what reason I do not know. — The positions of all the provinces are the means between the extreme longitudes and latitudes that Ptolemy assigns to each province.
@p 0661b018..0661b019
Of far greater weight than the table of the provinces is the series of the cities, in which the Greek tradition contends with the new geographical studies of the Arabs.
@p 0661b020..0661b031
In *Africa* few cities are mentioned, and these, except the Egyptian ones, with names and positions wholly Ptolemaic[^661.1]; Egypt, however, comprises Arabic names and Khwārizmian positions, though both Oases, Diospolis Magna and Alexandria betray a Greek origin. *Europe*, that is, the four cities of Rome, Ṭrāqiya (?), Athens and Constantinople, is wholly Ptolemaic, as are *Asia Minor* (except ʿAmmūriya) and the far *East* of Asia (Thinae and Sera). *India, Turkestan* and *Persia* are taken from al-Khwārizmī, 10 degrees being always added to the longitudes[^661.2]; of the Ptolemaic positions only Herāt (no. 191) and Nisibis in Herāt (no. 232) remain, which thus differ much from the other cities of Persia. In *Armenia* and the *Caucasian* regions the author has al-Khwārizmī as his guide, but he changes his coordinates somewhat, so as to be able to fit those positions in some way with the Ptolemaic ones of Asia Minor and the Khwārizmian ones of Persia. But strangely, together with Dabīl, Jorzān, Taflīs, Bardhaʿa and others, Madīnat al-Abwāb (no. 200), or the Gates of Albania, appears in the same position as in Ptolemy!

@p 0661b032..0661b039 + 0662b001..0662b002
Above all in *Syria*, *Mesopotamia* and *Arabia* the author's hard labour of reconciling Ptolemy with al-Khwārizmī appears. In *Syria* and *Mesopotamia* more than half the positions are Ptolemaic[^661.3]; many others are of Arabic origin but fitted to the Greek ones[^661.4]; only 11 are taken from al-Khwārizmī without changes (except the 10° added to the longitudes), namely al-Ramleh, Āmid, Ḥarrān, Baghdad, Surra man raʾā, al-Kūfa, al-Baṣra, Wāsiṭ, Ḥulwān, Beirut and Hīt. So the position of Beirut (no. 227) is a real monster, if it is compared with the other cities of Syria. — In *Arabia* 17 positions are taken from al-Khwārizmī; but al-Ṭāʾif, al-Jār, al-Yamāma and the unverified place of the city of Mecca show another origin, and cannot be reconciled with the Khwārizmian ones. Of the Ptolemaic map only Sāffārā (no. 97), the island of Serapis (no. 98) and perhaps no. 230 have survived.[^n]
