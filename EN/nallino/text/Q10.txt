II, pp. 76–79, where he explains the Qurʾān II, 159. The commentary was composed in the year 728 (beginning 16 Nov. 1327)[^439.4]. Leaving out small descriptions of the adjacent regions, what he relates is this: from the *Surrounding Sea* there go out «four gulfs, of which the first, if we begin from the west, is the *Barbaric Gulf*, [so called] because it forms the border of the *Barbarians of Ethiopia*; its length from south to north is 160 parasangs [= 480 miles], its breadth 35 [= 105 miles] ..... — The second is the *Red Gulf*[^439.5], whose length from south to north is 460 parasangs [= 1380 miles], its breadth near its end (منتهاه) 60 parasangs [= 180 miles]; between its extremity (طرفه) and al-Fusṭāṭ of Egypt there is a land journey of three days ..... — The third is the *Gulf of Persia*, whose length from south to north is 460 parasangs [= 1380 miles], its breadth about 180 [= 540 miles] ..... — The fourth is the *Green Gulf*, of triangular figure, running from south to north .....; its western side is about 500 parasangs [= 1500 miles]. On the shores of this side are the regions of *al-Qatā* and *al-Ṣīn*, and so it is called the *Sea of al-Ṣīn*; at its eastern [sic] angle, near the Sea of Persia, it is called the *Sea of the Indians* ..... — [From the *Surrounding Sea*] a great gulf also enters the inhabited earth from the west, touching on the south many lands of *al-Maghrib*, facing *the land of the Blacks*, and reaching *Egypt* and *Syria*; and on the north the regions of *Spain*, of *al-Jalāliqa* and of the *Slavs*, as far as the lands of the *Romans* and *Syria*. From it a branch stretches out, to the north of the *Slavs* and to the land of the *Muslim Bulgars*[^440.1], which is called the *Sea of the Varangians*[^440.2]; its known length is 100 parasangs [= 300 miles], its breadth 33 parasangs [= 99 miles]. Having passed these regions it stretches to the east, beyond inaccessible mountains and uninhabited lands. Another branch, too, breaks off from it, called the *Sea of Trebizond* (*Ṭarābazūn*; the ed. طراترون). — ..... Of the seas not joined to the *Surrounding Sea* the greatest is the *Sea of Ṭabaristān, Jīlān, Bāb al-Abwāb, the Khazars and Ābaskūn* (the ed. البكون), which are the regions lying on its shores; of oblong figure, it stretches more than 250 parasangs [= 750 miles] from east to west, and about 200 [= 600 miles] from south to north. — ..... In the *Sea of the Indians* there are said to be 1370 islands, inhabited and uninhabited; of these a great island at the end of the sea, facing *India* on the east and near the lands of the *Chinese*, called the island of *Sarandīb*, has a circuit of 3000 miles; it has great mountains and many rivers, and rubies come from it. Around this island there are 19 inhabited islands, abounding in cities and villages. Among the islands of this sea are also *Kalah*, from which tin is brought, and *Sarboza* (the ed. سريرة), from which camphor comes».
