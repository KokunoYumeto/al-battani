@p 0134nl009..0134nr015
p. 45, n. 3 (*quae centrum circuli parecliptici*): That is, parallel or concentric to the ecliptic. Since it has no name in our writers, I so translate the Arabic *al-mumaththal bi-falak al-burūj*, «made like the ecliptic», or simply *al-mumaththal*, which in Ptolemy is sometimes called ὁ ὁμόκεντρος τῷ κόσμῳ κύκλος, «the circle concentric with the world», or ὁ ὁμόκεντρος τῷ διὰ μέσων τῶν ζῳδίων κύκλος, «the circle concentric with the circle through the middle of the signs» (that is, with the ecliptic). In translating the Syriac astronomy of Barhebraeus, Nau calls it *intersphère de la similitude*; Golius, in his version of al-Farghānī, and Sédillot, in his version of Ulugh Beg, *sphaera* or *circulus homocentricus*. — It is a circle concentric with the ecliptic, in whose plane it lies; it is described on the surface of the sphere of the Sun or of the planets that is also called *al-mumaththal*, which is a solid body, concentric with the ecliptic and contained by two parallel surfaces, of which the outer touches the outer surface of the sphere of the eccentric circle (the deferent) at the apogee of the eccentric, the inner the inner surface of the sphere of the eccentric at the perigee. — It moves as the signs of the zodiac move because of the precession, from east to west.
