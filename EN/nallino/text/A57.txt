@p 0408b021..0408b023 + 0408b024..0408b026
The *pole* (in Arabic *al-quṭb*, whence *alchiloth* in our writers), or *axis* (in Arabic *al-miḥwar*, whence *almehuar* in our writers), or *key*, is a pin with a large head that passes through the middle of the alidade, the plates, the rete and the mother of the astrolabe. Near its point, which comes out of the back of the astrolabe, it has a hole, into which a small pin of copper or bronze is put, to hold and fasten the parts of the instrument pierced by the pole; because of its shape this is called the *little horse* (*al-faras*, «the horse», whence *alphorat* or *alferath* in our writers).

@p 0408b032 + 0409b001..0409b005
This instrument, most ingeniously and elegantly constructed, brings together all the uses of two Greek instruments, namely the *spherical astrolabe*[^409.1] and the *solid sphere*[^409.2]. Al-Battānī was perhaps the first to invent and make it, and he could not lack the skill of craftsmen; for in that age the best instruments of all were made chiefly in the city of Ḥarrān, from which al-Battānī came. The author of the book *al-Fihrist*[^409.3] has handed down to us the names of the chief craftsmen, among whom al-Battānī's father too is perhaps to be counted[^409.4].

Since the description of the instrument in al-Battānī's text is somewhat obscure, I think it worth while to set it out much more briefly and clearly; my words are best illustrated by the picture that Schiaparelli most kindly drew[^n]. — But first I ask that in the text, vol. III, p. 210, l. 2, some words left out in the printing be restored: after الى هذا الموضع, in place of the word فوصفنا, put واتينا على كلّ ما يحتاج ان يُقَدَّمَ ذكرُهُ بقدر الطاقة فنرى ان نختِم في هذا الموضع بوصفنا. So the translation, vol. I, p. 139, l. 20[^n], is to be corrected thus: «Since in this book we have come to this place, and have brought, as far as [our] strength allowed, everything whose mention had to come first, it seemed good to us to conclude [the book] at this place by describing the instrument», etc.

@p 0410b009..0410b021
The *celestial globe*, hung at the two poles of the equator by means of two axles, stands in the smallest of all the rings, which represents the *movable meridian* AAA; this meridian turns in its own plane within the *fixed meridian* BBB (whose rim bears the numbers of the degrees), so that the instrument can be adjusted to any terrestrial latitude. The two meridians are of the same thickness and closely fitted to each other, so that they seem to make only one ring. The fixed meridian is joined unchangeably to two rings, HHH and CC, which represent the *horizon* and the *prime vertical*; the outer diameters of these three rings are equal, whence a single spherical system arises, in which two spikes or pins are fixed along the lines that show the zenith and the nadir, making two poles. In this way an axis is produced about which the whole system turns within the *largest of the rings*, DDD; this, in turn, turns freely about the same axis SS, represents any vertical circle, and gives the count of the degrees on its rim. Its office is to carry the *index of the solar rays* E, which, by means of two pinnules, can be set in the direction of the rays, as the figure shows.
@p 0410b022..0410b026
[To the index, moreover, is joined a needle F, which can be pushed towards the centre of the machine until it touches the globe and marks on it the direction of the sun, or drawn back so as not to hinder the rotation of the inner rings with respect to the largest, outer one. The index of the rays, finally, is carried by a slider G, or movable attachment, which moves in a wide circular slot L of the largest ring; so it can be raised above the horizon at will, according to the various altitudes of the sun.
@p 0410b027..0410b033
The whole instrument, by means of two pins or axles, a lower and an upper one, is fitted to any stand of wood or iron, as al-Battānī indicates. If it is not too heavy, however, it can be hung by a ring joined to the upper axle and carried in the hand; then the axis stays vertical through the very weight of the instrument. But in this case I think it necessary to balance the index of the rays with another piece of the same weight, which moves in a slot of the largest ring made at the azimuth opposite the index itself. This double way of supporting the armillary sphere, either on a fixed stand or by hand, al-Battānī indicates, but somewhat confusedly.
@p 0410b034..0410b036
All the circles of the sphere are found in this instrument, so that all the problems that require the use of an armillary sphere can be solved, as al-Battānī shows by some examples on p. 142. — Schiaparelli.]

@p 0410b038
The *triquetrum*, or *parallactic rules*.
@p 0410b039..0410b045
The name of the instrument in al-Battānī is *al-ʿiḍāda al-ṭawīla*, «the long alidade», or *al-ʿiḍādatāni al-ṭawīlatāni*, «the two long alidades»; among other Arabs it is *dhāt al-shuʿbatayn*, «[the instrument] provided with two branches», with which the two movable rods or rules can be compared. Barhebraeus, *Astronomy*, p. 205 of the text, renders the Arabic name in Syriac ܐܘܪܓܢܘܢ ܬܪܰܝܳܢܳܝ ܦܶܫܚܳܐ *ōrganōn trayānāy peshḥā*, «the instrument provided with two forked branches» (less rightly *instrument à deux fentes*, «instrument with two slits», in Nau's French translation, p. 180).
@p 0411b001..0411b005
It differs from Ptolemy's parallactic rules in this, that in al-Battānī the degrees are inscribed on the rule HM, in Ptolemy on the rule FH, to whose division HM must then be referred. According to R. Wolf, *Handbuch der Astronomie*, vol. II, p. 26, § 333, Regiomontanus was the first to mark the division of the degrees on the rule HM[^411.1]; it is most probable that Regiomontanus learned it from al-Battānī's book.
