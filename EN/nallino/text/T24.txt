On pp. 95–101: the lunar parallaxes in longitude and latitude
@p 0686b016..0686b017
The tables are mentioned in chs. XL and XLIV (vol. I, pp. 84, 105, 110); their use is taught in ch. XXXIX (vol. I, pp. 82–84).
@p 0686b018..0686b028
As the author himself points out in vol. I, p. 82, they are taken from Theon's *Handy Tables* (ed. Halma, vol. II, pp. 98–111), and they do not always serve well for more exact computations; see also the note in vol. I, p. 262, and the addenda of the same volume, p. LXXV. In the headings he changed Theon's latitudes of the climates (computed for the obliquity of the ecliptic 23° 51′ 20″), as the new obliquity 23° 35′ required. In the places where I wrote *culm*[*ination*] in the translation there is nothing in the codex but a straight line running across the whole page; at one end of it خط الزوال «line of noon» is written, at the other خط نصف النهار «line of the meridian». On Schiaparelli's advice I preferred to translate *culmination*, for at those places it is a question of the passage of the first point of Cancer, the first of Leo, etc., through the meridian. — So that it might appear better than from the headings *austr.* and *bor.* alone which parallaxes in latitude were negative and which positive, I put the sign + before the positive ones. When the parallaxes in longitude are additive and when subtractive is clear from the rules in vol. I, pp. 84 and 262–63[^686.1].
@f 686.1 0686nl001..0686nr002
The parallaxes in longitude *at the hour of culmination* of the given sign are positive from Cancer to Capricorn, negative from Capricorn to Cancer, and nil in Cancer and Capricorn.
@p 0686b029..0686b032
The faulty numbers of the codex were easy to heal, for in each climate the same parallaxes are always repeated twice; the comparison with Theon made the corrections still more certain. Theon's tables teem with not a few slips of the compositor in Halma's edition, and with more in its French translation.
