I. **The signs of the Arabs.** — As in the preceding table, the signs of the years are diminished by one, those of the months increased. So one must proceed in the same way; but the division by 210 is avoided. Yet the former table can be called perpetual.
@p 0763b025..0763b033
II. **The signs of the Persians.** — They serve for the era of Yazdajird, whose years always comprise 365 days; and since 365 = (7 × 52) + 1, plainly after 7 complete years the signs repeat in their former order, and a cycle of seven years suffices for ever. So the use of the table is this: divide the number of the given year by 7; the remainder will show the column from which the sign is to be taken. The beginning of the month Tīr-māh of the year 200 is sought; 200 = (7 × 28) + 4, so from column IV the number 5 is taken, which means that that month began on a fifth weekday, or Thursday. — The months of the Persians consist of thirty days each; the five intercalary days, according to the common usage, are placed after the end of Ābān-māh, as is gathered from the table itself. There are, however, among the Arab astronomers some who for convenience put the intercalary days at the end of the year, as Ulugh Beg does, p. 20 of the text, 19 of the translation.
