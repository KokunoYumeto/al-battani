LXXVIII and 297: The astrological use of the words *mumāzaja* and *mizāj* will be clear from the following examples. Abū Maʿshar, *Kitāb al-qirānāt*, «book of the conjunctions [of the planets]»[^435.2], preface: {ar:فهذه اسطر تتضمن شرح قرانات الكواكب في البروج الاثني عشر وشرح ممازجاتها بالاتصالات بعضها ببعض} «these lines contain the exposition of the conjunctions of the planets in the twelve signs, and the exposition of their mixtures with one another through the applications»[^435.3]; and in the same little book there occurs a table (Pusey, p. 278 a) {ar:يشتمل على شرح اتصالات الكواكب العلوية وممازجاتها الكواكب السفلية} «giving the exposition of the applications of the superior planets and their mixtures with the inferior». — In Abū Naṣr al-Qummī, *al-Bāriʿ*, treatise V, chapter I is entitled {ar:في افعال الكواكب في الممازجات وذكر طرق الاحكام} «on the effects of the planets in the mixtures, and the exposition of the methods of [drawing] the judgements» (Ahlwardt, *Verzeichn.*, vol. V, p. 148, no. 5661). — Al-Tahānawī, *Dictionary of technical terms*, ed. Sprenger, p. 1386 (s.v. نظر): {ar:ونظرات القمر تسمى امتزاجات وممازجات القمر} «the [four astrological] aspects of the Moon are called the Moon's *imtizājāt* or *mumāzajāt*». — Naṣīr al-Dīn al-Ṭūsī, *al-mukhtaṣar fī ʿilm al-tanjīm*, has chapter XIII entitled {ar:في مزاجات القمر مع الكواكب وانتقالاته} «on the mixtures of the Moon with the planets and on its translations» (in Nicoll, *Catal. Bibl. Bodleianae*, no. 301, p. 300 a). — Oloug Beg, p. 119 of the text, 164 of the translation, promises a table {ar:از براى اتصال قمر با كوكب} «for the applications of the Moon to each of the planets»; but the table (see the translation, p. 164, n. 1) is entitled {ar:جدول مزاجات قمر بالكواكب} «table of the mixtures of the Moon with the planets». — It is clear that *mumāzaja*, and more rarely *mizāj*, are the applications of the planets to one another or to the Moon.

In the same sense the Greeks used the word σύγκρασις, «mixture»; Pseudo-Proclus on the *Tetrabiblos*, p. 76, l. 26 f.: χρὴ τὰς συγκράσεις μὴ μόνον ποιεῖσθαι τοὺς πλανωμένους πρὸς ἀλλήλους λαμβάνοντα, ἀλλὰ καὶ πρὸς τὰ ζῴδια καὶ πρὸς τοὺς ἀπλανεῖς ἀστέρας τοὺς παρανατέλλοντας αὐτοῖς, «you must make the mixtures taking the planets not only with respect to the planets, but also with respect to the signs and to the fixed stars that rise together with them». Vettius Valens begins the chapter on the influences of the applications of the planets thus: ὑποτάξωμεν δὲ καὶ τὰς συμπαρουσίας καὶ συγκράσεις ἑκάστου ἀστέρος (*Catal. codd. astrol. Graec.*, IV, p. 174). In a certain Laurentian codex (in Boll, *Sphaera*, p. 10) there is a chapter entitled ἀνάκρισις ἐκ τῆς τῶν ἀστέρων συγκράσεως περὶ οὗ τις ἐρωτᾶν βούλεται, «to find out from the mixture of the stars what someone wishes to ask about». More rarely μῖξις, as in a fragment of Palchus (*Catal. codd. astrol.*, I, p. 114): οὐ μόνον γὰρ τὴν τῶν εʹ ἀστέρων καὶ ἡλίου καὶ σελήνης μῖξιν πρὸς ἀλλήλους δεῖ σκοπεῖν, ἀλλὰ καὶ πρὸς τοὺς τῆς αὐτῆς φύσεως κεκοινωνηκότας τῶν ἀπλανῶν. — In this sense Firmicus Maternus uses «mixtura»; ed. Kroll, I, 2: «si stellarum *mixturis* mores hominibus coloresque tribuuntur ...» (if characters and complexions are given to men by the *mixtures* of the stars ...); I, 4: «quid difficilius putes esse ... an inventis stellarum cursibus definire postea quid per omnem terrarum tractum *mixtura* ipsarum radiatioque perficiat?» (which do you think harder ... or, once the courses of the stars have been found, to determine then what their *mixture* and their radiation bring about over the whole extent of the earth?); and often. — In Syriac *mūzāgā*, «mixture»; Bardesanes (AD 154–222), *Le livre des lois des pays, texte syriaque et traduction française par F. Nau*, Paris 1899, pp. 18–19 of the text, 44 of the translation: {syr:ܩܪ̈ܝܢ ܠܟ ܟܬܒ̈ܐ ܕܟ̈ܠܕܝܐ ܕܒܒܠ: ܗ̇ܢܘܢ ܕܒܗܘܢ ܟܬܝܒ ܡܢܐ ܣܥܪ̈ܝܢ ܟܘܟ̈ܒܐ ܒܡ̈ܘܙܓܝܗܘܢ ܒܒܝܬ ܝ̈ܠܪܐ ܕܒ̈ܢܝܢܫܐ’} «have you read the books of the Chaldaeans of Babylon, in which it is written what the planets bring about in the nativities of men by their mixtures?».
