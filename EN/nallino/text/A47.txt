«*The column of the northern latitude, called afījiyūn of the north* … *The column of the southern latitude, called ferījiyūn of the south*». — Plato: «*effregion septentrionalis*», «*effregio meridiana*»; likewise in the table of the latitudes of the superior planets, vol. II, p. 140, which is taken from the *Almagest*, ed. Halma vol. II, pp. 412–13, one reads written in the margin of the last column: افيثخون هو الشمال فرنجيون هو الجنوب, «ἀπόγειον is the north, περίγειον the south». How these Greek words belong to the sides of the latitudes of the superior planets is not clear at first sight. In Ptolemy's table the column of the northern latitude is headed βόρειον πέρας, «northern limit», the column of the southern latitude νότιον πέρας, «southern limit». But in the Latin version of the Almagest that Gerard of Cremona made from the Arabic[^371.1], the first column is headed «*Efegion–Septentrio*», the second «*Efregion–Meridies*». Al-Battānī, then, borrowed these from the Arabic copies of the Almagest. I think that such names arose from the fact that in Ptolemy's system the latitude of the three superior planets becomes northern whenever the centre of the epicycle is in that segment of the eccentric in which the apogee of the eccentric is found, and southern when it is in the one in which the perigee is.

I find the same Greek words, much disfigured in Arabic script, in al-Khāzinī, *al-Zīj al-Sanjarī*, but in a sense contrary to al-Battānī's usage. For (Vatican codex, f. 167 for Saturn, 178 for Jupiter, 176 for Mars) in the tables of the latitudes of the superior planets he always has these headings: افرنجيون زحل وهو صاعد في الشمال, «περίγειον of Saturn [or of Jupiter, or of Mars], and it [is] ascending on the northern side»; افحون زحل وهو هابط في الجنوب, «ἀπόγειον of Saturn, and it [is] descending on the southern side».

From Plato's version, I think, these words crept into the book of Johannes Schöner[^371.2] entitled *Tabulae resolutae astronomicae* (Wittenberg 1587). For there, p. 27, I find this: «and what you have found of northern or southern latitude in the common angle you will note outside with its part of latitude, either the northern, which is called *efregion septentrionale*, or the southern, which is named *efregion meridionale*, as the titles at the heads of the tables advise». So in the tables of the latitudes of the superior planets the column of the northern latitude is headed «*effregion septentrionale*», that of the southern latitude «*effregion meridionale*».

@p 0372b002..0372b012
I. — «*Because of their swift course about the sun*». — Likewise below, p. 117, l. 11. The inferior planets are called by Plato the Athenian ἰσοδρόμοι ἡλίῳ, «equalling the course of the sun»; by Ptolemy, IX, 1 (ed. Halma vol. II, p. 115, where the translation is not good), περὶ αὐτὸν ἀεὶ φερόμενοι, «always carried about it [namely the sun]». G. Schiaparelli, *I precursori di Copernico nell’antichità*, Milan 1873, has shown most learnedly that by these and similar expressions of the Greek and Latin writers only the apparent motion of Venus and Mercury about the sun, to the right and to the left, is meant. See also Hultzsch, s. v. *Astronomie*, in Pauly–Wissowa, *Real-Encyclopädie der classischen Altertumswissenschaft*, vol. II, col. 1859. Likewise in the thirteenth century al-Qazwīnī, *Cosmography*, treatise I, ch. III (vol. I, p. 34 in the Cairo edition of 1311 H), says that Mercury «has many retrogradations and advances, and revolves *about* the sun, يدور حول الشمس».
@p 0372b013..0372b017
II. — Ibn Yūnus writes in his introduction (Caussin p. 70): «[Among the errors] are also those that befell Muḥammad ibn Jābir ibn Sinān al-Battānī in particular in [fixing] the side[^372.1] of the latitude of Mercury». I find no error; the precepts of pp. 115–16 and the tables do not differ from what Ptolemy has. Perhaps Ibn Yūnus had in his hands a copy of al-Battānī's work that was faulty in this place, as the Escorial codex itself is (though not Plato), p. 116.
