{ar:بُهْتٌ}, pl. {ar:أَبْهَاتٌ}, *the true* (or *unequal*) *motion* of the Sun, the Moon and the planets *in a day* (nychthemeron); the word is unknown to al-Battānī; I dealt with it in vol. I, p. XLIII, n. 2. The definition of the *Tāj al-ʿarūs*, I, 529, l. 10 from the end ({ar:والبهت حساب من حساب النجوم وهو مسيرها المستوي في يوم}), which Lane follows, becomes true if {ar:الكواكب السيّارة} and {ar:المختلف} are put for {ar:النجوم} and {ar:المستوي}. Rightly Ḥabash, f. 113r: «the *buht* of the wandering stars is their unequal motion ({ar:مسير}) for a day ({ar:ليوم})»; cf. al-Tahānawī, *Dictionary*, ed. Sprenger, p. 1553. If the true (or unequal) motion over ten or five days is divided by 10 or 5, the *mean buht* ({ar:اوسط}) results; if this *buht* is then corrected so as to become the true motion in a given nychthemeron, the *equated buht*, {ar:البهت المعدّل}, is had. See Ḥabash, f. 113r–v; Oloug Beg, pp. 116–120 of the text = 161–165 of the translation (where «vitesse», speed); *Mafātīḥ*, 223 (where only: {ar:البُهْت المعدَّل هو سير الكوكب المعدَّل ليوم وليلة}). The plural {ar:أَبْهَات} also occurs in the appendix of an unknown author to the Tables of Ibn al-Shāṭir (Catal. Paris., no. 2522) and in Oloug Beg, p. 118, ll. 1 and 2 of the text. The word is Persian, from the Indian *bhukti*, whose Prakrit form is *bhuttī* (see Sachau on al-Bīrūnī, *India*, translation, vol. II, p. 346).[^n]
@n The *Tāj*: «the *buht* is a computation of the computation of the stars, and it is their equal motion in a day»; to be read «of the planets» and «unequal». *Mafātīḥ*: «the equated *buht* is the equated motion of the star for a day and a night».
