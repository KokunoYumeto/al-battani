@p 0284b015
To compute the *half arc of the day* P of a star of known declination δ.
@p 0284b016..0284b017
The arc *P* = 90° + the ascensional difference if the declination is northern; *P* = 90° − the ascensional difference if the declination is southern.
@p 0284b018..0284b024
The ascensional difference the author now calls *the difference* (or *variation*) *of the quadrant of the circle of the star*; for the quadrant (90°) must be increased or diminished by this quantity to give the half arc of the day, that is, the arc of the parallel that the star runs through on the given day from its rising to its passage through the meridian. In chapter XIII, where he had considered not any star but only the points of the ecliptic, he had called the ascensional difference *the argument of the difference of the day in the quadrant*. He repeats formula (β) of chapter XIII, which serves for all stars:
\[ [[(α)]] \sin(\text{asc. diff.}) = R\,\frac{\sin\varphi\,\sin\delta}{\cos\varphi\,\cos\delta}. \]
@p 0284b025..0284b026
For the rest, he had already taught the computation of the half arc of the day of the sun in chapter IX, whose formula (β) is general and applies to any star.
