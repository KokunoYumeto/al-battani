**Excursus on the red colour of some stars.** — In the Greek copies of the *Almagest* six stars are called ὑπόκιῤῥος, a word usually rendered «reddish», which denotes a colour between yellow and red, or also what the Germans call *hellgelb*. These stars are:
@t spec=lll 0732b048..0732b049
Arcturus (α Bootis) | Pollux (β Geminorum) | Betelgeuze (α Orionis)
Aldebaran (α Tauri) | Antares (α Scorpii) | Sirius (α Canis maioris).
@p 0732b050..0732b051 + 0733b001..0733b005
But in the *Arabic tradition* there is no lack of discrepancies, as will appear from the following survey, which comprises the Latin version of Gerard of Cremona published by P. Liechtenstein (from the Arabic version of al-Ḥajjāj ibn Yūsuf ibn Maṭar), the Arabic recension of the Almagest by Naṣīr al-dīn al-Ṭūsī, the text of al-Battānī, the Arabic catalogue of al-Ṣūfī (who in defining the stars mostly follows the version of al-Ḥajjāj) and the Persian catalogue of Ulugh Beg[^733.1]. For α Scorpii and α Orionis I can, moreover, add an Arabic version *different* from al-Ḥajjāj's (in the codex of the British Museum no. 390), on the authority of E. B. Knobel[^733.2].
@f 733.1 0733nl001..0733nl017
Edited and translated into Latin in vol. I, pp. 1–151, of the work *Syntagma dissertationum quas olim auctor doctissimus Thomas Hyde S. T. P. separatim edidit. Accesserunt nonnulla ejusdem opuscula hactenus inedita ...... Omnia diligenter recognita a Gregorio Sharpe LL. D.*, Oxford 1767 (2 vols. in 4to). Baily gave the catalogue after this edition on pp. 79–125, leaving out the Persian text. — The first edition is entitled: *Tabulae longitudinis et latitudinis Stellarum fixarum ex observatione Ulug Beighi; ex tribus invicem collatis Mss. Persicis iamprimum luce ac Latio donavit et commentariis illustravit Th. Hyde. Accesserunt Mohammedis Tizini Tabulae declinationum et rectarum ascensionum*, Oxford 1665. But the later edition is to be preferred; for it was made from a copy of the first corrected by Hyde's own hand.
@f 733.2 0733nl018..0733nr008
*Note on the Descriptions of two Stars in Ptolemy's Catalogue* (Monthly Notices of the R. Astron. Society, vol. XLV, 1885, pp. 145–51), pp. 147–48. Knobel himself reported the Arabic texts again in *Note on an obscure arabic word in ms. of Ptolemy, Al-Sufi and other derivative arabic mss.* (Actes du onzième Congrès international des Orientalistes [Paris 1897], Troisième section, Paris 1899, pp. 35–37). — The *other* Arabic version, which Knobel cites from a codex of the Bodleian Library, is the recension of the Almagest made by Naṣīr al-dīn al-Ṭūsī.

All five Hyades are often called *al-dabarān* by the Arabs. — «*Et est ut cerea*» is no doubt a gloss taken from another Arabic version of the Almagest and found by Gerard in his copy. See what I shall say of the word ὑπόκιῤῥος in the Arabic Almagest of the British Museum. — But from what corruption of the word الخوصي the rendering «*ad aerem*», which also occurs at Pollux, arose, I do not understand.

*Antares*, α Scorpii:
@p 0734b012
Ptolemy: ὁ μέσος αὐτῶν καὶ ὑπόκιῤῥος καλούμενος ἀντάρης.
@p 0734b013
Liechtenstein: «the middle one of them, which tends to rapine (*ad rapinam*)[^734.2], which is called the heart of the scorpion».
@f 734.2 0734nl002..0734nl019
«Rapinam» can hardly be thought a corruption of «rubedinem», for it also occurs at Betelgeuze, where, according to Knobel, the reading of the edition is confirmed by three codices. Perhaps Gerard had found الحرص *al-ḥirṣ*, «greed, cupidity», in his Arabic copy for الخوصي. — I think it hardly likely, as Knobel says on p. 150: «It is not an improbable suggestion that Gerard of Cremona found in his MS. the same untranslatable word which I have found in the MSS. quoted [i.e. الخوصي with its variant readings]; and one case being the principal star of *Scorpio*, ‘the accursed constellation and the baleful source of war and discord’, and the other, the principal star of *Orion* who was held tho ‘portend tempests and misfortune’, he translated it by the word ‘rapine’, having the *astrological* significance of these constellations and of these particular star».

@f 734.5 0734nr004..0734nr018
Knobel rightly points out, p. 145, that the abbreviation q (with the r-shaped sign of the scribes) of Liechtenstein's edition is to be read *quia*, not *quae* as in Baily [or *quum* as in Schiaparelli, *Rubra Canicula*, p. 7]; and also (p. 146) that the words within the signs < > are a later *addition* by some reader, for they are missing in all three codices of Gerard's version (one of about 1300, another of about 1387, the third of the fifteenth century) that he collated himself. Of this addition he writes on p. 150: «And, as has been suggested to me by Dr. Garnett, Liechtenstein's addition of ‘quia appropinquat ad terram in humero Orionis’, was a continuation of the astrological idea erroneously started by the mistranslation by Gerard of Cremona of الخوصي, or one of the words given above» (see my note 2 above).
