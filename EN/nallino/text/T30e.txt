@p 0729b029..0729b046
6. Name in Vh.: «Cor de Leo a quien dizen el Real» (the Heart of the Lion, which they call the Royal one), which seems to confirm my correction. The Arabs call the star *Qalb al-asad*, «the Heart of the Lion»; yet in the catalogues of stars derived from Ptolemy's *al-malakī*, «the Royal one», also occurs, as in al-Ṣūfī, pp. 35, 152, 157; in the *Libro de la ochaua espera et de sus XLVIII figuras* (Libros del saber de astronomia del Rey D. Alfonso X, vol. I), p. 69: «la ochena [estrella] es en el coraçon. et dizenle *el real*» (the eighth [star] is in the heart, and they call it *the royal one*), and p. 134: «et dízenle otrossí *al-meliqui*. que quier dezir *real*» (and they also call it *al-meliqui*, which means *royal*); and in Barhebraeus, *Astron. Syriaca*, p. 116 of the text, 101 of the translation: «that bright one which is in its heart, of the first magnitude, is called *royal* (*malkāyā*)». Gerard of Cremona, in translating the Almagest from the Arabic, seems to have read الملك for الملكى; for the Venice edition of 1515, f. 83, has: «the one that is on the heart and is called *king*». — So the Arabs had read βασιλικός, «royal», instead of βασιλίσκος, «little king», as also appears from this passage of the *Tetrabiblos* (book I, ch. 9) in the version of Ḥunayn ibn Isḥāq as corrected by Thābit, cod. Laurent., f. 202r: فاما الكوكب النيّر الذي في قلب الاسد الذي يسمى باسلقوس وتفسيره الملكي.... «but the bright star which is in the heart of the Lion and is called *bāsiliqūs*, whose interpretation is *al-malakī* [= royal]....». Plato of Tivoli translates: «but the bright star placed in the heart of the lion, which they call the *royal* one....»; the translation from the *Spanish* (Venice 1519, f. 12v): «and the bright one which is in the heart of the lion, which is also called *regal*....», to which the commentary of Haly Heben Rodan [ʿAlī ibn Riḍwān] adds: «This is the heart of the lion, to which the ancient sages gave the name royal, because it is closer to the zodiac than the other great fixed stars, and for this reason they placed it in the first magnitude, although it is certainly of the second magnitude»!
