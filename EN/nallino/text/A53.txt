@p 0393b021..0393b030
I have used the name *revolution of the years* because it is in common use among all our astrologers; but it would have been clearer to say «on the conversion of civil years into tropical years». For a very frequent problem in astrology is this: someone was born in the year 1100 of Dhū l-Qarnayn, on the 26th of the month Aylūl, at 17ʰ 45ᵐ, the sun being at the degree *x* of the ecliptic; one seeks the moment at which, in the year 1126 (i.e. at the completion of the twenty-sixth year of his age, or in the course of the twenty-seventh), the sun will return to the same point. This computation is called the *revolution of the years of the nativities* (*taḥwīl sinī al-mawālīd*); but if it concerns not the life of a man but the vicissitudes of cities, peoples, nations and so on, for which the computation is mostly drawn from the great conjunctions of the planets, it is called the *revolution of the years of the world* (*taḥwīl sinī al-ʿālam*). — Al-Battānī treats the same subject more briefly in appendix E, p. 148.
@p 0393b031..0393b034 + 0394b001..0394b006
The mathematical computation is very easy, and it is set out in all the astronomical books of the Arabs. But the astrological predictions to be drawn from it make up an enormous and extremely obscure and intricate matter; so very many of the Arabs[^393.3] composed special books on the revolutions of the years. The Arabs themselves knew a book of the philosopher Hermes on the revolutions of the nativities, translated from the Greek, whose Latin version, made from the Arabic, has been printed several times. The Greek texts, however, are unknown today, as Bouché-Leclercq attests, *L'astrologie grecque*, Paris 1899, p. 506, note 2: «Je suppose que ce sont ces renouvellements et contrôles du thème de géniture que l’annotateur de Paul d’Alexandrie appelle des ἀντιγενέσεις. A part cette mention problématique et de basse époque[^394.1], je ne trouve pas trace du système dans les textes grecs publiés jusqu’ici» («I suppose that these renewals and checks of the theme of nativity are what the annotator of Paul of Alexandria calls ἀντιγενέσεις. Apart from this problematic mention, of a late age, I find no trace of the system in the Greek texts published so far»).
