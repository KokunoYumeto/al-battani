**§ 4. The astronomical work.**
@p 0040b012..0040b020 + 0041b001..0041b002
I. *The name and the date of the work.* — It has no title in the Escorial codex; and the titles found in the editions of Plato's translation, «*de motu stellarum*» (on the motion of the stars), «*de scientia stellarum*» (on the science of the stars), «*de numeris stellarum*» (on the numbers of the stars), were taken from the very beginning of the book (p. 1). Undoubtedly the author had called his book simply *zīj*, the name[^40.3] — wrongly rendered *Tables* by our scholars[^40.4] — by which the Arabs designate books that, mostly leaving out the rudiments of cosmography, set out the higher doctrines of spherical astronomy and the methods of the celestial computations, and comprise all the necessary tables[^40.5][^n]. Nor was the book known by any name other than «*zīj al-Battānī*» to the older witnesses to al-Battānī's life, as we have seen, and to the Arab astronomers. But because they had thought our author a Sabian, the later bibliographers not rarely called this book *al-zīj al-ṣābiʾ*, «the Sabian astronomical work»; so Ibn Khallikān and Abū l-Fidāʾ, mentioned in § 1, and Ḥājjī Khalīfa[^41.1].
@n Bullialdus's *Astronomia philolaica* appeared at Paris in 1645.
@p 0041b003..0041b010
Al-Battānī is said to have put out two editions of the work, or rather, which is what *nuskha* properly means, two copies, the later one excelling the earlier. That is no wonder; for in books of this kind the author himself may judge, some years later, that some things should be added or corrected. Yet I think it hardly probable that he made changes of great weight in the first edition, especially if, as we have learned that Ibn al-Muktafī related, he began his observations in the year 264 H (beginning 12 Sept. AD 877); for the radices of the mean motions and the great catalogue of the fixed stars, established for 1 Ādhār 1191 of Dhū l-Qarnayn, or 1 March AD 880, must necessarily be thought to have had their place already in the first edition of the book.
@p 0041b011..0041b018
In what year the book first came out I do not know; undoubtedly before 288 H (beginning 25 Dec. 900), for chapter LII is already mentioned by Thābit ibn Qurra, who died in the month of February 901 (see pp. 301–02, and the Addenda). From this it also follows that the edition that the Escorial codex and Plato's translation give is the *second*; for two fundamental observations made at Antioch in the months of January and August AD 901 (1211 of Dhū l-Qarnayn) are mentioned in it[^41.2]. It is certain that all that Ibn Hibintā, al-Masʿūdī, al-Ṣūfī, al-Bīrūnī, Ibn Yūnus, al-Zarqālī, al-Khāzinī, Alpetragius, Abraham bar Ḥiyya and others have from al-Battānī — and it is not little — which I have cited in my notes in their places, is found in our edition.

From the Pahlavi *zīk*, Neo-Persian {img:AB01-PDF0040-G01:1.3:0.4} *zīg*, that is, the threads by which weavers set up the warp, or the beginning of the web, so as to have a rule from them in weaving variegated garments. From the likeness of the lines, the Persians had already called the astronomical tables *zīk* in the seventh century AD.

Tables are always called *jadwal*, from the likeness of the columns to rivulets (*jadwal*).

Rightly, then, the Περσικὴ σύνταξις ἀστρονομίας, made in Greek in 1323 by an unknown man from the lectures of the Persian Σὰμψ μπουχαρῆς (Shams al-Dīn al-Bukhārī) (on which Suter, *Mathematiker*, pp. 219–20, correcting Usener's opinion), begins thus: Τὸ βιβλιον τόδε καθ' Ἕλληνας μὲν σύνταξις λέγεται, κατὰ δὲ τὴν τῶν Περσῶν διάλεκτον ζῆσι «this book is called *Syntaxis* by the Greeks [which is the true name of the Almagest], but *zīj* in the language of the Persians» (*Catalogus codicum astrologorum Graecorum*, Brussels 1898 ff., vol.
