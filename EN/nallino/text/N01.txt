p. 1, n. 1 (*de chordis dimidiatis duplorum arcuum*): That is, on the sines.[^n]
@n Nallino's Latin translation of the canons is not repeated here: the canons are translated from the Arabic in the English *Zīj*, chapter by chapter. Each of his notes to the translation is given with the page of Part I, its number on that page and, in italics, the words of his translation to which it is attached.
@p 0090nl002..0090nr005
p. 1, n. 2 (*ac de earum usu*): Plato of Tivoli seems to have had a fuller text before his eyes; for he writes: «in cognitione quantitatum chordarum partium circuli et in positione medietatum cordarum duplicitatis arcus in tabulis, ut usu exercitentur in omnibus numerorum manerijs loco cordarum, et in his quae secuntur ad cordarum notitiam, nec non in scientia cordarum convenientium per arcus, et arcuum convenientium per cordas, cordarum quoque perfectarum per arcus et arcuum per cordas» (on knowing the sizes of the chords of the parts of the circle, and on setting the halves of the chords of the double arc in tables, so that they may be practised in use in every kind of number in place of the chords; and on what follows for the knowledge of the chords, and also on the science of the chords that answer to the arcs and of the arcs that answer to the chords, and of the whole chords from the arcs and of the arcs from the chords).

@p 0091nr001

@p 0091nr002..0091nr007
p. 2, n. 6 (*a circulo aequinoctiali*): This part of the title is not a mere repetition of the preceding one; for in Arabic the author always distinguishes the declination of a star placed outside the ecliptic (which he calls its distance from the equator) from the declination of the degrees of the ecliptic or of the Sun (which he calls *mayl* {ar:ميل}).
@p 0092nl001..0092nr002
p. 3, n. 1 (*locis eius apogei cum eo apparentibus*): The text, like Plato, is rather ambiguous: «et de eo quod apparet cum eo ex (vel ab) locis distantiae Solis maximae ex (vel ab) gradibus signorum» (and on what appears with it from the places of the greatest distance of the Sun from the degrees of the signs). The author means: what the longitude of the Sun, counted from the apogee, becomes because of the unequal motion of the Sun.[^n]
@n Nallino's Addenda (Part II, p. XX) correct the passage: read «et de longitudine apogei Solis quae simul cum hac [inaequalitate motus] eruitur» (and on the longitude of the apogee of the Sun, which is found together with this [inequality of motion]); cf. vol. II, p. 358, s.v. {ar:وضع}.
@p 0092nr003
p. 3, n. 2 (*De loco vero Lunae*): That is, on the true longitude of the Moon.

@p 0098nl003..0098nr003
p. 9, n. 2 ($3\frac{1}{10}$ *addita quarta parte unius sextae*): That is, $3\frac{1}{10}+\frac{1}{24}=3\frac{17}{120}$, or, as Ptolemy writes in sexagesimal parts, $3^{p}\,8^{m}\,30^{s}$ (*Almag.* VI, 7; ed. @p 0099nl001

@p 0099nl002..0099nr004
p. 10, n. 2 (*per chordas duplorum arcuum*): A rather obscure passage; Plato: «et quia quod ex quantitatibus arcuum se invicem ignotis arcubus intersectantium scire necesse est per cordas duplicitatum arcuum notas depraehenditur» (and because what must be known from the sizes of arcs that intersect one another in unknown arcs is found through the known chords of the doubles of the arcs). Following Schiaparelli, it is to be understood that all computations that involve combinations of several arcs are made through the chords of the doubled arcs.[^n]

p. 14, n. 2 (*rotae aquariae [cuius ope agri irrigantur]*): Cf. al-Jaghmīnī, p. 262: «Es erscheint dort [in linea aequinoctiali] die Rotation der Weltkugel wie die eines Schöpfrades, bei dem die am Rande befindlichen Schöpfgefässe in rechten Winkeln aus der Wasserfläche hervorkommen» (there [on the equator] the rotation of the celestial sphere appears like that of a water wheel, whose buckets on the rim come up out of the surface of the water at right angles). IX, vol. VII, 1896, p. 293. — The device is described in Lane, *Manners and customs of the modern Egyptians*, London 1890 (Minerva edition), p. 301.
