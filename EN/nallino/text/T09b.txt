It is plain enough that the author of the *Book of the Figure of the Earth*, not daring to reject the Greek tradition altogether, did not always blend his sources successfully. Sure traces of this are also found in some later astronomers, who almost copy out the geographical tables of older authors, sometimes keeping uncertain and useless things, as I have shown in the notes to nos. 234, 235, 238, 239 and 240.

To three places, namely Mecca (no. 103), al-Raqqa (no. 150) and Baghdad (no. 168), *mumtaḥan*, «[position] tested, verified», is added. Without doubt the position was verified by his own astronomical observations as regards al-Raqqa; by those of others as regards Baghdad, for the coordinates of this city are the same as al-Khwārizmī's, and the longitude had been so determined already by the astronomers of the caliph al-Maʾmūn in *al-zīj al-mumtaḥan*, «the tested astronomical work», as al-Masʿūdī, *al-Tanbīh*, p. 45, attests. The position of Mecca seems to have been fixed by al-Battānī himself, but I think that its longitude in the codex is affected by an error. — Why al-Battānī did not correct the position of Antioch by his own observations I do not know.
@p 0662b016..0662b031
It remains for me to say something about the *prime meridian*. In my dissertation *Al-Ḫuwârizmî e il suo rifacimento* etc., pp. 24–25, I showed that al-Khwārizmī's prime meridian is the same as Ptolemy's meridian of the *Fortunate Isles*; but that, since the Arab had most happily lessened Ptolemy's longitude of the Mediterranean Sea by 9° or 10°, and by about 10° the positions east of this sea as well, the later Arab geographers wrongly thought that *al-Khwārizmī's prime meridian differed from Ptolemy's* and passed along the coasts of *Western Africa*, 10° east of the Fortunate Isles. — Al-Battānī says, vol. I, pp. 19 and 20, that the longitudes are counted «from the Inhabited Islands (*al-jazāʾir al-ʿāmira*), which are in the sea of the Western Ocean (*ūqiyānus al-gharbī*)»; but in these tables the longitudes of all the places taken from al-Khwārizmī are always *increased* by 10°[^662.1][^n]. Hence it is plain that already the author of the *Book of the Figure of the Earth* wrongly believed al-Khwārizmī's prime meridian to differ from Ptolemy's, and in this way set up a new and false meridian of the Fortunate Isles, which made void the excellent correction that al-Khwārizmī had made to the longitude of the Mediterranean. In this way, indeed, the author of the *Book of the Figure of the Earth* could reconcile al-Khwārizmī's longitudes with Ptolemy's well enough; but he would have achieved the same thing, and far better, if he had kept al-Khwārizmī's longitudes and lessened Ptolemy's by about 10 degrees east of the Mediterranean.
