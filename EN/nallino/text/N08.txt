@p 0156nl001..0156nl012
p. 67, n. 1 (*sunt: aylūl [= September]*): The other Arab writers, such as al-Farghānī, Ḥabash, al-Masʿūdī, al-Bīrūnī, Kūshyār al-Jīlī, Abū l-Ḥasan al-Marrākushī, Ulugh Beg, etc., make tishrīn I, or October, the first month of the Roman (that is, Byzantine) year, as was the common custom among the Syrians. Our author on the other hand begins with 1 September, following the usage of the *indictions*, as was the custom in the Byzantine empire, especially at Constantinople and Antioch. Cf. Abū l-Faraj, *Historia dynastiarum*, ed. Ṣālḥānī, Beirut 1890, p. 98; and Ideler, *Handbuch der mathem. u. techn. Chronologie*, Berlin 1825–26, vol. I, pp. 434, 453–454; vol. II, p. 359 f.
