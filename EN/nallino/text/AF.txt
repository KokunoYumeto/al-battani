@p 0414b006..0414b015
In the title of the appendix it would have been better to translate: «and know that this direction is what Ptolemy explains in the *direction* of the hyleg». In the text, vol. III, p. 224, l. 11, I accepted تسيّر (i.e. walking, travelling through), which I rendered by the word «profection»; but not rightly. For never, as far as I know, did the Arabs so call the *profection*; when they distinguish it from the direction (تسيير), they designate it by the word *intihāʾ* (انتهاء). I had been misled by the faulty writing of the codex in that place, تسير for تسيير (i.e. direction), and by the words of the book *Mafātīḥ al-ʿulūm*, p. 230 (s.v. هيلاج): وذلك انها تسيُّر الى السعود والنحوس, in which تسيُّر is doubtless an error of the editor for تُسَيَّر, «they are directed». — In what follows, l. 14 of the text (twice), it would have been better to read تُسَيِّر for تَسِير; so I think that in the translation (ll. 15 and 16) only «dirigere» («to direct») should be restored for «directionem incipere» («to begin the direction») and «directionem pervenire» («the direction to arrive»). The sense, however, is not changed.
@p 0414b016..0414b023
The word *juwīrāst*, which Ptolemy is here said to use in the *Tetrabiblos* when he speaks of the profection of the significator, certainly shows a Persian origin[^414.1]; but I do not find it either in the Greek text or in the two Latin versions of the Arabic redaction of the *Tetrabiblos*[^414.2]. I have met the word in only one of the Arabic books printed so far, namely in the *Mafātīḥ al-ʿulūm*, which is a small lexicon explaining many technical words of all the sciences, composed in northern Persia between the years 363 and 381 H (973–991): «The ascensions of the right sphere are the amounts of the celestial equator that rise with the arcs of the ecliptic on the equinoctial line; in Persian [they are called] *juwī rāst*[^414.3]».
@f 414.1 0414nl001..0414nl008
*Rāst* = straight, direct, directly; the first part of the word plainly comes from the verb *justan* (present stem *jūy*), «to seek, search out». — The corruption of the codex, الحواراسب, arose from the archetype's having الجوى راست; the copyist, not knowing the word, took the first part of the name for an Arabic word ending in *alif maqṣūra*, and so, in the Maghribi way of writing, put ا for ى.

In the Persian lexicons the word is missing. — In the list of chapters of the astronomical book entitled *al-zīj al-jāmiʿ*[^414.4], composed in northern Persia in the second half of the fourth century H (the tenth AD) by Kūshyār ibn Labbān, I had found tables not only of ascensions «on the line of the equator» (*bi-khaṭṭ al-istiwāʾ*), that is, of right ascensions, but also of the «ascensions of the signs by *juwīrāst*» (مطالع البروج بالجويراست). At my request the distinguished Ed. Sachau, with the great kindness that is his, transcribed the tables for my use from the Berlin codex, and I saw at once that they give the *right* ascensions of all the degrees of the zodiac counted *from the beginning of Capricorn* (see pp. 163–64). — So what is said in the title of this appendix seems false, namely that the name *juwīrāst* is given both to the right and to the oblique ascensions in the astrological problem of the directions and profections.

@p 0415b005..0415b017
The words «appella» (*sammi*, «name») and «nomen» (*ism*, «name») have here a technical sense that never occurs either in the lexicons or in the other parts of this book, but is found among the Arab mathematicians of western Africa and Spain[^415.1], such as al-Ḥaṣṣār[^415.2], Ibn al-Bannāʾ[^415.3] and al-Qalaṣādī[^415.4]. For they call *naming*, or *denomination* (*tasmiya*), the division of a smaller number by a larger, so that it is expressed by a sum of fractions whose denominators are the factors (prime or not) of the larger number[^415.5]. Examples of it are \[ 7 : 15 = \frac{2}{5} + \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{5}, \qquad \text{which is written} \qquad \frac{\text{\makebox[1.2em]{2}\makebox[1.2em]{1}}}{\text{\makebox[1.2em]{5}\makebox[1.2em]{3}}}\,, \] and likewise \[ 196 : 385 = \frac{5}{11} + \frac{4}{11} \cdot \frac{1}{7} + \frac{1}{11} \cdot \frac{1}{7} \cdot \frac{1}{5}, \qquad \text{which is written} \qquad \frac{\text{\makebox[1.2em]{5}\makebox[1.2em]{4}\makebox[1.2em]{1}}}{\text{\makebox[1.2em]{11}\makebox[1.2em]{7}\makebox[1.2em]{5}}}\,, \] and is called «to name 196 *from* 385». The numerator is then called «the named» (*al-musammā*), the denominator «that from which it is named» (*al-musammā minhu*). — They sometimes use the same verb in a more general sense, to indicate a fraction whose denominator is greater than its numerator; so al-Qalaṣādī, part I, ch. IV (Fez edition of 1315, p. 13 in the margin): وان بقي لك بقية اقلّ من المقسوم عليه فسَمِّها منه, «if [at the end of the division] a remainder smaller than the divisor is left to you, name it from it [the divisor]», which Woepcke rightly rendered: «faites en une fraction ayant pour dénominateur le diviseur» («make of it a fraction having the divisor as denominator»).[^n]
@n Nallino's Addenda et emendanda (Part I, pp. LXXIX–LXXX) add that among the eastern Arabs the verb «to name» seems to be used only of fractions: so in the Algebra of ʿUmar al-Khayyām, p. 42, l. 6 of the text (p. 69 of the translation: «la partie d’un nombre est la partie dénommée d’après ce nombre, comme le tiers d’après trois», «the part of a number is the part named after that number, as the third after three»), and l. 8 («pareillement la partie du carré est la partie dénommée d’après le nombre égal à ce carré», «likewise the part of the square is the part named after the number equal to that square»); and in the *Liber algorismi de pratica arismetrice* of John of Seville (twelfth century), drawn from Arabic writings (Trattati d'aritmetica pubblicati da B. Boncompagni, Rome 1857, fasc. II), p. 49: «Although the denomination of the parts of any number can be made in infinitely many ways, according to infinitely many numbers, it pleased the Indians to make the denomination of their fractions from sixty»; there are many other examples on pp. 55–70. On the word *nisba*, «ratio», used in the same sense as *tasmiya*, see pp. 326–27. Likewise in Hebrew: in the arithmetic composed by Abraham bar Ḥiyya in the first half of the twelfth century the chapter *ḥālōq minyān ʿal minyān*, «on the division of a [larger] number by a [smaller] number», is distinguished from the chapter *lē-daʿat qeṣeb minyān mim-minyān*, «on knowing the ratio of a number to a number», that is, on dividing the smaller by the larger (in Steinschneider, *Abraham ibn Esra*, in Abhandlungen zur Geschichte der Mathematik, vol. III, 1880, p. 108, who did not understand the sense of the word); and Abraham ibn ʿEzra (twelfth century) uses the word *ʿērek*, «ratio», in the same sense (ibid., p. 101, note 157, and p. 108, note 184, wrongly rendered «Proportion» by Steinschneider).

Because of all these unusual words and new ways of speaking, wholly foreign to al-Battānī's, it can hardly be doubted that this appendix, a superfluous summary of chapter LV, is *spurious*. Who that interpolator was I cannot judge; for, as we have seen, I have so far found the word *juwīrāst* (wrongly interpreted here) only in two Arabic-Persian writers, and the others only in Arabic-African and Spanish ones.
