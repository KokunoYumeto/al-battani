On p. 92: the azimuths of the risings and settings of the signs in the climates
@p 0681b018
This figure is mentioned in chapter XLIII, vol. I, pp. 101–102.
@p 0681b019..0681b026
A similar ὁριζόντων καταγραφή is found in Ptolemy, VI, 11 (ed. Halma, vol. I, pp. 450–51), but with other numbers because of the different obliquity of the ecliptic. The figure gives the rising and setting amplitudes of the beginning of each sign, which are derived by the formula $\sin \text{amplit.} = R\,\dfrac{\sin \delta}{\cos \varphi}$ (vol. I, p. 21). It serves to compute the inclination of the eclipsed part with respect to the horizon; see above, p. 231. The numbers on the left of the radius «*azimuth of noon*» give the climates, the numbers on the right the latitudes of the climates; perhaps al-Battānī had written all the latitudes with their minutes, so that the right side should be restored thus: [16° 39′], 24° 5′, 30° 40′, [36° 22′], 41° 15′, 45° 22′, [48° 53′].
@p 0681b027..0681b037 + 0682b001..0682b003
The circles are divided into 16 equal parts, answering to the chief winds of what we call our *rose*. On twelve radii within the smallest circle Ptolemy (or some interpolator) writes the names of the winds that blow from those directions; the other four radii he calls «winter rising» χειμερινὴ ἀνατολή (SSE), «summer rising» ϑερινὴ ἀνατολή (NNE), «summer setting» ϑερινὴ δύσις (NNW) and «winter setting» χειμερινὴ δύσις (SSW). In al-Battānī's figure[^681.1] these four names are placed at the four corners of the page, with Greek names added that are so corrupted that it is very hard to restore them; and I find the same arrangement in the *Arabic Almagest* of the Bibliothèque Nationale in Paris, no. 2482, f. 128v, in which the names are: «winter rising» *al-mashriq al-shatawī*, «winter setting» *al-maghrib al-shatawī*, «summer rising» *al-mashriq al-ṣayfī*, «summer setting» *al-maghrib al-ṣayfī*[^681.2]. The Arabic Almagest of the same library, no. 2483, f. 191v, gives the circles without numbers and names. — Within the smallest circle, however, in place of those four signs of Ptolemy (?), al-Battānī writes other Greek names of winds, much corrupted, like the rest; which has probably happened already in the *Arabic Almagests*[^682.1].

@p 0682b004..0682b008
To restore the Greek one must have before one's eyes a survey of the winds, which Schiaparelli most kindly excerpted from Salmasius's *Exercitationes Plinianae in Solinum* and Ideler's *Metheorologia Graeca et Romana*. These are the winds whose direction is established with certainty, or with sufficient certainty; they are referred to the normal rose of twelve winds which was used by Ptolemy and by most writers after Aristotle.

@p 0682b023..0682b035 + 0683b001..0683b004
**[**I can hardly believe that Ptolemy wrote the names of the winds in this figure[^682.2]. In the figure of the Almagest the directions of the winter and summer risings and settings make angles of only 22½ degrees with the meridian, which in no way answers to the truth; how, then, in that space of 22½° between those points and the line of south and north, will winds be placed such as Boreas and Thrascias, Euronotus and Libonotus, which in any climate are much nearer to the meridian than the points of the summer and winter risings and settings? In the figure the directions of all the winds are wrong, and those of the solstitial rising and setting most wrong of all. I think the names were added by a *later* Greek hand, except perhaps those of the four cardinal winds, whose directions in the diagram are right; but plainly al-Battānī had already found them in his copy of the Almagest. Ptolemy, or rather the interpolator, places only 12 winds; for the normal rose of the ancients contained twelve, distributed at equal intervals of 30 degrees. But since the figure of the Almagest has 16 radii, the four superfluous directions were marked with the names of the summer rising, the winter rising, the summer setting and the winter setting, which are not winds[^682.3]. In this way all the directions received some name, but the angles that the eight secondary winds make with the four cardinal ones came out wrong; for in a rose of 12 winds drawn in this way the intervals next to the points S and N are of 45 degrees, the others of 22½°. — The rose of 16 winds is unknown to all the Greek and Roman writers. — Schiaparelli.**]**

@p 0683b024..0683b027
From this survey it is plain that in the *Greek* copy of the Almagest from which the *Arabic* version known to al-Battānī flowed, the copyist repeated at the four redundant radii (in place of the *risings* and *settings* of our Greek recension) neighbouring winds under other Greek names. The Arabic version, moreover, seems to have been made from the *Syriac*, because the name of the zephyr is written *zehfiros* in Arabic; see pp. 210–11.

**[**The four names at the four corners of the page, too, are not a little corrupted. In the figure of the *Almagest* the directions of the risings and settings are collateral to north and south, for they make an angle of 22½ degrees with the meridian. The winds that agree with these directions are: for the *winter rising*, εὐρόνοτος, which in the Greek rose is collateral to the south wind on the side of the east; so the codex's اورى نطس is اورونطس *ewrūnoṭos*. — Nearly collateral to the north wind on the side of the east is ὀρνιθίας, whose name probably stood at the *summer rising*; then for the codex's اريقنفوس one should read ارنثيس *ornīthiyas*. — To the *summer setting* ἀργέστης answers exactly; so the codex's ارقطوس seems to need correcting to ارغسطوس *arghesṭūs*. — At the *winter setting* the codex has قاطرغربا, with which no Greek name of a wind can be reconciled; perhaps κάρβας, but the direction does not fit. — Schiaparelli.**]**
