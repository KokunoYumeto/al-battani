Albohazen = Albohazen Haly filii Abenragel, *scriptoris Arabici, de iudiciis astrorum libri octo, doctorum aliquot virorum opera in latinum sermonem conversi, postremo autem summa cura et diligenti studio a barbarie vindicati et puritati linguae donati, per Antonium Stupam Rhaetum Praegalliensem. Accessit huic operi hac demum editione, Compendium duodecim domorum caelestium, ex clarissimis et vetustissimis authoribus, scilicet, Messahalla, Aomare, Alkindo, Zaele, Albenait, Dorotheo, Iergi, Aristotele et Ptolemaeo collectum ... authore Petro Liechtenstein*[^428.1]. Basel 1571, in quarto, 32+586 pp. — The first 410 pages contain the book of Albohazen, and they agree exactly (though with less clean letters and smaller page numbers) with the pages of the earlier edition: Albohazen Haly filii Abenragel *libri de iudiciis astrorum, summa cura et diligenti studio de extrema barbarie vindicati, ac latinitati donati, per Antonium Stupam Rhaetum Praegalliensem. Additus est huic authori Index capitum singularium octo partium, seu librorum*, Basel 1551, in quarto, 18+410 pp.

For Stupa had polished the barbarous style of the Latin version made from the Spanish about the year 1256 by Aegidius de Tebaldis and Petrus de Regio, which first came out in print with this title: *Praeclarissimus liber completus in iudiciis astrorum: quem edidit* Albohazen Haly, filius Abenragel. *Finit feliciter liber ... bene revisus et fideli studio emendatus per dominum Bartholomeum de Alten de Nursia. Impressus est arte et impensis Erhardi Ratdolt de Augusta* 1485, Venice, in quarto. A second edition had followed it: *Praeclarissimus in iudiciis astrorum* Albohazen Haly filius Abenragel *noviter impressus et fideliter emendatus*, Venice 1503, in quarto, 98 leaves. A third edition: Albohazen Haly filius Abenragel *in iudiciis astrorum, noviter impressus et emendatus*, Venice 1523, in quarto, 84 leaves.

@p 0428b030..0428b037 + 0429b001..0429b002
I have taken the geographical data from chapter 37 of book VIII (pp. 406–08 of the Basel editions), in which the longitudes and latitudes of the chief cities, arranged by the seven climates, are given «secundum quod tradidit Harix, incipiens in suis tabulis ab occidente» (according to what Harix handed down, beginning in his tables from the west), that is, from the meridian of the western coast of Africa. The *Arabic* text (cod. Paris. no. 2590, fol. 349v: {ar:على ما ذكر حٮس فى رٮحه}[^n]) shows that this Harix, about whom some had proposed false conjectures, is corrupted from Habix, that is, Ḥabash. This chapter is so carelessly written in the *Arabic* codex of Paris that I thought no use could be got from it; I have therefore relied only on the *Latin* version, whose Basel editions show in this chapter no reading that differs from the Venice copy of 1523. — Albohazen gives only the *degrees* of the longitudes and the latitudes, leaving out the *minutes*.
@n The Arabic, written without its points, reads «as Ḥabash mentions in his *zīj*».

Naṣīr al-Dīn, *Recension of the Almagest* (*taḥrīr al-Majisṭī*), the codex kept at Florence in the Laurentian Library, marked «Orient. 156» (no. 284 in Assemani's *Catalogue*); it has 136 leaves, which one more should have followed, so that the codex now ends abruptly in the table of the appearances and disappearances of the planets. The ink, especially in the red headings, is sometimes almost effaced; in the margins there are very full glosses written in the *taʿlīq* script, at whose end (f. 136v, margin) we read: {ar:وفرغ من كتابة الحواشي زين العابدين بن محمد الحسيني المشتهر بالا ... في حادي عشرين المحرم سنه ٩١٢} (or ٩١٣)[^n]. — D. S. Margoliouth most kindly supplied me with photographic sheets of the passages I needed from the codex of the Bodleian Library «Pococke 369», which in Uri's *Catalogue* (vol. I, p. 192 b, no. 888, 2°) is wrongly taken for an anonymous *version* of the Almagest, as also in Knobel, in the passages cited in vol. II, p. 272, n. 1, and 284, n. 2[^429.1]. No copy of the Arabic Almagest is found in the Bodleian Library[^429.2].
