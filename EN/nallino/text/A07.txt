To compute the *solstitial amplitude*[^266.1], given the greatest half arc of the day (P) and the obliquity of the ecliptic (ε).
@f 266.1 0266nl001..0266nr005
It is the arc of the horizon between the point of the east or the west and the place of the horizon from which the solstitial point of the ecliptic rises; if it is counted from the east point it is called the *rising amplitude* (*saʿat al-mashriq*), if from the west point, the *setting amplitude* (*saʿat al-maghrib*). The author can also call the amplitudes *azimuth*, since with the Arabs the beginning of the azimuths is taken from the east or the west point, not, as always with us, from the north or the south point.
@p 0266b030..0266b036 + 0267b001..0267b005
Let ABGD be the meridian circle, AEG the equinoctial circle (whose poles are P and Z), BED the horizon, H the solstitial point of the ecliptic; then TH = ε, AT = P, HE = the solstitial amplitude. With this construction Ptolemy, *Almag.* II, 2 (ed. Halma, vol. I, pp. 67–69), proves
\[ \frac{\text{chord}\,2AT}{\text{chord}\,2AE} = \frac{\text{chord}\,2TZ}{\text{chord}\,2ZH} \times \frac{\text{chord}\,2HB}{\text{chord}\,2BE}, \]
that is,
\[ \frac{\text{chord}\,2P}{\text{chord}\,180^\circ} = \frac{\text{chord}\,180^\circ}{\text{chord}\,(180^\circ - 2\varepsilon)} \times \frac{\text{chord}\,(180^\circ - 2\ \text{sol. ampl.})}{\text{chord}\,180^\circ}. \]
Using sines instead of chords, one finds
\[ \frac{\sin P}{R} = \frac{R\,\cos(\text{sol. ampl.})}{\cos\varepsilon \times R}, \]
whence al-Battānī's formula:
\[ [[(α)]] \cos(\text{sol. ampl.}) = \frac{\cos\varepsilon\,\sin P}{R}. \]

To compute the *amplitude* of any point of the ecliptic that is not the solstitial point, given the half arc of the day of that point and the obliquity of the ecliptic.
@p 0267b009..0267b012
In the formula of the preceding problem it is enough to call P the half arc of the day of the given point of the ecliptic, and to use δ, the declination of the same given point, instead of the whole obliquity. Al-Battānī's formula follows at once:
\[ [[(α)]] \cos(\text{ampl.}) = \frac{\cos\delta\,\sin P}{R}. \]

@p 0267b014..0267b015
Given the latitude φ of the city and the declination δ of the proposed point of the ecliptic, to compute the *amplitude*.
@p 0267b016..0267b025
In the preceding figure BZ is plainly the altitude of the pole above the horizon of the given city, that is, the latitude φ. Ptolemy, *Almag.* II, 3 (ed. Halma, vol. I, pp. 71–72), proves geometrically
\[ \frac{\text{chord}\,2ZA}{\text{chord}\,2AB} = \frac{\text{chord}\,2ZT}{\text{chord}\,2TH} \times \frac{\text{chord}\,2HE}{\text{chord}\,2EB}, \]
that is[^n],
\[ \frac{\text{chord}\,180^\circ}{\text{chord}\,(180^\circ - 2\varphi)} = \frac{\text{chord}\,180^\circ}{\text{chord}\,2\varepsilon} \times \frac{\text{chord}\,2\ \text{sol. ampl.}}{\text{chord}\,180^\circ}. \]
With sines put for chords, the amplitude of any point of the ecliptic for that of the solstitial point, and so the declination δ of the given point for the whole obliquity ε, one finds
\[ \frac{R}{\cos\varphi} = \frac{\sin(\text{ampl.})}{\sin\delta}, \]
whence at once al-Battānī's formula:
\[ [[(α)]] \sin(\text{ampl.}) = R\,\frac{\sin\delta}{\cos\varphi}. \]
@n In the numerator of the third fraction the print has «chord sol. ampl.» for the chord of the double arc, «chord 2 sol. ampl.», as in the formula before it; the 2 is set here.
@p 0267b026..0267b033
Ḥabash, a contemporary of al-Battānī, fol. 92r, has three other formulas besides this one. The first (since cosec $x = R^2/\sin x$, and Ḥabash, ff. 85r–87v, gives a table of the cosecants divided by *R*, that is, cosec/R = R/sin) is
\[ \sin(\text{ampl.}) = \frac{\operatorname{cosec}(90^\circ - \varphi)}{R}\,\sin\delta. \]
The second, since $\sin\delta = \sin\varepsilon \sin L / R$ (where L is the longitude of the point of the ecliptic), is
\[ \sin(\text{ampl.}) = \frac{\sin\varepsilon\,\sin L}{\cos\varphi}. \]
The third was plainly found by orthographic projection:
\[ \sin(\text{ampl.}) = \sqrt{(\operatorname{tg}\varphi\,\sin\delta)^2 + \sin^2\delta}. \]
