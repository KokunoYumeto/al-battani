**§ 2. The minor writings of al-Battānī.**
@p 0028b005..0028b006
I. *Kitāb maʿrifat maṭāliʿ al-burūj fī mā bayna arbāʿ al-falak* «The book of knowing the ascensions of the signs in the spaces between the quarters of the celestial sphere».
@p 0028b007..0028b018
That the scholars of our countries who have written about al-Battānī did not understand what this title meant[^28.1] is no wonder, since in the Arab and Persian astronomers so far published in their own language the same things are designated by another name. De Slane, in his translation of Ibn Khallikān IV, 317, rendered it «knowledge of the rising-places of the zodiacal signs in the interval between the quarters of the sphere», and added this note: «This is perhaps a treatise on the mode of calculating the amplitude of the signs of the Zodiac for every latitude. Such a work would be useful for the history of spherical astronomy»[^28.2]. — Steinschneider on Baldi, p. 457, n. 17 (Extr. p. 30): «Libro della scienza delle ascensioni dei segni del Zodiaco secondo che sono (?) nei quattro quarti della sfera» (book of the science of the ascensions of the signs of the zodiac according as they are (?) in the four quarters of the sphere); but he plainly did not know what this was, since he had thought that al-Battānī's writing on the applications and the little book on the rising of the triplicities were parts of this book. — Suter, *Die Mathematiker*, p. 46: «Ueber die Kenntniss der Aufgänge der Häuser nach den vier Quadranten der Sphäre» (on the knowledge of the risings of the houses according to the four quadrants of the sphere).
@p 0028b019..0028b024
From the title itself it appears most clearly that this little book treats the same subject as chapter LV of the astronomical work (vol. I, pp. 131–34) and the spurious appendix F (p. 149), namely the computation of the astrological *directions*, which I have explained sufficiently on pp. 313–17. Whether he proposed in it a new solution, more in accord with exact geometrical rules than the Ptolemaic one (which is nevertheless sufficient), I do not know; for the little book has not come down to our time. The same problem must also have had a place in al-Battānī's commentary on the Tetrabiblos.
@p 0028b025..0028b026
II. *Risāla fī taḥqīq aqdār al-ittiṣālāt* «Epistle on the verification of the amounts of the applications»[^28.3].
@p 0028b027..0028b030
This writing too has not come down to us; the title is the same as that of chapter LIV (vol. I, pp. 129–131), which I rendered less barbarously in vol. I, pp. 4 and 129, «on the true amounts of the applications». In it he had perhaps for the first time brought the doctrine of the projection of the rays back to true geometrical principles; for the rest, on the subject of the epistle, cf. my notes, pp. 305–13.
@p 0028b031..0028b032 + 0029b001..0029b006
He composed the epistle for Abū l-Ḥasan ʿAlī ibn Muḥammad ibn Mūsā ibn al-Ḥasan Ibn al-Furāt, who, born on 22 Rabīʿ II 241 (9 Sept. 855), was three times minister of the caliph al-Muqtadir[^29.1], by whose order he was put to death on 13 Rabīʿ II 312 (19 July 924); he greatly favoured poets and those who cultivated literary studies. On him especially Weil, *Geschichte der Chalifen*, Mannheim 1846–51, vol. II, pp. 543–56; Ibn Khallikān, ed. Wüstenfeld, no. 498 (Cairo ed., al-Waṭan 1299, vol. II, pp. 79–85); ʿArīb, *Tabari continuatus*, ed. de Goeje, passim between pp. 28 and 121; Ibn al-Athīr, *Annales*, Cairo ed. 1301–02, vol. VIII, passim between pp. 3 and 57; al-Masʿūdī, *Prairies*, vol. VIII, pp. 251, 266, 272–73; al-Nadīm, *Kitāb al-Fihrist*, p. 128, l. 23, 151.
@p 0029b007..0029b018
Some[^29.2] have thought that this epistle does not differ from the book of the ascensions of the signs, translating the passage of Ibn al-Nadīm of which I spoke on p. ix, n. 1, thus: «The book of knowing the ascensions of the signs in the spaces between the quarters of the celestial sphere, and it is known [also by the title] his epistle on the verification of the amounts of the applications; he composed it for Abū l-Ḥasan Ibn al-Furāt». They interpreted it so to avoid the difficulty that arises from the masculine pronoun («he composed *it* (*eum*)»); but in avoiding Charybdis they fell into Scylla. For to save the reading عمله «he composed *it* (*eum*)» one would have to change both تعرف «is known» (*nota*, feminine) into يعرف «is known» (*notus*, masculine), and رسالته «his epistle» into بالرسالة, as the rules of Arabic grammar would require. My emendation is far more likely; for al-Nadīm had listed al-Battānī's *books* before, and now he adds: «And his *epistle* is also famous, etc.». Ibn al-Qifṭī and Ibn Khallikān confirm my interpretation; and it becomes quite certain if we consider that the book of the ascensions of the signs deals with a question wholly different from the problem of the applications.

But astrological strength or force (ἐνέργεια) is always called *quwwa* by al-Battānī (vol. III, p. 195, ll. 3–15 and passim = vol. I, p. 130); therefore in vol. III, p. 195, l. 8, *qadr* is very plainly distinguished from *quwwa*: {ar:وقوّة الأقدار القويّة} «the force of the stronger amounts». — That *ittiṣālāt* in the astrological sense are not «conjunctions» (as De Slane, Steinschneider and Suter translated) but «applications» (συναφαί) is deduced from chapter LIV itself.
