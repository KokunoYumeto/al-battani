Since the descriptions of the work made by Antonio and Ochoa were not enough for a sure judgement, I had not a few parts of the Paris codex copied for my use, with the very kind help of Georges Salmon. From these it became clear that the codex is *mutilated* (for chapters XXVI–LIII are all missing), that the Gothic script belongs to the *end of the thirteenth century*, and that the Spanish language agrees very well with the *age of Alfonso X* (1252–82); so that there is no reason for us to subscribe to the judgement of Rico y Sinobas. The codex, of parchment, in folio, of 142 leaves, contains on f. 1–27 the *Canones de Albateni* (mutilated, as I said), on f. 28–93 the *Tablas de Albatheni*, on f. 94r–135r *De las Tablas de Zarquiel*[^67.2], on f. 136–42 the *Libro del quadrante sennero*[^67.3]. Al-Battānī's tables agree almost exactly with the Arabic text of the Escorial, as I shall show better in vol. II. That the chapters derive from a codex of the same family as that from which Plato's version derives is certain; for chapter XXV shows the very same corruptions as the Escorial text and Plato; the last chapter describes, together with the quadrant and the triquetrum, an armillary sphere such as we saw earlier in the codices of Plato; the appendices of the Escorial codex are missing.

The last chapter is entitled *De encerrar este libro y como se deue fazer ell estrumente el que es en figura del cielo a que dizen la spera* [*y*] *los dos estrumentes para rectificar* (on closing this book, and how the instrument is to be made that is in the figure of the heaven, which they call the sphere, [and] the two instruments for verifying); it begins: «Quanto auemos dicho en este libro de las causas y de las rayzes de los cuentos quanto fue dello por la carrera geometriana y la prueva. sepas que fincara todauia y non se mudara. Et quanto auemos dicho por los rectificamientos.....» (as much as we have said in this book of the causes and the roots of the computations, as much of it as was by the geometrical way and the proof, know that it will remain always and will not change. And as much as we have said by the verifications.....). These are the general considerations of the Arabic text, which we have seen to be found in some codices of Plato too. When they are finished, the codex goes on thus (f. 23r): «De como deuen fazer la spera. Et parte un quarto del cerco primero por XC partes y toma dello tanto como toda la declinacion la que es XXIII grados y XXXV menudos. Et ell otro cerco de los signos» (how the sphere must be made. Take a sphere of copper, well rounded, well made and very exact, of whatever size you wish, and make on it two poles, the one opposite the other on the diameter of the sphere. And divide what lies between these two poles on the surface of the sphere into two equal halves, and turn about each of them a line that cuts the sphere into two halves. And then divide it into four equal quarters and put a point on each quarter, and make one of these points a centre and turn about it a circle as large as the first circle, which passes over the two poles of the sphere and cuts the first circle into two halves at two opposite places at right angles. And divide a quarter of the first circle into 90 parts and take of it as much as the whole declination, which is 23 degrees and 35 minutes. And if you wish, divide the circle into 360 parts, which are of the straight heaven. And with this you will have marked two circles that cut each other at two opposite points; and let one of the two circles be the circle of the equator of the day, and the other the circle of the signs).
