P. VIII: Almost half of the preface had already been printed when *Ibn al-Qifṭī's Taʾrīḫ al-ḥukamāʾ herausgegeben von J. Lippert*, Leipzig 1903, came out; it is to be regretted that the editor often fails to indicate the variant readings and the additions of the codices. In the life of al-Battānī, pp. 280–281, after the words «known [by the surname] al-Battānī» these are added, undoubtedly found in very few codices (cf. Chwolsohn, I, 611, n. 1): «and in the book of the judge Ṣāʿid al-Andalusī he is Abū Jaʿfar Muḥammad ibn Sinān ibn Jābir al-Ḥarrānī, known [by the surname] al-Battānī». After the words «who equalled him in investigating their motions» Lippert's edition also gives: «He had moreover devoted his study to the judgements of the stars, which led him to write [books] on this matter; among his writings on those [judgements] is a book explaining Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos». Lastly, Lippert's edition follows the reading of the book *al-Fihrist* that I mentioned on p. VIII, n. 8.
@p 0079b013..0079b018
The name Abū Jaʿfar in place of Abū ʿAbd Allāh occurs also in the life of Abū Maʿshar (Ibn al-Qifṭī, ed. Lippert, p. 153), probably on the authority of the same Ṣāʿid al-Andalusī (of whom I spoke on p. IX, n. 4). Wrongly, it seems; for Abū ʿAbd Allāh is confirmed not only by the other biographers but also by al-Masʿūdī, *Prairies*, IX, 49, and by Ibn Yūnus, introd. (in Caussin, p. 72). That the names Sinān and Jābir are also wrongly transposed in Ṣāʿid is abundantly clear from the agreeing tradition of so many Arabic writers.

@p 0079b029..0079b034
6, 32–36, to be rendered word for word thus: *Significatio multiplicationis est quod sibi ipsi adiicias alterum numerorum iuxta quantitatem unitatum alterius; intelligo multiplicationem unitatum in unitates. Multiplicatio fractionum in unitates est quod sibi ipsi adiicias fractiones iuxta quantitatem unitatum, vel quod dividas unitates iuxta quantitatem fractionum ex uno. Multiplicatio fractionum in fractiones est quod dividas alteram fractionum, quamcumque volueris, iuxta quantitatem alterius fractionis ex uno* (the meaning of multiplication is that you add one of the numbers to itself according to the number of the units of the other; I mean the multiplication of units by units. The multiplication of fractions by units is that you add the fractions to themselves according to the number of the units, or that you divide the units according to the amount of the fractions of one. The multiplication of fractions by fractions is that you divide one of the fractions, whichever you wish, according to the amount of the other fraction of one).
@p 0079b035..0079b038 + 0080b001..0080b012
What I have rendered *sibi ipsi adiicere* (to add to itself) is in Arabic *ḍāʿafa*, which means both to double and to take a given quantity several times so that its multiple results; cf. the definitions of the Arab lexicographers in Lane, *Arabic–english Lexicon*, s.v. Plato rightly: *coacervatio et aggregatio* (heaping up and gathering). But other Latin translators of the Middle Ages always render it *duplicare* (to double); so Algoritmi [i.e. al-Khwārizmī] *de numero Indorum*: «necesse est omni numero qui multiplicatur in aliquo quolibet numero, ut *duplicetur* unus ex eis secundum unitates alterius» (for every number that is multiplied by any number whatever, it is necessary that one of them be *doubled* according to the units of the other) (*Trattati d'aritmetica pubblicati da B. Boncompagni*, Rome 1857, p. 10). Likewise, from the Arabic word badly translated, it came about that the Jewish mathematicians of the twelfth century, such as Abrāhām bar Ḥiyyāʾ and Abrāhām Ibn ʿEzrā, said in Hebrew *kāphal* (i.e. to double) for «to multiply», and *kephel* (i.e. doubling) for «multiplication»; examples in Steinschneider, *Abraham ibn Esra*, in the periodical Abhandlungen zur Geschichte der Mathematik, vol. III, 1880, p. 106 (n. 179) and 113. Likewise the use of the word *kephel* to express multiplicative adverbs (like *volta* in Italian, *mal* in German) is to be derived not from the verb *kāphal* itself, as Steinschneider[^80.1] thinks, but from the Arabic *ḍiʿf*, «the double of a thing, or a quantity equal to another given one», which is used in the same sense; cf. in al-Battānī himself, vol. III, p. 88: وتَضَاعَفَ البُعْدُ اضْعَافًا كثيرة and اعظم منه بأضعاف مُضْعَفَة[^80.2][^n].
@n The two Arabic phrases mean «and the distance grew many times over» and «greater than it by manifold multiples».
