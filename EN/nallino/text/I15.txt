Misled by the placing of the figure, I had thought that on p. 143, lin. 4 and 5, C was to be read for the B of the manuscript and of Plato. Not at all, as Ph. Angelitti points out to me. The letters E and F, which the manuscript has in the figure of the quadrant near B and D (vol. I, p. 143, n. 2), are to be kept; for EF represents the meridian line drawn on the plane of the ground, to which the side BD of the instrument is fitted. So the vertical side AB faces south and the vertical side DC north; the face divided into degrees faces east, that is, it is on the left of an observer placed on this side of the tropic and looking at the sun in the meridian. — If the side AC stood vertical and turned to the south, the face divided into degrees would face west and, with the numbering it has, would show not the zenith distances but the altitudes of the sun; nor would G be the summer limit and K the winter one. — It should be noted that the Arabs of the Middle Ages, like the ancient Greeks, put the north at the bottom and the south at the top in geographical maps.
