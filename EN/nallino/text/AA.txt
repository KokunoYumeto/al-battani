@p 0411b008
*General remarks on the appendices A–H.*
@p 0411b009..0411b011
These appendices, which bear no number in the Arabic text, are wholly missing in Plato, both in the printed editions and in the manuscripts. There is, however, no reason to doubt that they are genuine (except D and probably F). For:
@p 0411b012
1. As I have pointed out in their places in the translation, they always refer to al-Battānī's genuine tables.
@p 0411b013..0411b014
2. The astronomical elements and the Arabic language do not differ from those of the other parts of the book.
@p 0411b015..0411b016
3. Appendices A, B and C are a necessary complement of chapter XXXII, at whose end the author had promised them[^411.2]; without them the use of the tables of vol. II, pp. 7–8, cannot be understood.
@f 411.2 0411nl007..0411nr006
On p. 71, ll. 4–5, the translation should be corrected thus: «… of the months we have set down; how one must work with them we have explained near those tables, so that, if God wills, …». Likewise Plato: «for we have set down tables by which the beginnings of the months may be known, whose working is explained in them, so that the knowledge that we need may always be easy» (where the editions have *opus huius*, but the Vatican codex rightly *opus habemus*, «we need»).
@p 0411b017..0411b020
4. Appendix E, necessary for understanding the tables of vol. II, pp. 187–88, had already been announced at the end of chapter LIII (p. 129) in these words: «… we have made tables, whose use we have explained *in the last of the chapters after the end of the book*, so that easily, etc.». Plato leaves out the words printed in italics, so that the sense is not understood.
@p 0411b021..0411b024
5. Likewise appendix H had been announced in chapter XLII (p. 94, ll. 10–11) in these words: «… after adding to the motion of the moon, or taking from it, the seconds that you find under the difference between the sun and the moon, in the way that we have taught in that chapter *near those tables*». Plato too renders this: «the way that we have taught in that very chapter in those very tables».
@p 0411b025..0411b028
From this last passage and from the end of chapter XXXII just mentioned it is clear that al-Battānī placed some of these appendices near the very tables whose use they were to illustrate[^411.3], and that the copyists later brought them all down to the end of the chapters. If this is so, it is easy to understand why they bear no number, and why Plato, who had left out the tables, neglected them too.[^n]
@n Nallino's Addenda et emendanda (Part I, p. LXXIX) add that indeed the Spanish version (which also lacks these appendices) has, f. 63v, between the tables of the parallax of the moon in the climes and the tables of the mean motions of the planets, a *Capitulo de saber quando es la planeta endereçada y quando es estacionaria y quando es retrograda* («Chapter on knowing when the planet is direct, when it is stationary and when it is retrograde»), probably the spurious appendix D of the Arabic codex.
@f 411.3 0411nr007..0411nr008
See also the title of appendix C, p. 146: «Chapter on finding by *these* tables the eras of the Arabs, etc.».

@p 0411b030..0411b032
I. — When the «signs» are sought in the table of the collected years alone, al-Battānī bids us always add one to the sign found there; but not so when they are taken from the table of the single years alone, or from both together.
@p 0411b033 + 0412b001..0412b002
In the interval of one common year the signs increase by 4 units, in the interval of 30 years by 5 units. Since, according to the Arab astronomers, the epoch of the hijra was a fifth weekday (Thursday), the sign of the first year of the hijra is 5; so the sign 1 is supposed to have answered to a fictitious year 0.
@p 0412b003..0412b006
After 210 complete Arab years the signs of the years always return to their former order; so, when the first 210 (or 7 × 30) are known, all the rest are known. Therefore al-Battānī could make only two tables, in one of which the first thirty years from the hijra are set out, in the other the first seven multiples of thirty.
@p 0412b007..0412b011
A year exceeding 30 is represented by the sum of two quantities, one of which is 30 or $x \times 30$, the other 0 or a number less than 30. The term 30 or $x \times 30$ is an interval of years counted from a fictitious year 0, to which the sign 0 answers; the term less than 30, on the other hand, is counted from the hijra, or from a fictitious year 0 whose sign is 1. So the sign of the thirtieth year of the hijra is greater by one than the sign of the last year of the thirty-year cycle taken by itself.
@p 0412b012..0412b015
This was the reason why al-Battānī *lessened the signs by one* in the table of the collected years; the collected years are thus regarded as any intervals of years to be added to the years set out singly. But if the collected years indicate the interval of time that has elapsed since the hijra, their signs must plainly be increased by one, as al-Battānī teaches.
@p 0412b016..0412b017
That in doing this al-Battānī followed a method other than that of the authors of the *Tested Tables* is attested by al-Khāzinī, Vatican codex, f. 110v.
@p 0412b018..0412b023
II. — In this place and in the table of vol. II, p. 7, the Escorial codex counts among the leap years those that hold the places … VIII, … XIX, … XXVII in the thirty-year cycle. In both places I have restored … VII, … XVIII, … XXVI, as almost all the Arab astronomers have, and as the Muslims have always accepted in civil use; for the numbers of the codex plainly come from a most inept glossator, who worked so foolishly that he did not notice that his corrections conflict both with the very signs of the years in the table of vol. II, p. 7, and with all the tables of vol. II, pp. 9–18, 20 and 25!
@p 0412b024..0412b028
The glossator perhaps borrowed his order of intercalation from the method of the astronomer Ḥabash, who put 8, 11, … 19, … 27 for 7, 10, … 18, … 26. Besides the Berlin codex of Ḥabash, where all the heavenly motions are computed in Arab years according to such a method of intercalation, see what al-Bīrūnī says of Ḥabash, *Chronology*, pp. 197–98 of the text, 178–80 of the translation (and Sachau's notes, p. 416).
