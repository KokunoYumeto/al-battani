[What he says of the moon is true; as regards the sun, he ought to have added that for the numbers 95° and 265° one must put 90° + the greatest equation of the centre and 270° − the greatest equation of the centre; so, in round numbers, 92° and 268°.
@fig AB01-PDF0339-F01 AB01-PDF0339-body-F01
@p 0339b001..0339b011
The cause of this is as follows. The mean motion, in the figure drawn here, is the motion of the centre O of the epicycle; when the sun is at I, that is, on the line tangent to the epicycle, it also moves with the mean motion, for then its motion on the epicycle neither accelerates nor retards its angular velocity with respect to the earth T; and this happens when the arc MI (that is, the anomaly) = 90° + OTI = 90° + the greatest equation of the centre. Similarly when the sun is on the other tangent; then the anomaly = MIK = 270° − KTO = 270° − the greatest equation of the centre. In the sun the greatest equation is about 2°; so MI = 92°, MIK = 268°. In the moon, whose greatest equation at the syzygies amounts to 5°, MI = 95°, MIK = 265°, as al-Battānī has it.
@p 0339b012..0339b015
As regards the moon, however, it must be noted that the greatest equation can change because of the eccentricity and reach 7° 40′; so at the quadratures, where the effect of the eccentricity is greatest, MI = 97° 40′, MIK = 262° 20′. All these considerations al-Battānī left out from too great a desire for brevity. — Schiaparelli.]
