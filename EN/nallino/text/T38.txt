@p 0763b037..0763b038 + 0764b001..0764b016
Part of the title, «*the intercalation being put in the month of December according to the method of the Romans*», is a most inept interpolation; the signs of the table themselves show that the intercalation is always put at the end of February, for in common years (in which February comprises 28 = 7 × 4 days) the signs of February and March are equal, while in leap years (since 29 = 7 × 4 + 1) the sign of March exceeds the sign of February by one. The signs of January, on the other hand, always exceed those of December by three; for December consists of 31 = (7 × 4) + 3 days. — But a witless reader, seeing the leap years marked in the table between the column of December and the column of January, did not understand the construction of the table and thought that the intercalation was made at the end of December and that the month had 32 days. A thing altogether unheard of in chronology! But it is even more remarkable that the same most foolish error was propagated by later writers of Spain and western Africa on astronomical and chronological matters. Writing the *Libro de las taulas alfonsíes* by order of Alfonso X, about AD 1272, Yhuda son of Moses and Rabiçag Aben Cayut (ch. II; in *Libros del saber de astronomia*, vol. IV, p. 121) relate this of the Roman years: «They disagree about the month to which they should add the bissextile day; for some add it to the month of December, others to February. So [that is, to February] all the Romans do now in this our time; so we too have laid it down in these our tables»[^764.1]. Muḥammad al-Sūsī, *al-Mumtaʿ fī sharḥ al-maqnaʿ*, Fez 1313 H, fasc. III, p. 3, dealing with the *ʿajamiyya* «foreign» or Julian year[^764.2], in the commentary on the verse[^n]

Since the first month in the table is October, and the sign of October in the first year is 2, it is certain that the era of Dhū l-Qarnayn or of the Seleucids is considered here according to the *common* usage (different from al-Battānī's), whose beginning is a second weekday, or Monday, 1 October 312 BC. The table also differs from al-Battānī's on p. 8 in that it gives the true signs, not signs deliberately diminished by one. It agrees with the tables of al-Bīrūnī, *Chron.* p. 195 of the text, 175 of the translation, and of Ulugh Beg, p. 18 of the text, 17 of the translation, which, however, do not give the convenient survey of the numbers divisible by 28. On this cycle of 28 years see vol. I, p. 323.
