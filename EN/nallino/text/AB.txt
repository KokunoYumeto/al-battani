That the era of Dhū l-Qarnayn, or of the Seleucids, begins according to al-Battānī on 1 September (Aylūl), not on 1 October, 312 BC, I have pointed out already in note *a* on chapter XXXII (p. 242) and elsewhere.
@p 0412b033..0412b036
The table of vol. II, p. 8, contains only the first 28 years, for after 28 years have elapsed the signs return to their former order. But why al-Battānī lessened the signs of the years and the months by one I do not understand; for afterwards one must add one again to the sign taken from the table. It would have been much more convenient to put the signs in the table without lessening them.
@p 0412b037..0412b043
Al-Battānī's rule, moreover, namely always to add a year to the given years, is false. It would be true if the Roman years were always common; for since in the interval of a common year the signs increase by one, to look up the year $x + 1$ in the table is the same as to increase by one the sign found for the year $x$. But in a leap year the signs increase by two units; so al-Battānī's precept becomes false whenever the given year $x$ is a leap year, for the sign of the year $x + 1$ is then the sign of the year $x$ increased by *two* units. The true rule was always to enter the table with the given year $x$, and to add one to the corresponding *sign*.
