On pp. 19–23: the mean motions of the sun and the moon for the Arab calendar
@p 0652b038
These tables are dealt with in chapters XXVII and XXXIII (vol. I, pp. 42 and 71).
@p 0652b039..0652b040 + 0653b001..0653b006
In the headings of the columns the codex always has *al-masīr al-awsaṭ*, «the mean course», which fits only pp. 20–23, for they give the *mean motions*, that is, the degrees that the sun, the moon, etc. complete by their mean motion in a given interval of time. But p. 19 gives the *mean longitudes* that the sun, the moon, etc. held at the beginning of the years 1, 31, etc. of the hijra; so its numbers are the *epochs*, or *radices*, of the mean motions, from which the longitudes for any moment of time are afterwards derived. Therefore I have changed a little, in the translation, the headings of the columns of p. 19, which I kept in the Arabic as they stood in the codex. See also the notes on pp. 24–28, 72–77 and 102–103.
@p 0653b007..0653b009
The motion of the *node* is retrograde; but in al-Battānī's tables it is computed as if it were direct, so that the author himself, vol. I, pp. 75–76 (ch. XXXVII), bids us always take the motion of the node taken from the tables from 360°.
@p 0653b010..0653b011
**P. 19**, under the years 661 ff.: it was easy to restore the degrees of the anomaly of the moon, which are left out in the codex.
@p 0653b012..0653b015
**P. 21**: the codex gives only the Dhū l-Ḥijja of the common years, as on p. 74 Subāṭ (February); for al-Battānī's tables for the collected and the single years are so made that in computing there is never any need to distinguish the leap years from the common ones. On Schiaparelli's advice, however, I have added the Dhū l-Ḥijja of the leap years.
@p 0653b016..0653b020
**P. 22**: two errors of the codex, which escaped me, are to be corrected: in the seconds of the course of the sun in 10 days (where the codex and the translation have 25″ كه instead of 24″ كد), and in the seconds of the node in 21 days (where 45″ مه stands for 44″ مد). — The same table is on p. 75, with three discrepancies that come from al-Battānī himself, namely 15″ in the motion of the sun in nine days, 23″ in ten, and 38′ 59″ (for 39′ 0″) in the anomaly of the moon in ten days.
@p 0653b021..0653b025
**P. 23**: two errors of the codex, which crept into the translation, are to be corrected: 25″ كه of the codex for 24″ كد in the course of the sun in three hours, and 13″ يج (codex لج 33″) for 14″ يد in the lunar anomaly of 23 hours. — The same table is on p. 76, where there are 11″ instead of 10″ in the lunar motion of fourteen hours and 37″ instead of 38″ in the anomaly of ten hours (exactly 5° 26′ 37″ 28‴ 17$^{\mathrm{IV}}$ …); both discrepancies seem to come from al-Battānī.
