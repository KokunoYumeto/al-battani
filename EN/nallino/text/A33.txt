[«*But do not count with them the year in which you are, unless the noon of the last day of Subāṭ* [February] *has already passed*».
@p 0335b012..0335b018
To avoid the inconvenience that arises from an intercalary day placed in the middle of the year, al-Battānī set up the epochs of the tables for a fictitious year, whose beginning is not 1 Aylūl (September) but the preceding 1 Ādhār (March); and so in the tables of the monthly motions (vol. II, pp. 74, 87, 104, 107) he always put Ādhār as the first month, not Aylūl. In this way the intercalary day is the last of his fictitious year; the numbers that answer to the single months in the tables remain unchanged in leap years and in common years alike, except for Subāṭ, which, however, never needs to be used, for when Subāṭ is complete the year too is complete.
@p 0335b019..0335b023
So the epochs of the mean motions for the single years on p. 72 of vol. II of the tables are not for the beginning of Aylūl of that year, but for the noon of the first day of the preceding Ādhār (March). This will be plainer if one considers that for the year 1191 of Dhū l-Qarnayn the longitude of the sun is given as 342° 48′ 50″, which can never happen on the first day of September in the Julian reckoning, unless times most remote from al-Battānī and from us are to be thought of.
@p 0335b024..0335b029
It is also to be noted that on p. 72 of vol. II the mean longitude of the sun in the year 1211 of Dhū l-Qarnayn is a whole number of degrees, without minutes and seconds; so I think that al-Battānī, in constructing his table, set out from the *year 1211* of Dhū l-Qarnayn as from a fundamental epoch, and derived the other epochs from it with the help of the mean motions. — For the anomaly of the moon and the motion of the node, on the other hand, he seems to have taken the *year 1191* as the fundamental epoch, for under this year the numbers are round. — Schiaparelli.]
