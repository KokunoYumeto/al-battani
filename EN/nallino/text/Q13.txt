318: Suter rightly points out (Bibl. Math., 3rd series, vol. 5, 1904, p. 88) that my demonstration must be corrected. Let the meridians be drawn from the pole of the Earth as far as the equator[^n], passing through the given city A and through Mecca B; let A and B be joined by the arc of a great circle, which will measure the distance of the cities; let the arc AC of a great circle also be drawn perpendicular to the meridian of the *given city*, that is, the line of rising and setting; the azimuth of the qibla will be the angle BAC. In the triangle PAB
\[ \sin PAB = \frac{\sin PB\,\sin P}{\sin AB} = \frac{\sin(\lambda - \lambda')\cos\varphi}{\sin\text{distance}}; \]
and since PAB = 90° + azimuth,
\[ \cos\text{azimuth} = \frac{\sin(\lambda - \lambda')\cos\varphi}{\sin\text{distance}}, \]
which formula differs from mine in having φ put in place of φ′.
@n The print has «usque ab aequatorem» for «usque ad aequatorem».

III, nr. 2), p. 155, has shown that it stood near *Khotan* ({ar:ختن}) or *Ilči*, 80° E of Greenwich, in the valley of the river Tarīm.
