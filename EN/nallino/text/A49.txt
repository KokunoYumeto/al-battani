@p 0373b016
*The configurations of the fixed stars* with the sun.
@p 0373b017..0373b022
By the words *tashrīq* and *taghrīb* the Arab astronomers mostly designate the heliacal *rising* and *setting* of the stars, as appears from the definition of the lexicon *Mafātīḥ al-ʿulūm*: «*Tashrīq* takes place when the star is seen to rise in the eastern quarter before sunrise; *taghrīb* when it is seen to set in the western quarter after sunset»[^373.2]. But this definition does not always agree with al-Battānī's usage in this chapter; so I have preferred to use the words *earlier rising* and *earlier setting*.

What is described in this chapter are the nine kinds (τρόποι) of configurations (σχηματισμοί) of the fixed stars with respect to the sun, already described by Ptolemy, VIII, 4 (ed. Halma vol. II, pp. 100–104), whose Greek names, with Halma's translation, I am pleased to give, so that they may be compared with al-Battānī's:

@p 0374b012..0374b013
*The equatorial arcs of seeing* of the fixed stars, according to the commonly received tradition («they say»).
@p 0374b014..0374b023
These arcs are computed on the equinoctial circle, as I have already shown on p. 283. — Ptolemy, *Almag.* VIII, 6 (ed. Halma vol. II, pp. 108–113), treats of the appearances and disappearances of the fixed stars; he shows that the solution of the problem is very difficult, but gives no amount of those arcs. L. Ideler, *Handbuch der mathematischen und technischen Chronologie*, Berlin 1825–26, vol. I, p. 55, rightly says that what circulates in many astronomical books rests on no foundation, namely that Ptolemy determined the arc of seeing of the stars of the first magnitude as 12°, of the second 13°, of the third 14°, of the fourth 15°, of the fifth 16°, and lastly of the sixth (which are seen when the twilight is wholly gone) 18°. Nothing of this kind is found in the known books of Ptolemy. It must also be noted that these arcs are the *depression* of the sun below the horizon, which is computed on the *vertical circle*.
@p 0374b024..0374b030
Ptolemy says that he wrote a little work in which he determined the arcs of seeing of the fixed stars both on the vertical circle and on the ecliptic. The little book has not come down to us; yet L. Ideler, *Ueber den Kalender des Ptolemaeus*[^374.1], deduced from the information contained in Ptolemy's book *On the appearances of the fixed stars* (φάσεις ἀπλανῶν ἀστέρων) that the Greek astronomer computed a depression of the sun of 11° and 7° for stars of the first magnitude, and of 14° and 8 1/2° for stars of the second magnitude; the numbers 11 and 14 hold when the star and the sun rise or set in the same quarter of the horizon, 7 and 8 1/2 when they are in two opposite quarters.
@f 374.1 0374nl001..0374nl006
In Schriften der k. Akademie der Wissenschaften zu Berlin, 1816 and 1817. — A French version is at the end of the work *Table chronologique des règnes; apparition des étoiles fixes de C. Ptolémée, Théon etc., publiées et traduites par Halma*, Paris 1819.
@p 0374b031..0374b035
The number 15° for the stars of the first magnitude perhaps comes from the book of Autolycus *On risings and settings* (περὶ ἐπιτολῶν καὶ δύσεων), already very well known to the Arabs in al-Battānī's time; for Qusṭā ibn Lūqā had translated it into Arabic, and Thābit ibn Qurra had corrected it. Autolycus (fourth century BC), however, counts the arc of seeing on the *ecliptic*, and sets 15° for all the fixed stars[^374.2].
@f 374.2 0374nl007..0374nr006
P. Tannery, *Autolycos de Pitane*, in Mémoires de la Société des sciences physiques et naturelles de Bordeaux, 3rd series, vol. II, fasc. 1, 1885, pp. 173–199. — Eudoxus in the *Parapegma* gives different arcs, which vary from 20 1/4° or 21° (the Pleiades, the Hyades, Orion, the Dog) to 8 3/4° (the Dolphin); Tannery, loc. cit., pp. 190–191.

But on what principle they laid down that the equatorial arcs of seeing grow regularly by three degrees, so that in the stars of the sixth magnitude they are about 30°, is not clear. [It is probable that all this comes from rough computations and observations; namely that the Arabs before al-Battānī laid down that the stars of the first magnitude appear 1ʰ (= 15°) before sunrise and 1ʰ after sunset, and the stars of the sixth magnitude 2ʰ (= 30°) before sunrise and after sunset, and that the other orders of the magnitude of the stars were placed regularly within these limits. — Schiaparelli.]
@p 0375b005..0375b008
Ulugh Beg, p. 143 of the text, 193 of the translation, considers the *depression* of the sun below the horizon; he computes it at about 12° for stars of the first magnitude, 14° for stars of the second, and so, with a regular increase of 2° in the following orders, he sets 22° for stars of the sixth magnitude. He adds that this holds for stars that are near the ecliptic[^375.1].
