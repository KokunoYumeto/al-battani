LXXVIII and 301: Suter points out (Bibl. Math., 1904, pp. 84–85), as Steinschneider had already noticed, that that book, the *Introductorium Ptolemaei ad artem sphaericam*, is the Arabic version of the *Elements of Astronomy* (Εἰσαγωγὴ εἰς τὰ φαινόμενα) commonly attributed to Geminus (Γεμῖνος)[^436.1]. The passage quoted by al-Bīrūnī occurs on p. 286 of Manitius's edition, in the Latin version that fills the gap of the Greek text at I, 9: «and the two turnings [i.e. the tropics] and the two equalities fall, according to the opinion of the judgements of the Greek astrologers, in the first degrees of these signs, and according to the opinion of the Chaldaeans they fall in the eighth degrees of these signs. ... And there is no difference between the Greeks and the Chaldaeans except in the division of the signs, since the first points of the signs do not coincide with them, but with the Chaldaeans they are 8 degrees ahead. The point of the summer tropic, then, according to the opinion of the Greeks is in the first part of Cancer, and according to the opinion of the Chaldaeans in its eighth part. And the matter runs likewise in the other points». In al-Bīrūnī's text edited by Sachau, therefore, وحَكَى, «and [Ptolemy too] relates», must be read for وحُكي, «and [the Chaldaeans] are said». The passage quoted by al-Masʿūdī is put together from Geminus XVI, 19 and 21–24.

The years of the world are known only when the Sun enters the first minute of Aries; at that hour you must set up the ascendant in its degree and its minute, so that nothing of it is hidden from you, and then set up the four cardines too in their degrees and their minutes».
