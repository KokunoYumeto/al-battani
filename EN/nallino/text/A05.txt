The computation of the *right ascension*. Let δ be the declination of the given degree of the ecliptic, *α* its right ascension, ε the obliquity of the ecliptic; Ptolemy, *Almag.* I, 13 (ed. Halma, vol. I, pp. 60–61), proves by the theorem of Menelaus
\[ \frac{\text{chord}\,2\alpha}{\text{chord}\,180^\circ} = \frac{\text{chord}\,2\delta}{\text{chord}\,(180^\circ - 2\delta)} \times \frac{\text{chord}\,(180^\circ - 2\varepsilon)}{\text{chord}\,2\varepsilon}. \]
Using sines instead of chords, one derives
\[ \frac{2\sin\alpha}{2R} = \frac{2\sin\delta}{2\cos\delta} \times \frac{2\cos\varepsilon}{2\sin\varepsilon}, \]
whence al-Battānī's formula
\[ \sin\alpha = R\,\frac{\sin\delta\,\cos\varepsilon}{\cos\delta\,\sin\varepsilon}. \]
Ḥabash, who lived in the same age as al-Battānī, had constructed tables of tangents and cotangents (taking a radius of 60 parts), and so could teach in his work (fol. 88r), more briefly[^n],
\[ \sin\alpha = \frac{\operatorname{tg}\delta\,\operatorname{cotg}\varepsilon}{R}. \]
@n The print has sin α = R tg δ cotg ε; Nallino's Addenda et emendanda (Part II, p. XXIV) correct it to tg δ cotg ε : R, which is right when the sine and the tangents are all taken to the radius R.

@p 0253b018..0253b030
Ḥabash always counts the right ascensions from the beginning of Aries, as the other Arab astronomers published so far usually do; Abū ʿAlī al-Ḥasan, however (Aboul Hhassan, ch. XXXII, pp. 218–227 and in the tables), follows al-Battānī's usage. Ibn Yūnus (on the evidence of Delambre, p. 122) made a double table of right ascensions, counting them both from the beginning of Aries and from the solstitial colure of Capricorn; and so did Ulugh Beg (Oloug Beg, p. 67 of the text, 93 of the translation, in ch. VII of the second part), who afterwards uses the ascensions counted from the winter solstitial colure in the astrological computations of the direction of the significator (p. 161 of the text, 214 of the translation) and of the projection of the rays (p. 158 of the text, 210 of the translation), in which it is more convenient to take the distances from the solstitial colure than from the equinoctial one. Tables of right ascensions beginning from the winter solstitial colure, for every minute of the ecliptic, were also constructed by ʿAbd al-Qādir ibn Muḥammad al-Fayyūmī, who died in 1022 H (1613–14); they are kept in the Cairo Library; see Suter, *Die Mathematiker und Astronomen der Araber*, p. 193, no. 479.

The oblique ascensions, on the other hand, are always counted from the beginning of Aries.
