@p 0339b019..0339b023
The names *head* and *tail* were unknown to the Greek astronomers, among whom ὁ ἀναβιβάζων [σύνδεσμος], «the ascending [node]», is the northern node, and ὁ καταβιβάζων, «the descending», the southern. Head and tail refer to the celestial Dragon, which the Gnostics and certain Greek astrologers of the first century AD had taken over from the superstitions of the Chaldaeans; see A. Bouché-Leclercq, *L’astrologie grecque*, Paris 1899, pp. 121–123.
@p 0339b024..0339b032
The word *jawzahar*, from the Persian *gawzahr* ڭوزهر, means: 1) the parecliptic circle of the moon (see p. 45, note 3), or the lunar orbit; 2) the nodes of the lunar orbit, and especially the ascending node; 3) the nodes of the orbit of any planet. It is probably the Zend word *gaocithra*, «[keeping] the seed of cattle», an epithet of the moon in the book called *Avesta*; for they thought that the moon had kept the seed of the first bull and had produced from it the various kinds of herds[^339.1]. In Latin writers of the eleventh century and after, the Arabic name is often found corrupted into *iozahar*, *iehuzar*, *gehuzahar*, *geuzaar*, *genzahar*, *zeunzaar*, *çeuçahat*; see M. Steinschneider, *Lettera seconda a D. B. Boncompagni, intorno al libro «Saraceni cuiusdam de eris»*, Rome 1863, pp. 15–16 and 19.
