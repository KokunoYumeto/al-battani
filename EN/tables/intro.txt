In the Escorial codex the tables follow the chapters. Of them Nallino printed in Part III only those that contain words, the chronological and the geographical tables and the names of the fixed stars, as the codex has them; the tables of numbers only are in the Latin version (Part II). The English below translates the words of these tables and gives their numbers in figures, read from the abjad letters with their Western values. Where Nallino corrects a number of the codex in Part II, the English gives Nallino's value, marked *, and the codex's reading in the notes; where Part II differs from Part III without a note of Nallino's, the English keeps the value of Part III, marked †, and gives that of Part II in the notes. In the star catalogue the longitudes are counted in degrees from the beginning of the Ram through the whole circle of the signs, and N or S gives the side of the ecliptic on which the latitude lies; a magnitude «greater» or «lesser» is a little brighter or fainter than its number. The column «Star» gives Nallino's identification in the modern designation. Where Nallino's translation corrects the wording of the Arabic, the English follows it and the notes give the Arabic. The tables of the stars for the year 1211 give, for the latitude of al-Raqqa, each star's distance from the equator, its altitude when it culminates, half the time it stays above the earth in degrees of the equator, and the degrees of the ecliptic that culminate, rise and set with it; their stars are numbered as in Nallino's translation. The codex draws the two figures of pp. 243 and 244 as circles, and the English sets out what they contain as tables: in the figure of the seven climes each line of the circle becomes a row, with the climes as columns; in the astrological figure each sign becomes a column, with its circles as rows. The numbers of the figures are those of the codex; where Nallino's Latin figure differs, the English marks the codex's number † and gives Nallino's in the notes. Names of persons and places are transliterated as they are written, with Nallino's reading of them beside them. The pages of Part III are given in the margin.
