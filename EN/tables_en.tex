\clearpage\pdfbookmark[0]{The tables of Part III}{tables}{\Large\bfseries The tables of Part III\par}\medskip
In the Escorial codex the tables follow the chapters. Of them Nallino printed in Part III only those that contain words, the chronological and the geographical tables and the names of the fixed stars, as the codex has them; the tables of numbers only are in his Latin version (Part II). The English below translates the words of these tables and gives their numbers in figures, read from the abjad letters with their Western values. Where Nallino corrects a number of the codex in Part II, the English gives his value, marked *, and the codex's reading in the notes; where Part II differs from Part III without a note of his, the English keeps the value of Part III, marked †, and gives that of Part II in the notes. In the star catalogue the longitudes are counted in degrees from the beginning of the Ram through the whole circle of the signs, and N or S gives the side of the ecliptic on which the latitude lies; a magnitude «greater» or «lesser» is a little brighter or fainter than its number. The column «Star» gives Nallino's identification in the modern designation. Where his translation corrects the wording of the Arabic, the English follows it and the notes give the Arabic. The tables of the stars for the year 1211 give, for the latitude of al-Raqqa, each star's distance from the equator, its altitude when it culminates, half the time it stays above the earth in degrees of the equator, and the degrees of the ecliptic that culminate, rise and set with it; their stars are numbered as in Nallino's translation. The codex draws the two figures of pp. 243 and 244 as circles, and the English sets out what they contain as tables: in the figure of the seven climes each line of the circle becomes a row, with the climes as columns; in the astrological figure each sign becomes a column, with its circles as rows. The numbers of the figures are those of the codex; where Nallino's Latin figure differs, the English marks the codex's number † and gives his in the notes. Names of persons and places are transliterated as they are written, with Nallino's reading of them beside them. The pages of Part III are given in the margin.
\par\needspace{8\baselineskip}\bigskip\pdfbookmark[1]{Notice}{tab-notice}{\large\bfseries Notice\par}\ArPage{227}\nobreak\medskip
Carlo Nallino, who has taken care of establishing and correcting the text of this book, says: All the chapters are complete, and in the Escorial copy the tables follow them. We, however, have taken from them only what concerns chronology, geography and the names of the fixed stars, and have printed it without correcting the errors and the corruptions in the abjad letters — and, by God, how many these mistakes are! Whoever wishes to correct them should consult our Latin translation of this book, in which we have also printed the rest of the tables, which contain numbers only. The reader must also be told that the abjad letters have the same values in the tables as in the Escorial copy, that is, by the usage of the people of the West: so Ṣ stands for 60, Ḍ for 90, S for 300 and Ẓ for 800.\par{\centering End, end.\par}\par{\centering End.\par}
