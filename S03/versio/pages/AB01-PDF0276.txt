@obs body L1-L8: the formulas are checked. In the first figure E is the centre of the meridian and AY = φ, so that EX = cos φ and XY = sin φ (the perpendicular from Y to the horizon and its distance from E, marked cos φ and sin φ); the parallel FC of the Sun meets the horizon in C and the vertical in M, so that EC = sin ampl. ort. and EM = sin alt. sine azimuth. FC is parallel to YE, so that the right triangles MEC and YXE have equal angles at M and E (the marked angles), and EM : EC = EX : XY gives formula I; formula II is formula I written as a division by the fraction sin φ / cos φ.
@obs body L9-L15: formula III follows from I with sin amplit. ortivae = R sin δ / cos φ, the rule of chapter VII. In the second figure E is the centre, EP the axis to the pole P, whose arc to the horizon is φ, PZ the perpendicular from P to the horizon (sin φ) and Q the point where the parallel through M meets the axis, so that EQ = sin δ (marked) and EP = R. The parallel is perpendicular to the axis and PZ is parallel to the vertical EM, so the right triangles MEQ (right angle at Q) and EPZ (right angle at Z) have equal angles at E and P, and EM : EQ = EP : PZ, as printed.
