% Printed p. 93: the end of chapter 30: the figure for the moon's light. The moon's circle goes on, by a day or
% less, to the opposition, where its centre is ز; the centres of the moon's circles are set between ز and ن at the
% marks د, one after another. From the ends ا and ج of the sun's diameter lines are drawn to all the moon's circles,
% two touching each circle at the ends of a diameter, marked ك and ط; the line ك ط through the centre د parts the
% lit half, which faces the sun and is drawn in red, from the other half, which is blotted out. From ي, the centre
% of the earth, two lines touch each circle at م and ل; the line م ل through د parts off the half that the sight
% meets. The light seen in the moon is what the half facing the earth holds of the lit half: at the muhaq م ل fall
% on ك ط; as the moon leaves the sun a small piece of the lit half enters the half facing the earth and grows, half
% of it at the quarter of the month, until at opposition the lit half is the whole half facing the earth. Then (its
% first words between the ornamental brackets) the figure is to have ten circles of the moon, each of the nine
% after the circle of conjunction twenty parts of the half sphere from the next, and shows the growth and waning of
% the moon's light as it is seen in the plane of the circle; in the round body it is twice each piece; this will
% be shown later. The chapter ends with «إِن شاء اللّٰه تعالى وباللّٰه التوفيق».
