% Printed p. 143: chapter 42 continued: the hours of the conjunction turned to the unequal days, to another town and to seasonal hours. For an opposition a hundred and eighty degrees are added to the place of the moon that was worked like the mean place of the sun, so that the true place of the moon is opposite the true place of the sun. The absolute equal hours of the conjunction, which are the mean ones, are turned into hours of the unequal days: the degree of the sun is entered in the tables of the right sphere and the degrees and minutes beside it in the table of the equation of the days, written in the sign of the sun, are divided by 15: the hours and parts of an hour are always added to the hours of the true mean conjunction; the result is the equal hours of the true conjunction turned to the unequal days found by observation, after noon at the city of ar-Raqqa. They are turned to the hours of the town wanted by the difference between its longitude and the longitude of ar-Raqqa, which is 73;15, divided by 15: the hours or parts of an hour are added if the longitude of the city is more than that of ar-Raqqa and taken away if it is less; the result is the true equal hours after noon in that city. Then, within ornamental brackets that open on line 11 and close on line 12: to know the ascendant of the conjunction, all these hours are multiplied by 15 and the product is added to the ascensions of the degree of the sun in the right sphere: with the result the ascendant and the midheaven are known as usual. To turn these equal hours into seasonal hours they are multiplied by fifteen degrees and the product is kept; the hours of the night and of the day are found by the degree of the sun in that clime; then the times of the hours of the day are taken out of the kept product up to six hours: if there are less than six, six seasonal hours, those from sunrise to midday, are added, and the sum is what has passed of the day from sunrise to the time of the conjunction in seasonal hours; if six full hours come out and a remainder is left, it is taken out by the times of the hours of the night up to twelve hours, and a further remainder by the times of the hours of the day a second time: what results is what has passed of the hours of the day from sunrise of the morrow. The ascendant may be found from these hours as usual; and when the hours of the conjunction are more than half the equal hours of the day of that day and are taken from twelve [...]. 22 lines; one note.
