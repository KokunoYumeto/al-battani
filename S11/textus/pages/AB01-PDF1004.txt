% Printed p. 148: chapter 43 continued: the times of the eclipse of the moon, first absolutely and then exactly. The hours of the fall, or of the fall and the stay, are taken from the hours of the opposition, which are the hours of the middle of the eclipse: the remainder is the hours of the beginning of the eclipse; added to the hours of the middle they give the equal hours of the end of the clearing. If the moon has a stay, the corrected diameter of the moon is taken from the half of the two diameters: the remainder is the minutes of the whole stay; they are multiplied by themselves, the latitude of the moon multiplied by itself is taken away, the root of the remainder is divided by the gain of the moon: the result is the hours of the stay; taken from the middle of the eclipse they give the hours of the beginning of the stay, and added to it the hours of the beginning of the clearing. When the moon is not wholly eclipsed the eclipse has three times, and so too when it is wholly eclipsed without a stay; when it has a stay it has five times. These times are in absolute terms and not the strict truth in reckoning, for the latitude of the moon changes between the beginning of the eclipse and its middle and between its middle and the end of the clearing, so that the amounts of the times on either side of the middle change with it; the middle itself does not change. For the exact reckoning: half a sixth of the minutes of the fall and the stay, or of the fall, which were to be divided by the gain of the moon, is added to them; the sum is taken from the corrected motion in latitude for the time of the opposition: this is the motion in latitude for the absolute beginning of the eclipse; the same minutes with half their sixth added to the corrected motion in latitude for the time of the opposition give the motion in latitude for the absolute end of the clearing. The latitude of the moon is found at each of the two times. The latitude for the beginning of the eclipse multiplied by itself is taken from the half of the two diameters multiplied by itself; to the remainder is added the difference between the latitude for the beginning of the eclipse and the latitude for its middle, multiplied by itself; the root of the sum is the minutes of the fall from the beginning of the eclipse to its middle; divided by the gain of the moon and taken from the hours of the opposition it gives the hours of the exact beginning of the eclipse. The same is done with the latitude for the end of the clearing: the root is the minutes of the fall and the stay; divided by the gain of the moon and added to the hours of the opposition it gives the hours of the exact end of the clearing.
