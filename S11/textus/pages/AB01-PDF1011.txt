% Printed p. 141: chapter 42 continued: from the mean to the true conjunction or opposition. Every two and a half minutes of a day make an equal hour, and less than that is parts of an hour: the days and hours found are those of the mean conjunction or opposition, the hours counted from noon at the city of ar-Raqqa. The mean place of the sun and the moon is written down twice, once for the sun and once for the moon, and both are corrected as usual; for the moon only the single equation is needed, since at this distance from the sun no perceptible error comes from the second equation. If the sun and the moon come out in the same minute, that is the time of the true conjunction or opposition, and the motion in latitude is treated as the mean place of the moon was: the single equation is added to it or taken from it as it was added to or taken from the mean moon. If their places differ, a sixth and an eighth of the difference in degrees and minutes are added to the anomaly of the moon when the excess is the sun's and taken from it when it is the moon's: the result is the corrected anomaly, with which the single equation is taken from the second table of the moon's equation, in the two rows of the number; if the anomaly is less than 180 the equation is taken from the mean moon and from the motion in latitude, if more it is added: the result is the true place of the moon. The difference between the sun and the moon is taken again; then their motions in an hour are taken from the tables of the unequal motion of the sun and the moon, in the two rows of the number that go by steps of six degrees, with the corrected anomaly of the moon and the anomaly of the sun, the seconds written under the difference between the sun and the moon being added to the motion of the moon or taken from it as explained at those tables; the sun's motion is taken from the moon's: the remainder is the moon's unequal gain on the sun in an hour; the difference between the sun and the moon divided by this gain gives the hours of the difference, which are kept; if the excess is the sun's, the hours of the difference are added [...]. 22 lines; two notes in one line.
