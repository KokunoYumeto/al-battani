% Printed p. 87: chapter 30 continued: by Ptolemy's reckoning only a half and a third of the moon's diameter would
% have been eclipsed, and the middle about a half and a third of an hour earlier. The times, the magnitudes and the
% places of the two luminaries thus differ, as in many other eclipses of the sun and the moon that the author
% observed. In the two lunar eclipses the sun stood near its greatest distance and the moon at one distance, a degree
% and a half less than its mean distance; the two latitudes differ by 0;3,50 and the two magnitudes by one part in
% eight and three quarters of the moon's diameter, so that the whole diameter comes to about 0;33,30; with Ptolemy's
% ratio of the shadow to the moon, two and three fifths, the radius of the shadow there is about 0;43,30. As the
% hourly motion 36;10 (the greatest) is to 35 1/3 minutes, so is 30;12 (the least) to 29 1/2 minutes, the moon's
% diameter at its greatest distance, where Ptolemy took 0;31,20. The radius of the shadow is then about 0;38,30 at
% the moon's greatest distance and about 46 minutes at its least: at the greatest distance about two minutes and a
% sixth less than Ptolemy's, at the least the same in both reckonings.
