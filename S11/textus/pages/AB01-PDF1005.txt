% Printed p. 147: chapter 43 continued: the diameters of the moon and of the shadow, the digits of the eclipse, the minutes of the fall and of the stay. [The diameter of the moon at its highest] distance with the addition is the corrected diameter of the moon, which is kept; likewise the like part, out of sixty, of the minutes of the third table is taken of the seven minutes and a half by which half the diameter of the shadow varies and added to thirty-eight minutes and a half, the amount of [half] the diameter of the shadow at the farthest distance of the moon: the result is the corrected diameter of the shadow. Then, within ornamental brackets that open on line 3 and close on line 4: to know this by reckoning in another way, the unequal motion of the moon in an hour is multiplied by six less an eighth, and a sixth of the minutes that result is the corrected diameter of the moon; half the [corrected] diameter of the moon multiplied by two and three fifths is the corrected half diameter of the shadow. When the diameter of the moon and the half diameter of the shadow are known in either way, half the corrected diameter of the moon is added to the corrected half diameter of the shadow: the sum is the half of the two diameters, which is kept. If the true latitude of the moon is equal to the half of the two diameters, the moon touches the outermost line of the shadow and nothing of it is eclipsed; if it is less, it is taken from the half of the two diameters and the remainder is looked at: if it is equal to the diameter of the moon, the moon is eclipsed wholly and has no stay; if it is more, the moon is eclipsed wholly and has a stay; if it is less, the moon is not eclipsed wholly: the minutes left when the latitude was taken from the half of the two diameters are multiplied by twelve and divided by the corrected diameter of the moon: the result is the part eclipsed of the diameter of the moon in parts of which the diameter has 12, called the digits of the eclipse, which are kept. If the moon has a stay, the corrected diameter of the moon is taken from those remaining minutes: the rest is the minutes of the stay, which are also multiplied by 12 and divided by the diameter of the moon; the digits that result are added to the twelve digits of the whole diameter of the moon: the sum is the digits of the eclipse from its beginning to its middle. Or the remaining minutes of the half of the two diameters, whether they are less or more than the diameter of the moon, are multiplied by 12 and the product is divided by the diameter of the moon: the result is the digits of the eclipse. Then the half of the two diameters is multiplied by itself, the latitude of the moon multiplied by itself is taken from it, and the root of the remainder is taken: the result is the minutes of the fall and the stay together, if the moon has a stay, and the minutes of the fall if it has none. Whichever of the two it is, it is divided by the gain of the moon: the result is the hours of the fall, or the hours of the fall and the stay [...]. 22 lines; two notes in one line.
