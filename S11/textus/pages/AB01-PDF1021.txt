% Printed p. 131: chapter 41 continued: the rule for the sighting of the new moon: the moon's apparent distance from the equator and the degree that culminates with it are found, and from them half the arc of the moon's day, as in the chapters on a star's distance from the equator and on half the arc of its day; this is added to the times of the right ascension of the degree that culminates with the moon: the result is the times of the rising of the degree opposite the one that sets with the moon in that clime; the times of the rising that stand against the degree opposite the sun's degree are subtracted: the remainder is the distance between the sun and the moon in degrees of setting; then the distance between the true places of the sun and the moon is squared, the square of the moon's latitude is added and the root is taken: this is about the distance of the moon from the sun; its excess over 12;11 or its defect from it is taken as a share of the light in the moon needed for the sighting, and this part increases or lessens the arc of vision; then the corrected anomaly of the moon is entered in the table of the equation and the minutes of the third table are taken: if they are thirty, the moon is at its mean distance, and the part is added to 11;45, the arc of vision, or subtracted from it; if they are more or less than thirty, half a sixth of the part is taken in the share of the difference, as the moon's diameter varies by about half a sixth of its mean. 22 lines, four notes in one line.
