% Printed p. 144: chapter 42 continued: the conjunction before or after midnight, and why the anomaly of the moon is corrected by a sixth and an eighth of the difference. If the hours are less than twelve, what remains is by how much the conjunction comes before midnight in equal hours; if they are more than 12, up to half the equal hours of that night, twelve hours are cast out and the rest is by how much the conjunction comes after midnight; if they are more than twelve with half the hours of the night added, they are taken from 24 hours and the remainder is by how much the conjunction comes before noon of the morrow. By what has been described the time of the conjunction or opposition is known, with the places of the sun and the moon, the anomaly of the moon and the motion in latitude at those times. What forces the correction of the anomaly of the moon by a sixth and an eighth of the difference is that the time of the mean conjunction is not at all times the time of the true conjunction: if what falls in the anomaly from the doubled distance between the sun and the moon were neglected, a difference of at most about a quarter of an hour could fall in the time of the conjunction or of the opposition; for when the equation of the sun is two degrees and that of the moon three degrees, one of them added to the mean motion and the other taken from it, they make five degrees, and their double, ten degrees, is the doubled distance; the equation of the anomaly at such a distance, by addition or by subtraction, is about a degree and a half, about a sixth and an eighth of the difference; and where the corrected equation of the moon on the epicycle must be three degrees, the share of the degree and a half is about an eighth of a degree, which comes by the gain of the moon to about a quarter of an hour. Then, within ornamental brackets: as for Ptolemy, he made the reckoning by the greatest of the two equations, where the equation of the moon is five degrees and that of the sun two degrees and twenty-three minutes by the reckoning on which he worked: the difference between the sun and the moon is seven degrees 23 minutes and its double 14 degrees 46 minutes about; on this reckoning no more than an eighth of an hour need come of it, as was said; but when the equation of the moon is five degrees, the share of one degree or of two degrees added to the anomaly of the moon or taken from it is only a small amount of no account, and at three degrees it differs more than at five; and so the matter is as we have said.
