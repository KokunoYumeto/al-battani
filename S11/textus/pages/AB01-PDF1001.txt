% Printed p. 151: chapter 43 continued: the sides of the darkening and of the clearing. To know the azimuth of the side of the moon's circle from which the darkening begins and of the side from which it clears, on the circle of the horizon, and the figure of the eclipse: the ascendant is set up for each of the times of the eclipse and its azimuth found on the circle of the horizon by the drawing given at the head of the book. The latitude of the moon is taken for the beginning of the eclipse and for the end of the clearing, if the moon is not wholly eclipsed, and also for the beginning of the clearing, if it is wholly eclipsed and has a stay. Each latitude is multiplied by the half diameter; the products for the beginning of the stay and of the clearing are divided by the minutes of the whole stay, those for the beginning of the eclipse and the end of the clearing by the half of the two diameters; the arcs of the results in the table of the halved chords are the inclination of the darkening at each of those times. If the true centre of the moon is on the ecliptic at one of the times, the first darkening and the first clearing are towards the azimuth of the ascendant, the beginning of the stay and the end of the clearing towards the azimuth of the setting degree. If the moon has a latitude, the inclination is laid off, for the beginning of the eclipse, from the azimuth of the ascendant on the circle of the horizon towards the side opposite to the moon's latitude, and for the end of the clearing from the azimuth of the setting point, likewise towards the opposite side; for the beginning of the clearing and the beginning of the stay from the azimuths of the rising and of the setting degree towards the side of the moon's latitude. Where the count ends on the circle of the horizon, towards the azimuth of that degree is the inclination of the darkening and of the clearing on the circle of the moon. If the moon is not wholly eclipsed, the inclination of the darkening at the middle of the eclipse is always at a right angle to the ecliptic, where the arc through the two poles of the ecliptic and the place of the moon meets the circle of the horizon; it is found by taking the angle of the longitude known for the time.
