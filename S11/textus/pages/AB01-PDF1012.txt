% Printed p. 140: chapter 42 continued: the reckoning of the conjunctions and oppositions by the era of the Copts. The quarter of the years gives the days of the quarters; a fraction in it is not counted, and if there is none the year is a leap year: in a leap year one day is taken from the days of the quarters until Shubat has ended and has come out as twenty-nine days, and when Shubat has ended that day is added again. To the days of the quarters are always added the three days by which the Copts are ahead of the Greeks in Tut, and to the sum the days from the first of Aylul to the end of the Rumi month before the month of the reckoning. If the days are more than 365, 365 is cast out and a year is added to the years of Dhu l-Qarnayn (366 in a leap year after Shubat); what remains after the casting out of the year, or the days themselves if they are less than a year, are the days of the Copts, and are set aside. The years of Dhu l-Qarnayn, with the year added from the days if there is one, are then entered in the table of the collected Egyptian years, which go by steps of twenty-five, in the row of the collected years of the tables of conjunction or of opposition; what stands beside the same or the nearest smaller number is taken from the four tables, and the remaining years are sought in the row of the single years. The days of the Copts are cast out by thirties; the complete months are entered in the row of the number of the tables of the Coptic months, and what stands beside them in the table of the days is summed with the days from the two tables of the collected and the single years. If the sum is equal to the days of the Copts, or more by less than a lunar month, those days and what stands under them in the three other tables are written down; if it is more by more than a lunar month, one month is taken from the Coptic months entered. The whole is summed as before, and the days of the Copts are taken from the days: what remains of days and minutes is the days of the conjunction or opposition and its hours elapsed of the month; what comes from the three tables is the mean place of the sun and the moon, the anomaly of the moon and the motion in latitude. When this is known by either era, the minutes of the days are reckoned [...]. 22 lines; two notes in one line.
