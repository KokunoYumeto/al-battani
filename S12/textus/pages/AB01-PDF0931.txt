% Printed p. 221: the chapter on the eras of the Arabs and of the Romans ended, and the beginning of a chapter without a number on the forward course, the stations and the retrogradations of the planets. The count goes on from the years of Dhu l-Qarnayn until Aylul is reached; when one day of Aylul has entered, another year is added to the years of Dhu l-Qarnayn. If the year is a leap year and the count reaches Shubat, twenty-nine days are taken for it in that year; the leap year is known from the mark drawn in the table of the signs of the Roman months. In the same way the day of the Arab month of the year of the Hijra is learnt from the Roman era: look in this table in the line of the years of Dhu l-Qarnayn and take what stands opposite the year you are in of the days gone of the Roman month; on that day of the month falls the first of Muharram of the year you are in of the years of the Hijra; keep it. Then take what is left of that Roman month, add the months and days after that month up to the day you want, taking for every month the number of its days; to the sum add one day, always; then cast out what has gathered from Muharram, for every month the number of its days: the day at which the count ends is the day of the Arab month you are in, of the year of the Hijra which you found, until you reach Muharram; when you have reached it and one day of it has entered, add another year to the years of the Hijra you have. By this era you will work, if God wills. Title: «Chapter on knowing the forward course of the planets, their stations, their retrogradations and their traversing of the spheres of their epicycles.» He said: of the planets, Saturn goes forward from the top of the sphere of its epicycle one hundred and eighteen days and a half; then it stands two days and a half and a quarter and an eighth; then it goes back in its course one hundred and thirty-five days and a third and a quarter; then it stands a second time like its first station; then it goes forward like its first forward course until it comes back to the top of the sphere of its epicycle. All the days in which it traverses the sphere of the epicycle are three hundred and seventy-eight days and two hours, nearly. When its corrected anomaly is from 0 to 112;45 it is direct; when it passes that, up to the completion of 115;29, it is stationary; when it passes that, up to the completion of 244;31, it is retrograde; when it passes that, up to the completion of 247;15, it is stationary, and up to the completion of the revolution direct, if God wills
