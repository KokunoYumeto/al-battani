% Printed p. 209: the heading and the title of chapter 57, on the close of the book and the making of the instruments for observing named in it, the bayda (the «egg»), the libna (the «brick») and the alidade; and its beginning. What the book has told of the causes of things and of the roots of reckoning by the way of geometrical proof stays as it is and no doubt falls on it at any time; but what rests on measurings, observations, trials and testings can be added to and taken from. The part of it that comes of falling short of the very truth of the thing grows small when it is divided over a long time, even if it could be felt, and great when it is divided over a short time, even if it was small. The error that comes of the instrument, in its dividing, its setting up and its rectifying: tried with that same instrument in its first state, the error is one at the two times; an error of the dividing alone may have its setting up and rectifying mended while the error of the dividing stays, and shows when another instrument is used. The instrument may also change with time, widening, shrinking or warping, so that the error grows or lessens; a sound measuring afterwards gathers, between the two times, as much as that first error, if it went by one rule. Such things come out true only with two instruments made sure in all their parts, or with one sound instrument that has not changed. What comes of the shortcoming of man's nature in reaching the truth of things in deed as he reaches them in thought is small and not felt, with care and heed, above all over long spans; nature, a willing mind, true sight, the working of thought and patience help in this, though the things be hard to reach; and want of patience, love of glory and of favour with kings hinder much of it.
