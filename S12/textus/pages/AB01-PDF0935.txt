Then the pillar ا ب ج د, in which the ruler ز ط ح is fixed, is turned until its line ب ج stands on the line of midday and the plumb line let down from the point ز falls on the point ط, so that the ruler stands at right angles and its face stands on the line of midday, weighed on it, facing the east; so too the two sights mounted on the second ruler, and the parts on the ruler ط م, which are drawn along the half of the ruler in which the notch falls. When the sun passes the line of midday, the ruler with the two sights is moved to north and south until the upper sight shades the whole lower sight and the ray of the sun passes from the hole of the upper sight into the hole of the lower one; with it the ruler ط م is drawn and moved to north and south until its line ط م is brought to the point ل of the ruler ز ل, by the two notches; one notes on which number of the divisions of the ruler ط م the point ل has fallen, enters with it the table of the halved chords and finds its arc: what the arc comes to is doubled, and that is the distance of the sun from the zenith, if the numbers of the ruler begin at the point ط. Likewise, had the line ط ك been divided into sixty parts, by the measure of the half diameter, and the line ك م up to eighty-five parts in all, and the number on which the point ل falls been taken, halved, its arc found and the arc doubled, the meaning would be one.
