% Printed p. 158: chapter 44 continued: the hours of the middle of the eclipse of the sun. [The hours of the second difference are taken from the hours of the true conjunction when the distance of the moon] from this second ascendant is less than ninety, and the minutes of the second difference from the place of the moon and from its anomaly; if the distance is more than 90, the hours are added to the hours of the true conjunction and the minutes to the place of the moon and to its anomaly. «The place of the moon and its anomaly» here means its place at the time of the true conjunction: what was set down from the first difference is struck out. With the hours of this second conjunction the ascendant and the midheaven are found as usual and, with the place of the moon and its anomaly, the parallax in longitude a third time on the same side. If this third difference is the same as the second, the hours obtained from the hours of the conjunction corrected by the hours of the second difference are the hours of the middle of the eclipse, because the parallax in longitude then equals the minutes between the sun and the moon at that time, without excess or defect. If the third difference is more than the second, the parallax at that time is more than the minutes between the sun and the moon by the excess of the third over the second; if it is less, the parallax is less than those minutes by what the third falls short of the second. So the time must be singled out at which the distance between the sun and the moon equals the parallax of the moon in longitude, to be the middle of the eclipse as it is seen. The way is this: if the difference is more than the second difference, something is taken from the hours obtained that does not pass a full hour: if with those hours, by which the third difference was found, there is a fraction of more than a sixth of an hour, a sixth of an hour is taken from them; if it is less than a sixth of an hour, an eighth or a tenth of an hour, as far as is possible without breaking one of the hours. This subtraction is used when the distance of the moon from the ascendant at that time is less than ninety; when it is more than ninety, addition is used in its place, on the condition that what is added to those hours does not fill up a whole hour: when the fraction is less than a half and a third, a sixth of an hour is added to it, and when [the next page continues].
