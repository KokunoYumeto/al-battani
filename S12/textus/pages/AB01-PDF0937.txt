% Printed p. 215: chapter 57 continued, with the figure of the «brick», the mural quadrant. He said: take a «brick» of brass, or of stone or wood, square, two cubits a side, and the larger the better: it is the «brick» ا ب ج د. The point ا is made a centre and about it, with the measure of ا ب and ا ج, the arc ب ج is drawn and divided into ninety parts by lines that pass through the centre, with the minutes between the parts as far as may be; the face of the «brick» is smooth and truly level. Two equal pegs of brass, turned round and pointed, are fixed at the centre ا and at the centre ج; the line of midday ه ز has been found before, and by the plumb line the «brick» is set upright along it, its face with the marks to the east and its side ا ب to the south. The shadow of the peg at ا is watched at midday on the parts of the quarter; a piece of brass ط, curved to the arc, with a line in its middle, is moved under the shadow to mark its place. Thus the greatest distance of the sun from the zenith in summer and winter is found: the point ح is the summer end and ك the winter end, the arc ك ح is the arc between the two solstices and its middle is the mark ل, on which the shadow falls when the sun passes the equinox
