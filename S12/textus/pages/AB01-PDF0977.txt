[Then there is added to the centre corrected] by the third table, for Venus 90 and for Mercury 270; what is left after a revolution has been cast off, if it is more than a revolution, is entered in those tables, and what is opposite it is taken in the ninth table, headed «the shares of the latitude for all of them». The amount of the minutes that come out, of sixty, is found, and so much is taken of the inclination that was set down: the result is the first latitude, which is kept. If the number of the centre corrected by the addition for the planet, with which the minutes of the ninth table were found, and the corrected anomaly of the planet both fall in one half, whichever of the two halves it is, the upper or the lower, that latitude is southern; if they differ, one being in one half and the other in the other half, that latitude is northern: it is noted with its side. Then the centre corrected by the third table is taken, for Venus as it is and for Mercury with an addition of 180; what comes of the centre after a revolution has been cast off, if it exceeds one, is entered in those tables as well, and what is opposite it of the minutes of the shares written in the ninth table is taken and set down in two places. The amount of one of the two places, of sixty, is found, and so much is taken of the slant: the result is the second latitude, which is set down. If the number with which these minutes were found fell in the upper half and the corrected anomaly is less than 180, the second latitude is northern, and if the anomaly is more than 180, the latitude is southern; if it fell in the lower half and the anomaly is less than 180, the latitude is southern, and if it is more than 180, it is northern. Then of those minutes that were set down in the other place there is taken, if the reckoning is for Venus, a sixth, which is always northern, and if the reckoning is for Mercury, a half and a quarter, which is always southern. Those of these three latitudes that are on one side are added together; if one differs, the lesser is cast off from the greater and the side of the remainder is noted: that is the resulting latitude of the planet on the side on which it comes out. «Know» that the whole latitude of Saturn in the north, as Ptolemy found it, is 3;2 and in the south 3;5; the whole latitude of Jupiter in the north 2;4 and in the south 2;8; the whole latitude of Mars in the north 4;21 and in the south 7;7; the whole latitude of Venus in the north and in the south alike 8;56; and the whole latitude of Mercury in the north and in the south alike 4;18 as well. «If you want to know» whether the planet is rising or sinking on the side on which it is, its latitude is reckoned after ten days [as the next page continues].
