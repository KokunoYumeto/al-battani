% Printed p. 157: chapter 44 begins: «On knowing the eclipse of the sun, its magnitudes and its times in every town, and the sides of its darkening and of its clearing, by reckoning and by the table». To reckon an eclipse of the sun the conjunctions of the sun and the moon are examined: if the mean motion in latitude falls between the eclipse limits written for the sun at the head of the page of the months of conjunction and opposition, the sun can be eclipsed; if it is more or less, it cannot be eclipsed in any of the climates. If it can, the time of the conjunction is examined, whether it falls by day or near sunrise or sunset, to know whether the eclipse, if there is one, can be seen wholly or in part. Then the true equal hours of the conjunction after noon in the town are found and the ascendant and the midheaven of the ecliptic set up for that time; the parallax of the moon in longitude alone is found by the reckoning of the angles and of the arcs of the distance from the zenith on the circle of altitude, as explained at the head of the book, from the parallax in the circle of altitude less the parallax of the sun made by that drawing, and divided by the unequal motion of the moon in the hour: the hours of the first difference. If the distance of the degree of the conjunction from the ascendant is less than ninety, the moon is in the eastern quadrant of the sphere: the hours of the first difference are taken from the hours of the conjunction, and the minutes of the difference from the place of the moon and from its anomaly for the time of the conjunction. If it is more than ninety, the moon is in the western quadrant: they are added. With the hours of the conjunction so increased or diminished the ascendant is found a second time and the parallax of the moon in longitude a second time, on the same side, with the second place and anomaly of the moon; the result of this second difference is divided by the gain of the moon over the sun in that hour: the hours of the second difference, which are taken from the hours of the true conjunction when the distance of the moon [is as the next page says].
