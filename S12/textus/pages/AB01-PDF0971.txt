[«We have made clear»] the knowledge of the distances between the stars in drawing them on the sphere, and we have shown the knowledge of the degrees of the ecliptic that rise, set and culminate with them, in what went before of this book. As for the visibility of the stars that are called fixed and their disappearance on account of the sun: when the amount is known at which each kind of the kinds of their six magnitudes appears and is hidden, the reckoning for that is as we described for the visibility. It is said that the amount of the arc at which those of the stars appear and are hidden that are of the first magnitude — like the southern and the northern Shiʿrā (Sirius and Procyon), the Heart of the Lion, the two Simāks, the End of the River and what is like them in magnitude — is fifteen degrees, I mean the arc at which they appear and are hidden, and these degrees are of the times of the equator; for the rest of their kinds, which are below this magnitude, the arc grows above fifteen degrees in the order of the magnitude, until it comes to the smallest magnitude of them, which is seen and hidden at the amount of a sign, approximately. Chapter 50 («the chapter that completes fifty»): «On knowing the distances of the stars, their diameters, the magnitude of their bodies and the width of their spheres, mentioned loosely, in the way in which the ancients and the first ones mentioned it». The distance of the sun and of the moon, their diameters and the magnitude of their bodies have been shown, according to what is said in the book Almagest and to what came to us by the measuring of the eclipses; so let us begin to mention what is other than that of the spheres, up to the sphere of Saturn and the sphere of the fixed stars, in the way in which the later ones among the excellent wise men before Ptolemy took the trouble to do it. Their saying about it was that it had become plain that the farthest distance of the moon from the earth, in the measure in which the half diameter of the earth is one part, is 64;10, and that is the nearest nearness of Mercury; that the two spheres of Mercury and of Venus are between the farthest distance of the moon and the nearest nearness of the sun; and that the end of the air and of the fire is the nearest nearness of the moon to the earth, which is when it is on the two sides of the full moon, I mean at the mean quadrature of the sun; and it has become plain that its distance from the earth then is [the next page continues]. The notes: for «their bodies» the codex has «their parts»; for «loosely» the codex has two separate words; for «before» Plato has, better, «post».
