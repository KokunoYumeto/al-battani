% Printed p. 187: the beginning of chapter 51: «On knowing the motion of the fixed stars, which move in their sphere or whose sphere moves with them, by testing, and knowing the places of those of them that are needed, from the tables». He said: the states of the fixed stars in their rising, their setting, their culmination and their stay above the earth and under it, and the state of those of them that are near to the pole and far from it in every land, we have set out earlier in this book. The course of the fixed stars is about the two poles of the ecliptic; they have not left it since a motion was known for them; likewise their latitude is one latitude that does not change, and they are in this state as long as no change is known in their motion; likewise the distances between them are fixed in one state since they were observed, and for that they were named the fixed stars, by which they meant fixed in their distances; for the motion of all of them together is one motion, as though they were in one sphere, whether they move together in it or it moves them by its motion and carries them from the direction of one place of the sphere to the direction of another, likewise from the west to the east, as the other motions of the running stars, which are the seven planets. We found their places in longitude and latitude in the book of Ptolemy for the first year of the reign of Antoninus, which is the year eight hundred and eighty-six of the reign of Bukhtanassar the first. One of the observations on which Ptolemy worked was the observation that Menelaus recorded, of the year eight hundred and forty-five of the reign of the said Bukhtanassar: he recorded that the northern star of those between the eyes of the Scorpion was in that year, when he tested it by the moon with the instrument of the rings, at five degrees and fifty-five minutes of Scorpio; and by what he set down in his book the Heart of the Lion must in this said year be at two degrees and a sixth of a degree of Leo, and the southern Sirius at seventeen degrees of Gemini. We ourselves observed these stars that we have named, and others of the fixed stars, many times in successive years; one of our observations on which we rely was in the year one thousand one hundred and ninety-one of the years of Dhu l-Qarnayn; we observed by the moon and by the passage of the stars through the middle of the sky, and so knew their distances [the next page continues]. The notes give the spellings of the names of Antoninus and Menelaus in the codex.
