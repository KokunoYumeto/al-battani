(So that the shadow of the gnomon falls) at the amount of the azimuth of that altitude on the circle drawn; if the shadow does not reach the circumference of the circle, you fasten a thin thread at the foot of the gnomon and stretch it at the amount of the azimuth from the limit of the east or of the west, on the side on which it is at the time of the observation; then you turn the slab until the middle of the shadow of the gnomon falls on that thread; then the slab is set right, and the line of the sixth hour lies level with the line of midday, in its direction, if God will. If you wish to know the azimuth of Mecca, which is the azimuth of the qibla for prayer, by this chapter, and to draw on it a line from the centre of the circle, so that that line is the azimuth of the qibla in that town: find the latitude of the town in which you are and the latitude of Mecca, and find on which side Mecca, the guarded, lies from that town, in the north of it or in the south; find the longitude of Mecca and the longitude of the city, and take the lesser of them from the greater, so that you know the amount between them in longitude, and where the place of Mecca is from that city, toward the east or toward the west: for when the longitude of Mecca is greater than the longitude of the city, as set down in the tables of the latitudes of the cities and their longitudes, Mecca is to the east of the city, and when it is less, Mecca is to the west of the city. Then lay the end of the ruler at the number of the latitude between them, beginning from the line of the east toward the side on which Mecca is in latitude, and likewise from the line of the west toward that side, on the circumference of the circle, so that the edge of the ruler passes at the like of the latitude between them; and draw along the edge of the ruler a line that joins the eastern mark and the western. Take also the difference between them in longitude and count the like of it on the circumference of the circle from the line of midday toward the side on which Mecca is in longitude, on the southern part of the circumference of the circle, and count the like of it also on its circumference toward the north; lay the edge of the ruler on the two marks and draw along its edge a straight line. Where these two lines cut each other is the place of Mecca in its direction from that town. Lay the edge of the ruler on the centre of the circle and on the place of the cutting, and draw on it a straight line that you carry through on the slab toward the southern circumference of the circle: that line is the azimuth of the qibla in that town. If you wish to know the amount of the azimuth of the qibla by reckoning, take the chord of what lies between the two towns in longitude and the chord of what lies between them in latitude, multiply each of them by itself, add them and take the root of the sum: the result is the hypotenuse of the triangle that subtends the right angle, which is the distance between the centre of the circle and the place of the cutting of the two lines of longitude and of latitude on the circumference of the circle; keep it. Then go back to the chord of what lies between the two towns in latitude, multiply it by the half diameter and divide it by the hypotenuse of the triangle; take the arc of the result; the arc that comes out is the azimuth of Mecca; count the like of it on the circumference of the circle from the point of the azimuth of the east or of the west, according to the place of Mecca from (the sentence runs on).
