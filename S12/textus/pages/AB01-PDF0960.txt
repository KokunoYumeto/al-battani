[This motion, in what Ptolemy found by his observations and] according to what was worked on by those before him also, is one degree in every hundred years; and there was not, between the observations of Ptolemy and the observations with which he compared them, a length of time that would make a plain change show in a motion such as this: for between the observation that he himself made and the observation with which he compared it there are only about two hundred years; and when the time between us and him grew long, the excess in this motion became plain, so that one degree was found in every sixty-six solar years; and it is from the difference of these two rates that the excess came about, together with the whole. Chapter 53: on the knowledge of the times of the revolutions of the years and of their ascendants, and of the places of the stars at the return of the sun to the degree in which it was at the root. He said: when you wish to know the revolution of any year you like, of the years of nativities and of other things for which a fixed beginning is taken, which is the time at which the sun returns to the degree in which it was at the time of the beginning, then know the year in which the beginning was, in the years of Dhu l-Qarnayn, and also the year in which you wish to know the revolution; then subtract the smaller from the greater: what remains is what has passed for the nativity or the beginning that you wished, in complete years, up to the like of the day of the Roman month on which the nativity or the beginning was. Multiply these complete years by eighty-six degrees and thirty-six minutes, which is the excess of the length of the year over the complete days; from what results cast out the circles, and what remains under a circle divide by 15: what comes out is equal hours. Add them to the hours of the computed position at the root: if they come to less than twenty-four, take them as they are, with the day passed of the month; and if they exceed 24, cast 24 from them and add one day to the days passed of the month. What the days and the hours come to after that, keep. If the year is a leap year and Shubāṭ has passed, subtract one day from the days passed of the month: what results is the day of the computed position; and if the year is not a leap year, you leave the days as they are; then what [the next page continues].
