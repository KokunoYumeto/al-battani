% Printed p. 167: chapter 44 continued: the eclipse of the sun by the tables approximately: the hours of the middle of the eclipse. [The parallax of the degree of the moon in longitude is found, corrected by the fourth table] of the tables of the equation, on that side: the result is the second difference; divided by the gain of the moon it gives the hours of the second difference. If the distance of the conjunction, that is of the degree of the moon, from the ascendant is less than ninety, they are taken from the hours of the true conjunction; if it is more than ninety, they are added to the hours of the true conjunction. Its distance from the meridian is taken a third time as well, and with it the corrected parallax of the degree of the moon in longitude is found a third time. If it is the same as the second difference, the hours that resulted from the hours of the true conjunction, after the hours of the second difference were added to them or taken from them, are the hours of the middle of the eclipse. If the third difference exceeds the second, the excess is noted; then a sixth of an hour is added to the hours of the distance of the conjunction from the meridian that resulted by the second difference, and with that the corrected parallax of the degree of the moon in longitude is found on that side; its excess over the second difference is taken, multiplied by six and taken from the gain of the moon for an hour; the remainder is the corrected motion. The excess of the third difference over the second, which was to be kept, is divided by it: the result is a part of an hour, which is added to the hours of the second difference; the result is the exact hours. If the third difference is less than the second, what it falls short is multiplied by six and added to the gain of the moon: the result is the corrected motion; the defect of the third difference from the second is divided by it: the result is a part of an hour, which is taken from the hours of the second difference; the hours of the second difference after that are the exact hours. When the exact hours of the difference are known, on whichever of the two sides they fall, they are multiplied by the unequal motions of the moon and of the sun in that hour, and what falls to each of them is kept. If the distance of the moon from the ascendant at that time is less than ninety, the exact hours of the second difference are taken from the hours of the true conjunction, and the motion of the sun in that amount from the place of the conjunction; likewise the motion of the moon is taken from the place of the conjunction, from the anomaly of the moon and from the motion in latitude, and from the motion in latitude the motion of the node in the amount of the hours of the second difference is taken besides. If the distance of the moon from the ascendant is more than ninety, addition is used in all this in place of subtraction. The hours of the true conjunction after the addition or the subtraction are the hours of the middle of the eclipse, and likewise the place of the sun and of the moon, the motion in latitude and the anomaly of the moon corrected for the middle of the eclipse. Then the latitude of the moon and its side are found by the motion in latitude at the middle of the eclipse, and kept; then the distance of the hours of the middle of the eclipse from the meridian is taken, and with it the parallax of the moon in latitude is found in that way, after [the next page continues].
