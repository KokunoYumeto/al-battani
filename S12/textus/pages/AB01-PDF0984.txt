% Printed p. 168: chapter 44 continued: the eclipse of the sun by the tables approximately: the digits, the minutes of the fall and the times. [The parallax of the moon in latitude is found, after] it has been corrected by the fourth table of the tables of the equation, and the side of the parallax is known. If the latitude of the moon and its parallax in latitude are on one side they are added together; if they differ, the less is taken from the greater and what remains is noted with its side; the result after the addition or the subtraction is the latitude of the moon as it is seen for the middle of the eclipse. Its like is sought in the table of the latitude of the moon drawn in the two tables of the eclipse of the sun. If it is found in the second table, which is for the least distance, and not in the greater table, what stands under it in the lesser table of the digits and of the minutes of the fall is taken; then the anomaly of the moon is entered in the tables of the equation, the minutes of the shares drawn in the third table under it are taken and their amount of sixty is found; of those digits and of the minutes of the fall the like part is taken: the result is the amount of the diameter of the sun that is eclipsed, in digits, and the minutes of the fall are the amount of the eclipse in its duration from its beginning to its middle. If the latitude of the moon falls in both tables, what stands under it in the greater, first table and in the lesser, second one of the digits and of the minutes of the fall is taken; then the excess between the two tables in the digits and in the minutes of the fall is found, and of each of them a part is taken in the measure of the minutes of the third table that fall opposite the anomaly of the moon in the tables of the equation, of sixty; what results of the digits is added to the digits that came out of the table of the greatest distance, the first, and likewise what results for the fall is added to the minutes of the fall of the greater table; what each of them comes to is the amount of the digits of the eclipse and of the minutes of the fall. It is known that when the latitude of the moon is not found in either of the two tables, the sun is not eclipsed. Then the minutes of the fall are divided by the gain of the moon: the hours of the fall; taken from the hours of the middle of the eclipse they leave the hours of the absolute beginning, and added to the hours of the middle of the eclipse they give the hours of the absolute clearing. «If you wish to follow in this what I have drawn» at the beginning of the chapter, so that the time of the beginning or the time of the visible clearing is corrected and made plain, it is done in that way; and if it is to be known approximately, the hours of the distance of each of these three times from the meridian are entered in the tables of the parallax drawn for the climates, and what stands under each of them in the table of the parallax in longitude alone is taken, in the sign of the moon and in the sign that follows it on that side, until the parallax of the degree of the moon in longitude is known, without correcting it [as the next page continues]. The notes: «and the fifth» is to be added, see p. 166, note 2; the codex has «in the first chapter», Plato «in huius primordio capituli»; a reference to 123,1.
