[If the parallax is less] than it, what it falls short of it is multiplied by six and added to the gain of the moon; the result is the corrected motion. The excess between the minutes that are between the sun and the moon at the time of the absolute beginning and the minutes of its fall is divided by this corrected motion: the result is a part of an hour, which is taken from the hours of the absolute beginning. If the minutes between the sun and the moon are more than the minutes of the fall, the moon has not reached the place at which it can cover anything of the sun: a sixth of an hour is added to the hours of the absolute beginning and the parallax of the moon in longitude is found in the same way; if this parallax is more than the parallax of the absolute beginning, the excess is multiplied by six and added to the gain of the moon; if it is less, what it falls short is multiplied by six and the product taken from the gain of the moon; the gain of the moon after that is the corrected motion; the excess between the minutes between the sun and the moon and the minutes of the fall is divided by this corrected motion and the resulting part of an hour is added to the hours of the absolute beginning. The hours after the addition or the subtraction are the hours of the beginning as it is seen. As for the time of the absolute clearing: if the minutes between the sun and the moon in it are more than the minutes of its fall, the moon has passed the place at which it covers the sun; a sixth of an hour is taken from the hours of the absolute clearing and the parallax of the moon in longitude is found in the same way; if this parallax is more than the parallax of the time of the absolute clearing, the excess is multiplied by six and added to the gain of the moon; if it is less, what it falls short is multiplied by six and taken from the gain of the moon; the result is the corrected motion; the excess between the minutes between the sun and the moon and the minutes of the fall of the absolute clearing is divided by this corrected motion, and the resulting part of an hour is taken from the hours of the absolute clearing. If the minutes between the sun and the moon in it are less than the minutes of the fall, the moon has not reached the place at which it leaves the sun and it cannot cover it there; a sixth of an hour is then added to the hours of the absolute clearing and the parallax of the moon in longitude is found with it in the way set out before; if this parallax is more than the parallax of the absolute clearing, the excess is multiplied by six and taken from the gain of the moon; if it is less, what it falls short is multiplied by six and added to the gain of the moon; the result after the addition or the subtraction is the corrected motion; the excess between the minutes between the sun and the moon and the minutes of the fall is divided by this corrected motion, and the resulting part of an hour is added to the hours of the absolute clearing; the hours after [the addition or the subtraction, as the next page continues].
