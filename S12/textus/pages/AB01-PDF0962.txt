[The place of the star and its latitude] is what is set down in the table, on the side set down, and likewise its magnitude among the six magnitudes. Likewise you know the degrees with which they rise, set and culminate from the later tables, by taking what is in each of the three tables and casting it off from the beginning of Aries. After this year for which we set down these tables with these states, you add to what is in the tables according to the increase that is due, since it changes much and its reckoning needs to be worked out at every time; we have set it down for our time only, so that it may be known in it approximately; and the way by which these sides are known is explained in what has gone before in this book. From these tables are known for our time the nine states named that the stars have with the sun, above all the great ones among them, which are those set down in the tables. Chapter 52: «On knowing what the masters of the talismans said in their saying that the sphere has a motion of displacement, forward and backward, and what appears of the unsoundness of their saying». He said: Ptolemy recorded in his book that the masters of the talismans claimed that the sphere has a slow motion of displacement, one degree in every eighty years, and they said that this motion goes as far as eight degrees, advancing and then retreating. The meaning of their saying is that the ecliptic moves from the west to the east, with the motion of the sphere of the fixed stars likewise in this direction, eight degrees, and then moves from the east to the west eight degrees likewise, against the first motion; and with that it must move, with the motion of the fixed stars, the first motion, which is from the west to the east; and that is not so and cannot be, unless something else moves it or the fixed stars are what moves upon it; for one body cannot move two motions in two different directions at once. They recorded that the end of the advance was a hundred and twenty-eight Egyptian years before the reign of Augustus, which is the year one hundred and sixty-six of Alexander the Macedonian, and that what is after that of the years must be taken and one degree reckoned for every eighty years of it; what comes of that [is taken away] until it ends at eight degrees, and what is left is added to the motions [the next page continues].
