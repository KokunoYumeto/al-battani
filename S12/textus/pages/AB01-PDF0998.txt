% Printed p. 154: chapter 43 continued, with the figure of the eclipse of the moon. [If] the moon has a stay, the parts of the fourth table are laid off from the azimuth of the setting point at the beginning of the stay, and from the azimuth of the rising degree at the beginning of the clearing, towards the side of the moon's latitude: where the count ends on the circle of the horizon is the azimuth of the darkening and of the clearing that happens in the circle of the moon. This is the figure of the eclipse, with its side and the sides of the darkening and of the clearing in it. The construction begins: a straight line is drawn and divided into as many equal parts as one likes, provided they are as many as the half of the two diameters or more. With as much of this line as the half of the two diameters a circle is drawn, the circle of the half of the two diameters, on which the centre of the moon falls at the time of the beginning and of the end of the clearing; with as much as the half diameter of the shadow a circle is drawn about the same centre, inside the first: the circle of the shadow. The two circles are quartered, and at the ends of the lines the sides are written: east, west, north and south. From the divided line as much as the moon's latitude for the middle of the eclipse is taken with the compasses; one of its points is set on the centre of the two circles and the other turned towards the side of the moon's latitude: where it falls on the line of the north or of the south a mark is made on the line, the centre of the moon for the middle of the eclipse. With the moon's latitude for the beginning of the eclipse a second mark is made at its place on the line, on the side of the moon's latitude, and with the latitude for the end of the clearing another mark on the side of the latitude. The figure: three circles about one centre with their upright and level diameters, three small circles for the moon and five more lines, with the letters of the construction and the words south (at the top), north (at the foot), east (at the left) and west (at the right). The note gives a reading of the codex.
