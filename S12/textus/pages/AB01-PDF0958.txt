% Printed p. 194: the beginning of chapter 54, on making exact the amounts of the aspects that depend on the latitudes of the stars. He said: since the circle of the signs is divided into twelve signs, and nothing is found that measures twelve, of what has a whole part in it, I mean a standing ratio, except six twice, four three times, three four times and two six times, these amounts were used and no others; they are four figures besides conjunction. One of them is opposition, which is of six signs and takes in two right angles and one hundred and eighty degrees; its cause is a cause plain of itself in strength. After it comes the figure of quartile, which is half of opposition and takes in three signs, a right angle and ninety degrees; then trine, which takes in four signs, a right angle and a third of an angle, and one hundred and twenty degrees; then sextile, which is half of trine and takes in two signs, two thirds of a right angle and sixty degrees. The cause of conjunction is not of the same kind. These parts of the sphere of the signs are the ones that share with one another in the figures, and no others; the signs that stand so to one another are called the connected signs, and the rest, which are not connected with one another and do not share in the figures, are those whose distance from one another is one sign, five signs and seven signs. Since the rays of the stars, when they share in the four figures, only gather at the centre of the earth and meet there, the study of their latitudes is then superfluous; what most needs the study of their latitudes is conjunction alone. For when two stars meet with a bodily meeting and their latitude is not both in one direction and of one amount, their conjunction is not a true conjunction and the lower of them does not cover the higher, because their conjunction, when it is in the state that we have described, falls in longitude and in latitude; when they are not in one direction and of one amount, their conjunction is in longitude and not in latitude, and that conjunction is not called a true bodily union, least of all when they are in two different directions: then no meeting is counted for them. When they are bodily united in longitude, in latitude and in direction, they remain conjoined until one of them is away from the other by the amount of half of their two bodies. The notes give two readings of the codex for the word for the cause.
