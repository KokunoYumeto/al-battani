The ascensions of the degree of midheaven are subtracted from those of the degree used when it lies on the eastern side of midheaven, and the ascensions of that degree from those of the degree of midheaven when it lies on the western side; the same is done with its ascensions and those of the part of the cardine of the earth in the right sphere, until the distance between the degree and the degree of midheaven or of the cardine of the earth is known by the right sphere. The times of this distance are divided by the times of the day hours when the degree used is above the earth, or by the times of the night hours when it is below the earth: the hours that result are the distance of the star or the degree from one of the two cardines, midheaven or the cardine of the earth. Whether the star or the degree is below or above the earth is known as follows: look at the part that you want to use of the two parts; if it lies between the degree of the ascendant and the degree of the setting in the order of the signs, that degree is below the earth, and if it lies between the degree of the setting and the degree of the ascendant in the order of the signs, it is above the earth. It is known in another way too: look at half of the stay of the star above the earth; if it is more than the times between the degree of midheaven and the degree that culminates with the star in the right sphere, the star is above the earth; if half of its stay above the earth is the less, it is below the earth. When you know how many seasonal hours the star, or the degree in which it stands, is distant from midheaven or from the cardine of the earth, and want to know its distance from the ascendant or from the setting, subtract those hours from six; the remainder is its distance from one of these two cardines, the cardine of the east or the cardine of the west. Whenever you want to know the ascensions of any degree in the place in which it falls of the regions of the sphere, it is plain from what we have described that this makes known the amount, in times of the equator, between the preceding degree of the sphere of the signs and the degree that follows it, as the amount between two degrees is known by the ascensions of the climate and the ascensions of the right sphere; that is, that you know in how many times of the equator the following degree of the sphere of the signs travels to the place in which the preceding degree was. Look then: if the preceding degree supposed is between midheaven and the cardine of the earth on the side of the east, it is in the eastern half of the sphere; if it is between midheaven and the cardine of the earth on the side of the west, it is in the western half of the sphere. When it is in the eastern half of the sphere, find its distance from midheaven (the sentence runs on). The note: a word that is wanting in the codex.
