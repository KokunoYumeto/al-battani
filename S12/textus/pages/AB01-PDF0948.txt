(Then you count from) the parts of the ruler, from the point of the centre, the amount of the shadow of the one hour, and mark at it, beside the edge of the ruler, a point that is a sign for the shadow of an hour; then you do the like for the shadow of two hours and the azimuth of two hours, and three and four and five, until you come to six hours, and you mark the place of the shadow at them on the line that cuts between north and south toward the wide side, which is the line of midday. Then you turn the ruler to the other quarter, which adjoins the line of midday, and do in it as you did in the quarter before it, so that the shadow of one hour and of two and three and four and five falls on both sides of the line of midday, on the side of the width of the slab, in the direction of east and of west, for the beginning of Capricorn; and you mark a point at the shadow of every hour. Then you do the like with the azimuth of the hours of the head of Cancer, and put their shadow in the other, narrow direction of the slab, as you did with the hours of Capricorn, on both sides of the line of midday, so that the shadow of the end of the sixth hour falls on the line of midday. It is known that the azimuth, when it is northern, is toward what adjoins the narrow side of the slab from the line between east and west, and when it is southern it is toward the side of the width from this line. Then you join the points marked for the hours of the head of Cancer and of the head of Capricorn by straight lines, going from the point of the one hour of the hours of Cancer to the point of the one hour of the hours of Capricorn, and likewise from the point of two hours to the point of two hours, to the end of the five hours on both sides of the line of midday.
