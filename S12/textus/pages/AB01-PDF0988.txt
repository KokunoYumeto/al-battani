% Printed p. 164: chapter 44 continued: the places of the moon at the two visible times, and the eclipsed part of the sun's disc by reckoning. [The hours after] the addition or the subtraction are the hours of the visible clearing that is found. When these two visible times are known, that is the time of the beginning and the time of the clearing, the part of an hour that resulted for the corrected time of the beginning is multiplied by the unequal motion of the moon in the hour; the result is added to the place of the moon for the time of the absolute beginning if the time of the visible beginning is after the absolute time, and taken from it if it is before it; the same is done with the motion in latitude. Likewise the part of the hour that resulted for the clearing is multiplied by the motion of the moon, and it is added to the place of the moon and to the motion in latitude at the time of the absolute clearing if the time of the visible clearing is after the time of the absolute clearing, and taken from them if it is before it. The place of the moon and the motion in latitude after the addition or the subtraction at each of the two times is its place in it. Then the ascendant is set up and the parallax of the moon in latitude is found for it on that side; the latitude of the moon is found as well at each of the two times, with its side, so that the visible latitude of the moon at each of the two times is known in that way, and it is kept. If the digits of the eclipse are to be corrected, so that the amount of the disc of the sun that is eclipsed to the eye is known when its whole area is 12, the way is to make the diameter of the sun always twelve digits, whether its diameter is small or great, and to multiply it by three parts, eight minutes and a half: the result is the circumference of the disc of the sun, 37 digits and 42 minutes of a digit; half of these digits, 18;51, is multiplied by the digits of the half diameter of the sun, which is six, and the area of the disc of the sun comes to 113;6. Then half the corrected diameter of the moon is multiplied by six and divided by half the corrected diameter of the sun: the result is the digits of the half diameter of the moon, which are kept; they are doubled: the digits of the whole diameter of the moon, which are multiplied by three parts, eight minutes and a half: the circumference of the disc of the moon, which is kept. Then six digits, the half diameter of the sun, are added to the digits of the half diameter of the moon, and the digits of the eclipse are taken from the sum: the remainder is the amount between the two centres; doubled it is twice the distance between the two centres. Then the digits of the eclipse are taken from twelve, the remainder is multiplied by the digits of the eclipse and the product divided by twice the distance between the two centres: the result is the sagitta of the disc of the moon; taken from the digits of the eclipse it leaves the sagitta of the disc of the sun; this is taken from twelve, the remainder is multiplied by the sagitta of the disc of the sun and the root of the product is taken: half the common chord, which is kept. Then one looks whether [the next page continues].
