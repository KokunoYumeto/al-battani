% Printed p. 197: the end of chapter 54. The ray of trine is cast at more than one hundred and twenty by as much as that of sextile falls short. When you want to know at how many parts the star casts its ray of sextile and of trine on the circle of the signs when it has a latitude, subtract the latitude of the star from ninety and find the chord of what remains in the tables of the halved chords: it always falls as the chord of the complete second side, which we have mentioned in the chapter on the diagonals of the squares earlier in this book, and in this chapter in which we are, in this place only; keep it, it is the chord of the second side. Then find the complete chord of the latitude of the star, by taking half of its latitude, finding its halved chord and doubling it; what results is the complete chord of the latitude of the star. Multiply it by itself and keep the result under its heading. Then take the chord of the complete second side that you kept and multiply it by sixty; add to the result the product that you kept under its heading and take the root of the sum; take what the result exceeds sixty by and multiply it by itself; divide the result by the chord of the complete second side that you kept and subtract the quotient from sixty; the remainder is the corrected chord; keep it. Then take the excess of the root over sixty a second time and multiply it by the chord of the complete second side that was kept; divide the result by the corrected chord; the quotient is the portion of the correction; keep it. Then take the complete chord of the latitude of the star multiplied by itself and subtract it from three thousand six hundred, which is the product of the chord of the complete sextile by itself; take the root of the remainder and subtract from the root the portion of the correction that you kept; the remainder is the corrected second side. Then subtract the complete chord of the latitude multiplied by itself from three thousand six hundred again and divide the remainder by the corrected second side; the quotient is the chord that you want. Find its arc as the arcs of the complete chords are found, by taking its half, finding its arc in the table and doubling the arc that comes out; the result is the amount of the sextile of the star, in whichever of the two directions its latitude is. Subtract it from 180; the remainder is the amount of the trine of the star. Subtract each of these two amounts from the part of the star and add each of them to the part of the star: the lesser place is the place of its first sextile and trine, the greater place is the place of the second trine and sextile that fall from it on the circle of the signs, if God wills.
