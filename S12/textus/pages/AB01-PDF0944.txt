(The longer line is the line between) east and west, and the shorter line, which runs across the slab, the line between north and south: the line of midday; the sides of the horizon are written at the ends of the lines. The azimuth begins on the circle at the points of east and west: what is southern is counted toward the north and what is northern toward the south, after every quarter of the circle is divided into ninety parts, in black or in red, so that no lasting trace stays on the face of the slab; so too the circle; but the two diameters are cut in, and their trace stays. On every azimuth of the hours of Cancer the mark م is set and on those of Capricorn the mark ل, and at the place of the shadow of every hour its number, beginning from the west; lines join the points along and across the slab. Mecca is set on the side of east and south: on the arc of the difference in latitude, م ك, its amount is taken from the west; a line through the two marks ك parallel to the line of east and west; on the arc between them ن ض; at the crossing of the two lines لا; and the line ه لا ع is the azimuth of Mecca. The length of the gnomon is the line ه س, set upright on the centre ه. Tables are given for the azimuth of the hours of Capricorn and of Cancer, their shadow and their altitude where the latitude is 36 degrees. The upright slab, whose face looks south, is made in the same way in the azimuth; only the shadows change, as told for the upright shadow: it is set upright on the line of east and west, its wide side toward the ground and its narrow side upward; the longest shadow on it is at the head of Cancer and the shortest at the head of Capricorn; the gnomon is again twelve parts of the ruler by which the shadow is measured, and where the tip of the shadow falls on the hour lines shows what has passed of the day in seasonal hours. The hours are known in many ways and by different instruments; these two are the soundest the author has made.
