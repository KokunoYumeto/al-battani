% Printed p. 219: the chapter on the years of the Arabs ended, and the chapter on the months of the Romans. With the sign of the year, count from Sunday: where the count ends is the day with which the year enters, the first day of Muharram of that year. For another month take the sign of the month from the table of the months and add it to the sign of the year; if the sum is more than seven cast out seven, and count what is left from Sunday: the count ends on the name of the day with which that month enters. By this reckoning one works in finding the motions of the stars in the tables laid down for the era of the Arabs. To know the leap year of the Arabs, cast out the years of the Hijra by thirties and keep what is left for these letters: a year that meets one of them is a leap year, and one that does not is none; the letters are 2, 5, 7, 10, 13, 16, 18, 21, 24, 26, 29; this is shown in the single years of the Arabs in the table for finding the signs, if God wills. Title: «Chapter on knowing the beginnings of the months of the Romans by the era of Dhu l-Qarnayn, divided by 28, with the addition of a year.» He said: to know the first day of any month of the months of the Romans and on which day of the week it falls, take the years of Dhu l-Qarnayn with the year you are in, counted from the entry of Aylul, even if only one day of it has entered, and add to them one year, always; cast out twenty-eight after twenty-eight, look for what is left, less than twenty-eight or twenty-eight, in the tables of the single Roman years and take what stands beside it in the line of the month sought: that is the sign of that month; count it from Sunday, and the count brings you to the name of the day with which that month enters; the first of Aylul is the first day of the year. The leap year is known from the mark found beside the month Shubat, where «leap» stands every four years, if God wills.
