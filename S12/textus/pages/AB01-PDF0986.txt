% Printed p. 166: chapter 44 continued: the sides of the darkening of the eclipse of the sun on the horizon, and the eclipse of the sun by the tables approximately. [The degrees are turned into their arc from the table of] the chords; the arc that results is the amount of the inclination of the darkening or of the clearing at each of the two times. At the beginning of the eclipse the inclination of the darkening is drawn from the point of the azimuth of the setting degree at that time on the circle of the horizon toward the side of the visible latitude of the moon in it; at the end of the clearing the arc that results is drawn from the point of the azimuth of the rising degree then toward the side of the visible latitude of the moon at the time of the clearing. At the middle of the eclipse the side of the darkening is at a right angle to the ecliptic, and its azimuth on the circle of the horizon is bounded by the arc that passes through the two poles of the ecliptic and through the centre of the moon and the horizon, according to the distance of the sun and of the moon from the equator. To know the side of the darkening at the middle of the eclipse, the angle of longitude in it is found in the way used for knowing the parallax of the moon and drawn from the point of the azimuth of the ascendant of the middle of the eclipse, or of its setting point, on the circle of the horizon, according to the place of the eclipse from one of the two horizons, toward the side of the latitude of the moon; where it ends on the circle of the horizon, toward the azimuth of that degree of the horizon the darkening inclines at the middle of the eclipse: that is, if the sun is toward the west, one counts from the side of the azimuth of the setting degree, and if it is toward the east, from the side of the azimuth of the rising degree. «If you want to know the eclipse of the sun» by the tables approximately: when it is known that the sun is eclipsed or that it can be eclipsed, the distance of the true equal hours of the conjunction from the time of noon in the town wanted is taken; this is known by turning the hours of the conjunction into the hours of that town; then, if the conjunction is before noon, the hours are taken from twenty-four hours, and if it is after noon, until sunset, the hours are taken as they are; the result in either way is the distance of the hours of the conjunction from the meridian. With it the parallax of the moon in longitude is found from the tables drawn for the parallax of the moon in the climates, until its parallax is known corrected by the fourth table of the tables of the equation for the degree in which the moon is, in that way; this is divided by the motion of the moon in the hour: the result is the hours of the first difference. If the distance of the moon from the ascendant is less than ninety, the hours of the first difference are taken from the hours of the true conjunction; if the distance is more than 90, they are added to the hours of the conjunction. Then its distance from the meridian is taken a second time, and with it the parallax of the degree of the moon in longitude is found, corrected by the fourth table [the next page continues].
