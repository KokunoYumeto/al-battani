% Printed p. 184: chapter 50 continued: Mars, Jupiter and the beginning of Saturn. [They found the diameter of Mars one part of] twenty of the diameter of the sun; when its mean distance is divided by the twenty, it comes to 229 times and a fifth of a time; when that is divided by 201;30, which is the diameter of the earth, its diameter comes to be like the diameter of the earth once and less than a seventh of a time, approximately. Therefore the diameter of Mars subtends two degrees, a minute and thirty-seven seconds, approximately, of the circle of the sphere. When that is multiplied in length, breadth and depth, the magnitude of Mars comes to be like the magnitude of the earth once and a third, less a little something that cannot be confined. As for the magnitude of its epicycle and its width, it is known from the motion of the planet Mars from its first station to its second station. The epicycle goes in a day 0;31, and Mars moves in the epicycle in a day 0;28, so that its motion is left at the amount of three minutes; from that it is known that it stays in a sign five months or six, because of the difference of its motion as it is seen; in truth it neither grows nor lessens, but is one motion for all time, for it and for the rest of the planets. The diameter of the epicycle of Mars subtends 82 degrees and 28 minutes. «Jupiter». As for the distance of Jupiter and its magnitude, they found its magnitude when it is at its nearest distance to its magnitude when it comes to its farthest distance like thirty-seven to twenty-three, that is one and a half and a ninth. When that is multiplied by the farthest distance of Mars, which is eight thousand and twenty-two, the farthest distance of Jupiter comes to twelve thousand nine hundred and twenty-four, approximately, and its mean distance is likewise ten thousand four hundred and seventy-three. They found the magnitude of Jupiter at its mean distance one part of twelve of the diameter of the sun; when its mean distance is divided, its diameter comes to eight hundred and ninety-two and a half and a quarter, approximately; and when it is measured against the two hundred and one and a half, it is like the diameter of the earth four times and a third, less a little something. When that is multiplied in length, breadth and depth, its magnitude is like the magnitude of the earth nearly eighty-one times, and its diameter subtends 8;18 of the circle of the sphere, approximately. From [its motion from] its first station to its second station, and the motion of its epicycle, which is five minutes in a day in the order of the signs, and its motion at the bottom of its epicycle, 54 minutes in every day, in what is seen as though toward the west, it is plain that the diameter of its epicycle subtends 22 degrees. As for the distance of «Saturn»: the difference of its magnitude to the sight between its nearest nearness and its farthest distance is, as they found it, like one and two fifths of one to one, and that is the amount of seven to five. When that is multiplied by the farthest distance of Jupiter, the farthest distance of Saturn comes to eighteen thousand and ninety-four [the next page continues].
