% Printed p. 177: chapter 48 continued: the first appearances and disappearances of the planets, and the arc of vision. [Mercury: from 180 to] 248 it rises in the mornings, and from 248 to 360 it sets in the mornings; its state in swiftness and slowness is as was defined for Venus. «If you want to know» the first appearance of Saturn, Jupiter and Mars in the mornings in the east, which is their first coming out from under the rays to the limit of visibility, when the sun has passed them: reckon for one of them, and if its corrected anomaly is about 20 degrees, say that it is seen. As for its setting in the evenings, when the sun overtakes it and hides it and covers it with its rays, it is reckoned so when the corrected anomaly is about 340. As for Venus and Mercury: if the anomaly of one of them is about 20, it is its first appearance in the evenings in the west; if it is about 340 degrees, it is its first disappearance in the mornings in the east; if it is about 160, it is its first disappearance in the evenings in the west; and if it is about 200, it is its first appearance in the mornings in the east. If you want to know the first rising of one of them and its disappearance by reckoning, know the arc of vision on the equator for each of them: the amount of the arc of vision is for Saturn 14 degrees, for Jupiter 12;45, for Mars 14;30, for Venus 5;40 and for Mercury 11;30. Then know the distance of the planet from the equator and the degree that culminates with it, according to its latitude; know from that the half of its day arc, which is the half of its stay above the earth, and the times of the ascension of the degree that rises with it or sets, for those sides. If there is between the planet and the sun, in degrees of the ascensions or of the settings, as much as the arc of vision named for it, the planet is seen on that day or is hidden. If the reckoning is for the visibility and the appearance from under the rays, and what is between it and the sun is less than the arc of vision, it has not appeared; if it is more, it has appeared. If the reckoning is for the disappearance, and the distance between it and the sun, in times of the settings and of the ascensions according to the horizon at which it is, is less than the arc of vision, it has disappeared; if it is more, it has not disappeared. «One ought» to reckon the planet for the rising of the sun or for its setting at the time at which its corrected anomaly comes to be about what was described. If you want to know since when it rose or until when it will rise, or since when it was hidden or until when it will be hidden, know the amount between the arc of vision and the distance of the planet from the sun and keep it; then divide it by the true corrected course of the planet. «The knowledge of that» is as I describe: reckon the planet either one day after that day or one day before it, as is needed; what is between the two places is the true motion of the planet, which is taken from [the next page continues]. The notes give the readings of the codex: 26 for 20, twice; the verb without its pronoun; for 340 a numeral whose first letter is the Maghrebin 300, twice; 170 for 200; 16 for 14; 16;30 for 14;30; 6;40 for 5;40.
