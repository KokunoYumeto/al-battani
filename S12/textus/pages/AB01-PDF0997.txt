% Printed p. 155: chapter 43 continued: the construction of the figure of the lunar eclipse. From the two marks of the moon's latitude for the beginning of the eclipse and for the end of the clearing a line is drawn parallel to the line between east and west: that of the beginning of the eclipse towards the west from the centre of the circle, that of the end of the clearing towards the east; where the two lines meet the circumference of the circle of the half of the two diameters two marks are made and joined by a straight line that passes through the centre of the moon for the middle of the eclipse. On that line the moon passes from the beginning of the eclipse to the end of the clearing: its part from the western circumference to the mark of the latitude for the middle of the eclipse is the amount of the minutes of the fall and of the stay from the beginning of the eclipse to its middle, and the part from that mark to the eastern point the minutes of the fall and of the stay from the middle to the end of the clearing; the two necessarily differ in length. With the half diameter of the moon, taken from the divided line, three circles are drawn: one about the western point of the circle of the half of the two diameters, where the parallel line bounds it, one about the eastern point (these two necessarily touch the circle of the shadow: the western one is the moon at the beginning of the eclipse, the eastern one the moon at the end of the clearing), and one about the mark of the latitude for the middle of the eclipse. If this one falls wholly in the circle of the shadow, the moon is wholly eclipsed and stays in the darkness according to what lies between its circle and the circle of the shadow; if it is inside the circle of the shadow and touches it, the moon is wholly eclipsed and has no stay; if it does not fall wholly in it, the part of the moon's circle that the circle of the shadow cuts off is eclipsed, the segment that falls in the circle of the shadow, whose diameter and area are known. The example: the circle of the two diameters, with K M on it, about the centre Z, and inside it the circle of the shadow, S H, about the same centre. To show the azimuth of the inclination of the darkenings and of the clearing on the circle of the horizon, a third, great circle is drawn about Z, with the circle of the half of the two diameters inside it: the circle of the horizon, A B G D. The three circles are quartered by two lines that cut each other at right angles at Z, the diameters A G and B D: A is the point of the south, G of the north, B of the east and D of the west. The latitude of the moon is taken to be southern; on its latitude for the beginning of the eclipse the point T is marked, on that for the middle of the eclipse the point E, on that for the end of the clearing the point L; the lines T K and L M are drawn parallel to the diameter B D, and K is joined to M by a line that passes through a point (the sentence runs on to the next page).
