[The beginning of the eclipse is from the point Ṭ, in the azimuth, on the circle of the horizon, of the arc A Q;] and likewise the end of the clearing is from the point N, which is in the azimuth, on the circle of the horizon, of the arc S G. It is known that the point A is the azimuth of the setting degree and the mark G the mark of the azimuth of the rising degree; likewise the azimuth of the middle of the eclipse is at a right angle, where the line E D cuts the circle of the horizon, according to its distance from the middle of the sphere and its nearness to the horizon, as was shown for the moon and the sun. «In this there is enough», and that is what we wanted to show, if God wills. The figure stands at the left of these lines: the circle of the half of the two diameters and the circle of the sun about the centre E, the two diameters with the four sides written at their ends (south at the top, north at the foot, east at the left, west at the right), the three circles of the moon about Q, Ḥ and S, and the lines E Q, E S, ʿ Q and S M. Chapter 45: «On knowing the places of the wandering stars on the ecliptic by the era of the Arabs and of the Romans». To know the place of any of the planets, the mean motion of the planet is found for the day wanted and the hour supposed, in hours of al-Raqqa, by whichever of the two eras, as described for knowing the mean motion of the sun; the mean motion of the sun is found as well for the time for which the mean motion of the planet is found. If the reckoning is for Saturn, Jupiter and Mars, the mean motion of whichever of them is reckoned is taken from the mean motion of the sun: the remainder is the anomaly of the planet. If the reckoning is for Venus and Mercury, what comes out of the table is the anomaly of the planet, and the mean motion of the sun [is the mean motion of the planet, as the next page continues]. The notes: of the letter S of the arc only the first part is left in the codex; the codex has «the azimuth» for «the point»; the codex has «from» for «the circle», and Plato has «hemisphaerium» for «the circle of the horizon».
