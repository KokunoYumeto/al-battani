Title, in two lines: «Chapter on knowing the times of the revolutions of the years and their ascendants, and the excesses of the mean places of the planets in them over their mean places at the root, and that by the tables set for it at the end of the book.» He said: whenever you want to know the times of the revolutions of the years, their ascendants and their excesses in the mean places over the root mean places of the planets, take the whole years gone for the nativity from the year of the root to the year of the revolution, having learnt the mean and the true place of the Sun at the root. Seek the like of the number of whole years you have in the table of the collected Roman years; where you find it, or what is nearest to it of what is less than it, take what you find under it of the times of the ascensions and keep it. Then enter with what is left of the years in the table of the single years, take what stands opposite it of those times also and add it to the first; what it comes to, after a revolution is cast out if it is more than a revolution, keep it and divide it by 15: the result is equal hours. Add them to the hours of the reckoning at the root; if the hours come to more than twenty-four cast out twenty-four and add a day to the days gone of the month of the root; if they are less than twenty-four work with them. What comes out are the days gone of the month and the hours. See whether the year is a leap year and Shubat has passed: then take one day from the days gone of the month; if it is not a leap year take nothing away. Then compute the Sun for that date of that year: the mean place of the Sun comes out for you like the first mean place of the root; correct it by the equation according to the place of its greatest distance in the year of the revolution. If the true place of the Sun comes out like the first, well; if not, learn its excess over the first or its defect from it, and divide that excess or that defect by the unequal motion of the Sun in an hour: the hours that result add to the hours of the reckoning if the second place of the Sun is less than the first, and take them away if it is the greater. Divide them by fifteen and add to them the ascensions of the degree of the Sun in the right sphere, and learn by it the ascendant and mid-heaven as usual.
