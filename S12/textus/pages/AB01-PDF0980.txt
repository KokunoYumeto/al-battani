% Printed p. 172: chapter 45 continued: the reckoning of the place of a planet. [For Venus and Mercury the mean motion of the sun] is what is used in the place of their two mean motions, since it is what drives their places. When the mean motion of the planet and its anomaly are known, the apogee of the planet is taken from the mean motion of the planet: the remainder is the centre. Its like is sought in the two columns of the number of the tables of the equation of the planet, and what is opposite it in the third table, headed «equation of the anomaly and of the centre», is taken and set down under the anomaly and under the centre together. If the centre is less than 180 degrees, the equation is taken from the centre and added to the anomaly; if the centre is more than 180, the equation is added to the centre and taken from the anomaly: what each of the two comes to after the addition or the subtraction is the corrected centre and the corrected anomaly. The corrected centre is entered in the two columns of the number, and what is opposite it of the minutes of the difference of the two distances, written in the fourth table, is taken; one finds out whether they are additive or subtractive, which is known from the mark at the head of the column or from its increase or decrease following the increase of the number; except for Mercury, whose increase and decrease are known from the mark only, because by the swiftness of its motion in its epicycle it falls on the side of its nearest distance twice in the circuit of the ecliptic. If these minutes are subtractive, the corrected anomaly of the planet is entered in the tables of the equation of the planet, in the two columns of the number, and what is opposite it is taken in the fifth table, headed «the farthest distance», and also in the sixth table, headed «the mean distance»; if the minutes are additive, what is opposite the corrected anomaly is taken in the sixth table and in the seventh table, headed «the nearest distance». What came from the fifth table is multiplied by the minutes that came from the fourth table and divided by sixty: the result is taken from what was set down from the sixth table. What came from the seventh table is multiplied by the minutes of the fourth table and divided by sixty: the result is added to what was set down from the sixth table. What the sixth table comes to after the addition or the subtraction is taken from the centre corrected by the third table if the number of the corrected anomaly is more than 180, and added to it if the corrected anomaly is less than 180. What the centre comes to after the addition or the subtraction is the place of the planet from the point of the farthest distance; the apogee of the planet, which was taken from its mean motion at the beginning, is added to it: the sum is the true place of the planet. It is cast off from the beginning of Aries, thirty for every sign, in the usual way: where it ends, there is the place of the planet on the ecliptic. If there are minutes with the centre or the anomaly, their share of the difference of the equation is taken in the way shown at the opening of the book for the equation of the sun and of the moon. «The apogees of the planets» in their epicycles in the year 1191 of the years of Dhū l-Qarnayn [were as the next page says].
