% Printed p. 216: chapter 57 continued, with the figure of the long alidade. By this one knows always the distance of the sun from the zenith on every day and its altitude above the horizon; and the «brick» must be squared evenly, with a right angle. «And this is the figure of the long alidade». He said: take three rulers of wood, even, with squared faces, and draw along the middle of each a line through its length; the lines through the middles of the rulers are shown in the figure: the ruler ز ح, the ruler ز ل and the ruler ط م. Mark on the ruler ز ح the mark ط and make the line ز ط five cubits, and fix the line ط ح, the rest of the ruler, in a stone or a pillar so firmly that it does not give way, change or shake. Then take the second ruler, the smaller one, and make its line ز ل equal to the line ز ط, and put on it two sights of brass across its breadth, equal in size, like the sight of the astrolabe, mounted firmly on the face of the ruler, with two holes facing each other in their middles, one sight near the point ز and the other near the point ل. Pierce these two rulers at the mark ز and join them by a pivot, fastened as the pivot of the astrolabe is fastened, so that the ruler ز ل moves toward north and south as wished, without wobbling, shaking or bending. Then take the ruler ط م and make its line ط ك equal to each of the lines ز ط and ز ل, then divide the line ط ك into thirty parts and between the parts into as many minutes as possible, a true division in equal measures, and divide the line ك م
