% Printed p. 182: chapter 50 continued: the elements, and the distance and the magnitude of Mercury. [The distance of the moon from the earth at that time is,] in that measure, 33;33; what is above that is the ether, in which the stars run. As for the two remaining elements, which are water and earth, their end is the amount of the half diameter of the earth. These four elements, which are earth, water, air and fire, are the roots of the earthly natures and the cause of coming to be and of passing away; by their difference the things differ, according to what stands over them of the sun, the moon and the stars, and their change is in what lies between the nearest nearness of the moon and the earth; by that every thing of animal and plant changes. What is in this bound between the centre of the earth and the ether is, as we said, at a distance from the earth of 33;33 in the measure in which the half diameter of the earth is one part, and that is the utmost of earth, air, water and fire; what is above that is a fifth nature, of which no truth is said, which sense does not reach, and whose quality reason does not encompass. Of it is the sphere of «Mercury», which is above the sphere of the moon. What appeared of its distance and its magnitude, according to what they attained: it is told that they measured its magnitude at its farthest distance and its nearest nearness to the earth, and found the difference of its magnitude like the amount of two and a third and a quarter to one. If the nearest distance of Mercury is like the farthest distance of the moon, which has been shown to be 64;10, then, when that is multiplied by the two and the third and the quarter that are the difference of the magnitude of its diameter, its farthest distance comes to a hundred and sixty-six times the half diameter of the earth; and when the half of what is between its farthest distance and its nearest distance is taken and added to its nearest distance, its mean distance is 115. Then they measured its magnitude when it was at its mean distance, against the sun at its mean distance, and found its diameter one part of fifteen of the diameter of the sun. When the hundred and fifteen are divided by the fifteen, there come seven parts and two thirds of a part. Since the diameter of the sun is five times and a half the diameter of the earth, when the mean distance of the sun is made 1108, as we showed by measuring, the diameter of the earth in that measure is two hundred and one and a half; and when those seven parts and two thirds of a part are measured against the two hundred and one and a half, they are found to be one part of twenty-six and a quarter of them, approximately. Since the diameter of the earth subtends one degree and fifty-seven minutes of the circle of the sphere, the diameter of Mercury subtends four minutes and a half and a sixth of a minute, approximately. When that is multiplied in length, breadth and depth, the magnitude of the body of Mercury comes to one part of seventeen [thousand] parts of the body of the earth, approximately. Then they looked at the magnitude of «Venus» and its distance, and found the difference of its magnitude between its farthest and its nearest distance like the amount of two of thirteen; so when the hundred and sixty-six are multiplied [as the next page continues].
