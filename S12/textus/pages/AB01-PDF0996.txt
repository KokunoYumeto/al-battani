% Printed p. 156: the end of chapter 43: the example on the figure of the lunar eclipse. [The line from K to M passes through the point] E; the text names K the centre of the moon's circle for the middle of the eclipse and M the centre of its circle for the end of the clearing, and the line K E M passes through the three centres of the moon's circles: on it the moon passes from the beginning of the eclipse to the end of the clearing. The circle about K touches the circle of the shadow at the mark S, and the circle about M touches it at the point H. So, when the lines M H Z La and K S Z Q are drawn, the line K S Z Q is the azimuth of the beginning of the eclipse on the circle A B G D, bounded by the arc B Q, and the line M H Z La the azimuth of the end of the clearing on that circle, bounded by the arc D La; D is the azimuth of the setting degree and B that of the rising degree at every time. Each of the triangles Z T K and Z L M is right-angled; each of the lines Z K and Z M is the known half of the two diameters, and each of Z T and Z L is known: so each of T K and L M, the remaining sides, is known. Likewise the angles of the triangles M L E and K T E are right and the lines L E and T E are known, so each of K E and E M is known: they are the amount of the fall and of the stay, K E from the beginning of the eclipse to its middle and E M from the middle to the end of the clearing; in the figure K E is plainly greater than E M, which was to be shown. It is also plain that, if the moon's circle about E, for the middle of the eclipse, has entered the circle of the shadow and touches it on no side, the moon is wholly eclipsed and enters the darkness by the amount of the stay between the two circles; and that, when the moon is not wholly eclipsed, the azimuth of the darkening at the middle of the eclipse is always on the line Z Ḍ, which stands at a right angle on the ecliptic: for the line B D is always the line of half the ecliptic, B being the east point, from which the rising degree rises, and D the west point, by which the setting degree is known on the circle of the horizon; as the line stands in its distance from the east or the west point, towards that the azimuth of the darkening inclines at the middle of the eclipse.
