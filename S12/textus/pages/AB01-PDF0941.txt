The places of the fixed stars of all the constellations, or of as many as wished, are drawn in this way: the latitude of the star is taken from the equator with the compasses; one leg is set on the part where the star is and the other turned toward the latitude, drawing a faint line that leaves no lasting trace on the globe; another pair of compasses is opened by the quarter of the circle that goes round the globe, one leg set on the quadrature of the star's part of the zodiac, ninety parts from the star's degree, so that the other leg falls of necessity on the part where the star is; it is turned toward the line drawn for the latitude: where the two lines cut is the centre of the star, and it is drawn there; so for all the stars wished, each by its place in longitude and latitude, after a circle has been passed, if wished, through every sign and through the two poles of the zodiac, to make the cuts of the signs plainer: twelve circles on the back of the globe through the poles of the zodiac, dividing the signs. Then a ring of brass is made, with upright faces, truly round, its edges even, its thickness the breadth of a thumb and its depth as much as its strength needs so that it does not shake; and another ring like it by the same rule, whose inside is turned with the compasses of the inside of the first and its outside with those of its outside, filed until it is even on every side and truly round; the width of each of these two rings is the diameter of the globe, so that the globe turns inside them, filling them. Two more rings are made, the thickness of the one a third of the thickness of one of the two rings and of the other two thirds, so that the smaller set in the greater they are as one ring of the two: the inside of the smaller is turned with the compasses of the inside of the two rings and its outside as needed, and the inside of the greater with the compasses of the outside of the smaller and its outside with the compasses of the outside of the two rings. A fifth ring is made, its inside turned with the compasses of the outside of the great rings named and its outside as needed, so that these rings turn inside it, filling it, without shaking, their faces even. One of the two first rings is made the ring of the horizon; it and the other rings are divided into four equal quarters, every quarter into eighteen houses and every house (…).
