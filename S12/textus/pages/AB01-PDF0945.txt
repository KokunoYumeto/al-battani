% Printed p. 207: chapter 56 continued, with the figure of the sundial. (According to the place of Mecca from) that town in longitude, toward the side of Mecca on which it lies in latitude; where the count ends, mark a sign on the circumference of the circle, and draw a straight line from the centre of the circle to that sign: that line is the azimuth of Mecca from that town. [The figure: the slab with its circle, the lines of the hours between the curve of Cancer and the curve of Capricorn, south at the top and north at the foot.] And in the manner of the division of these two quarters you divide the two remaining quarters, if God will. He said: you take a wide slab of marble, an oblong square, and mark at its corners A, B, G, D; you take, at two thirds of its breadth and at the middle of its length, a centre, with the sign E on it, and describe a circle on it and quarter it by two lines that cut each other at right angles; you carry them through to the edges of the slab, and you make the one line, the longer, which stretches along the length of the slab, the line between (the sentence runs on). The notes give an addition of the codex after the first paragraph (that the picture of the slab is drawn on the other side) and a reading of the codex.
