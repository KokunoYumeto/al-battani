% Printed p. 174: the beginning of chapter 47: «On knowing the latitudes of the five wandering stars». To know the latitudes of the five planets and their sides: if the reckoning is for Saturn, Jupiter and Mars, the centre of one of them corrected by the third table is entered — for Saturn with an addition of fifty, for Jupiter with a subtraction of twenty, and for Mars as it is, without addition or subtraction — in the two columns of the number of the tables of the latitudes of the five planets, which go up by six degrees, and what is opposite it of the minutes of the shares of the latitude for all of them is taken, which are written at the end of those tables, in the ninth table: the result is set down. If the number entered falls in the upper half, which is the half of the number from 0 to 90 and from 270 to 360, the corrected anomaly of the planet is entered in the two columns of the number of those tables, and what is opposite it is taken in the table of the northern latitude of the planet, called the apogee («afījiyūn») of the north: of it the amount of the minutes of the shares of the latitude out of sixty is taken. If it falls in the lower half, which is from 90 to 180 and from 180 to 270, what is opposite that corrected anomaly is taken in the table of the southern latitude of the planet, called the perigee («farījiyūn») of the south: of it the amount of the minutes of the shares out of sixty is taken. What comes from whichever of the two sides it turned out to be is the latitude of the planet on the side on which it is found. If the reckoning is for Venus and Mercury, their corrected anomaly is entered in those tables and what is opposite it is taken in the table of the inclination and in the table of the slant: each of the two is set down as it is. If the reckoning is for Venus, it is left as it is; if it is for Mercury only and the centre corrected by the third table falls in the upper half mentioned, a tenth of it, that is one part of its ten parts, is taken from the slant alone; if it falls in the lower half, one part of its ten parts is added to the slant alone. What it comes to after the addition or the subtraction is the corrected slant of Mercury: it is set down in the place of what came from the table, and the first is struck out. Then there is added to the corrected centre [what the next page says]. The notes: for the two Greek names the codex has «afnjiyūn» and «frnjiyūn», Plato «effregion» and again «effregio»; of the word «the centre» only the last letter is left in the codex.
