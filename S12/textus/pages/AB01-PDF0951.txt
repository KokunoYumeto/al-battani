Find the distance of the preceding degree from midheaven, from the cardine of the earth or from the ascendant, whichever you wish, in seasonal hours, and keep it. Then look at the following degree: if it is with the preceding one in the eastern half of the sphere, take the distance between the two degrees by the ascensions of the right sphere and the distance between them by the ascensions of the given climate. If the two numbers are equal, that is the distance of the preceding degree from the following degree in times of the equator. If they differ, subtract the less from the greater and take a sixth of the remainder, which is the share of one hour in the difference; multiply it by the hours of the distance of the preceding degree from the cardine to which you wish to refer it, midheaven, the cardine of the ascendant or the cardine of the earth. If you multiplied it by the hours of the distance of the degree from midheaven or from the cardine of the earth, add the product to the times of the ascensions found between the two degrees in the right sphere when these are less than those between them by the ascensions of the climate, and subtract it from them when they are the greater. If you multiplied it by the hours of the distance of the degree from the ascendant, add the product to the times between the two degrees by the ascensions of the climate when these are the less, and subtract it from them when they are the greater. What the times of the ascensions of the cardine of reference come to, after the addition or the subtraction, is the distance between those two degrees by the ascensions of the place of the preceding degree in the eastern half of the sphere. If the preceding degree supposed is in the western half of the sphere and the following degree with it, take the times of the ascensions between them in the right sphere and the times of the ascensions between the two degrees opposite to those two degrees in that climate, which is the amount between the two degrees in times of the settings of the climate; then take a sixth of the difference between these two numbers and multiply it by the hours of the distance of the degree from whichever of the cardines you wish, the cardine of the earth, the cardine of the west or the cardine of midheaven. Add the product to the times of the ascensions or of the settings found for the cardine of reference when they are the less, and subtract it from them when they are the greater, in the manner described before: if your reference is to the cardine of the west, you add it to the settings between the two degrees in the climate when they are the less and subtract it from them when they are greater than those between them in the right sphere; if your reference is to the cardine of the earth or to midheaven, you add it to what is between the two degrees by the ascensions of the right sphere when they are the less and subtract it from them when they are the greater. The result is the distance between the two degrees by the settings of the place in which the preceding degree was (the sentence runs on). The notes give three readings of the codex.
