% Printed p. 224: the chapter on the revolutions of the years ended, and the beginning of a chapter without a number on the directing of degrees. Of the excess of the mean places of the planets and of the node: for the Moon, its anomaly and the node, add what resulted for each of them to its mean place at the root; for the three upper planets add it to their mean places at the root and take it from the anomaly of each of them at the root; for Venus and Mercury add what results for each of them to its anomaly at the root, and their mean places are like the mean place of the Sun. When you have done that, correct them as usual. Know that the times of the revolutions fall short by one day in every hundred and six years; so whenever a hundred and six years have passed, take one day, always, from the days gone of the month of the root, if God wills. This does not come about in nativities; it comes about in the lives of cities, dynasties and religions, when their revolution is needed, if God the exalted wills. Title: «Chapter on directing the degrees from where you will to where you like, nearly.» Know that this directing is what Ptolemy explained on the directing of the hyleg; he called the ascensions in it al-juwī rāst, and said: the juwī rāst of Capricorn, which are the ascensions of the right sphere, and the juwī rāst of the country, which are the ascensions of the country; it is the equation of their distance from the cardines, if God the exalted wills. He said: when you want to direct some degree to some place, look at the degree from which you want to direct, which is the first degree, and at the second degree to which you want to direct, which is the other. Then take the ascensions of the right sphere of the first degree from the ascensions of the right sphere of the second degree; what is left is the first remainder; keep it. Then take the ascensions of the first degree in the clime from the ascensions of the second degree in that clime, as you did first with them in the ascensions of the right sphere; what is left is the second remainder; keep it also. Then take the excess between the first remainder and the second remainder, and learn whose the excess is, the first remainder's or the second's; keep it and make it plain by its name: it is the remainder of the two remainders. Then take the distance of the first from the degree of the cardine that is before it; if the cardine before it is the tenth, call this distance from the half of the arc of the day; take that name from the remainder of the two remainders: what results is the outcome.
